# Chapter Four: Trade Union Act

## Chapter Four: Trade Union Act (Slide 1)

## Trade Union

Trade union, also called labour union, association of workers in a particular trade, industry, or company created for the purpose of securing improvements in pay, benefits, working conditions, or social and political status through collective bargaining.

---

<!-- Slide 3 -->
## Objectives of Trade Union

1. To improve the standard of living of employees.
2. To protect the interests of the employees.
3. To negotiate regarding employees’ interests.
4. To improve the financial support of employees.
5. Improve the social status of the employees.
6. To prevent employee and employer exploitation.
7. Taking effective measures for promoting relations between two parties.
8. To secure bonus for the workers from the profit.
9. To secure the welfare of employees through group schemes.
10. To secure organizational growth, stability, and leadership.

---

<!-- Slide 4 -->
## Functions of Trade Union

1. Establishing contacts between the workers and the employers.
2. Receive appropriate wages
3. Ensuring the safety of work
4. Reducing working hours
5. Stop exploitation
6. Improve the condition of work
7. Arrangement of insurance

---

<!-- Slide 5 -->
## Registration of Trade Union

Registration of a trade union is the process by which the trade union gets the legal existence as an entity that is separated from its members.

- According to the Bangladesh Labour Code 2006, the Registration of a trade union obtains certain benefits, and legal existence as an entity separate from its members.

---

<!-- Slide 6 -->
## Legal Status of A Registered Trade Union

1. A registered trade union is a statutory body.
2. It has a legal entity.
3. It can own property and can act as an agent.
4. It can acquire and hold both moveable and immovable properties
5. It can contract and sue and be sued by the name in which it is registered.

---

<!-- Slide 7 -->
## Rights & Privileges of A Registered Trade Union

1. Incorporation
2. Protection from criminal prosecution
3. Protection from civil suit in certain cases
4. Enforceability of agreements
5. Right to inspect books
6. Right of minors to be a member
7. Change the name
8. Amalgamation of unions

---

## Methods/Process for Registration of A Trade Union

1. Application for registration(Sec-177)
2. Requirements for application(Sec-178)
3. Requirements for Registration(Sec-179)
4. Registration(Sec-182)

---

<!-- Slide 9 -->
## Requirements for Application (Part 1)

1. the name of the trade union and the address of its head office,
2. the date of formation of the trade union,
3. the names, ages, addresses, occupations, and posts in the union of the officers of the trade union;
4. statement of total paid membership;
5. the name of the establishment to which the trade union relates and the total number of member- unions;

---

<!-- Slide 10 -->
## Requirements for Application (Part 2)

6. in case of a federation of trade unions, the name, addresses and registration numbers of member-unions;
7. three copies of the constitution of the trade union together with a copy of the resolution by the members of the trade union adopting such constitution bearing the signature of the Chairman of the meeting;

---

<!-- Slide 11 -->
## Requirements for Application (Part 3)

8. a copy of the resolution by the members of the trade union authorizing its president and secretary to apply for its registration; and
9. in case of a federation of trade unions a copy of the resolution from each of the constituent unions agreeing to become a member of the federation.

---

<!-- Slide 12 -->
## Requirements for Registration (Part 1)

1. the name and address of the trade union;
2. the objects for which the trade union has been formed;
3. the manner in which a worker may become a member of the trade union specifying therein that no worker shall be enrolled as its member unless he applies in the form set out in the constitution declaring that he is not a member of any other trade union;

---

<!-- Slide 13 -->
## Requirements for Registration (Part 2)

4. the sources of the fund of the trade union and statement of the purposes for which such fund shall be applicable;
5. the conditions under which a member shall be entitled to any benefit assured by the constitution of the trade union and under which any fine or forfeiture may be imposed on him;

---

<!-- Slide 14 -->
## Requirements for Registration (Part 3)

6. the maintenance of a list of the members of the trade union and of adequate facilities for the inspection thereof by the officers and members of the trade union;
7. the manner in which the constitution shall be amended, varied, or rescinded;

---

<!-- Slide 15 -->
## Requirements for Registration (Part 4)

8. the safe custody of the funds of a trade union, its annual audit, the manner of audit, and adequate facilities for inspection of the books of account by the officers and members of a trade union;
9. the manner in which the trade union may be dissolved;

---

<!-- Slide 16 -->
## Requirements for Registration (Part 5)

10. the manner of election of officers by the general body of the trade union and the term, not less than two years and not exceeding three years,
11. the number of members of the executive which shall not be less than five and more than thirty-five as may be prescribed by rules;

---

<!-- Slide 17 -->
## Requirements for Registration (Part 6)

12. the procedure for expressing want of confidence in any officer of the trade union; and
13. the meetings of the executive and of the general body of the trade union, so that the executive shall meet at least once every three months and the general body at least once every year.

---

<!-- Slide 18 -->
## Registration (Sec-182)

The director of Labour, on being satisfied that a trade union has complied with all the requirements of this chapter, shall register the trade union in a prescribed register and issue a registration certificate in the prescribed form within a period of sixty days from the date of receipt of the application for registration.

---

<!-- Slide 19 -->
## Reasons for Cancellation of Registration of Trade Union (Slide 19)

1. If the trade union has applied for such cancellation.
2. If the existence gets abolished.
3. If the trade union has obtained registration by fraud or misrepresentation of facts.
4. If the trade union has contravened or violated any of the fundamental provisions of the constitution.

---

<!-- Slide 20 -->
## Reasons for Cancellation of Registration of Trade Union (Slide 20)

5. If the trade union has committed any unfair labor practice.
6. If the trade union has a membership that has fallen below the number required.
7. If the trade union has contravened any of the provisions of this chapter or the rules.

<!-- Slide 21 -->
## Collective Bargaining

Collective bargaining is the process in which working people, through their unions, negotiate contracts with their employers to determine their terms of employment, including pay, benefits, hours, leave, job health and safety policies, ways to balance work and family, and more.

---

<!-- Slide 22 -->
## Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA)

Collective bargaining agent is a registered trade union elected by secret ballot.

According to Bangladesh Labour Code 2006,
Where there is only one registered trade union in an establishment that trade union shall be deemed to be the collective bargaining agent for such establishment.

---

<!-- Slide 23 -->
## Power & Rights of Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA) (Slide 23)

1. bargain with the employer in matters of the state of jobless, conditions of work, or environment of work of the workers;
2. represent all or any of the workers in any proceedings;
3. give notice of, and declare, a strike in accordance with the provisions of this Chapter;

---

<!-- Slide 24 -->
## Power & Rights of Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA) (Slide 24)

4. nominate representatives of the workers in any welfare institution or provident fund and in the board of trustees of the workers participation fund established under Chapter XV; and
5. conduct cases on behalf of any individual worker or a group of workers under this Act.

---

<!-- Slide 25 -->
## Industrial Dispute (Slide 25)

Industrial dispute means disagreement between an employer and its employees usually represented by a trade union over some aspect of the terms or conditions of employment.

According to the Bangladesh Labour Code 2006,
Industrial dispute is any dispute or difference between employers and employers between employers and workers, or between worker and worker which is connected with the employment or the conditions of work of any person.

---

<!-- Slide 26 -->
## Industrial Dispute (Slide 26)

**Disputes by Employers:**
1. Lock-out
2. Lay-off
3. Retrenchment
4. Dismissal

**Disputes by Workers:**
1. Go-slow
2. Strike
3. Boycott

---

## Settlement of Industrial Dispute (Sec-210) (Slide 27)

1. If at any time an employer or a collective bargaining agent finds that an industrial dispute is likely to arise between the employer and the workers, the employer or the collective bargaining agent shall communicate his or her views in writing to the other party.

---

## Unfair Labour Practice

“Unfair labour Practice” as any unfair act or omission that arises between an employer and an employee.

---

<!-- Slide 44 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Employer (Sec-195) (Slide 44)

1. impose any condition in a contract of employment restraining the right of the worker concerned to join a trade union or continue his membership of a trade union ;
2. refuse to employ or refuse to keep in employment any worker on the ground that such worker is, or is not, a member or officer of a trade union;

---

<!-- Slide 45 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Employer (Sec-195) (Slide 45)

3. discriminate against any worker in regard to any employment, promotion, conditions of employment, or working conditions on the ground that such worker is, or is not, a member or officer of a trade union;
4. dismiss, discharge, or remove any worker from employment or threaten to do so, or threaten to do any harm to his employment by reason that he is, or proposes to become, or persuades any other person to become, a member or officer of a trade union, or that he participates in the formation, activities, and expansion of a trade union;

---

<!-- Slide 46 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Employer (Sec-195) (Slide 46)

5. induce any worker or any other person to refrain from becoming a member or officer of a trade union or to quit such post, by conferring or offering to confer any advantage or by procuring or offering to procure any advantage for him;
6. compel or attempt to compel any officer of the collective bargaining agent to arrive at a settlement or to sign a memorandum of settlement by intimidation, coercion, pressure, threat, confinement to a place, physical injury, disconnection of water, power and telephone facilities or by any other means;

---

<!-- Slide 47 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Employer (Sec-195) (Slide 47)

7. interfere with or in any way influence the election held under section 202;
8. recruit any new worker during the continuance of the strike
9. deliberately fail to take measures recommended by the participation committee;
10. fail to give a reply to any communications made by the collective bargaining agent in respect of any industrial dispute;

---

<!-- Slide 48 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Employer (Sec-195) (Slide 48)

11. transfer the Chairman, general secretary, organizing secretary, or treasurer of any trade union in contravention of the provisions of section 187; or
12. commence or continue or instigate others to take part in any illegal lock-out.

---

<!-- Slide 49 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Workers (Sec-196) (Slide 49)

1. intimidate any worker to become or not to become a member or officer of a trade union or to continue in or to refrain from such post;
2. induce any worker or any other person to refrain from becoming a member or officer of a trade union or to quit such post by conferring or offering to confer any advantage or by procuring or offering to procure any advantage for him;

---

<!-- Slide 50 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Workers (Sec-196) (Slide 50)

3. compel or attempt to compel any worker to pay or refrain from paying any subscription to the fund of any trade union by intimidation, coercion, pressure, threat, confinement to a place, physical injury, disconnection of telephone, water or power facilities or by any other means;

---

<!-- Slide 51 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Workers (Sec-196) (Slide 51)

4. compel or attempt to compel the employer to sign a memorandum of settlement or to accept or agree to any demand by intimidation, coercion, pressure, threat, confinement to or eviction from a place, dispossession, assault, physical injury, disconnection of water, power or telephone facilities or by any other means;

---

<!-- Slide 52 -->
## Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Workers (Sec-196) (Slide 52)

5. commence or continue an illegal strike or a go-slow, or instigate others to take part in it; or
6. resort to gherao, obstruction to transport or communication system, or destruction of any property in furtherance of any demand or object of a trade union.

---

<!-- Slide 53 -->
## Penalties for Unfair Labour Practices

1. Penalty for illegal Strike or Lockout
2. Penalty for Lay- off or Closure

---

<!-- Slide 54 -->
## Penalties for Illegal Strike or Lockout

- For a workman penalty for illegal strike or lockout is six months’ imprisonment or a fine of two hundred taka.
- For employer the penalty for illegal strike, or lockout is imprisonment for one year or fine of five thousand taka or both.

---

<!-- Slide 55 -->
## Penalties for Lay-off or Closure

- An employer may be punishable with imprisonment for one month or a fine of three thousand taka or both.
- Moreover, the worker should be compensated up to 50% of the total of the basic wages.
