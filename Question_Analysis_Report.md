## 4. How a Question Is Built

Almost every 10-mark final question follows a three-step ladder.

| Part | Marks | Typical verbs | What the examiner wants |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| (a) | 2 (sometimes 3 or 4) | Define, What is, List | One crisp textbook or statutory definition. Quote the section number for law |
| (b) | 3 to 4 | State, Discuss, Explain, Briefly discuss, Summarize | A numbered list of points, rules, or steps from the notes |
| (c) | 4 to 6 | Critically evaluate, Assess, Propose, Apply, Develop a scenario, Analyze | Your own application. Use an ECE or Bangladeshi industry example |

**Three big observations from the Finals and CTs:**

1. **Part (c) and the CTs test application, not just memorization.** Finals and CTs ask things like "Propose strategies for mitigating contractual risks in R&D agreements within ECE" (2023 Q3c), "Analyze which provisions of the BLC apply to a factory with excessive hours, unsafe machines, inadequate fire protection, and insufficient welfare" (Legal CT-2 Q1), and "Mention two human, physical, and financial resources of a Telecom firm" (IM CT-1 Q9).
2. **The ECE and Telecom industry angle appears everywhere.** Look at the examples used by the examiners:
   - R&D agreements in ECE (2023 Q3c)
   - An ECE firm bidding for a government project (2024 Q6b)
   - Bias against technical staff (2024 Q8c)
   - Resources of a Telecom firm (IM CT-1 Q9)
   - An ECE equipment warehouse distributing microcontroller units / MCUs (OM CT-2)
3. **Short 2-mark conceptual probes from IM CT-1 can easily appear as part (a) or (b) in the final.** Questions like *"Management is universal / a social process. How?"*, *"All leaders are managers, but all managers are not leaders. Why?"*, and *"Is a university Vice-Chancellor a manager?"* come straight from MRI Sir's Lectures 1 to 3.

## 6. Ten Key Findings

1. **Four topics appear in every single final paper.** Define management. Motivation meaning and importance. Performance appraisal and rating-scale problems. EOQ / ROP / lead time / safety stock definitions. These are guaranteed marks. Learn them word perfect.

2. **Questions are recycled almost word for word across Finals and CTs.** Look at these pairs:

   | Earlier paper | Later paper |
   | :--- | :--- |
   | 2021 Q1a "Define management, efficiency and effectiveness." | 2024 Q5a: identical |
   | 2021 Q1c "Critically discuss the skills necessary to accomplish managerial tasks." | 2024 Q1c "Critically evaluate the skills necessary to accomplish managerial tasks." |
   | 2021 Q3a "What is employee motivation? Why is it important?" | 2023 Q8a: identical |
   | 2021 Q4b "Define EOQ, lead time, re-order point and safety stock inventory." | 2024 Q7a: nearly identical |
   | 2021 Q4c "What is performance management?" | 2023 Q8c: identical opening |
   | 2020 Q2b / 2021 Q4c rating-scale appraisal problems | 2024 Q8b: same topic |
   | Class 10/11 Exercise 1.2 (hand drills, 5 days/week, 52 weeks) | 2024 Q7b (identical numbers) and 2025 OM CT-2 (same template, adapted to MCUs) |

3. **IM CT-1 exposes MRI Sir's conceptual short-question bank.** Previously, Lecture 2 (*Nature of Management: universal, social process, resource optimization*) and Lecture 3 (*roles/titles by level, first-line vs. top-level skills*) had few standalone final hits. IM CT-1 tested all of them via crisp 2-mark "How?" and "Why?" questions. Expect 1 or 2 of these in part (a) or (b) of the final.

4. **The motivation theory rotates every year.** Maslow (2020), Maslow (2021), Vroom (2023), Herzberg (2024). Because IM CT-1 only covered up to Organizing, Motivation was not tested in the CTs and is 100% locked in for the final. Prepare all three theories, with extra care on Maslow and Vroom (taught in Class 8).

5. **The Continuous Review ($Q$) System numerical is 100% guaranteed.** It appeared in 2023 Q7c, twice in 2024 (Q4c and Q7b), and took up the entire 20-mark OM CT-2 paper. Every single step in OM CT-2 (`EOQ`, `ROP`, `IP` decision, 3-lot cost comparison, `N`, `TBO`, and sawtooth graph) matches Raidah's Class 10 and 11 notes.

6. **Productivity is the prime candidate for a second CO3 question.** It was taught in Class 11 (after CT-2) with explicit exam instructions from MRI Sir: compare current productivity with the previous month's index, diagnose why it dropped, and propose fixes using Human Factors and Industry 4.0.

7. **Why Legal CT-2 and Shoaib Sir's Final Suggestion differ on Chapter 3.** In Legal CT-2, SI Sir tested `Sec 100–108` (adult working hours), `Sec 64` (moving machinery), and `Sec 40 / 39(3)` (adolescents on dangerous machines). Because those heavy sections were already evaluated in CT-2, Shoaib Sir narrowed Chapter 3 for the final to **Maternity Benefits (`Sec 45–50`)**, **Fire Safety ONLY (`Sec 62`)**, **Adolescent Working Hours ONLY (`Sec 41`)**, **Welfare (`Sec 89, 91–94`)**, and **Short Definitions (`Sec 2`)**.

8. **Four suggested legal topics were untouched in both Finals AND CTs.** Maternity benefits (`Sec 45–50`), Considerations & exceptions (`Sec 2(d), 25`), Trade Union rights/immunities/legal status (`Sec 194, 197–199`), and standalone Welfare measures (`Sec 89–94`) were not asked in 2023, 2024, or Legal CT-1 (and Welfare was only 1/4 of Legal CT-2 Q1). Unasked in CTs + explicitly prescribed in the voice note = highest probability for the final.

9. **The 10-mark Contract Case Study and Chapter 4 Analytical Question are confirmed.** Shoaib Sir's voice note states that one full 10-mark question will be a 3-part Case Study on **Discharge / Termination of Contract** (specifically **Subsequent / Supervening Impossibility**, `Sec 56`), and the Chapter 4 question on Trade Unions / CBA / Industrial Disputes will be an **Applied / Analytical question**.

10. **HRM (recruitment, selection, training) remains low priority.** It appeared only in 2020 Q3 and was skipped in both 2023–2024 finals and the 2025 CTs.

### 8.2 Formula Sheet

$$
\begin{aligned}
EOQ &= \sqrt{\frac{2DS}{H}} \\
SS &= z \, \sigma_d \sqrt{L} \\
R &= \bar{d} L + SS \\
IP &= OH + SR - BO \\
TC &= \frac{Q}{2}H + \frac{D}{Q}S + (SS)H \\
N &= \frac{D}{EOQ}, \qquad TBO = \frac{EOQ}{D} \times \text{ operating days/year}
\end{aligned}
$$

Rule: place an order of size $EOQ$ if and only if $IP \le R$.

### 8.3 z-Values to Memorize

Both **2024 Q4c** (88%) and **2025 OM CT-2** (95%) omitted the $z$-value from the question paper. Memorize this table:

| Cycle service level | 85% | 88% | 90% | 92% | 95% | 97% | 99% |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **z** | 1.04 | 1.175 (≈ 1.18) | 1.28 | 1.41 | **1.645** (≈ 1.65) | 1.88 | 2.33 |

### 8.4 Worked Answer Keys (All 4 Problems)

**1. 2023 Q7c (Walton company).** $D = 650 \times 52 = 33,800\text{ units/year}$, $S = \text{Tk } 50,000$, $H = \text{Tk } 30$, $L = 2\text{ weeks}$, $\sigma_d = 50\text{/week}$, $z = 1.43$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
EOQ &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 33{,}800 \times 50{,}000}{30}} \approx 10{,}614\text{ units} \\

R &= 650 \times 2 + 101 = 1{,}401\text{ units} \\
IP &= 400 + 10{,}614 - 200 = 10{,}814 > R \Rightarrow \text{ do not order}
\end{aligned}
$$

| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $Q = EOQ \approx 10,614$ | Tk 159,217 | Tk 159,217 | **Tk 318,434** |
| $Q = 15,000$ | Tk 112,667 | Tk 225,000 | **Tk 337,667** |

$Q = 15,000$ costs **Tk 19,233 more per year**.

**2. 2024 Q4c (current policy check).** $D = 64 \times 52 = 3,328$, $S = \text{USD } 50$, $H = \text{USD } 13$, $\sigma_d = 12\text{/week}$, $L = 2\text{ weeks}$, $z(88\%) \approx 1.175$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
EOQ &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 3{,}328 \times 50}{13}} = 160\text{ units} \\
SS &= 1.175 \times 12 \times \sqrt{2} \approx 20\text{ units} \\
R &= 64 \times 2 + 20 = 148\text{ units}
\end{aligned}
$$

| Policy | Cycle holding | Ordering | Safety stock holding | Total |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Current: $Q = 200, R = 180$ ($SS = 180 - 128 = 52$) | USD 1,300 | USD 832 | USD 676 | **USD 2,808** |
| Optimal: $Q = 160, R = 148$ ($SS = 20$) | USD 1,040 | USD 1,040 | USD 260 | **USD 2,340** |

The optimal policy saves **USD 468 per year**.

**3. 2024 Q7b (hand drills, same as Class 10 Exercise 1.2).** $D = 100 \times 260 = 26,000$, $S = \text{USD } 35$, $H = \text{USD } 9.40$, $\sigma_d = 30\text{/day}$, $L = 3\text{ days}$, $z = 1.41$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
EOQ &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 26{,}000 \times 35}{9.40}} \approx 440\text{ drills} \\
SS &= 1.41 \times 30 \times \sqrt{3} \approx 73.27 \approx 73\text{ drills} \\
R &= 100 \times 3 + 73 = 373\text{ drills} \\
IP &= 40 + 440 - 0 = 480 > 373 \Rightarrow \text{ do not order}
\end{aligned}
$$

| $Q$ | Cycle holding | Ordering | Cycle cost | + SS cost (≈ USD 689) |
| :---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 400 | USD 1,880 | USD 2,275 | USD 4,155 | ≈ USD 4,844 |
| **440 (EOQ)** | USD 2,068 | USD 2,068 | **USD 4,136** | **≈ USD 4,825** |
| 500 | USD 2,350 | USD 1,820 | USD 4,170 | ≈ USD 4,859 |

Also $TBO = \frac{440}{26,000} \times 260 = 4.4\text{ operating days}$ and $N = \frac{26,000}{440} \approx 59.09\text{ orders/year}$.

**4. 2025 OM CT-2 (ECE MCU warehouse).** $D = 120 \times 260 = 31,200\text{ MCUs/year}$, $S = \text{USD } 45$, $H = \text{USD } 6.50$, $\sigma_d = 35\text{/day}$, $L = 4\text{ days}$, $\text{CSL} = 95\% \implies z = 1.645$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
EOQ &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 31{,}200 \times 45}{6.50}} = \sqrt{432{,}000} \approx 657\text{ MCUs} \\
SS &= 1.645 \times 35 \times \sqrt{4} = 115.15 \approx 115\text{ MCUs} \\
R &= 120 \times 4 + 115.15 = 595.15 \approx 596\text{ MCUs} \\
IP &= 80 + 500 - 0 = 580 \le 596 \Rightarrow \mathbf{\text{YES, place an order of } 657\text{ MCUs}} \\
N &= \frac{31{,}200}{657} \approx 47.49\text{ orders/year}, \qquad TBO = \frac{657}{31{,}200} \times 260 \approx 5.48\text{ operating days}
\end{aligned}
$$

| $Q$ | Ordering cost | Cycle holding cost | Cycle cost | + SS cost (USD 748.48) |
| :---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 400 | USD 3,510.00 | USD 1,300.00 | USD 4,810.00 | USD 5,558.48 |
| **657 (EOQ)** | USD 2,136.99 | USD 2,135.25 | **USD 4,272.24** | **USD 5,020.72** |
| 700 | USD 2,005.71 | USD 2,275.00 | USD 4,280.71 | USD 5,029.19 |

Notice that in OM CT-2, $IP = 580 \le 596$, so the decision is **YES, place a new order** (unlike 2023 Q7c and 2024 Q7b where $IP > R$). Always check the inequality carefully!

## 11. Exam Hall Strategy

**Time.** 3 hours for 6 questions gives 30 minutes each. Spend 5 minutes reading all 8 questions first. That leaves about 28 minutes per question and a few minutes to check.

**Picking questions.**

1. Read all four questions in each section. Mark each sub-part with the CO it belongs to.
2. Take any question with an inventory or productivity numerical first. It is the fastest way to lock in 10/10.
3. Next take questions built around Tier 1 management recall (definitions, efficiency/effectiveness, motivation, appraisal).
4. Pick the legal case study if you know `Sec 56` (grounds of supervening impossibility vs. non-qualifying excuses) cold.
5. Avoid a question where you only know part (a). Part (c) carries 4 to 6 marks.

**Writing legal answers.** Use the four-step legal format for every applied or case question:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["1. Facts of the case"] --> B["2. Applicable law & section"]
    B --> C["3. Legal analysis"]
    C --> D["4. Direct conclusion"]
```
