## 2. In-Depth Chapter-by-Chapter Topic Breakdown

### Chapter 2: Law of Contract

1. **Foundations of Contract & Agreements:**
   - Detailed understanding of Contract, Agreement, and Voidable Agreements.
   - Core Maxim: *"All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts."*
   - Essential elements of a valid contract (Sec 10): Offer & Acceptance, Intention to create legal relations, Lawful consideration, Capacity of parties, Free consent, Lawful object, Certainty, Possibility of performance.
   - Rules governing valid **Offer** and **Acceptance**.

2. **Consideration:**
   - Concept of consideration (*Quid pro quo* — something in return).
   - Rules regarding valid consideration.
   - The doctrine and exceptions to *"No Consideration, No Contract"* (*Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio*).

3. **Discharge & Termination of Contract (Critical Target Area):**
   - Thorough study of all **Methods of Termination or Discharge of Contracts**:
     1. Discharge by Performance.
     2. Discharge by Mutual Agreement / Consent (Novation, Rescission, Alteration, Remission).
     3. Discharge by Breach of Contract (Actual vs. Anticipatory).
     4. Discharge by Operation of Law (Death, Insolvency, Merger, Unauthorized material alteration).
     5. Discharge by Lapse of Time.
     6. **Discharge by Impossibility of Performance**:
        - **Subsequent and Supervening Impossibility**: Doctrine of Frustration, grounds (Destruction of subject matter, Death/incapacity for personal service, Non-existence of state of things, Outbreak of war, Change of law) and non-qualifying difficulties (commercial hardship, strikes/lockouts, third-party failure).

4. **10-Mark Applied Case Study Blueprint:**
   - Sir explicitly confirmed that an **Applied Case Study** will be set from Chapter 2 (centered on contract validity, breach, or termination / supervening impossibility).
   - **Structure**: A scenario/case narrative describing a real-world dispute will be provided, followed by **three specific sub-questions**.
   - **Total Value**: Exactly **10 Marks**.
   - **Answering Strategy Prescribed by Sir**:
     1. Read the given factual scenario with high attentiveness.
     2. Identify the governing legal principle and relevant statutory section.
     3. Answer all three sub-questions step-by-step with clear logical deduction and legal justification.

### Chapter 3: The Bangladesh Labour Code, 2006 (BLC 2006)

Sir has drastically streamlined Chapter 3 by omitting heavy general sections and specifying exact, narrow targets:

1. **Maternity Welfare Facilities & Benefits (Sections 45–50):**
   - **Section 45**: Prohibition of employment of women workers during certain periods (immediately after delivery).
   - **Section 46**: Right to, and liability for, payment of maternity benefit (qualifying employment period).
   - **Section 47**: Formal procedure for claiming and disbursement of maternity benefit.
   - **Section 48**: Calculation and statutory amount of maternity benefit (daily average wage calculation for 16 weeks / 8 weeks before + 8 weeks after).
   - **Section 49 & 50**: Protection against dismissal/termination during maternity leave.

2. **Safety of Workers — FIRE SAFETY ONLY (Section 62):**
   - *Sir's Explicit Exclusion*: **Do NOT spend time on general machine fencing, machinery in motion, cranes, hoists, revolving machinery, or pressure plants.**
   - Focus exclusively on **Fire Safety**:
     - **Section 62 — Precautions in Case of Fire**: Means of escape in case of fire, unfastened fire exit doors opening outwards, markings of fire exits in red Bengali letters, audible fire alarms, mandatory mock fire drills (at least once every 6 months in factories employing 50+ workers), unobstructed passages, and training of designated firefighting personnel (at least 18% of workforce).

3. **Employment of Young Persons — WORKING HOURS FOR ADOLESCENTS ONLY (Section 41):**
   - *Sir's Explicit Exclusion*: **Do NOT spend time memorizing the entire young person regulation catalog.**
   - Focus strictly on **Working Hours for Adolescents**:
     - **Section 41 — Working hours for adolescents**:
       - Maximum 5 hours per day and 30 hours per week in factories and mines.
       - Maximum 7 hours per day and 42 hours per week in other establishments.
       - Absolute prohibition of work between 7:00 PM and 7:00 AM (night shift prohibition).
       - Spreadover restriction: Maximum spread over of 7.5 hours per day.
       - Strict prohibition against double employment across multiple establishments on the same day.

4. **Welfare Measures of Workers (Selected 3–4 Sections Only):**
   - Sir advised focusing only on the core practical statutory facilities:
     - **Section 89**: First-aid appliances (mandatory first-aid boxes per 150 workers, trained first-aid attendants, ambulance room for 500+ workers).
     - **Section 91**: Washing facilities (adequate, separate, and screened washing places for male and female workers).
     - **Section 92**: Canteens (mandatory canteen in any factory employing more than 100 workers, non-profit standard prices, hygiene requirements).
     - **Section 93**: Shelters, rest rooms, and lunch rooms (mandatory in factories employing more than 50 workers, drinking water facilities).
     - **Section 94**: Rooms for children / Crèches (mandatory suitable rooms for children under 6 years in factories employing 40+ female workers).

5. **Essential Statutory Short Definitions (From the Start of Slides):**
   - Definitions frequently appearing in short-answer questions:
     - **Worker** [Sec 2(lxv)]: Any person employed directly or through a contractor for hire/reward.
     - **Factory** [Sec 2(vii)]: Any premises where 5 or more workers are employed and manufacturing process is carried out.
     - **Manufacturing Process** [Sec 2(lxi)]: Making, altering, repairing, ornamenting, finishing, packing, pumping, generating electricity, printing, etc.
     - **Labour Code**: What is the scope and objective of the consolidated BLC 2006.
     - **Age Categories**: Child (< 14), Adolescent (14 to 18), Adult (18+).

### Chapter 4: Trade Union Act & Industrial Relations

1. **Foundations of Trade Union:**
   - Statutory definition and nature of a Trade Union.
   - Core objectives and legitimate functions of Trade Unions in industrial harmony.

2. **Legal Status, Rights & Privileges of a Registered Trade Union:**
   - Legal personality: Body corporate with perpetual succession, common seal, power to hold property, and right to sue and be sued in its registered name (Sec 182).
   - Statutory immunity from criminal conspiracy in trade disputes (Sec 192).
   - Immunity from civil suit in certain cases of breach of contract (Sec 193).
   - Enforceability of collective agreements.

3. **Collective Bargaining & Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA):**
   - What is Collective Bargaining?
   - Determination of CBA: Procedure for appointment or election of a CBA when there is only one union or multiple unions in an establishment.
   - Exclusive powers, rights, and responsibilities of the CBA (negotiation, notice of dispute, declaration of strike, representation of workers in proceedings).

4. **Industrial Disputes & Dispute Resolution:**
   - Definition of Industrial Dispute (dispute between employers and workers, workers and workers, or employers and employers regarding employment, non-employment, terms of employment, or conditions of work).
   - Direct negotiation, Conciliation by the Conciliator, Arbitration, and adjudication before the Labour Court (Sec 210).

5. **Applied / Analytical Question Alert:**
   - Sir announced that the question from Chapter 4 will be an **Applied / Analytical question**.
   - It will evaluate whether the student genuinely grasps the *philosophy, purpose, practical rights, and operational mechanics* of a trade union and CBA rather than just blind memorization.
   - Students must be ready to analyze an industrial relations problem or evaluate trade union conduct using their own intellect and legal knowledge.
