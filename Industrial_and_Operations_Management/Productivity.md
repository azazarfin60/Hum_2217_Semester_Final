## Definition of Productivity

- **Productivity** is a measure of how efficiently inputs (such as labor, materials, and capital) are used to produce outputs (goods or services).
- In its simplest form, productivity is expressed as the ratio of **output** to **input**:

$$
\text{Productivity} = \frac{\text{Output}}{\text{Input}}
$$

---

<!-- Slide 3 -->
## Key points:

- **Output**: What is produced (e.g., number of units, dollar value of goods or services).
- **Input**: Resources used to produce output (e.g., labor hours, materials, money, machine time).
- Higher productivity means more output is produced with the same amount of input, or the same output is produced with fewer inputs.

---

<!-- Slide 4 -->
## Types of productivity

1. **Single-factor productivity (SFP)**: Focuses on one input (e.g., labor productivity, capital productivity).
2. **Multifactor productivity (MFP)**: Considers multiple inputs together (e.g., labor, materials, energy).

## Challenges faced by an operations manager to improve productivity

### 1. Managing human factors
- Low employee motivation, absenteeism, or resistance to change
- Need for continuous training and skill development
- Balancing workload to avoid burnout while maintaining efficiency

### 2. Technological issues
- High cost of adopting new technologies and automation
- Integration problems between new and existing systems
- Rapid technological change making investments quickly outdated

---

<!-- Slide 13 -->
## Productivity improvement challenges…

### 3. Quality vs. productivity trade-off
- Efforts to speed up production may reduce product or service quality
- Quality failures lead to rework, scrap, or dissatisfied customers, which ultimately lowers productivity

### 4. Resource constraints
- Limited availability of skilled labor, raw materials, or machine capacity
- Fluctuating costs of inputs (e.g., fuel, materials)

---

<!-- Slide 14 -->
## Productivity improvement challenges…

### 5. Process inefficiencies
- Outdated processes or equipment causing delays and bottlenecks
- Difficulty identifying and eliminating waste in processes

### 6. External factors
- Changes in market demand requiring adjustments in production volume
- Supply chain disruptions (e.g., late deliveries from suppliers)
- Economic or political instability is increasing costs or reducing reliability

### 7. Measurement and data issues
- Lack of accurate, real-time data to monitor productivity
- Difficulty choosing the right productivity metrics, especially in service operations

---

<!-- Slide 15 -->
## Summary of Challenges

- **In summary**:
  An operations manager must balance people, technology, process improvements, and external factors — all while ensuring quality, cost control, and timely delivery.

- These challenges make improving productivity a **continuous and complex task**.

## Case Study: Improving Productivity at Bright Packaging Ltd.

- **Background**:
  Bright Packaging Ltd. is a company in Bangladesh that manufactures cardboard boxes for various clients. The company employs 150 workers and operates three production shifts daily. Despite steady demand, management observed that their productivity levels were stagnating, causing delays in delivery and increased operational costs.

- **Problem**:
  The production manager noted that the main bottleneck was at the box assembly section, where workers manually folded and glued boxes. The average output per worker per shift was below the company’s target by 25%. Additionally, there was a high rejection rate of defective boxes (about 15%), causing rework and waste of materials.

- The manager suspected that outdated work methods, lack of standardized procedures, and insufficient worker training were contributing factors. Moreover, there was no clear incentive system to encourage higher output or quality.

---

<!-- Slide 17 -->
## Current Situation Data:

- Average boxes assembled per worker per shift: 800
- Target boxes per worker per shift: 1,060
- Defect rate: 15% (requiring rework)
- Number of workers in assembly section: 40

### Your Task:
Analyze the situation and suggest a productivity improvement plan that addresses the key issues.

- **Q1**: What steps would you recommend to increase the average output per worker while reducing the defect rate?
- **Q2**: How can the company motivate workers to improve both productivity and quality?
- **Q3**: Suggest methods to identify and eliminate bottlenecks in the box assembly process.
- **Q4**: What role could technology or automation play in improving productivity here?

---

<!-- Slide 18 -->
## Evaluative Answers:

### Q1: What steps would you recommend to increase the average output per worker while reducing the defect rate?
**A1**:
To increase output and reduce defects, Bright Packaging Ltd. should start by standardizing the assembly process with clear work instructions. Training programs focused on correct folding and gluing techniques can reduce mistakes. Introducing quality checkpoints during production can catch defects early. Organizing workstations ergonomically will reduce worker fatigue and improve speed. Also, implementing regular performance tracking helps identify workers who may need additional support.

---

<!-- Slide 19 -->
### Q2: How can the company motivate workers to improve both productivity and quality?
**A2**:
Motivation can be enhanced by introducing performance-based incentives such as bonuses for meeting or exceeding targets and maintaining low defect rates. Recognition programs, such as “Worker of the Month,” can boost morale. Involving workers in problem-solving sessions fosters ownership and commitment to quality improvement.

---

<!-- Slide 20 -->
### Q3: Suggest methods to identify and eliminate bottlenecks in the box assembly process.
**A3**:
Conducting a time-and-motion study can help identify bottlenecks in the assembly process. Using tools like process flowcharts and value stream mapping will highlight inefficient steps. Once identified, management can reallocate resources or redesign the workflow to balance workloads across workers and stations.

---

<!-- Slide 21 -->
### Q4: What role could technology or automation play in improving productivity here?
**A4**:
Automation, such as semi-automatic folding and gluing machines, can significantly boost output and consistency. Even partial automation can reduce manual errors and speed up the process. However, cost-benefit analysis should be conducted before investment. Technology can also support quality control through sensors and cameras to detect defects early.

## Case Study: Boosting Productivity at GreenLeaf Agro Processing

- **Background**:
  GreenLeaf Agro Processing is a medium-sized company in Bangladesh specializing in packaging and processing fresh fruits for local and export markets. The company employs 200 workers and operates two production lines.

- **Problem**:
  Despite growing demand, the production output of the fruit sorting and packaging line was consistently below expectations. Management reported that the average packed boxes per shift were 15% less than the target. Additionally, there was a high rate of damaged fruits during handling, leading to product loss and customer complaints.

---

## Key issues identified include:

- Manual sorting causing delays and inconsistencies
- Workers lacked proper training on handling delicate fruits
- Poorly organized workspace leading to movement inefficiencies
- Inadequate equipment for packaging

### Situation Data:

- Actual packed boxes per shift: 4,250
- Fruit damage rate: 12%
- Number of workers on the packaging line: 50
