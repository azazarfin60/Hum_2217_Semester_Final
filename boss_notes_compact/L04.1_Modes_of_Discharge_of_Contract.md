# L04.1: Modes of Discharge of Contract

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A (CO1 · Legal Issues · Shoaib Islam Sir) · 🔴 Critical
> - **Read Time:** ~4 mins
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2024 Final Q.6(c) (4 Marks) + Model Questions on Sections 62–63 (4 Marks) and Section 39 Breach (3 Marks)
> - **Scoring Rule:** Enumerate all 7 statutory modes with sections. Distinguish actual vs anticipatory breach, and explain both options available to the promisee under Section 39.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Discharge of Contract:** "When the rights and obligations created by a contract come to an end, the contract is said to be discharged." *(Legal Definition)*

*Plain meaning:* The contractual relationship terminates, releasing both parties from further performance and extinguishing their primary legal liabilities.

> **Actual Performance [Section 37]:** "The parties to a contract must either perform, or offer to perform, their respective promises, unless such performance is dispensed with or excused." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* Both contracting parties fulfill all their agreed obligations completely according to the stipulated terms, time, and manner.

> **Attempted Performance (Tender) [Section 38]:** "Where a promisor has made an offer of performance to the promisee, and the offer has not been accepted, the promisor is not responsible for non-performance, nor does he thereby lose his rights under the contract." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* The promisor offers to perform their promise unconditionally at the proper time and place, but the promisee refuses to accept it.

> **Novation [Section 62]:** "If the parties to a contract agree to substitute a new contract for it, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract need not be performed." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* The contracting parties mutually replace the existing agreement with a fresh contract, completely discharging the original agreement.

> **Anticipatory Breach [Section 39]:** "When a party to a contract has refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* A contracting party unequivocally repudiates their obligations or disables themselves from performing before the scheduled due date arrives.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### The Seven Statutory Modes of Discharge

Under the Contract Act 1872, a contract may be discharged in seven distinct ways (`P-M-I-O-L-A-B`):

1. **Performance or Tender [Sections 37 and 38]:** Performance is the normal and intended mode of terminating a contract.
   - **Actual Performance [Section 37]:** Both parties fully and completely carry out their respective promises according to agreed terms.
   - **Attempted Performance or Tender [Section 38]:** The promisor makes an unconditional offer of performance, but the promisee wrongfully rejects it.
   - **Legal Effect of Tender:** A valid tender of goods or services completely discharges the promisor from non-performance and preserves their right to sue the buyer for breach. In contrast, tender of money does not erase the debt; it merely stops interest from accumulating and shields the debtor against legal costs.
   - **Three Essentials of Valid Tender:** (1) It must be unconditional, (2) made at the agreed time, proper place, and during business hours, and (3) provide the promisee a reasonable opportunity to inspect the goods.

2. **Mutual Agreement or Consent [Sections 62 and 63]:** What was created by mutual agreement may be terminated or modified by subsequent mutual consent (`N-R-A-R-W-M`):
   - **Novation [Section 62]:** Substituting a new contract in place of the original contract, either between the same parties or involving the substitution of a new debtor or creditor.
   - **Rescission [Section 62]:** Mutually agreeing to cancel the contract before performance is due, releasing both parties from their commitments.
   - **Alteration [Section 62]:** Modifying one or more terms of an existing contract with mutual consent, keeping the contracting parties unchanged.
   - **Remission [Section 63]:** Accepting a lesser sum or lesser performance in full discharge of the promise. Under Bangladesh law, remission requires **no fresh consideration**.
   - **Waiver:** Deliberately surrendering or abandoning a known contractual right without requiring consideration or a formal deed.
   - **Merger:** An inferior contractual right extinguishes automatically when it coincides with a superior legal or property right in the same individual (such as a tenant purchasing the freehold ownership of the rented property).

3. **Supervening Impossibility or Frustration [Section 56]:** An unexpected external event occurring after contract formation makes performance physically impossible or unlawful without default by either party.
   - **Legal Effect:** Future obligations are automatically discharged under the Doctrine of Frustration. Any advance payment or benefit received must be restored under Section 65. *(See L04.2 for the comprehensive 10-mark Applied Case Study framework).*

4. **Operation of Law:** A contract terminates automatically through the operation of statutory legal rules:
   - **Death:** Contracts dependent upon personal artistic talent or individual skill terminate upon the promisor's death. In ordinary commercial transactions, rights and liabilities pass to legal heirs up to the value of the inherited estate.
   - **Insolvency:** When a court adjudicates an individual as insolvent, assets and liabilities pass to the Official Assignee, and the subsequent discharge order frees the debtor from prior contractual debts.
   - **Merger:** An inferior contract right dissolves into a superior property right held by the same person.

5. **Lapse of Time [Limitation Act, 1908]:** Contractual rights must be enforced in court within statutory deadlines.
   - **Three-Year Statutory Limit:** The limitation period for instituting a lawsuit for breach of a simple contract or debt recovery is **3 years** from the date of default.
   - **Legal Consequence:** Once the 3-year period expires without a lawsuit, the judicial remedy is barred and the debt becomes time-barred, effectively discharging the contract.

6. **Unauthorized Material Alteration:** A unilateral physical alteration made to a written contract document without the consent of the other parties that alters its legal effect or operation.
   - **Legal Consequence:** The entire contract is discharged and the written document becomes void. The court will not admit the altered document, and the party making the unauthorized change forfeits all rights under it.

7. **Breach of Contract [Sections 39 and 73]:** A failure or refusal by one party to perform their contractual commitment without lawful excuse.
   - **Legal Effect:** Excuses the innocent party from performing remaining obligations and generates an immediate right to claim damages under Section 73.
   - **Two Categories:** Operates either as an **actual breach** (occurring on the due date or during performance) or an **anticipatory breach** (occurring prior to the scheduled due date).

### Actual Breach vs. Anticipatory Breach

| Comparison Point | Actual Breach | Anticipatory Breach [Section 39] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Occurs on the scheduled due date or during ongoing performance. | Occurs before the scheduled due date of performance arrives. |
| **Mode of Occurrence** | Party fails or refuses to perform when performance falls due. | Promisor repudiates liability by express words or disables performance. |
| **Governing Law** | General breach principles under Section 73. | Governed explicitly by **Section 39** of the Contract Act, 1872. |
| **Injured Party Rights** | Rescind immediately and file a suit for damages under Section 73. | **(1)** Rescind immediately and sue for damages (*Hochster*).<br>**(2)** Elect to keep contract alive until the scheduled due date. |
| **Risk of Waiting** | None (the breach has already taken place). | Intervening impossibility under Section 56 excuses the promisor (*Avery v. Bowden*). |
| **Leading Cases** | General breach precedents under Section 73. | *Hochster v. De La Tour* (1853); *Frost v. Knight* (1872). |

> [!tip] Memory Hooks: Modes of Discharge & Mutual Forms
> - **7 Modes of Discharge (P-M-I-O-L-A-B):** **P**erformance [Sec 37–38], **M**utual agreement [Sec 62–63], **I**mpossibility [Sec 56], **O**peration of law, **L**apse of time, **A**lteration, **B**reach [Sec 39].
> - **6 Mutual Consent Forms (N-R-A-R-W-M):** **N**ovation [Sec 62], **R**escission [Sec 62], **A**lteration [Sec 62], **R**emission [Sec 63], **W**aiver, **M**erger.

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### 2024 Final Q.6(c) · Methods of Termination of a Contract (04 Marks)

> **Question:** Discuss the methods of termination of a contract. *(2024 Final Exam · Question 6(c) · 04 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Opening Definition:** Define termination (discharge) of a contract as the cessation of contractual rights and obligations, releasing both parties from further legal liabilities.

2. **Seven Statutory Modes:** Enumerate all seven statutory modes (`P-M-I-O-L-A-B`) with exact section numbers:
   - (1) Performance or Tender [`Sec 37–38`]
   - (2) Mutual Agreement [`Sec 62–63`: Novation, Rescission, Alteration, Remission, Waiver, Merger]
   - (3) Subsequent Impossibility / Frustration [`Sec 56`]
   - (4) Operation of Law [Death in personal skill contracts, insolvency, merger]
   - (5) Lapse of Time [Limitation Act 1908: 3-year statutory limit]
   - (6) Unauthorized Material Alteration
   - (7) Breach of Contract [`Sec 39 & Sec 73`: Actual vs Anticipatory]

3. **Core Descriptions:** Write a precise 1-sentence legal explanation for each mode to secure full marks.

4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Summarize that performance is the natural and intended mode of discharge, whereas breach gives rise to secondary liabilities for compensatory damages.

### Model Exam Question 1 · Discharge by Performance and Essentials of Valid Tender (03 Marks)

> **Question:** Explain discharge of contract by performance. Distinguish between actual performance and attempted performance (tender). State the essentials of a valid tender under Section 38. *(03 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Performance Principle [Section 37]:** State that parties must either perform or offer to perform their obligations according to agreed terms.

2. **Actual vs Attempted Performance (Tender):** Contrast actual performance (complete execution by both parties) with attempted performance under Section 38 (promisor offers performance, but promisee wrongfully refuses acceptance).

3. **Three Essentials of Valid Tender:** List the three mandatory conditions under Section 38: (1) unconditional offer, (2) proper time, place, and business hours, and (3) reasonable opportunity for inspection.

4. **Tender of Goods vs Money:** Emphasize that tender of goods discharges the promisor, whereas tender of money does not erase the debt but stops interest and legal costs.

---

### Model Exam Question 2 · Discharge by Mutual Agreement under Sections 62 and 63 (04 Marks)

> **Question:** Explain how a contract is discharged by mutual agreement under the Contract Act, 1872. Define Novation, Rescission, Alteration, Remission, and Waiver with examples. *(04 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Statutory Foundation [Section 62]:** State the principle that obligations created by mutual consent may be discharged or altered by subsequent mutual consent without requiring performance of the original contract.

2. **Forms of Mutual Discharge:** Define the five primary forms with concise examples:
   - *Novation [Sec 62]:* Substituting a new contract between same or new parties.
   - *Rescission [Sec 62]:* Mutually cancelling the contract before performance falls due.
   - *Alteration [Sec 62]:* Modifying contract terms between the original parties.
   - *Remission [Sec 63]:* Accepting a lesser sum or lesser performance.
   - *Waiver:* Deliberately abandoning a known contractual right.

3. **Crucial Statutory Nuance:** Highlight that under Section 63 in Bangladesh, remission requires **no fresh consideration**, distinguishing it from English common law (*Pinnel's Case*).

4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Summarize that all parties to the original agreement must consent; unilateral alteration does not discharge under Section 62 and instead voids the instrument.

---

### Model Exam Question 3 · Actual vs Anticipatory Breach and Section 39 Remedies (03 Marks)

> **Question:** Distinguish between Actual Breach and Anticipatory Breach of contract under the Contract Act, 1872. What options are available to the injured party upon an anticipatory breach under Section 39? *(03 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Definition & Comparison Table:** Define actual breach (failure/refusal on the due date) and anticipatory breach under Section 39 (repudiation or disabling act prior to the due date). Reproduce the comparison table covering timing, mode of occurrence, governing statutes (`Sec 73` vs `Sec 39`), and case law (*Hochster v. De La Tour*).

2. **Two Remedies under Section 39:** Detail the choices available to the promisee:
   - *Option 1:* Rescind immediately and sue for damages without waiting for the due date (*Hochster v. De La Tour*).
   - *Option 2:* Elect to keep the contract alive until the due date, giving the promisor an opportunity to perform.

3. **Critical Risk of Waiting:** Explain that keeping the contract alive carries the risk of supervening impossibility under Section 56 (such as outbreak of war or trade ban), which excuses the promisor and destroys the injured party's damage claims (*Avery v. Bowden*).

4. **Concluding Takeaway:** State that anticipatory breach grants flexible election, but exercising immediate rescission is generally safer for preserving damage remedies.

---

### Model Exam Question 4 · Discharge by Operation of Law, Lapse of Time, and Material Alteration (03 Marks)

> **Question:** Describe how a contract is discharged by operation of law, lapse of time, and unauthorized material alteration. *(03 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Discharge by Operation of Law:** Explain termination arising automatically by legal rules: (1) death in contracts requiring personal skill/talent (ordinary commercial liabilities pass to heirs up to estate value), (2) insolvency adjudication followed by court discharge order, and (3) merger of inferior contractual rights into superior property rights.

2. **Discharge by Lapse of Time:** Cite the Limitation Act 1908. State that simple contract debts expire after **3 years** from default. Explain that failure to institute a suit bars the court remedy, rendering the contract unenforceable.

3. **Unauthorized Material Alteration:** Define material alteration as an unauthorized physical tampering with terms (such as price, date, or interest rate). Explain that the altering party forfeits all rights and the document becomes void in court.

4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Emphasize that these three modes operate independently of mutual agreement, enforcing statutory finality and document integrity.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Tender of Money vs Goods:** A valid tender of goods completely discharges the contract. In contrast, tender of money does not erase the principal debt; it only stops interest from running and shields against court costs.
> - **Remission Needs No Consideration:** Under Section 63 in Bangladesh, accepting a lesser sum requires no fresh consideration (unlike the English common law rule in *Pinnel's Case*).
> - **Risk of Keeping Contract Alive:** If the injured party waits until the due date following an anticipatory breach, intervening frustration under Section 56 excuses the promisor and destroys the damage claim (*Avery v. Bowden*).
> - **Novation vs Alteration:** Novation creates a new contract and can introduce new parties. Alteration modifies terms within the same contract between original parties only.
