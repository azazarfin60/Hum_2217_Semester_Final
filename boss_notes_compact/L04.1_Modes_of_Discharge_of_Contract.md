# L04.1: Modes of Discharge of Contract

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A · SI Sir · 🔴 Critical | **Read Time:** ~3 mins
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2024 Final Q.6(c) (4m) + Model Questions on Sec 62–63 (4m) and Sec 39 Breach (3m)
> - **Scoring Rule:** Cite all 7 modes with sections. Distinguish actual vs anticipatory breach, and state both Sec 39 options.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Discharge of Contract:** "When the rights and obligations created by a contract come to an end, the contract is said to be discharged."
- *Plain meaning:* Contractual duties end and both parties are released from further legal liabilities.

> **Anticipatory Breach [Sec 39]:** "When a party to a contract has refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract." *(Contract Act, 1872)*
- *Plain meaning:* Promisor refuses or disables performance before the scheduled due date arrives.

---

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### The Seven Statutory Modes of Discharge

Under the Contract Act 1872, a contract may be discharged in seven distinct ways:

1. **Performance or Tender [Sec 37–38]:** Actual performance completes all terms. Attempted performance (tender) occurs when promisor offers performance but promisee rejects it. Tender of goods discharges the contract; tender of money stops interest without erasing debt. *Valid tender essentials:* unconditional, at proper time/place, with inspection opportunity.
2. **Mutual Agreement [Sec 62–63]:** Dissolved by mutual consent (`N-R-A-R-W-M`):
   - *Novation [Sec 62]:* Substituting a new contract between same or new parties.
   - *Rescission [Sec 62]:* Mutual cancellation of contract before performance.
   - *Alteration [Sec 62]:* Modifying terms by mutual consent between original parties.
   - *Remission [Sec 63]:* Accepting lesser sum. Requires **no fresh consideration** under Bangladesh law.
   - *Waiver:* Intentionally abandoning a known contractual right.
   - *Merger:* Inferior contract right dissolves into superior property title in same person.
3. **Supervening Impossibility [Sec 56]:** Unforeseen post-formation event makes performance impossible or illegal without party fault (*Doctrine of Frustration*). Excuses future performance; benefits restored under Sec 65. *(See L04.2).*
4. **Lapse of Time [Limitation Act 1908]:** Claims must be sued within statutory limits. Simple contract debts expire in **3 years**. Failure to sue bars court remedy and discharges debt.
5. **Operation of Law:** Automatic termination upon death in personal skill contracts, insolvency discharge by court order, or merger of rights.
6. **Material Alteration:** Unilateral physical alteration altering legal effect voids written contract. Altering party forfeits all rights.
7. **Breach of Contract [Sec 39 & Sec 73]:** Failure or refusal to perform without legal excuse. Occurs as actual breach or anticipatory breach.

### Actual Breach vs. Anticipatory Breach

| Comparison Point | Actual Breach | Anticipatory Breach [Sec 39] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | On due date or during performance. | Before scheduled due date. |
| **How Occurs** | Fails or refuses to perform when due. | Express refusal or disabling act before due date. |
| **Governing Law** | General breach under Section 73. | Governed by **Section 39** of Contract Act. |
| **Injured Party Rights** | Rescind immediately and sue for damages. | **(1)** Rescind and sue immediately (*Hochster*).<br>**(2)** Keep contract alive until due date. |
| **Risk of Waiting** | None (breach has already occurred). | Intervening frustration (Sec 56) may excuse promisor (*Avery v. Bowden*). |
| **Leading Case** | General breach under Section 73. | *Hochster v. De La Tour*; *Frost v. Knight*. |

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 7 Modes & 6 Mutual Consent Forms
> - **7 Modes (P-M-I-O-L-A-B):** **P**erformance [37–38], **M**utual agreement [62–63], **I**mpossibility [56], **O**peration of law, **L**apse of time, **A**lteration, **B**reach [39].
> - **6 Mutual Forms (N-R-A-R-W-M):** **N**ovation, **R**escission, **A**lteration, **R**emission, **W**aiver, **M**erger.

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### 2024 Final Q.6(c) · Methods of Termination of a Contract (04 Marks)
> **Question:** Discuss the methods of termination of a contract.

**Answer Blueprint:**
- Define discharge of contract from §1.
- Cite all 7 modes from §2: performance, agreement, frustration, limitation, law, alteration, and breach.
- State 1-line definitions for each mode for full 4 marks.

---

### Model Exam Question 1 · Discharge by Mutual Agreement (04 Marks)
> **Question:** Explain how a contract is discharged by mutual agreement under the Contract Act, 1872. Define Novation, Rescission, Alteration, Remission, and Waiver with examples.

**Answer Blueprint:**
- State Section 62 principle: mutual consent dissolves contracts.
- Define the 6 forms (`N-R-A-R-W-M`) from §2 Mode 2 with examples.
- Emphasize that Section 63 remission needs no fresh consideration under Bangladesh law for full 4 marks.

---

### Model Exam Question 2 · Actual vs Anticipatory Breach (03 Marks)
> **Question:** Distinguish Actual vs Anticipatory Breach under Section 39 and state injured party options.

**Answer Blueprint:**
- Reproduce the comparison table from §2 covering timing, occurrence, governing sections, and leading cases.
- State two Section 39 options: (1) rescind and sue immediately (*Hochster*); (2) keep contract alive until due date.
- State the risk of waiting: supervening impossibility under Section 56 excuses the promisor (*Avery v. Bowden*).

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Tender of Money vs Goods:** Tender of goods discharges the contract. Tender of money only stops interest; it does not erase the principal debt.
> - **Remission Needs No Consideration:** Under Section 63 in Bangladesh, accepting lesser payment requires no consideration (unlike English *Pinnel's Case*).
> - **Risk of Keeping Contract Alive:** Waiting risks intervening frustration under Sec 56. This excuses the promisor and destroys damage claims (*Avery v. Bowden*).
> - **Novation vs Alteration:** Novation substitutes a new contract and can introduce new parties. Alteration modifies terms between the original parties only.
