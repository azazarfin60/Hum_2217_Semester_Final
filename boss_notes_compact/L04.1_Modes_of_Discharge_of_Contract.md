# L04.1: Modes of Discharge of Contract

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A (CO1 · Legal Issues · Shoaib Islam Sir) · 🔴 Critical
> - **Read Time:** ~4 mins
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2024 Final Q.6(c) (4 Marks) + Model Questions on Sections 62–63 (4 Marks) and Section 39 Breach (3 Marks)
> - **Scoring Rule:** List all 7 statutory modes with sections. Distinguish actual vs anticipatory breach, and explain both options available to the promisee under Section 39.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Discharge of Contract:** "When the rights and obligations created by a contract come to an end, the contract is said to be discharged." *(Legal Definition)*

*Plain meaning:* The contract comes to an end. Both sides are freed from their duties, and neither side has to do any more work under it.

> **Actual Performance [Section 37]:** "The parties to a contract must either perform, or offer to perform, their respective promises, unless such performance is dispensed with or excused." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* Both sides completely finish everything they promised to do, on time and in the right way.

> **Attempted Performance (Tender) [Section 38]:** "Where a promisor has made an offer of performance to the promisee, and the offer has not been accepted, the promisor is not responsible for non-performance, nor does he thereby lose his rights under the contract." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* You show up ready and willing to deliver your promise at the right time and place. But the other side refuses to accept it. You are no longer to blame for not performing.

> **Novation [Section 62]:** "If the parties to a contract agree to substitute a new contract for it, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract need not be performed." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* Both sides agree to replace the old contract with a brand-new one. The old contract is cancelled immediately.

> **Anticipatory Breach [Section 39]:** "When a party to a contract has refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* One side clearly states they will not perform before the due date arrives. Alternatively, they make it impossible for themselves to perform.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### The Seven Statutory Modes of Discharge

Under the Contract Act 1872, a contract can end in seven distinct ways (`P-M-I-O-L-A-B`):

1. **Performance or Tender [Sections 37 and 38]:** Performance is the normal and intended way to finish a contract.
   - **Actual Performance [Section 37]:** Both sides do exactly what they promised under the contract.
   - **Attempted Performance or Tender [Section 38]:** The promisor offers to perform unconditionally, but the other side wrongfully rejects it.
   - **Legal Effect of Tender:**
     - **Tender of Goods or Services:** The seller is completely freed from duty. The seller can sue the buyer for breach of contract.
     - **Tender of Money:** Offering cash does not cancel the debt. But it stops interest from building up and shields the debtor from court costs.
   - **Three Essentials of Valid Tender:**
     - (1) It must be unconditional.
     - (2) It must be made at the proper time, place, and during business hours.
     - (3) It must give the other side a fair chance to inspect the goods.

2. **Mutual Agreement or Consent [Sections 62 and 63]:** A contract created by agreement can also be ended or changed by agreement (`N-R-A-R-W-M`):
   - **Novation [Section 62]:** Both sides replace the old contract with a new one. This can happen between the same parties or with a new party.
   - **Rescission [Section 62]:** Both sides agree to cancel the contract before it is carried out. Both sides are released.
   - **Alteration [Section 62]:** Both sides agree to change one or more terms. The parties stay the same.
   - **Remission [Section 63]:** Accepting a smaller payment or lesser performance to settle the whole promise. Under Bangladesh law, remission needs **no fresh consideration**.
   - **Waiver:** Giving up a legal right intentionally. You do not need any payment or deed to waive a right.
   - **Merger:** A smaller right disappears when it joins a bigger right in the same person. For example, a tenant buys the apartment they rent. The rental contract ends.

3. **Supervening Impossibility or Frustration [Section 56]:** An unexpected event happens after the contract is signed. This event makes performance physically impossible or illegal, through no fault of either side.
   - **Legal Effect:** Both sides are freed from future duties under the Doctrine of Frustration. Any advance money or benefit must be returned under Section 65. *(See L04.2 for the full case study framework).*

4. **Operation of Law:** The contract ends automatically under legal rules without any action by the parties:
   - **Death:** Contracts that rely on personal skill or talent end when the promisor dies. For example, a painter dies before painting a portrait. In regular business contracts, rights and debts pass to legal heirs up to the value of inherited property.
   - **Insolvency (Bankruptcy):** When a court declares a person bankrupt, their property passes to the Official Assignee. A court discharge order frees them from past contract debts.
   - **Merger:** A smaller contract right dissolves into a larger property right held by the same person.

5. **Lapse of Time [Limitation Act, 1908]:** You must enforce your contract rights within legal deadlines.
   - **Three-Year Time Limit:** You must sue for breach of contract or unpaid debt within **3 years** from the date of default.
   - **Legal Result:** If 3 years pass without a lawsuit, you lose your right to sue. The debt is time-barred, and the contract effectively ends.

6. **Unauthorized Material Alteration:** One party secretly changes an important term in a written contract without the other party's consent. For example, changing the price, date, or interest rate.
   - **Legal Result:** The entire contract is cancelled, and the document becomes void. The court will reject it. The person who made the change loses all rights under the contract.

7. **Breach of Contract [Sections 39 and 73]:** One party fails or refuses to carry out their promise without a legal excuse.
   - **Legal Effect:** The innocent party does not have to perform their side. They can sue right away for compensation (damages) under Section 73.
   - **Two Types of Breach:**
     - **Actual Breach:** Happens on the due date or while doing the work.
     - **Anticipatory Breach:** Happens before the due date arrives.

### Actual Breach vs. Anticipatory Breach

| Comparison Point | Actual Breach | Anticipatory Breach [Section 39] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Occurs on the due date or during performance. | Occurs before the scheduled due date arrives. |
| **How It Happens** | A party fails or refuses to perform when the due date comes. | The promisor refuses to perform, or makes performance impossible, before the due date. |
| **Governing Law** | General breach rules under Section 73. | Governed specifically by **Section 39** of the Contract Act, 1872. |
| **Innocent Party Options** | Cancel immediately and sue for damages under Section 73. | **Option 1:** Cancel immediately and sue for damages (*Hochster*).<br>**Option 2:** Keep the contract alive until the due date. |
| **Risk of Waiting** | None (the breach has already taken place). | Unexpected impossibility under Section 56 can excuse the promisor (*Avery v. Bowden*). |
| **Leading Cases** | General breach cases under Section 73. | *Hochster v. De La Tour* (1853); *Frost v. Knight* (1872). |

> [!tip] Memory Hooks: Modes of Discharge & Mutual Forms
> - **7 Modes of Discharge (`P-M-I-O-L-A-B`):**
>   - **P:** **P**erformance [`Sec 37–38`]
>   - **M:** **M**utual agreement [`Sec 62–63`]
>   - **I:** **I**mpossibility [`Sec 56`]
>   - **O:** **O**peration of law
>   - **L:** **L**apse of time
>   - **A:** **A**lteration
>   - **B:** **B**reach [`Sec 39`]
> - **6 Mutual Consent Forms (`N-R-A-R-W-M`):** **N**ovation [`Sec 62`], **R**escission [`Sec 62`], **A**lteration [`Sec 62`], **R**emission [`Sec 63`], **W**aiver, **M**erger.

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### 2024 Final Q.6(c) · Methods of Termination of a Contract (04 Marks)

> **Question:** Discuss the methods of termination of a contract. *(2024 Final Exam · Question 6(c) · 04 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Definition of Discharge:**
   - Define discharge as the end of contract rights and duties.
   - Both sides are freed from further legal duties.

2. **List All Seven Modes (`P-M-I-O-L-A-B`):**
   - (1) **Performance or Tender** [`Sec 37–38`]: Both sides finish their promises, or one offers and the other refuses.
   - (2) **Mutual Agreement** [`Sec 62–63`]: Novation, Rescission, Alteration, Remission, Waiver, or Merger.
   - (3) **Supervening Impossibility** [`Sec 56`]: An unexpected external event makes performance impossible or illegal.
   - (4) **Operation of Law**: Death in personal skill contracts, insolvency, or merger.
   - (5) **Lapse of Time**: 3-year deadline under the Limitation Act 1908.
   - (6) **Unauthorized Material Alteration**: Secretly changing terms voids the document.
   - (7) **Breach of Contract** [`Sec 39 & Sec 73`]: Actual breach or anticipatory breach.

3. **Core Descriptions:**
   - Write a short, 1-sentence explanation for each mode to secure full marks.

4. **Concluding Takeaway:**
   - Performance is the normal way to end a contract. Breach forces the breaking party to pay damages.

### Model Exam Question 1 · Discharge by Performance and Essentials of Valid Tender (03 Marks)

> **Question:** Explain discharge of contract by performance. Distinguish between actual performance and attempted performance (tender). State the essentials of a valid tender under Section 38. *(03 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Rule of Performance [Section 37]:**
   - State that both parties must perform or offer to perform their promises.

2. **Actual vs Attempted Performance (Tender):**
   - **Actual Performance:** Both parties fully carry out their promised duties.
   - **Attempted Performance (Tender) [`Sec 38`]:** The promisor offers to perform, but the buyer refuses to accept it.

3. **Three Essentials of Valid Tender [`Sec 38`]:**
   - (1) The offer must be unconditional.
   - (2) It must be made at the proper time, place, and business hours.
   - (3) It must give the buyer a fair chance to inspect the goods.

4. **Tender of Goods vs Tender of Money:**
   - Tender of goods completely frees the seller from further duty.
   - Tender of money does not cancel the debt. But it stops interest and shields against court costs.

### Model Exam Question 2 · Discharge by Mutual Agreement under Sections 62 and 63 (04 Marks)

> **Question:** Explain how a contract is discharged by mutual agreement under the Contract Act, 1872. Define Novation, Rescission, Alteration, Remission, and Waiver with examples. *(04 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Statutory Rule [Section 62]:**
   - What started by mutual agreement can end or change by mutual agreement.
   - If parties agree to a new deal, they do not have to perform the original contract.

2. **Five Core Forms with Examples:**
   - *Novation [Sec 62]:* Replacing an old contract with a new contract, or substituting a party. Example: A owes B money; all agree C will pay B instead.
   - *Rescission [Sec 62]:* Cancelling the contract mutually before performance. Both sides are released.
   - *Alteration [Sec 62]:* Changing terms mutually between the same parties.
   - *Remission [Sec 63]:* Accepting less money or lesser performance to settle the whole debt.
   - *Waiver:* Giving up a contractual right voluntarily without any payment.

3. **Critical Statutory Nuance (Remission):**
   - In Bangladesh, Section 63 allows remission without fresh consideration.
   - This differs from English common law (*Pinnel's Case*), which required consideration to forgive debt.

4. **Concluding Rule:**
   - All parties must agree. One party cannot change terms alone.

### Model Exam Question 3 · Actual vs Anticipatory Breach and Section 39 Remedies (03 Marks)

> **Question:** Distinguish between Actual Breach and Anticipatory Breach of contract under the Contract Act, 1872. What options are available to the injured party upon an anticipatory breach under Section 39? *(03 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Definitions & Differences:**
   - **Actual Breach:** Happens when a party fails or refuses to perform on the due date.
   - **Anticipatory Breach [`Sec 39`]:** A party refuses to perform, or makes performance impossible, before the due date.
   - Present a concise comparison table covering timing, mode, sections (`Sec 73` vs `Sec 39`), and case law (*Hochster v. De La Tour*).

2. **Two Choices Under Section 39:**
   - **Option 1 (Sue Right Away):** Cancel the contract immediately and sue for damages (*Hochster v. De La Tour*). No need to wait for the due date.
   - **Option 2 (Wait for Due Date):** Keep the contract alive until the due date. Give the promisor a chance to change their mind and perform.

3. **The Big Risk of Waiting (*Avery v. Bowden*):**
   - If you wait, an unexpected event may make performance impossible under Section 56.
   - For example, war breaks out or the law changes.
   - If that happens, the promisor is excused from performance, and your claim for damages is lost (*Avery v. Bowden*).

4. **Exam Tip:**
   - Point out that suing immediately is usually the safer choice.

---

### Model Exam Question 4 · Discharge by Operation of Law, Lapse of Time, and Material Alteration (03 Marks)

> **Question:** Describe how a contract is discharged by operation of law, lapse of time, and unauthorized material alteration. *(03 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Discharge by Operation of Law:**
   - Automatic termination under the law:
     - *Death:* Contracts requiring personal talent or skill end on death. Ordinary business contracts pass to heirs up to the inherited estate.
     - *Insolvency (Bankruptcy):* A court order frees an insolvent debtor from past contract debts.
     - *Merger:* A smaller contract right merges into a larger property right.

2. **Discharge by Lapse of Time:**
   - Cite the Limitation Act 1908.
   - A lawsuit for contract breach or unpaid debt must be filed within **3 years**.
   - If 3 years pass without a lawsuit, the court remedy is barred. The contract cannot be enforced.

3. **Unauthorized Material Alteration:**
   - One party secretly alters key terms in a written document without consent.
   - Examples include changing the price, date, or interest rate.
   - The entire document becomes void in court, and the altering party loses all legal rights under it.

4. **Concluding Takeaway:**
   - These modes operate automatically without mutual consent to uphold legal deadlines and protect document honesty.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Tender of Money vs Tender of Goods:** Offering goods completely frees the seller if the buyer refuses. But offering money does not cancel the debt. It only stops interest and shields against court costs.
> - **Remission Needs No Consideration:** Under Section 63 in Bangladesh, accepting a smaller sum requires no new consideration. This is different from English law in *Pinnel's Case*.
> - **Risk of Waiting on Anticipatory Breach:** If the injured party waits until the due date, an unexpected event under Section 56 can excuse the promisor. The injured party loses the right to claim damages (*Avery v. Bowden*).
> - **Novation vs Alteration:** Novation replaces the old deal with a brand-new contract and can introduce new parties. Alteration only modifies terms in the existing contract between the same parties.
