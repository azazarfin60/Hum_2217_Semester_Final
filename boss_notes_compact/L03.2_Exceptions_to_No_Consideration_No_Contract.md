# L03.2: Exceptions to "No Consideration, No Contract"

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A (CO1 · Legal Issues · Shoaib Islam Sir) · 🔴 Highest
> - **Read Time:** ~3 mins
> - **PYQs Covered:** Prescribed Chapter 2 Core Target (4-mark theory question + 6-mark 3-scenario applied problem)
> - **Scoring Rule:** State the Section 25 rule, cite all 7 exceptions with exact statutory sections, and apply the 4 W-R-L-N conditions.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **No Right of Action [Legal Maxim]:** "Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio." *(Latin Legal Maxim)*

*Plain meaning:* A bare promise made without consideration gives no right to sue. You cannot enforce it in court.

> **Agreement Without Consideration [Section 25]:** "An agreement made without consideration is void." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* A one-sided promise where you get nothing in return is void from day one. You cannot force someone to keep it unless it meets a specific legal exception.

> **Agency Creation [Section 185]:** "No consideration is necessary to create an agency." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* You can appoint an agent without paying them any fee or commission. Their actions for you still bind you legally.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### The Seven Statutory Exceptions to Section 25

Under Section 25 of the Contract Act 1872, an agreement made without consideration is void. This rule stops people from being trapped by careless, casual, or one-sided promises. But everyday family life and commercial trade need practical exceptions.

The law recognizes seven situations where a contract without consideration is valid and legally binding:

1. **Natural Love and Affection [Section 25(1)]:** An agreement without consideration is legally binding if it meets four strict conditions (`W-R-L-N`):
   - **Written Document:** The agreement must be in writing.
   - **Registered by Law:** The document must be officially registered under the law.
   - **Natural Love and Affection:** The promise must come from genuine love and affection.
   - **Near Relation:** The parties must stand in a near relation to each other, like spouses, parents, children, or siblings.
   - **Valid Example:** A father signs and registers a deed gifting Tk. 100,000 to his son out of love. This promise is binding even though the son gives nothing in return.
   - **Negative Precedent (*Rajlukhy Dabee v. Bhootnath Mookerjee*):** A husband signed and registered a deed promising monthly maintenance to his wife after constant quarrels. The court declared the agreement void. It came from anger and hostility, not from genuine love and affection.

2. **Compensation for Past Voluntary Service [Section 25(2)]:** A promise to reward past voluntary service is valid without new consideration.
   - **Mandatory Conditions:** The person must have helped voluntarily without any prior request. Also, the service must be done directly for the promisor. Alternatively, it must fulfill a legal duty the promisor was bound to do.
   - **Valid Example:** An ECE student finds and returns a lost research laptop voluntarily. The owner later signs a written promise to pay Tk. 2,000 as a reward. This promise is an enforceable contract.

3. **Promise to Pay a Time-Barred Debt [Section 25(3)]:** Under the Limitation Act 1908, a lender cannot sue to recover an unpaid debt after 3 years. The debt becomes time-barred. But a fresh promise to pay that expired debt is valid if:
   - **Mandatory Conditions:** The promise is in writing and signed by the debtor or their authorized agent. It must also refer to a specific sum of money.
   - **Strict Statutory Rule:** An oral promise over the phone or in person is completely void. Only a written and signed promise revives the debt.
   - **Valid Example:** A borrower owes Tk. 50,000 on an expired 4-year debt. The borrower signs a letter promising to pay Tk. 30,000 to settle it. The lender can legally recover that Tk. 30,000.

4. **Creation of Agency [Section 185]:** Section 185 clearly states that you do not need consideration to create an agency.
   - **Operational Rule:** An agent has full legal power to represent the principal in deals with third parties. It does not matter whether the agent receives a salary, fee, or commission.
   - **Valid Example:** A director appoints an engineer as an unpaid agent to sell extra lab equipment. The sales agreements signed by the engineer legally bind the company.

5. **Completed Gifts [Section 25, Explanation 1]:** Section 25, Explanation 1 states that the rule of consideration does not apply to completed gifts.
   - **Operational Rule:** A promise to give a gift in the future is void without consideration. But once a gift is delivered and accepted, it is final. The giver cannot demand it back later claiming they received nothing in return.
   - **Valid Example:** A donor hands over testing equipment to a college workshop as an outright gift. The donor cannot take it back later by claiming lack of consideration.

6. **Contract of Guarantee [Section 127]:** A contract of guarantee does not need direct consideration between the creditor and the guarantor (surety).
   - **Statutory Rule:** Under Section 127, anything done or promised for the benefit of the principal debtor is sufficient consideration to bind the guarantor.
   - **Valid Example:** A bank lends Tk. 500,000 to a student startup because a friend signs as guarantor. Giving the loan to the student is sufficient legal consideration to bind the guarantor.

7. **Charitable Subscription with Detriment [Judicial Precedent]:** A bare promise to donate money to a charity is void because there is no consideration. But the promise becomes legally binding if the charity takes on real financial debt or liability relying on that promise.
   - **Leading Precedent (*Kedarnath Bhattacharji v. Gorie Mahomed*):** A subscriber pledged money to build a town hall. Relying on that pledge, the building committee hired contractors and took on debt. The court held the subscriber liable to pay the promised sum.
   - **Contrasting Precedent (*Abdul Aziz v. Masum Ali*):** A donor promised Tk. 500 for mosque repairs, but died before any work began or debt started. The court held the promise void. The mosque took on no financial debt or loss from the pledge.

### Summary Comparison of the Seven Exceptions

| # | Exception Name | Governing Section | Mandatory Requirements | Practical Example |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Natural Love & Affection | Section 25(1) | Written, registered, natural love, near relation | Registered deed gifting Tk. 100,000 to son |
| **2** | Past Voluntary Service | Section 25(2) | Voluntary act done for promisor without prior request | Returning lost laptop; owner promises Tk. 2,000 reward |
| **3** | Time-Barred Debt | Section 25(3) | Written and signed by debtor, ascertained debt | Signed letter promising Tk. 30,000 on 4-year debt |
| **4** | Creation of Agency | Section 185 | Appointment of agent to represent principal | Appointing unpaid agent to negotiate equipment sales |
| **5** | Completed Gifts | Section 25 Expl. 1 | Actual delivery and acceptance between parties | Delivering laboratory equipment as an outright gift |
| **6** | Guarantee Contract | Section 127 | Benefit to principal debtor supports surety promise | Signing guarantee for an entrepreneur's bank loan |
| **7** | Detrimental Charity | Judicial Precedent | Promisee takes on financial debt relying on pledge | Town hall building debt taken on (*Kedarnath* rule) |

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 7 Exceptions & Section 25(1) Keys
> - **7 Exceptions (`L-V-T-A-G-G-C`):**
>   - **L:** **L**ove and affection [`Sec 25(1)`]
>   - **V:** **V**oluntary past service reward [`Sec 25(2)`]
>   - **T:** **T**ime-barred debt in writing [`Sec 25(3)`]
>   - **A:** **A**gency creation [`Sec 185`]
>   - **G:** **G**ifts already delivered [`Sec 25 Expl. 1`]
>   - **G:** **G**uarantee contract [`Sec 127`]
>   - **C:** **C**haritable pledge where debt is taken on
> - **Section 25(1) Keys (`W-R-L-N`):** **W**ritten paper, **R**egistered by law, **L**ove and affection, and **N**ear relation.

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### Model Exam Question 1 · Rule of Section 25 and Seven Exceptions (04 Marks)

> **Question:** "An agreement made without consideration is void." Discuss this rule (*Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio*) under Section 25 of the Contract Act, 1872. Explain the statutory exceptions to this rule. *(04 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Legal Maxim & General Rule:**
   - State the Latin maxim *Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio* (no legal action arises from a bare promise).
   - Cite Section 25 of the Contract Act, 1872.
   - Explain that consideration is the price of a promise. Free promises have no legal force.

2. **List All Seven Exceptions:**
   - State each exception with its exact section number:
     - `Sec 25(1)`: Natural Love and Affection.
     - `Sec 25(2)`: Past Voluntary Service.
     - `Sec 25(3)`: Time-Barred Debt.
     - `Sec 185`: Agency Creation.
     - `Sec 25 Expl. 1`: Completed Gifts.
     - `Sec 127`: Contract of Guarantee.
     - Judicial Precedent: Charity with Detriment.

3. **Key Conditions & Landmark Cases:**
   - List the four `W-R-L-N` rules for Section 25(1).
   - Cite *Rajlukhy Dabee v. Bhootnath Mookerjee* to show that quarrels cancel out affection.
   - Emphasize that Section 25(3) strictly requires a written and signed paper.

4. **Concluding Takeaway:**
   - These statutory exceptions protect family gifts, rescue rewards, business agents, and lenders from unfair refusal.

### Model Exam Question 2 · Applied 3-Scenario Problem on Consideration Exceptions (06 Marks)

> **Question:** Evaluate the legal validity of the agreements in the following three independent scenarios under the Contract Act, 1872:
>
> **(i)** An elder brother executes and registers a written deed promising to give his younger brother a residential plot worth Tk. 2,000,000 out of natural love and affection. *(02 Marks)*
>
> **(ii)** An ECE student finds an expensive drone lost by a robotics lab and returns it. The lab director later signs a written note promising to pay the student Tk. 10,000 as a reward. *(02 Marks)*
>
> **(iii)** A telecom firm owes a component supplier Tk. 100,000 for circuit boards delivered four years ago. The supplier demands payment. The firm's director verbally promises over the phone to pay Tk. 60,000. Later, the firm refuses to pay. *(02 Marks)*

#### Scenario (i): Registered Land Gift to Brother (02 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:** An elder brother signs and registers a written deed. He promises to transfer a land plot worth Tk. 2,000,000 to his younger brother out of love and affection. The younger brother pays nothing in return.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:** Section 25(1) of the Contract Act, 1872 (agreement made on account of natural love and affection).

3. **Legal Analysis:** Under Section 25(1), an agreement without consideration is valid if it meets four strict rules (`W-R-L-N`):
   - **Written Document:** The agreement must be in writing.
   - **Registered by Law:** The document must be officially registered.
   - **Natural Love and Affection:** The promise must spring from genuine love.
   - **Near Relation:** The parties must stand in a near relation to each other.
   - **Application to Facts:** Brothers are near blood relatives. The deed is in writing, officially registered, and made out of genuine love. All four statutory requirements are fully satisfied.

4. **Direct Conclusion:** The agreement is valid, binding, and legally enforceable. The younger brother can enforce the land transfer in court.

#### Scenario (ii): Reward for Found Drone (02 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:** An ECE student voluntarily finds and returns an expensive drone lost by a robotics lab. The student acted without any prior request or reward promise. Later, the lab director signs a written note promising to pay the student Tk. 10,000 as a reward.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:** Section 25(2) of the Contract Act, 1872 (promise to compensate for past voluntary service).

3. **Legal Analysis:** Under Section 25(2), a promise to pay someone who already did something voluntarily for the promisor is valid without new consideration:
   - **Voluntary Act:** The student recovered the drone freely without any prior request.
   - **Direct Benefit:** The act directly benefited the promisor (the robotics lab).
   - **Subsequent Promise:** The lab director signed a promise to pay for that past act.
   - **Application to Facts:** Past voluntary service counts as valid statutory support. This makes the director's promise legally binding.

4. **Direct Conclusion:** The promise forms a valid and legally binding contract. The student can legally recover the Tk. 10,000 reward from the lab.

#### Scenario (iii): Verbal Promise on Old Debt (02 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:** A telecom firm owed a supplier Tk. 100,000 for circuit boards delivered 4 years ago. The debt became time-barred under the Limitation Act. The supplier asked for payment. The firm's director verbally promised over the phone to pay Tk. 60,000, but later refused.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:** Section 25(3) of the Contract Act, 1872, and the Limitation Act, 1908.

3. **Legal Analysis:** Under the Limitation Act 1908, a creditor must file a debt recovery suit within 3 years. After 3 years, the debt is time-barred and cannot be sued upon. Under Section 25(3), a promise to pay a time-barred debt is valid only if:
   - **Written Document:** The promise is put down in writing.
   - **Signed by Debtor:** The promise is signed by the debtor or their authorized agent.
   - **Application to Facts:** Here, the director made only a verbal promise over the phone. No written or signed paper exists. Under Section 25, an oral promise to pay an expired debt is completely void.

4. **Direct Conclusion:** The verbal promise is void and legally unenforceable. The supplier cannot recover the money in court.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Section 25(1) Demands All Four Keys:** Writing and registration alone are not enough if natural love is missing. In *Rajlukhy Dabee*, constant quarrels proved the lack of love, making the deed void.
> - **Oral Promises on Time-Barred Debts Are Void:** Under Section 25(3), promising to pay an old debt strictly requires a written document signed by the debtor. An oral phone promise has zero legal value.
> - **Charity Pledges Need Real Financial Loss:** A bare pledge to donate is not binding. It becomes binding only if the charity takes on debt or costs relying on that pledge (*Kedarnath* rule).
> - **Agency Creation Needs No Consideration:** Under Section 185, appointing an agent is legally valid without consideration. Do not confuse this with whether the agent is owed agreed wages later.
