# L03.2: Exceptions to "No Consideration, No Contract"

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A (CO1 · Legal Issues · Shoaib Islam Sir) · 🔴 Highest
> - **Read Time:** ~3 mins
> - **PYQs Covered:** Prescribed Chapter 2 Core Target (4-mark theory question + 6-mark 3-scenario applied problem)
> - **Scoring Rule:** State the Section 25 rule, cite all 7 exceptions with exact statutory sections, and apply the 4 W-R-L-N conditions.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **No Right of Action [Legal Maxim]:** "Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio." *(Latin Legal Maxim)*

*Plain meaning:* A bare agreement entered into without consideration gives rise to no legal cause of action and cannot be enforced in a court of law.

> **Agreement Without Consideration [Section 25]:** "An agreement made without consideration is void." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* A one-sided promise made without receiving anything of legal value in return is gratuitous and has no binding legal effect, subject to specific statutory exceptions.

> **Agency Creation [Section 185]:** "No consideration is necessary to create an agency." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* An agent is legally authorized to represent the principal and create binding legal relationships even if the agent receives no compensation or commission.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### The Seven Statutory Exceptions to Section 25

Under Section 25 of the Contract Act 1872, an agreement made without consideration is void. This rule prevents individuals from being bound by rash, casual, or one-sided promises. However, commercial necessity and domestic relations require practical exceptions. The Contract Act and judicial precedent establish seven recognized exceptions where an agreement without consideration is valid and legally binding:

1. **Natural Love and Affection [Section 25(1)]:** An agreement without consideration is enforceable if it satisfies four mandatory conditions (`W-R-L-N`):
   - **Written Document:** The agreement must be expressed in writing.
   - **Registered by Law:** The document must be officially registered under the law governing registration of documents.
   - **Natural Love and Affection:** The promise must spring from genuine natural love and affection.
   - **Near Relation:** The contracting parties must stand in a near relation to each other (such as spouses, parents, children, or siblings).
   - **Valid Example:** A father executes and registers a written deed gifting Tk. 100,000 to his son out of natural love and affection. The agreement is binding despite the absence of consideration from the son.
   - **Negative Precedent (*Rajlukhy Dabee v. Bhootnath Mookerjee*):** A husband executed a registered document promising separate maintenance to his wife following persistent domestic quarrels. The court declared the agreement void because it arose from hostility and disagreement rather than genuine natural love and affection.

2. **Compensation for Past Voluntary Service [Section 25(2)]:** A promise to compensate a person who has already voluntarily performed an act for the promisor is legally valid without fresh consideration.
   - **Mandatory Conditions:** The service must have been performed voluntarily without any prior request from the promisor, and it must have been done directly for the promisor or something the promisor was legally compellable to do.
   - **Valid Example:** An ECE student voluntarily recovers and returns a lost research laptop. The owner subsequently executes a written promise to pay the student Tk. 2,000 as a reward. The promise constitutes an enforceable contract.

3. **Promise to Pay a Time-Barred Debt [Section 25(3)]:** Under the Limitation Act 1908, a creditor cannot institute a lawsuit to recover a simple debt after three years. However, a fresh promise to pay the expired debt is valid if:
   - **Mandatory Conditions:** The promise is made in writing, signed by the debtor or their authorized agent, and refers to an ascertained sum of money.
   - **Strict Statutory Rule:** An oral promise to pay a time-barred debt is completely void. Only a written and signed promise revives the legal obligation.
   - **Valid Example:** A debtor owes Tk. 50,000 on an expired four-year debt. The debtor signs a letter promising to pay Tk. 30,000 in full settlement. The creditor can legally recover the Tk. 30,000.

4. **Creation of Agency [Section 185]:** Section 185 explicitly establishes that no consideration is necessary to create an agency.
   - **Operational Rule:** An agent is legally empowered to represent the principal in transactions with third parties, regardless of whether the agent receives remuneration, fees, or commission.
   - **Valid Example:** A company director appoints an engineer as an unpaid agent to negotiate the sale of surplus laboratory hardware. The transactions executed by the agent legally bind the company.

5. **Completed Gifts [Section 25, Explanation 1]:** Explanation 1 to Section 25 provides that the lack of consideration does not affect the legal validity of gifts completed between a donor and donee.
   - **Operational Rule:** While an executory promise to make a gift in the future is void without consideration, a gift already completed by actual delivery cannot be retracted by the donor on grounds of absent consideration.
   - **Valid Example:** A donor hands over testing equipment to a college workshop as an outright gift. The donor cannot later demand the equipment back by asserting lack of consideration.

6. **Contract of Guarantee [Section 127]:** A contract of guarantee does not require direct consideration passing between the creditor and the surety.
   - **Statutory Rule:** Under Section 127, anything done or any promise made for the benefit of the principal debtor provides sufficient legal consideration to support the surety's guarantee.
   - **Valid Example:** A bank lends Tk. 500,000 to a student startup because a guarantor signs a surety agreement. The disbursement of the loan to the student serves as sufficient consideration binding the guarantor.

7. **Charitable Subscription with Detriment [Judicial Precedent]:** A bare promise to contribute money to a charitable institution is unenforceable for want of consideration. However, the promise becomes legally binding if the promisee incurs financial debt or liability in reliance upon the pledge.
   - **Leading Precedent (*Kedarnath Bhattacharji v. Gorie Mahomed*):** A subscriber pledged money to a town hall construction fund. Relying on the subscription list, the building committee entered into contractor debts. The court held the subscriber liable to pay the promised amount.
   - **Contrasting Precedent (*Abdul Aziz v. Masum Ali*):** A subscriber promised Tk. 500 toward mosque repairs, but died before any work commenced or liabilities arose. The court held the promise void because no detrimental reliance had occurred.

### Summary Comparison of the Seven Exceptions

| # | Exception Name | Governing Section | Mandatory Requirements | Practical Example |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Natural Love & Affection | Section 25(1) | Written, registered, natural love, near relation | Registered deed gifting Tk. 100,000 to son |
| **2** | Past Voluntary Service | Section 25(2) | Voluntary act done for promisor without prior request | Returning lost laptop; owner promises Tk. 2,000 reward |
| **3** | Time-Barred Debt | Section 25(3) | Written and signed by debtor, ascertained debt | Signed letter promising Tk. 30,000 on 4-year debt |
| **4** | Creation of Agency | Section 185 | Appointment of agent to represent principal | Appointing unpaid agent to negotiate equipment sales |
| **5** | Completed Gifts | Section 25 Expl. 1 | Actual delivery and acceptance between parties | Delivering laboratory equipment as an outright gift |
| **6** | Guarantee Contract | Section 127 | Benefit to principal debtor supports surety promise | Signing guarantee for an entrepreneur's bank loan |
| **7** | Detrimental Charity | Judicial Precedent | Promisee incurs financial debt relying on pledge | Town hall building debt incurred (*Kedarnath* rule) |

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 7 Exceptions & Section 25(1) Keys
> - **7 Exceptions (L-V-T-A-G-G-C):** **L**ove and affection [Sec 25(1)], **V**oluntary service compensation [Sec 25(2)], **T**ime-barred debt in writing [Sec 25(3)], **A**gency appointment [Sec 185], **G**ifts completed [Sec 25 Expl. 1], **G**uarantee contract [Sec 127], **C**haritable pledge with detriment.
> - **Section 25(1) Keys (W-R-L-N):** **W**ritten document, **R**egistered by law, **L**ove and affection, **N**ear relation between parties.

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### Model Exam Question 1 · Rule of Section 25 and Seven Exceptions (04 Marks)

> **Question:** "An agreement made without consideration is void." Discuss this rule (*Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio*) under Section 25 of the Contract Act, 1872. Explain the statutory exceptions to this rule. *(04 Marks)*

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Legal Maxim & Rule:** State the Latin maxim *Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio* (no right of action arises from a bare agreement) and Section 25 of the Contract Act, 1872. Explain that consideration is the price of a promise, and gratuitous promises lack binding legal force.

2. **Statutory Exceptions:** State all seven exceptions with exact statutory citations: Section 25(1) (Natural Love & Affection), Section 25(2) (Past Voluntary Service), Section 25(3) (Time-Barred Debt), Section 185 (Agency), Section 25 Explanation 1 (Completed Gifts), Section 127 (Guarantee), and Judicial Precedent (Charity with Detriment).

3. **Key Conditions & Case Law:** Detail the four `W-R-L-N` criteria under Section 25(1) and cite *Rajlukhy Dabee v. Bhootnath Mookerjee* to demonstrate that quarrels negate affection. Emphasize that Section 25(3) strictly requires writing and signature.

4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Note that these statutory exceptions safeguard family settlements, voluntary rescue acts, commercial agency, and creditor reliance from arbitrary repudiation.

### Model Exam Question 2 · Applied 3-Scenario Problem on Consideration Exceptions (06 Marks)

> **Question:** Evaluate the legal validity of the agreements in the following three independent scenarios under the Contract Act, 1872:
>
> **(i)** An elder brother executes and registers a written deed promising to give his younger brother a residential plot worth Tk. 2,000,000 out of natural love and affection. *(02 Marks)*
>
> **(ii)** An ECE student finds an expensive drone lost by a robotics lab and returns it. The lab director later signs a written note promising to pay the student Tk. 10,000 as a reward. *(02 Marks)*
>
> **(iii)** A telecom firm owes a component supplier Tk. 100,000 for circuit boards delivered four years ago. The supplier demands payment. The firm's director verbally promises over the phone to pay Tk. 60,000. Later, the firm refuses to pay. *(02 Marks)*

#### Scenario (i): Registered Land Gift to Brother (02 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:** An elder brother executed and registered a formal written deed promising to transfer a residential plot worth Tk. 2,000,000 to his younger brother out of natural love and affection, without receiving any monetary consideration.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:** Section 25(1) of the Contract Act, 1872 (agreement made on account of natural love and affection).

3. **Legal Analysis:** Under Section 25(1), an agreement without consideration is valid and enforceable if it fulfills four mandatory conditions (`W-R-L-N`):
   - **Written Document:** The agreement must be expressed in writing.
   - **Registered by Law:** The document must be officially registered under document registration law.
   - **Natural Love and Affection:** The agreement must spring from genuine natural love and affection.
   - **Near Relation:** The contracting parties must stand in a near relation to each other.
   - **Application to Facts:** Brothers are near blood relatives. The deed is written, registered, and executed out of natural affection. All four statutory requirements are fully satisfied.

4. **Direct Conclusion:** The agreement is valid, binding, and legally enforceable. The younger brother can enforce the transfer of the plot in a court of law.

#### Scenario (ii): Reward for Found Drone (02 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:** An ECE student voluntarily found and returned an expensive drone lost by a robotics lab without any prior request or promise. Subsequently, the lab director signed a written note promising to pay the student Tk. 10,000 as a reward.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:** Section 25(2) of the Contract Act, 1872 (promise to compensate for past voluntary service).

3. **Legal Analysis:** Under Section 25(2), a promise to compensate a person who has already voluntarily done something for the promisor is legally valid without fresh consideration:
   - **Voluntary Act:** The student performed the service voluntarily without prior request.
   - **Direct Benefit:** The act directly benefited the promisor (the robotics lab).
   - **Subsequent Promise:** The promisor subsequently executed a written promise to compensate the student.
   - **Application to Facts:** Past voluntary service provides valid statutory support for the subsequent promise, making it legally binding.

4. **Direct Conclusion:** The promise constitutes a valid and legally binding contract. The student can legally recover the Tk. 10,000 reward from the lab.

#### Scenario (iii): Verbal Promise on Old Debt (02 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:** A telecom firm owed a supplier Tk. 100,000 for circuit boards delivered four years ago. The limitation period expired. When the supplier demanded payment, the firm's director verbally promised over the phone to pay Tk. 60,000, but later refused.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:** Section 25(3) of the Contract Act, 1872 and the Limitation Act, 1908.

3. **Legal Analysis:** Under the Limitation Act 1908, a suit to recover a simple debt must be instituted within three years. After three years, the debt becomes time-barred and the judicial remedy is barred. Under Section 25(3), a promise to pay a time-barred debt becomes enforceable only if it satisfies two strict criteria:
   - **Written Document:** The promise must be expressed in writing.
   - **Signed by Debtor:** The promise must be signed by the debtor or their authorized agent.
   - **Application to Facts:** Here, the director made a purely verbal promise over the phone. No written and signed document exists. An oral promise to pay a time-barred debt remains completely void under Section 25.

4. **Direct Conclusion:** The verbal promise is void and legally unenforceable. The supplier cannot recover the money.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Section 25(1) Demands All Four Keys:** Writing and registration alone are insufficient if natural love is missing. In *Rajlukhy Dabee*, domestic quarrels proved the absence of love, rendering the deed void.
> - **Oral Promises on Time-Barred Debts Are Void:** Under Section 25(3), a promise to pay an expired debt strictly requires a written document signed by the debtor or their agent. An oral promise over the phone has zero legal standing.
> - **Charity Pledges Require Financial Detriment:** A bare subscription promise is revocable at will unless the promisee incurred actual contractual liability or debt relying on the pledge (*Kedarnath* rule).
> - **Agency Creation Requires No Consideration:** Under Section 185, appointing an agent is legally valid without consideration. Do not confuse the validity of the agency with the agent's entitlement to agreed wages.
