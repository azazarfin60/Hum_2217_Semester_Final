# L01.1: Definitions of Law and Society

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A · Shoaib Islam Sir · High Priority | **Read Time:** ~3 mins
> - **PYQs Covered:** Prescribed under Ch. 1 "Study All" (0 past finals; core 2-3 mark target)
> - **Scoring Rule:** Quote jurists verbatim and cite Holland's 3 traits (H-E-S) for full marks.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

1. **Law (General Meaning):**
   > "Law is a system of rules that govern a society with the intention of maintaining social order, upholding justice, and preventing harm to individuals' property." (SI Sir Notes & Slides, Chapter 1)
   - *Plain meaning:* State-enforced rules that maintain social order and protect property.

2. **Holland's Definition:**
   > "Law is a rule of external human action enforced by the sovereign political authority." (Sir Thomas Erskine Holland)
   - *Plain meaning:* A sovereign rule regulating only outward human actions.

3. **Salmond's Definition:**
   > "Law is the body of principles recognized and applied by the State in the administration of justice." (Sir John William Salmond)
   - *Plain meaning:* Court-applied principles for administering justice.

4. **Woodrow Wilson's Definition:**
   > "Law is that portion of the established habit and thought of mankind which has gained distinct and formal recognition in the shape of uniform rules backed by the authority and power of the government." (Woodrow Wilson)
   - *Plain meaning:* Human habits recognized as uniform rules backed by state power.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### A. Holland's Three Essential Characteristics of Law

From Holland's definition, law has three core elements:

1. **Rule for Human Action:** Applies only to living people, never animals or machines.
2. **Regulation of External Conduct:** Regulates outward physical acts, not private thoughts or intentions.
3. **State Enforcement:** Enforced by sovereign political power through courts and penalties.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: H-E-S Traits
> Remember **H-E-S**: **H**uman action, **E**xternal conduct, and **S**tate enforcement.

### B. Jurist Comparison: Holland vs. Salmond vs. Woodrow Wilson

| Parameter | Thomas Erskine Holland | John William Salmond | Woodrow Wilson |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core View** | Command backed by state power. | Judicial rules applied by courts. | Customs given formal state backing. |
| **Focus** | Outward physical human actions. | Principles applied in courts. | Human habits and traditions. |
| **Authority** | Sovereign political authority. | The state through its courts. | Organized power of government. |
| **Enforcement** | Sovereign threat and penalties. | Court administration of justice. | Uniform rules backed by state power. |
| **Catchphrase** | *"Rule of external human action"* | *"Administration of justice"* | *"Established habit and thought"* |

### C. Dynamic Relationship: Law, Government, and Society

$$
\text{Law} \longrightarrow \text{Enforcement} \longrightarrow \text{Government / Parliament}
$$

1. **Society Requires Law:** Human groups need order. Without law, anarchy destroys safety.
2. **Government Enacts and Enforces Law:** Parliament enacts statutes; executive police enforce them.
3. **Society Empowers Government:** Citizens grant authority and taxes to government for security.
4. **Law Restrains Government:** Constitutional rules bind state officials, preventing tyranny.

### D. Domestic Labour Law Scope (BLC 2006)

Domestic labour law in Bangladesh is codified in **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (Act XLII of 2006)**:

1. **Consolidation:** Unified and repealed 25 outdated, scattered labour laws into one code.
2. **Structure:** Comprises **326 sections** across **21 chapters**, plus schedules.
3. **Coverage:** Applies to factories, commercial shops, transport, and workshops nationwide.
4. **Core Areas:** Covers employment conditions, hours, fire safety, welfare, maternity, trade unions, CBA, and Labour Courts.

### Model Q3 (Short Note · 02 Marks)
**Question:** "Law is the command of the sovereign." Explain briefly.

**Full Answer:**
This classical view defines law through three core elements:
1. **Sovereign Source:** Originates from the supreme political authority in the state.
2. **State Sanction:** Disobedience brings state penalties, fines, or prison.
3. **Compulsory Conduct:** Imposes a mandatory rule of action, not optional advice.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Holland vs. Salmond:** Holland emphasizes sovereign political command. Salmond emphasizes judicial administration of justice.
> - **Thought Control Fallacy:** Law regulates external physical conduct only. It never punishes private thoughts or unspoken intentions.
> - **Statutory Numbers:** Always cite **Act XLII of 2006**, **25 repealed statutes**, **326 sections**, and **21 chapters** for full marks.
