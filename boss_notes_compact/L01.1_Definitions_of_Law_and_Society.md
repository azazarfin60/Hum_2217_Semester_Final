# L01.1: Definitions of Law and Society

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Course & Section:** Section A · Shoaib Islam Sir (CO1 Legal Issues)
> - **Priority & Timing:** High Priority · Read Time: ~3 mins
> - **PYQs Covered:** Prescribed under Chapter 1 "Study All" (Core 2 to 3 mark target)
> - **Scoring Strategy:** Quote jurists verbatim, explain Holland's three traits (H-E-S), and cite BLC 2006 statutory numbers.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Law (General Meaning):** "Law is a system of rules that govern a society with the intention of maintaining social order, upholding justice, and preventing harm to individuals' property." *(SI Sir Notes & Slides, Chapter 1)*

*Plain meaning:* Law establishes state-enforced rules of conduct that preserve social order, protect private property, and uphold justice across the community.

> **Holland's Definition:** "Law is a rule of external human action enforced by the sovereign political authority." *(Sir Thomas Erskine Holland)*

*Plain meaning:* Law is an enforceable command issued by the sovereign state that regulates only outward human behavior rather than inner thoughts.

> **Salmond's Definition:** "Law is the body of principles recognized and applied by the State in the administration of justice." *(Sir John William Salmond)*

*Plain meaning:* Law consists of the binding legal principles recognized and enforced by state courts when administering justice and resolving disputes.

> **Woodrow Wilson's Definition:** "Law is that portion of the established habit and thought of mankind which has gained distinct and formal recognition in the shape of uniform rules backed by the authority and power of the government." *(Woodrow Wilson)*

*Plain meaning:* Law represents long-standing human customs and social habits that have been formally codified into uniform rules backed by the organized coercive power of the state.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### A. Holland's Three Essential Characteristics of Law

Sir Thomas Erskine Holland defined law as a rule of external human action enforced by sovereign political authority. From this definition, three essential characteristics emerge:

1. **Rule for Human Action:**
   Law applies exclusively to living human beings within an organized society. It does not govern animals, inanimate objects, or natural phenomena.

2. **Regulation of External Conduct:**
   Law controls only outward physical acts and overt behavior. It does not monitor or penalize silent thoughts, inner desires, or private intentions until translated into physical action.

3. **State Enforcement:**
   A moral or social precept does not become law merely because it is beneficial. It attains the status of law only when recognized and enforced by sovereign political power through courts, police, and statutory penalties.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: H-E-S Characteristics
> Remember the acronym **H-E-S** to recall Holland's three traits:
> - **H:** **H**uman action (applies strictly to living people).
> - **E:** **E**xternal conduct (regulates overt physical acts, not thoughts).
> - **S:** **S**tate enforcement (backed by sovereign power and penalties).

### B. Jurist Comparison: Holland vs. Salmond vs. Woodrow Wilson

The table below contrasts how three leading legal thinkers conceptualize the nature, source, and enforcement of law:

| Parameter | Thomas Erskine Holland | John William Salmond | Woodrow Wilson |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Viewpoint** | Command backed by sovereign state power. | Judicial principles recognized and applied by courts. | Customs and habits given formal state backing. |
| **Primary Focus** | Outward physical human actions. | Principles applied in courts during trials. | Shared human habits, traditions, and thoughts. |
| **Source of Authority** | The sovereign political authority. | The state through its judicial court system. | The organized power and machinery of government. |
| **Enforcement Mechanism** | Sovereign command backed by legal penalties. | Judicial administration of justice. | Uniform rules backed by government force. |
| **Exam Catchphrase** | *"Rule of external human action"* | *"Administration of justice"* | *"Established habit and thought"* |

### C. Dynamic Relationship: Law, Government, and Society

Law, government, and society operate in a continuous cycle of mutual interdependence. This relationship is structured into a three-step governance cycle:

$$
\text{Law} \longrightarrow \text{Enforcement} \longrightarrow \text{Government / Parliament}
$$

The reciprocal bonds connecting these three entities include:

1. **Society Requires Law:**
   Human communities need established rules to coexist peacefully. Without legal standards, anarchy prevails and personal safety disappears.

2. **Government Enacts and Enforces Law:**
   The legislature enacts formal statutes to address societal needs, while the executive branch and police ensure compliance across the jurisdiction.

3. **Society Empowers Government:**
   Citizens grant authority and tax revenue to the government in return for public order, institutional justice, and civil protection.

4. **Law Restrains Government:**
   Constitutional and administrative laws set explicit boundaries on state power, preventing arbitrary governance and official tyranny.

### D. Domestic Labour Law Scope (BLC 2006)

Bangladesh codified its labour and industrial regulations under **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (Act XLII of 2006)**. Its statutory scope comprises four foundational elements:

1. **Consolidation of Legislation:**
   The Act repealed and unified 25 scattered, outdated colonial and post-colonial labour laws into a single modern code.

2. **Statutory Architecture:**
   The Code contains **326 sections** organized systematically into **21 distinct chapters**, supplemented by schedules covering occupational diseases and compensation rates.

3. **Nationwide Jurisdictional Reach:**
   The law applies across Bangladesh to all factories, commercial establishments, transport services, dockyards, and industrial workshops.

4. **Comprehensive Subject Coverage:**
   The statute regulates conditions of service, working hours, fire safety, health and hygiene, welfare facilities, maternity benefits, trade unions, CBA negotiation, and Labour Courts.

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### Model Q1: Definitions and Characteristics of Law (SI Sir Ch. 1 Suggestion · 03 Marks)

**Question:** Define Law. State the definitions of law given by Holland, Salmond, and Woodrow Wilson, and highlight the three essential characteristics of law.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **General Definition:** Open with the standard definition stating that law is a system of rules governing society to maintain social order, uphold justice, and prevent harm to property.
2. **Three Juristic Quotes:** Quote Holland, Salmond, and Woodrow Wilson verbatim from Section 1, keeping quotation marks accurate.
3. **Holland's Three Characteristics:** Present the **H-E-S** framework from Section 2 Block A:
   - *Human action:* Rules apply exclusively to living human beings.
   - *External conduct:* Regulates outward actions rather than private thoughts.
   - *State enforcement:* Sanctioned and enforced by sovereign political authority.
4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Conclude in one sentence that law combines sovereign authority with moral justice to secure social equilibrium.

**Question:** Explain the reciprocal relationship between law, government, and society. Also, state the primary statutory scope of domestic labour law in Bangladesh.

   - Society requires law to prevent anarchy.

   - Society grants authority and resources to the government.

4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Note that modern domestic labour legislation balances state regulatory power with the economic security of industrial workers.

### Model Q3: Short Note on Sovereign Command Theory (02 Marks)

**Question:** "Law is the command of the sovereign." Explain briefly.

**Full Solution:**

This classical jurisprudence concept (rooted in John Austin's positivist theory and reflected in Holland's definition) describes law through three core pillars:

1. **Sovereign Source:**
   Law originates exclusively from the supreme political authority in a state (such as Parliament). It represents a formal directive issued by the sovereign power to the community.

2. **Mandatory Course of Conduct:**
   A legal rule is not optional moral advice or a polite suggestion. It prescribes an obligatory standard of external behavior that all citizens must follow.

3. **State Sanctions and Enforcement:**
   Disobedience invites coercive state action. The sovereign backs its directives with penalties, fines, or imprisonment administered through courts of law.

> - **Holland vs. Salmond Distinction:** Holland focuses on sovereign political command over outward conduct, whereas Salmond focuses on judicial principles applied by courts in administering justice.
> - **External Conduct Rule:** Law governs outward physical acts only. It never punishes unspoken thoughts, internal intentions, or silent desires.
> - **Statutory Numbers Matter:** Always cite **Act XLII of 2006**, **25 repealed statutes**, **326 sections**, and **21 chapters** to secure full marks on BLC 2006 scope questions.
