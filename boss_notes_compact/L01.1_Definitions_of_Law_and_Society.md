## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Law (General Meaning):** "Law is a system of rules that govern a society with the intention of maintaining social order, upholding justice, and preventing harm to individuals' property." *(SI Sir Notes & Slides, Chapter 1)*

*Plain meaning:* Law is a set of rules made by the state. It keeps peace in society, treats people fairly, and stops people from harming each other's property.

> **Holland's Definition:** "Law is a rule of external human action enforced by the sovereign political authority." *(Sir Thomas Erskine Holland)*

*Plain meaning:* Law is a rule enforced by the government. It only controls outward physical actions, not your secret inner thoughts.

> **Salmond's Definition:** "Law is the body of principles recognized and applied by the State in the administration of justice." *(Sir John William Salmond)*

*Plain meaning:* Law is the set of official rules used by courts. Judges rely on these rules to settle disputes and deliver justice.

> **Woodrow Wilson's Definition:** "Law is that portion of the established habit and thought of mankind which has gained distinct and formal recognition in the shape of uniform rules backed by the authority and power of the government." *(Woodrow Wilson)*

*Plain meaning:* People follow social habits for a long time. When the government writes them down as official rules backed by police and courts, they become law.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### A. Holland's Three Essential Characteristics of Law

Sir Thomas Erskine Holland defined law as a rule of outward human action enforced by the government. His definition gives us three key traits:

1. **Rule for Human Action:**
   Law applies only to living people in society. It does not govern animals, objects, or nature.

2. **Regulation of External Conduct:**
   Law controls only outward physical actions. It never punishes silent thoughts, secret desires, or bad intentions until you actually act on them.

3. **State Enforcement:**
   A moral rule is not a law just because it is good. It becomes real law only when the government enforces it through courts, police, and official penalties.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: H-E-S Characteristics
>
> Remember the word **H-E-S** to recall Holland's three traits:
>
> - **H:** **H**uman action (applies only to living people).
> - **E:** **E**xternal conduct (controls outward physical actions, not inner thoughts).
> - **S:** **S**tate enforcement (backed by government power, police, and courts).

### B. Jurist Comparison: Holland vs. Salmond vs. Woodrow Wilson

This table compares how three famous legal scholars explain the nature, source, and enforcement of law:

| Parameter | Thomas Erskine Holland | John William Salmond | Woodrow Wilson |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Viewpoint** | A command backed by state power. | Principles used by courts to deliver justice. | Old human customs turned into official rules. |
| **Primary Focus** | Outward physical actions of people. | Rules used by judges during trials. | Shared human habits, traditions, and thoughts. |
| **Source of Authority** | The sovereign political authority (the government). | The state court system. | The organized power of the government. |
| **Enforcement Mechanism** | State commands backed by legal penalties. | Court administration of justice. | Uniform rules backed by government force. |
| **Exam Catchphrase** | *"Rule of external human action"* | *"Administration of justice"* | *"Established habit and thought"* |

### C. Dynamic Relationship: Law, Government, and Society

Law, government, and society depend on each other in a continuous loop. We can draw this relationship as a three-step cycle:

$$
\text{Law} \longrightarrow \text{Enforcement} \longrightarrow \text{Government / Parliament}
$$

Four two-way connections link these three parts:

1. **Society Needs Law:**
   People need clear rules to live together peacefully. Without law, there is chaos and no one is safe.

2. **Government Makes and Enforces Law:**
   Parliament writes official statutes to meet people's needs. The police and executive branch make sure everyone follows them.

3. **Society Gives Power to Government:**
   Citizens give authority and pay taxes to the government. In return, the government keeps public order, protects property, and delivers justice.

4. **Law Limits Government:**
   The constitution and administrative laws set clear limits on state power. These limits stop government leaders from abusing their power.

### D. Domestic Labour Law Scope (BLC 2006)

Bangladesh organized its labour and factory rules under **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (Act XLII of 2006)**. Its legal scope has four key parts:

1. **Combines Past Laws (Consolidation):**
   The Act replaced and merged 25 scattered, outdated colonial and post-colonial labour laws into one single code.

2. **Code Structure (Architecture):**
   The Code contains **326 sections** organized into **21 chapters**. It also includes schedules listing work-related diseases and injury compensation rates.

3. **Nationwide Reach (Jurisdiction):**
   This law applies across all of Bangladesh. It covers all factories, commercial shops, transport services, dockyards, and industrial workshops.

4. **Broad Topic Coverage:**
   The law sets clear rules for job conditions, work hours, fire safety, and welfare. It also covers maternity benefits, trade unions, CBA bargaining, and Labour Courts.

### Model Q1: Definitions and Characteristics of Law (SI Sir Ch. 1 Suggestion · 03 Marks)

**Question:** Define Law. State the definitions of law given by Holland, Salmond, and Woodrow Wilson, and highlight the three essential characteristics of law.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **General Definition:**
   - Define law as a system of rules that governs society.
   - Mention its goals: maintain order, uphold justice, and protect property.

2. **Three Jurist Quotes:**
   - Quote Holland, Salmond, and Woodrow Wilson word-for-word.
   - Keep quotation marks accurate.

3. **Holland's Three Characteristics (H-E-S):**
   - *Human action (H):* Rules apply only to living people, not animals or nature.
   - *External conduct (E):* Rules control outward physical acts, never silent thoughts.
   - *State enforcement (S):* Rules are backed and enforced by government power.

4. **Quick Conclusion:**
   - State that law combines government authority with justice to keep society peaceful.

**Question:** Explain the reciprocal relationship between law, government, and society. Also, state the primary statutory scope of domestic labour law in Bangladesh.

4. **Quick Conclusion:**
   - Conclude that labour law balances business growth with fair protection for workers.

### Model Q3: Short Note on Sovereign Command Theory (02 Marks)

**Question:** "Law is the command of the sovereign." Explain briefly.

**Full Solution:**

This legal concept comes from John Austin's theory and appears in Holland's definition. It explains law through three key points:

1. **Sovereign Source:**
   Law comes only from the highest political authority in a country, such as Parliament. It is an official command issued by the government to the people.

2. **Mandatory Rule of Conduct:**
   A law is not friendly advice or an optional suggestion. It is a compulsory rule of outward behavior that every citizen must obey.

3. **State Sanctions and Enforcement:**
   If someone breaks the law, the government takes action. The state backs its rules with fines, penalties, or jail time through the courts.

>
> - **Holland vs. Salmond:** Holland focuses on government commands over outward physical actions. Salmond focuses on principles that judges use in court to deliver justice.
> - **External Conduct Rule:** Law only governs outward physical actions. It never punishes unspoken thoughts or silent desires.
> - **Statutory Numbers Matter:** Always cite **Act XLII of 2006**, **25 repealed laws**, **326 sections**, and **21 chapters**. Leaving these out loses easy marks.
