# L12.1: Unfair Labour Practices (Sections 195 and 196)

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A (CO1 · Shoaib Islam Sir) · High Priority
> - **Estimated Read Time:** ~3 minutes
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2023 Q.4(b) [04m], 2023 Q.4(c) [04m], Shoaib Sir Model Questions
> - **Key Statutory Focus:** Section 195 (Employer ULPs), Section 196 (Worker ULPs), Section 291 (Penalties)

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Unfair Labour Practice:** "Any unfair act or omission that arises between an employer and an employee." *(SI Sir Class Notes, Chapter 4)*

*Plain meaning:* It means any dirty trick, unfair rule, or illegal action by either the boss or the workers. It breaks the labour law, abuses power, or ruins workplace peace.

> **Yellow-Dog Contract [Section 195(a)]:** "An agreement where an employer forces a worker to promise not to join a trade union." *(Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 195(a))*

*Plain meaning:* It is an illegal deal where a boss forces a new hire to promise never to join a union. The law treats this promise as completely void from day one.

> **Go-Slow [Section 2(lviii) & Section 196(2)(e)]:** "An organized, deliberate and purposeful slowing down of normal output, or the deliberate curtailing of production by a body of workers." *(Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 2(lviii))*

*Plain meaning:* Workers show up to work and do not walk out. Instead, they purposely work at snail's pace to cut factory output and pressure the boss.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### Employer Unfair Labour Practices (Section 195)

Section 195 of the Bangladesh Labour Act 2006 lists 12 prohibited acts for employers, employer associations, and their agents:

1. **Yellow-Dog Contracts [Sec 195(a)]:** Forcing a worker to sign an agreement promising not to join or stay in a union.
2. **Refusal to Employ [Sec 195(b)]:** Refusing to hire an applicant or firing a worker just because they belong to a union.
3. **Service Discrimination [Sec 195(c)]:** Giving worse pay, promotions, or working conditions to workers because of their union ties.
4. **Retaliatory Dismissal [Sec 195(d)]:** Firing, removing, or threatening a worker for taking part in legal union activities.
5. **Bribery and Inducement [Sec 195(e)]:** Offering cash, promotions, or perks to convince a worker to step down from a union post.
6. **Coercing the CBA [Sec 195(f)]:** Forcing a Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA) officer to sign a deal through threats, violence, or cutting off electricity and water.
7. **Rigging CBA Ballots [Sec 195(g)]:** Tampering with or influencing secret ballot elections held under Section 202 to pick the CBA.
8. **Hiring Replacement Workers [Sec 195(h)]:** Hiring strikebreakers during a legal strike under Section 211. Only an Arbitrator can permit temporary workers to prevent machine damage.
9. **Ignoring the Participation Committee [Sec 195(i)]:** Deliberately refusing to carry out agreed plans made by the two-sided Participation Committee.
10. **Refusing Communication [Sec 195(j)]:** Refusing to reply to formal letters or statutory notices sent by the CBA about an industrial dispute.
11. **Punitive Transfer [Sec 195(k)]:** Transferring key union officers (President, General Secretary, Organizing Secretary, Treasurer) without their consent, violating Section 187.
12. **Illegal Lock-Out [Sec 195(l)]:** Starting, continuing, or instigating an illegal lock-out against the workforce.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 4 Logical Section 195 Clusters
> - **1. Individual Rights (a to d):** Yellow-dog contracts, refusal to employ, service discrimination, and retaliatory dismissal.
> - **2. Union Structure (e, g, k):** Bribery to leave office, rigging CBA ballots, and punitive transfers of the 4 key officers.
> - **3. Collective Dialogue (f, i, j):** Coercing CBA officers, ignoring the Participation Committee, and refusing dispute letters.
> - **4. Industrial Action (h, l):** Recruiting replacement workers during lawful strikes and declaring an illegal lock-out.

### Worker and Union Unfair Labour Practices (Section 196)

Section 196 protects enterprise management, factory machinery, and non-union workers from intimidation, violence, and unlawful disruption:

1. **Working Hours Prohibition [Section 196(1)]:**
   - No worker or union officer can conduct union activities during working hours without employer permission.
   - *Statutory Exception:* The President or General Secretary of the CBA can attend official bargaining, conciliation, or Labour Court hearings. But they must give advance notice to the employer.

2. **The Six Prohibited Worker and Union Acts [Section 196(2)]:**
   - **Membership Coercion [Sec 196(2)(a)]:** Threatening or bullying any worker to force them to join, stay in, or resign from a union.
   - **Bribery to Quit Office [Sec 196(2)(b)]:** Offering cash or gifts to push someone to quit a union post or avoid becoming an officer.
   - **Compelling Dues by Force [Sec 196(2)(c)]:** Forcing a worker to pay union fees through threats, physical intimidation, wrongful confinement, or cutting off power and water.
   - **Coercing the Employer [Sec 196(2)(d)]:** Forcing an employer to sign deals or accept demands through assault, threats, confinement, or cutting off utilities.
   - **Illegal Strike and Go-Slow [Sec 196(2)(e)]:** Starting an illegal strike without notice and secret ballot under Section 211, or staging an intentional go-slow.
   - **Gherao, Blockades, and Property Damage [Sec 196(2)(f)]:** Staging a gherao (trapping managers inside offices), blocking roads, disrupting communications, or damaging factory machinery and property.

3. **Interference in CBA Elections [Section 196(3)]:**
   - No trade union, worker, or agent can influence secret ballot CBA elections through threats, fake voting, undue influence, or bribery.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: Section 196(2) Split (3 Inward + 3 Outward)
> - **3 Inward Acts (Directed Against Fellow Workers):** Intimidating workers on union membership (`a`), bribing members to quit office (`b`), and compelling dues through duress (`c`).
> - **3 Outward Acts (Directed Against Enterprise):** Coercing the employer via utility cuts (`d`), starting illegal strikes or go-slows (`e`), and staging gherao or property damage (`f`).

### Statutory Comparison and Criminal Penalties (Section 195 vs. Section 196 & Section 291)

| Dimension | Employer ULPs (`Sec 195`) | Worker & Union ULPs (`Sec 196`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Statute** | Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 195 | Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 196 |
| **Targeted Parties** | Employers, employer associations, and their agents | Individual workers, trade unions, and their agents |
| **Protected Entities** | Individual workers, union officers, and CBA | Factory owners, management staff, and non-union workers |
| **Scope of Prohibitions** | 12 specific statutory prohibitions (`Sec 195(a)–(l)`) | Work-hour ban (`(1)`), 6 prohibited acts (`(2)`), and election rule (`(3)`) |
| **Key Forms of Coercion** | Yellow-dog contracts, illegal lock-outs, strike-breakers | Physical gherao, utility cuts, go-slows, and wildcat strikes |
| **Officer Protection** | Bans transfer of President, General Secretary, Org. Secretary, and Treasurer | Prohibits ballot tampering, officer bribes, and membership coercion |
| **Statutory Penalties (`Sec 291`)** | Imprisonment up to **2 years**, fine up to **10,000 Taka**, or both | **Worker:** Imprisonment up to **1 year**, fine up to **5,000 Taka**, or both<br>**Trade Union:** Imprisonment up to **2 years**, fine up to **10,000 Taka**, or both |

Under **Section 291** of the Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, unfair labour practices are criminal offences tried before the Labour Court:

- **Employers [Sec 291(1)]:** Any employer breaking Section 195 commits an offence. They face up to **2 years** in jail, up to **10,000 Taka** fine, or both.
- **Individual Workers [Sec 291(2)]:** Any worker breaking Section 196 commits an offence. They face up to **1 year** in jail, up to **5,000 Taka** fine, or both.
- **Trade Unions and Non-Workers [Sec 291(3)]:** Any union or outsider breaking Section 196 commits an offence. They face up to **2 years** in jail, up to **10,000 Taka** fine, or both.

### Consequences of ULPs on Employers and Employees (2023 Q.4(b))

**Consequences on Employers:**

1. **Criminal Prosecution and Penalties:** Company directors and factory managers face criminal trial before the Labour Court. They risk fines and prison terms of up to 2 years under Section 291(1).
2. **Severe Operational and Financial Losses:** Bad labour relations trigger sudden strikes and factory shutdowns. This leads to missed shipment deadlines and heavy financial penalties.
3. **Cancellation of International Export Orders:** Global buyers enforce strict ethical codes. Unfair labour practices can lead to canceled export orders and lost international certifications.
4. **High Turnover of Skilled Technical Personnel:** A hostile workplace drives away skilled engineers and technicians. As a result, hiring and training costs shoot up.

**Consequences on Employees:**

1. **Loss of Livelihood and Income:** Workers face unfair firing, demotions, or wage cuts. This suddenly cuts off family income and basic security.
2. **Suppression of Collective Voice:** When employers weaken the CBA, workers lose their collective bargaining voice. They can no longer negotiate safely for fair wages or safe working conditions.
3. **Workplace Fear and Psychological Distress:** Management spying, harsh interrogations, and sudden transfers create constant fear and mental stress.
4. **Disciplinary Dismissal and Criminal Records:** Workers who break Section 196 face immediate firing for misconduct under Section 23(4)(g). They also risk up to 1 year in jail under Section 291(2).

---

### Strategies for Trade Unions to Address ULPs (2023 Q.4(c))

**Statutory and Legal Strategies:**

1. **Filing Complaints Before the Labour Court [Sec 291 & Sec 214]:** The CBA can file formal criminal cases against offending managers under Section 291(1). Unlawfully dismissed workers can also petition under Section 33 for job reinstatement with full back pay.
2. **Invoking Statutory Dispute Settlement [Sec 210]:** The CBA serves a formal written dispute notice under Section 210(1). This forces management to hold bipartite talks within 15 days, or move to government conciliation.
3. **Administrative Petitions to the Director of Labour [Sec 187]:** Employers cannot transfer union officers unlawfully under Section 187. The union can petition the Director of Labour for swift relief.

**Internal and Organizational Strategies:**

4. **Maintaining a Complete Evidence Dossier:** Unions must collect and preserve hard proof. This includes copies of transfer letters, audio recordings, meeting minutes, and signed witness statements.
5. **Resolving Grievances via the Participation Committee [Sec 205]:** Worker members can raise anti-union abuses inside the bipartite Participation Committee before disputes blow up into open conflict.
6. **Using Buyer Compliance and National Federations [Sec 200]:** Unions can alert international buyers and national federations. Global brand audit pressure often forces factory owners to obey the law.
7. **Training Members on Lawful Conduct:** Unions must train workers to avoid violence, gherao, and wildcat strikes. This protects workers' statutory immunities under Section 197 and Section 198.

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### 2023 Final Q.4(b) [04 Marks] · Consequences of Unfair Labour Practices on Employers and Employees

**Exam Question:**

> Evaluate the potential consequences of unfair labor practices on both employers and employees.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Opening Statutory Framework:** Cite the key legal sections: Section 195 (Employer ULPs), Section 196 (Worker ULPs), and Section 291 (Statutory Penalties).
2. **Consequences on Employers:** Explain 4 major business impacts:
   - *Criminal liability:* Bosses face fines and up to 2 years in prison under Section 291(1).
   - *Financial losses:* Wildcat strikes halt production and trigger heavy delay penalties.
   - *Lost export orders:* Global buyers cancel purchase orders and revoke compliance certificates.
   - *Brain drain:* Experienced engineers quit, driving up recruitment and training costs.
3. **Consequences on Employees:** Explain 4 major worker impacts:
   - *Loss of livelihood:* Retaliatory firings and wage cuts destroy family income.
   - *Weakened collective voice:* Attacking the CBA strips workers of bargaining power for fair wages.
   - *Workplace fear:* Spying, interrogations, and forced transfers cause severe mental stress.
   - *Dismissal and jail:* Workers breaking Section 196 face firing under Section 23(4)(g) and jail under Section 291(2).
4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Unfair labour practices harm both sides. They destroy business viability and hurt worker welfare. Following the dispute steps in Section 210 is essential for lasting workplace peace.

### 2023 Final Q.4(c) [04 Marks] · Strategies for Unions to Address Unfair Labour Practices

**Exam Question:**

> Propose strategies that unions can employ to address unfair labor practices effectively.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Opening Statutory Context:** Note that when employers break Section 195, unions should use legal and organized methods rather than illegal violence.
2. **Statutory and Legal Routes:** Explain 3 legal actions:
   - *Labour Court cases:* File criminal charges under Section 291(1) and seek reinstatement under Section 33.
   - *Formal dispute notice:* Issue notice under Section 210 to trigger bipartite talks and conciliation.
   - *Director of Labour petition:* Complain under Section 187 against punitive transfers of union leaders.
3. **Organizational and Compliance Routes:** Explain 4 practical union steps:
   - *Gather solid proof:* Collect transfer letters, audio logs, and witness statements.
   - *Use the Participation Committee:* Raise anti-union actions internally under Section 205.
   - *Involve global buyers:* Use buyer compliance rules and national federations to pressure bosses.
   - *Keep worker discipline:* Ban violent gherao to protect legal immunities under Section 197 and Section 198.
4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Unions stay strongest when they combine Labour Court cases with international buyer audits while keeping members peaceful and disciplined.

### Shoaib Sir Target Question 1 [04 Marks] · Employer Unfair Labour Practices under Section 195

**Exam Question:**

> Define unfair labour practice. Explain the actions that constitute unfair labour practices on the part of employers under Section 195 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Opening Definition:** Quote the definition from SI Sir's notes: *"Any unfair act or omission that arises between an employer and an employee."*
2. **Statutory Scope:** State that Section 195 sets out 12 specific prohibitions binding employers, their associations, and agents.
3. **Core Answer Body:** Group the 12 employer prohibitions into 4 easy clusters:
   - *Individual Rights (`Sec 195(a)–(d)`):* Yellow-dog contracts, refusal to hire, job discrimination, and retaliatory firing.
   - *Union Structure (`Sec 195(e), (g), (k)`):* Bribing union officers, rigging CBA ballots, and punitive transfers of the 4 key leaders.
   - *Collective Dialogue (`Sec 195(f), (i), (j)`):* Forcing CBA officers to sign deals, ignoring the Participation Committee, and refusing dispute letters.
   - *Industrial Action (`Sec 195(h), (l)`):* Hiring replacement workers during legal strikes, and running illegal lock-outs.
4. **Penalties & Conclusion:** State the criminal penalty under Section 291(1). Offending employers face up to 2 years in prison, a fine up to 10,000 Taka, or both.

   - *Three Inward Acts against fellow workers:* Forcing workers to join a union (`a`), bribing officers to quit (`b`), and taking dues by force (`c`).
   - *Three Outward Acts against the plant:* Forcing the boss via utility cuts (`d`), staging illegal strikes or go-slows (`e`), and gherao or property damage (`f`).

### Shoaib Sir Target Question 3 [02 Marks] · Statutory Penalties under Section 291

**Exam Question:**

> State the statutory penalties for unfair labour practices under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Full Solution:**

Under **Section 291** of the Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, unfair labour practices are criminal offences tried before the Labour Court:

1. **Penalty on Employers [Section 291(1)]:** Any employer who breaks Section 195 faces punishment. They can get up to **2 years** in jail, up to **10,000 Taka** fine, or both.
2. **Penalty on Individual Workers [Section 291(2)]:** Any worker who breaks Section 196 faces punishment. They can get up to **1 year** in jail, up to **5,000 Taka** fine, or both.
3. **Penalty on Trade Unions and Non-Workers [Section 291(3)]:** Any trade union or outsider breaking Section 196 faces punishment. They can get up to **2 years** in jail, up to **10,000 Taka** fine, or both.

### Model Applied Case Study [04 Marks] · Illegal Strike, Cleanroom Sabotage, Gherao & Forced Agreement

**Exam Question:**

> During wage negotiations at an electronics factory, local trade union leaders demand an immediate 35% wage increase. They give no strike notice and hold no secret ballot under Section 211. Instead, they cut electricity to the cleanrooms and block factory gates. They confine the General Manager for 12 hours (gherao) until he signs a wage agreement. They also threaten non-striking technicians with physical assault if they enter the plant.
>
> Analyze the legality of these actions under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. Also determine the legal liabilities of the union and participating workers.

**Full Solution:**

#### 1. Facts of the Case

- The local trade union demanded a 35% wage increase without giving strike notice or holding a secret ballot under Section 211.
- Union members cut power to cleanrooms and blocked factory gates. They trapped the General Manager for 12 hours (gherao) to force a wage agreement.
- Striking workers threatened non-striking technicians with physical violence if they reported to work.

#### 2. Applicable Law and Statutory Sections

- **Bangladesh Labour Act 2006:**
  - `Section 196(2)(a)`: Intimidating workers regarding union activity or plant attendance.
  - `Section 196(2)(d)`: Coercing an employer to accept demands through confinement, assault, or disconnection of utilities.
  - `Section 196(2)(e)`: Starting, continuing, or instigating an illegal strike.
  - `Section 196(2)(f)`: Staging gherao, blocking transport routes, and damaging enterprise property.
  - `Section 211`: Mandatory 7 to 14 days strike notice and secret ballot requirement (three-fourths majority).
  - `Section 291(2)–(3)`: Criminal penalties for worker and union unfair labour practices.
  - `Section 23(4)(g)`: Dismissal for gross misconduct resulting from illegal strikes or riotous behavior.
- **Contract Act 1872:**
  - `Section 15 & Section 19`: Agreements obtained through coercion (wrongful confinement and threats of violence) are voidable at the option of the coerced party.

#### 3. Legal Analysis

- **Illegal Strike:** The strike is completely illegal. The union skipped both the statutory notice and the secret ballot required under Section 211. Starting an illegal strike breaks `Section 196(2)(e)`.
- **Coercion and Utility Sabotage:** Cutting electricity to cleanrooms risks severe thermal damage to sensitive semiconductor machines. Cutting power and trapping the manager directly breaks `Section 196(2)(d)`.
- **Unlawful Gherao and Blockade:** Trapping the General Manager for 12 hours and blocking factory gates is unlawful gherao. This directly breaks `Section 196(2)(f)`.
- **Worker Intimidation:** Threatening non-striking technicians with physical violence breaks `Section 196(2)(a)`.
- **Status of the Forced Agreement:** The General Manager signed under physical confinement and threats. Under Section 15 of the Contract Act 1872, coercion cancels real consent. The employer can legally cancel the agreement. It carries zero binding force under Section 196 of the Labour Act.

#### 4. Direct Conclusion

- Every action by the union leadership and participating workers is unlawful. They are severe unfair labour practices under Section 196.
- The signed wage agreement is voidable and unenforceable.
- Participating workers face up to 1 year in jail, a fine up to 5,000 Taka, or both under `Section 291(2)`. They can also be fired for gross misconduct under `Section 23(4)(g)`.
- Trade union leaders face up to 2 years in jail, a fine up to 10,000 Taka, or both under `Section 291(3)`.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Penalty Allocation:** Employers face up to 2 years imprisonment or a 10,000 Taka fine (`Sec 291(1)`). Individual workers face up to 1 year or 5,000 Taka (`Sec 291(2)`). Trade unions face up to 2 years or 10,000 Taka (`Sec 291(3)`).
> - **Protected Officers:** Section 195(k) specifically protects 4 key executive officers from transfer: President, General Secretary, Organizing Secretary, and Treasurer (`Sec 187`).
> - **Strike-Breakers:** Employers cannot hire replacement workers during lawful strikes under Section 195(h) without prior Arbitrator approval.
> - **Case Framework:** For problem scenarios, always structure your answer into 4 distinct headings: 1. Facts of the Case, 2. Applicable Law & Section, 3. Legal Analysis, 4. Direct Conclusion.
