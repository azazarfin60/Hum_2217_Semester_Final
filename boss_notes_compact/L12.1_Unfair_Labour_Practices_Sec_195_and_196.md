# L12.1: Unfair Labour Practices (Sections 195 and 196)

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A (CO1 · Shoaib Islam Sir) · High Priority
> - **Estimated Read Time:** ~3 minutes
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2023 Q.4(b) [04m], 2023 Q.4(c) [04m], Shoaib Sir Model Questions
> - **Key Statutory Focus:** Section 195 (Employer ULPs), Section 196 (Worker ULPs), Section 291 (Penalties)

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Unfair Labour Practice:** "Any unfair act or omission that arises between an employer and an employee." *(SI Sir Class Notes, Chapter 4)*

*Plain meaning:* An unfair labour practice refers to any unlawful action or omission by management or workers that violates statutory labour rights, abuses workplace authority, or disrupts industrial peace.

> **Yellow-Dog Contract [Section 195(a)]:** "An agreement where an employer forces a worker to promise not to join a trade union." *(Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 195(a))*

*Plain meaning:* This is an illegal employment condition requiring a worker to pledge not to join or support a trade union as a prerequisite for getting or keeping a job. Such agreements are strictly void under labour law.

> **Go-Slow [Section 2(lviii) & Section 196(2)(e)]:** "An organized, deliberate and purposeful slowing down of normal output, or the deliberate curtailing of production by a body of workers." *(Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 2(lviii))*

*Plain meaning:* A go-slow occurs when workers remain at their duty stations but intentionally reduce their pace and productivity to pressure management, without declaring a formal work stoppage.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### Employer Unfair Labour Practices (Section 195)

Section 195 of the Bangladesh Labour Act 2006 lists 12 prohibited acts for employers, employer associations, and their agents:

1. **Yellow-Dog Contracts [Sec 195(a)]:** Imposing any condition in an employment contract that restricts a worker's right to join or remain in a trade union.
2. **Refusal to Employ [Sec 195(b)]:** Refusing to hire an applicant or maintain a worker in employment because of union membership or union activities.
3. **Service Discrimination [Sec 195(c)]:** Discriminating against any worker regarding wages, promotion, or working conditions on grounds of union affiliation.
4. **Retaliatory Dismissal [Sec 195(d)]:** Dismissing, discharging, removing, or threatening a worker for participating in lawful union formation or activities.
5. **Bribery and Inducement [Sec 195(e)]:** Offering financial inducements, promotions, or special advantages to persuade a worker to resign from union office.
6. **Coercing the CBA [Sec 195(f)]:** Compelling any officer of the Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA) to sign an agreement through threats, assault, or disconnection of utilities.
7. **Rigging CBA Ballots [Sec 195(g)]:** Interfering with, influencing, or tampering with secret ballot elections conducted under Section 202 to determine the CBA.
8. **Hiring Replacement Workers [Sec 195(h)]:** Recruiting new workers during a lawful strike under Section 211, unless the Arbitrator permits temporary staffing to prevent machinery damage.
9. **Ignoring the Participation Committee [Sec 195(i)]:** Deliberately failing or refusing to implement agreed recommendations made by the bipartite Participation Committee.
10. **Refusing Communication [Sec 195(j)]:** Failing or refusing to reply to formal letters or statutory notices sent by the CBA regarding an industrial dispute.
11. **Punitive Transfer [Sec 195(k)]:** Transferring the President, General Secretary, Organizing Secretary, or Treasurer of an executive committee without their consent, violating Section 187.
12. **Illegal Lock-Out [Sec 195(l)]:** Starting, continuing, or instigating an illegal lock-out against the workforce.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 4 Logical Section 195 Clusters
> - **1. Individual Rights (a to d):** Yellow-dog contracts, refusal to employ, service discrimination, and retaliatory dismissal.
> - **2. Union Structure (e, g, k):** Bribery to leave office, rigging CBA ballots, and punitive transfers of the 4 key officers.
> - **3. Collective Dialogue (f, i, j):** Coercing CBA officers, ignoring the Participation Committee, and refusing dispute letters.
> - **4. Industrial Action (h, l):** Recruiting replacement workers during lawful strikes and declaring an illegal lock-out.

### Worker and Union Unfair Labour Practices (Section 196)

Section 196 protects enterprise management, factory machinery, and non-union workers from duress, coercion, and unlawful disruption:

1. **Working Hours Prohibition [Section 196(1)]:**
   - No worker or trade union official may conduct union activities during working hours without employer permission.
   - *Statutory Exception:* The President or General Secretary of the CBA may attend official bipartite negotiation sessions, conciliation proceedings, or Labour Court hearings after serving advance notice to the employer.

2. **The Six Prohibited Worker and Union Acts [Section 196(2)]:**
   - **Membership Coercion [Sec 196(2)(a)]:** Intimidating or threatening any worker to compel them to join, remain a member of, or resign from a trade union.
   - **Bribery to Quit Office [Sec 196(2)(b)]:** Offering money, gifts, or personal advantages to induce any person to resign from union office or refrain from becoming an officer.
   - **Compelling Dues by Force [Sec 196(2)(c)]:** Forcing a worker to pay or withhold union subscriptions through threats, physical intimidation, wrongful confinement, or disconnection of utilities.
   - **Coercing the Employer [Sec 196(2)(d)]:** Compelling an employer to sign a memorandum of settlement or accept demands through assault, wrongful confinement, physical intimidation, or cutting off electricity, gas, or water supplies.
   - **Illegal Strike and Go-Slow [Sec 196(2)(e)]:** Starting, continuing, or instigating an illegal strike (without notice or secret ballot under Section 211) or staging a deliberate go-slow.
   - **Gherao, Blockades, and Property Damage [Sec 196(2)(f)]:** Resorting to gherao (wrongfully confining managerial staff), blocking transit routes, disrupting communications, or damaging factory machinery and property.

3. **Interference in CBA Elections [Section 196(3)]:**
   - No trade union, worker, or agent may influence secret ballot CBA elections through intimidation, impersonation, undue influence, or bribery.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: Section 196(2) Split (3 Inward + 3 Outward)
> - **3 Inward Acts (Directed Against Fellow Workers):** Intimidating workers on union membership (`a`), bribing members to quit office (`b`), and compelling dues through duress (`c`).
> - **3 Outward Acts (Directed Against Enterprise):** Coercing the employer via utility cuts (`d`), starting illegal strikes or go-slows (`e`), and staging gherao or property damage (`f`).

### Statutory Comparison and Criminal Penalties (Section 195 vs. Section 196 & Section 291)

| Dimension | Employer ULPs (`Sec 195`) | Worker & Union ULPs (`Sec 196`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Statute** | Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 195 | Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 196 |
| **Targeted Parties** | Employers, employer associations, and their agents | Individual workers, trade unions, and their agents |
| **Protected Entities** | Individual workers, union officers, and CBA | Factory owners, management staff, and non-union workers |
| **Scope of Prohibitions** | 12 specific statutory prohibitions (`Sec 195(a)–(l)`) | Work-hour ban (`(1)`), 6 prohibited acts (`(2)`), and election rule (`(3)`) |
| **Key Forms of Coercion** | Yellow-dog contracts, illegal lock-outs, strike-breakers | Physical gherao, utility cuts, go-slows, and wildcat strikes |
| **Officer Protection** | Bans transfer of President, General Secretary, Org. Secretary, and Treasurer | Prohibits ballot tampering, officer bribes, and membership coercion |
| **Statutory Penalties (`Sec 291`)** | Imprisonment up to **2 years**, fine up to **10,000 Taka**, or both | **Worker:** Imprisonment up to **1 year**, fine up to **5,000 Taka**, or both<br>**Trade Union:** Imprisonment up to **2 years**, fine up to **10,000 Taka**, or both |

Under **Section 291** of the Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, unfair labour practices are classified as criminal offences tried before the Labour Court:

- **Employers [Sec 291(1)]:** Any employer who violates Section 195 faces imprisonment for up to **2 years**, a fine of up to **10,000 Taka**, or both.
- **Individual Workers [Sec 291(2)]:** Any individual worker who violates Section 196 faces imprisonment for up to **1 year**, a fine of up to **5,000 Taka**, or both.
- **Trade Unions and Non-Workers [Sec 291(3)]:** Any trade union or non-worker who violates Section 196 faces imprisonment for up to **2 years**, a fine of up to **10,000 Taka**, or both.

### Consequences of ULPs on Employers and Employees (2023 Q.4(b))

**Consequences on Employers:**

1. **Criminal Prosecution and Penalties:** Company directors and factory managers face criminal prosecution before the Labour Court, resulting in fines and prison terms of up to 2 years under Section 291(1).
2. **Severe Operational and Financial Losses:** Hostile labour relations trigger illegal strikes and production line shutdowns, causing missed delivery dates and heavy breach-of-contract penalties.
3. **Cancellation of International Export Orders:** Global apparel and electronics buyers enforce strict compliance codes; documented unfair labour practices result in factory decertification and canceled purchase orders.
4. **High Turnover of Skilled Technical Personnel:** An oppressive, anti-union work culture drives away experienced engineers and technicians, escalating recruitment and onboarding costs.

**Consequences on Employees:**

1. **Loss of Livelihood and Income:** Retaliatory dismissals, demotions, and unjustified wage cuts deprive workers and their dependent families of basic financial security.
2. **Suppression of Collective Voice:** Employer interference with the Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA) destroys worker bargaining strength, preventing legitimate negotiations for fair wages and workplace safety.
3. **Workplace Fear and Psychological Distress:** Hostile surveillance, coercive interrogations, and retaliatory transfers create a climate of chronic fear and mental anxiety.
4. **Disciplinary Dismissal and Criminal Records:** Workers engaging in Section 196 violations face summary dismissal for misconduct under Section 23(4)(g) and criminal sentences of up to 1 year under Section 291(2).

---

### Strategies for Trade Unions to Address ULPs (2023 Q.4(c))

**Statutory and Legal Strategies:**

1. **Filing Complaints Before the Labour Court [Sec 291 & Sec 214]:** The CBA can file formal criminal complaints against offending managers under Section 291(1). Concurrently, unlawfully dismissed workers can petition under Section 33 for immediate reinstatement with full back pay.
2. **Invoking Statutory Dispute Settlement [Sec 210]:** The CBA serves a formal written dispute notice under Section 210(1), compelling management to hold bipartite talks within 15 days or face mandatory government conciliation.
3. **Administrative Petitions to the Director of Labour [Sec 187]:** When employers order punitive transfers of executive officers, the union can lodge an administrative petition with the Director of Labour for swift regulatory intervention.

**Internal and Organizational Strategies:**

4. **Maintaining a Systematic Evidence Dossier:** Unions must preserve contemporaneous records, including copies of transfer letters, audio recordings, meeting minutes, and signed witness statements to substantiate charges in court.
5. **Resolving Grievances via the Participation Committee [Sec 205]:** Worker representatives on the bipartite Participation Committee can table anti-union grievances internally before disputes escalate into open conflict.
6. **Utilizing Buyer Compliance and National Federations [Sec 200]:** Unions can report statutory breaches to international buyer compliance teams and national federations, using brand pressure to compel employer compliance.
7. **Training Members on Lawful Conduct:** The union must educate rank-and-file members to reject gherao, utility sabotage, and wildcat strikes, ensuring workers retain their statutory immunities under Section 197 and Section 198.

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### 2023 Final Q.4(b) [04 Marks] · Consequences of Unfair Labour Practices on Employers and Employees

**Exam Question:**

> Evaluate the potential consequences of unfair labor practices on both employers and employees.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Opening Statutory Framework:** Cite the governing provisions: Section 195 (Employer ULPs), Section 196 (Worker ULPs), and Section 291 (Statutory Penalties).
2. **Consequences on Employers:** Detail the 4 major enterprise impacts from §2: (a) Criminal liability and imprisonment under Section 291(1), (b) Severe financial losses from wildcat strikes, (c) Cancellation of international buyer contracts and loss of export compliance certifications, and (d) High turnover of skilled technical personnel.
3. **Consequences on Employees:** Detail the 4 major worker impacts from §2: (a) Loss of livelihood and income through retaliatory dismissal, (b) Suppression of collective bargaining strength, (c) Workplace fear, stress, and anxiety, and (d) Disciplinary dismissal under Section 23(4)(g) and criminal penalties under Section 291(2) for worker misconduct.
4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Unfair labour practices cause mutual devastation, destroying business viability and worker welfare alike; strict adherence to statutory dispute channels under Section 210 is vital for sustainable industrial peace.

### 2023 Final Q.4(c) [04 Marks] · Strategies for Unions to Address Unfair Labour Practices

**Exam Question:**

> Propose strategies that unions can employ to address unfair labor practices effectively.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. **Opening Statutory Context:** Note that when employers engage in Section 195 practices, trade unions must counter employer abuses through legitimate legal and organizational mechanisms rather than unlawful self-help.
2. **Statutory and Legal Routes:** Detail the 3 formal legal avenues from §2: (a) Filing criminal complaints before the Labour Court under Section 291(1) and Section 214 while seeking reinstatement under Section 33, (b) Issuing a formal dispute notice under Section 210 to initiate bipartite talks and conciliation, and (c) Lodging administrative petitions with the Director of Labour under Section 187 against punitive officer transfers.
3. **Organizational and Compliance Routes:** Detail the 4 internal strategies from §2: (a) Maintaining a contemporaneous evidence dossier (logs, transfer orders, recordings), (b) Using the bipartite Participation Committee under Section 205, (c) Involving national federations and global brand compliance auditors, and (d) Enforcing member discipline to preserve statutory immunities under Section 197 and Section 198.
4. **Concluding Takeaway:** By combining Labour Court prosecution with international buyer audit pressure while maintaining internal discipline, unions can effectively neutralize employer abuses without forfeiting legal protections.

### Shoaib Sir Target Question 3 [02 Marks] · Statutory Penalties under Section 291

**Exam Question:**

> State the statutory penalties for unfair labour practices under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Full Solution:**

Under **Section 291** of the Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, unfair labour practices are criminal offences subject to prosecution before the Labour Court:

1. **Penalty on Employers [Section 291(1)]:** Any employer who violates Section 195 faces imprisonment for up to **2 years**, a fine of up to **10,000 Taka**, or both.
2. **Penalty on Individual Workers [Section 291(2)]:** Any worker who violates Section 196 faces imprisonment for up to **1 year**, a fine of up to **5,000 Taka**, or both.
3. **Penalty on Trade Unions and Non-Workers [Section 291(3)]:** Any trade union, federation, or non-worker official who violates Section 196 faces imprisonment for up to **2 years**, a fine of up to **10,000 Taka**, or both.

### Model Applied Case Study [04 Marks] · Illegal Strike, Cleanroom Sabotage, Gherao & Forced Agreement

**Exam Question:**

> During wage negotiations at an electronics factory, local trade union leaders demand an immediate 35% wage increase. They give no strike notice and hold no secret ballot under Section 211. Instead, they cut electricity to the cleanrooms and block factory gates. They confine the General Manager for 12 hours (gherao) until he signs a wage agreement. They also threaten non-striking technicians with physical assault if they enter the plant.
>
> Analyze the legality of these actions under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. Also determine the legal liabilities of the union and participating workers.

**Full Solution:**

#### 1. Facts of the Case

- The local trade union demanded an immediate 35% wage increase without serving statutory strike notice or conducting a secret ballot under Section 211.
- Union members cut power to factory cleanrooms, blocked main access gates, and subjected the General Manager to a 12-hour gherao (wrongful confinement) to force his signature on an agreement.
- Striking workers threatened non-striking technicians with physical assault if they reported to work.

#### 2. Applicable Law and Statutory Sections

- **Bangladesh Labour Act 2006:**
  - `Section 196(2)(a)`: Intimidating workers regarding union activity or plant attendance.
  - `Section 196(2)(d)`: Coercing an employer to accept demands through confinement, assault, or disconnection of utilities.
  - `Section 196(2)(e)`: Commencing, continuing, or instigating an illegal strike.
  - `Section 196(2)(f)`: Staging gherao, blocking transport routes, and damaging enterprise property.
  - `Section 211`: Mandatory 7 to 14 days strike notice and secret ballot requirement (three-fourths majority).
  - `Section 291(2)–(3)`: Criminal penalties for worker and union unfair labour practices.
  - `Section 23(4)(g)`: Dismissal for gross misconduct resulting from illegal strikes or riotous behavior.
- **Contract Act 1872:**
  - `Section 15 & Section 19`: Agreements obtained through coercion (wrongful confinement and threats of violence) are voidable at the option of the coerced party.

#### 3. Legal Analysis

- **Illegal Strike:** The work stoppage is strictly illegal because the union bypassed mandatory statutory notice and the secret ballot process required under Section 211. Commencing an illegal strike violates `Section 196(2)(e)`.
- **Coercion and Utility Sabotage:** Cutting electricity to cleanrooms (which risks catastrophic thermal damage to sensitive semiconductor equipment) and confining the manager directly violates `Section 196(2)(d)`.
- **Unlawful Gherao and Blockade:** Staging a 12-hour physical confinement of the General Manager and obstructing plant access gates constitutes unlawful gherao, directly violating `Section 196(2)(f)`.
- **Worker Intimidation:** Threatening non-striking technicians with physical assault violates `Section 196(2)(a)`.
- **Status of the Forced Agreement:** Because the General Manager signed under criminal confinement and duress, consent was vitiated by coercion under Section 15 of the Contract Act 1872. The agreement is voidable and carries zero binding legal effect under Section 196 of the Labour Act.

#### 4. Direct Conclusion

- All actions undertaken by the union leadership and participating workers are unlawful and constitute severe unfair labour practices under Section 196.
- The signed wage agreement is null, voidable, and unenforceable.
- Individual participating workers face criminal imprisonment for up to 1 year, a fine of up to 5,000 Taka, or both under `Section 291(2)`, along with disciplinary dismissal for gross misconduct under `Section 23(4)(g)`.
- Trade union leaders face criminal imprisonment for up to 2 years, a fine of up to 10,000 Taka, or both under `Section 291(3)`.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Penalty Allocation:** Employers face up to 2 years imprisonment or a 10,000 Taka fine (`Sec 291(1)`). Individual workers face up to 1 year or 5,000 Taka (`Sec 291(2)`). Trade unions face up to 2 years or 10,000 Taka (`Sec 291(3)`).
> - **Protected Officers:** Section 195(k) specifically protects 4 key executive officers from transfer: President, General Secretary, Organizing Secretary, and Treasurer (`Sec 187`).
> - **Strike-Breakers:** Employers cannot hire replacement workers during lawful strikes under Section 195(h) without prior Arbitrator approval.
> - **Case Framework:** For problem scenarios, always structure your answer into 4 distinct headings: 1. Facts of the Case, 2. Applicable Law & Section, 3. Legal Analysis, 4. Direct Conclusion.
