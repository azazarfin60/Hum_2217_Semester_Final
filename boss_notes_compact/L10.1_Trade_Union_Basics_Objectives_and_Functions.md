# L10.1: Trade Union Basics, Objectives, and Functions

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Curriculum & Weight:** Section A (CO1 · Legal Issues · Shoaib Islam Sir · High Priority)
> - **Estimated Study Time:** ~3 minutes
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2024 Final Question 3(a) (02 Marks · Key Objectives of a Trade Union) and Prescribed Model Questions on Section 176 Rights and Core Functions
> - **Scoring Rule:** List 5 to 6 distinct objectives covering living standards, job security, bargaining, and mutual welfare, and cite the five Section 176 statutory rights (`W-E-F-A-G`).

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Trade Union [BLC Section 2(61) / Section 2(15)]:** "Trade union means an association of workers or employers formed under Chapter XIII, and includes a federation of trade unions." *(The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, Section 2(61) / Section 2(15))*

*Plain meaning:* A trade union is an organized body of workers or employers registered under labour law to protect their occupational interests and collectively regulate workplace relations.

> **Trade Union [Bombay Industrial Relations Act, 1956]:** "Trade Union means any combination, whether temporary or permanent, formed primarily to regulate the relations between workmen and employers, or between workmen and workmen, or between employers and employers, or for imposing restrictive conditions on the conduct of any trade or business, and includes any Federation of two or more Trade Unions." *(SI Sir Notes & Slides, Chapter 4)*

*Plain meaning:* This comprehensive definition recognizes that a trade union may be temporary or permanent, regulating bilateral and mutual relations among workers and employers while also setting standardized business conditions.

> **Industrial Dispute [BLC Section 2(62)]:** "Industrial dispute means any dispute or difference between employers and employers, or between employers and workers, or between workers and workers, which is connected with the employment or non-employment or the terms of employment or with the conditions of work of any person." *(The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, Section 2(62))*

*Plain meaning:* An industrial dispute is any formal collective disagreement regarding hiring, firing, wages, work hours, or occupational safety conditions between management and labour.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### Right to Form Trade Unions & Exclusions (Sections 175–176)

Section 176 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 guarantees five fundamental statutory rights (`W-E-F-A-G`):

1. **Workers' Right to Form Unions [Section 176(a)]:** All workers have the fundamental right to establish and join trade unions of their own choosing without requiring prior authorization from employers or administrative authorities.
2. **Employers' Right to Form Associations [Section 176(b)]:** Employers possess an equal statutory right to establish trade associations to coordinate industry-wide policies and collective bargaining strategies.
3. **Right of Federation and Affiliation [Section 176(c)]:** Registered trade unions have the legal authority to combine into sector-wide federations and affiliate with national or international labour organizations, such as the International Labour Organization (ILO).
4. **Complete Internal Autonomy [Section 176(d)]:** Trade unions enjoy complete democratic independence to draft their own constitutions, freely elect executive officers, organize internal administration, and formulate legitimate action programs.
5. **Mandatory Gender Representation [Section 176(e)]:** Under the 2013 Labour Amendment, if female workers constitute at least 20% of the total workforce in an establishment, the trade union executive committee must include at least 10% female members.

*Statutory Exclusions under Section 175:* The law strictly prohibits security guards, watch and ward personnel, fire-fighting staff, and confidential assistants to top management from joining or forming worker trade unions.

---

### Key Objectives of a Trade Union (Answers 2024 Q.3(a))

1. **Securing Living Wages:** Securing living wages that keep pace with inflation, along with festival bonuses and statutory Worker Participation and Welfare Funds (WPPF).
2. **Guaranteeing Job Security:** Protecting workers against arbitrary dismissals, unlawful retrenchments, unfair disciplinary actions, and victimisation.
3. **Engaging in Collective Bargaining:** Negotiating standardized working hours, overtime compensation, shift schedules, and paid annual leave directly with management.
4. **Ensuring Workplace Safety and Health:** Enforcing occupational safety measures, including machinery safeguards, pure drinking water, fire prevention apparatus, and hygienic sanitary facilities.
5. **Maintaining Industrial Harmony:** Resolving shop-floor grievances and collective disputes peacefully through structured bipartite negotiation to prevent work stoppages and factory closures.
6. **Providing Mutual Welfare and Support:** Operating emergency injury relief funds, mutual benefit schemes, and educational assistance for worker families.

---

### Four Core Functional Classes (`M-F-S-P`)

1. **Militant or Protective Functions (Intramural):** Daily factory-floor protection of worker rights, including wage bargaining, fighting unfair dismissals under Chapter II, regulating working shifts, inspecting workplace safety safeguards, and conducting lawful strikes when negotiations collapse.
2. **Fraternal or Mutual-Aid Functions (Extramural):** Welfare and solidarity activities conducted outside direct work operations, including emergency accident and death relief funds, technical skills training, worker literacy classes, subsidized cooperative stores, and affordable housing initiatives.
3. **Social Functions:** Serving as a vital communication channel to bridge the psychological gap between shop-floor workers and top corporate executives, alongside organizing community healthcare drives and blood donation campaigns in worker colonies.
4. **Political and Civic Functions:** Engaging in legislative advocacy to lobby parliament for stronger labour protections, and nominating worker delegates to the Minimum Wages Board to establish statutory wage floors backed by realistic cost-of-living data.

---

### Comparison: Objectives vs. Functions of a Trade Union

| Feature | Objectives of a Trade Union | Functions of a Trade Union |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fundamental Meaning** | The ultimate goals and long-term targets the union aims to achieve | The actual operational tasks and daily activities performed by the union |
| **Core Question** | *"What do we want to achieve?"* | *"What practical actions do we take every day?"* |
| **Time Horizon & Scope** | Long-term, visionary, and outcome-oriented | Continuous, operational, and process-oriented |
| **Primary Scope** | Securing living wages, job security, and workplace dignity | Collective bargaining, filing court appeals, and operating credit co-ops |

### Memory Hooks

> [!tip] Memory Hook 1: Section 176 Rights (`W-E-F-A-G`)
> - **W** = **W**orkers have the right to form and join unions freely [`Sec 176(a)`].
> - **E** = **E**mployers have the equal right to form associations [`Sec 176(b)`].
> - **F** = **F**ederations can be established and affiliated internationally [`Sec 176(c)`].
> - **A** = **A**utonomy to draft constitutions and elect officers freely [`Sec 176(d)`].
> - **G** = **G**ender quota of 10% female executive seats where women make up $\ge 20\%$ of the workforce [`Sec 176(e)`].

> [!tip] Memory Hook 2: Four Core Functions (`M-F-S-P`)
> - **M** = **M**ilitant or Protective (wages, job defense, safety, strikes inside the factory).
> - **F** = **F**raternal or Mutual-Aid (relief funds, skill training, credit co-ops outside work).
> - **S** = **S**ocial (bridging worker-boss communication gaps and community welfare).
> - **P** = **P**olitical and Civic (lobbying labour legislation and Minimum Wage Board representation).

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### 2024 Final Q.3(a): Key Objectives of a Trade Union (2 Marks)

> **Appeared:** 2024 Final Exam Question 3(a) · (2 Marks)

**Question:**  
List the key objectives of the trade union.

**Exam-Ready Solution:**  
A trade union is an organized body formed to protect worker rights and maintain industrial harmony. The key objectives are:

1. **Securing Living Wages:** Guaranteeing fair pay scales that adjust for inflation, alongside festival bonuses and statutory profit-sharing funds (WPPF).
2. **Defending Job Security:** Protecting workers against arbitrary dismissals, unlawful retrenchments, and unfair disciplinary penalties.
3. **Collective Bargaining:** Negotiating standardized working hours, rest intervals, shift schedules, and paid annual leaves directly with management.
4. **Workplace Health and Safety:** Compelling employers to maintain machine guards, fire prevention apparatus, pure drinking water, and hygienic sanitary facilities.
5. **Promoting Industrial Harmony:** Resolving shop-floor grievances and disputes peacefully through structured bipartite negotiation to avoid strikes and factory shutdowns.
6. **Mutual Welfare Support:** Providing mutual aid funds, emergency accident relief, and educational assistance for worker families.

*(Tip: Providing 5 to 6 distinct, structured bullet points across economic, protective, and welfare dimensions ensures the full 2 marks.)*

### Model Question 1: Trade Union Definition and Rights under Section 176 (2 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question · Shoaib Sir Voice Note Blueprint · (2 Marks)

**Question:**  
Define Trade Union under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. State the statutory rights of workers and employers to form and join trade unions under Section 176.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. Open by quoting the statutory definition of Trade Union under Section 2(61) / Section 2(15) from §1, noting that it covers associations of workers, associations of employers, and federations.
2. Outline the five statutory rights under Section 176 (`W-E-F-A-G`) from §2 Block 1: workers' right to form unions [`176(a)`], employers' right to form associations [`176(b)`], federation and international affiliation [`176(c)`], internal constitutional autonomy [`176(d)`], and the 10% female executive quota where women form $\ge 20\%$ of the workforce [`176(e)`].
3. Conclude by briefly noting the Section 175 exclusions (security guards, fire-fighters, and confidential assistants are barred from joining worker unions).

---

### Model Question 2: Four Core Categories of Trade Union Functions (4 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question · Shoaib Sir Voice Note Blueprint · (4 Marks)

**Question:**  
Explain the core functions of a trade union in modern industry. Group these functions into their recognized categories.

**Exam Writing Plan:**

1. Distinguish functions (day-to-day operational activities) from objectives (ultimate goals) using the brief contrast table from §2.
2. Detail the four recognized functional categories (`M-F-S-P`) from §2 Block 3:
   - *Militant/Protective (Intramural):* Wage fixing, job security defense under Chapter II, working hour regulation, and safety enforcement.
   - *Fraternal/Mutual-Aid (Extramural):* Emergency accident relief, technical skills training, credit cooperatives, and subsidized canteens.
   - *Social:* Bridging the psychological gap between shop-floor staff and top executives, plus community healthcare campaigns.
   - *Political/Civic:* Lobbying parliament for labour law reforms and nominating representatives to the Minimum Wages Board.
3. Conclude with a 1-sentence summary emphasizing that modern trade unions must balance defensive workplace representation with extramural social welfare to remain effective.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Confusing Objectives with Functions:** Objectives represent ultimate goals (such as living wages and job security), whereas functions represent ongoing operational activities (such as collective bargaining, legal defense, and operating credit cooperatives). Clearly distinguish the two when prompted.
> - **Misquoting the Section 176(e) Gender Quota:** The executive committee requires a minimum of 10% female representation only when female workers make up at least 20% of the establishment's total workforce. Always state both percentage thresholds.
> - **Overlooking Section 175 Excluded Personnel:** Security guards, watch and ward personnel, fire-fighting staff, and confidential assistants to management are explicitly barred by law from joining worker trade unions. Mentioning these statutory exclusions earns full credit.
