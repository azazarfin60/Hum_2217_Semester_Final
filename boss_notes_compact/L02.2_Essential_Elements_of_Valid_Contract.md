# L02.2: Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A · Shoaib Islam Sir · 🔴 Critical
> - **Estimated Read Time:** ~4 minutes
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2023 Final Q.3(b) (04m), 2025 Law-CT1 Q.1 (10m)
> - **Core Scoring Rule:** Quote Section 10 word for word. List all 10 essential elements with statutory sections. Cite *Balfour v. Balfour* and *Mohori Bibee*.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Contract [Section 2(h)]:** "An agreement enforceable by law is a contract." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* A contract is a deal backed by law. If someone breaks their promise, you can take them to court.

---

> **Enforceable Contracts [Section 10]:** "All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* An ordinary deal only becomes a real contract if it passes basic tests. Both people must agree freely, have the legal right to sign, exchange real value, and follow the law.

---

> **Competent Parties [Section 11]:** "Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, and is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* You can only make a contract if you are an adult aged 18 or older and mentally sound. Also, no law can ban you from making deals.

---

> **Free Consent [Section 14]:** "Consent is said to be free when it is not caused by (1) coercion... (2) undue influence... (3) fraud... (4) misrepresentation... or (5) mistake, subject to the provisions of sections 20, 21 and 22." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* Your agreement must be 100% voluntary. Nobody can force you with threats, emotional pressure, deliberate lies, careless false statements, or major mix-ups.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### Foundational Formula & Core Maxim

$$
\text{Valid Contract} = \text{Agreement [Sec 2(e)]} + \text{Legal Enforceability [Sec 10]}
$$

- **Core Legal Maxim:** *"All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts."*
  - **Why all contracts are agreements:** Every contract starts as an agreement under Section 2(e). Two sides exchange promises. Without an agreement, there is nothing to build on.
  - **Why all agreements are not contracts:** An agreement only turns into a contract if it satisfies Section 10. Social invites, family promises, and illegal deals lack legal backing. So they stay mere agreements.

### The 10 Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

1. **Lawful Offer and Acceptance [Sections 2(a), 2(b), and 7]:**
   - **Rule:** One party makes a clear offer. The other party accepts it fully without any new conditions. Both sides must agree on the exact same thing in the same way (*consensus ad idem*).
   - **Example:** An engineering firm offers to design a custom PCB for Tk 20,000. The client accepts the exact terms right away.

2. **Intention to Create Legal Relations:**
   - **Rule:** Both parties must intend to create a binding legal deal that a court can enforce.
   - **Presumption:** Business deals automatically carry legal intent. But social and family promises do not (*Balfour v. Balfour*). A husband promising money to his wife is not a legal deal.

3. **Lawful Consideration [Sections 2(d) and 25]:**
   - **Rule:** Consideration is the price paid for a promise (*quid pro quo*, meaning "something for something"). Each party must exchange something of real legal value.
   - **Statutory Effect:** Under Section 25, an agreement made without consideration is void (invalid from day one).
   - **Example:** You pay Tk 50,000, and the developer writes custom telemetry software for you.

4. **Capacity of Parties [Sections 11 and 12]:**
   - **Rule:** Parties must have the legal right to sign a contract. Under Section 11, a person must be an adult aged 18 or older. They must also be of sound mind and not disqualified by any law.
   - **Statutory Effect:** A contract with a minor is completely invalid from day one (*void ab initio*). This rule comes from the landmark case *Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*.

5. **Free Consent [Sections 13 to 22]:**
   - **Rule:** Parties must agree on the same thing in the same sense (*consensus ad idem* under Section 13).
   - **The 5 Vices (C-U-F-M-M):** Consent is not free if caused by any of five legal vices:
     - Coercion (`Sec 15`)
     - Undue Influence (`Sec 16`)
     - Fraud (`Sec 17`)
     - Misrepresentation (`Sec 18`)
     - Mistake (`Sec 20–22`)
   - **Statutory Effect:** Flawed consent caused by coercion, fraud, or misrepresentation makes the contract voidable. Under Section 19, the injured party (victim) has the sole right to cancel it.

6. **Lawful Object [Section 23]:**
   - **Rule:** The purpose of the agreement must be legal. It cannot break the law, harm people, damage property, or go against public policy.
   - **Example:** A contract to hack a competitor's telecom server or smuggle unapproved chips is void.

7. **Certainty of Terms [Section 29]:**
   - **Rule:** The terms of the deal must be clear, definite, and easy to understand.
   - **Statutory Effect:** Under Section 29, agreements with vague or confusing terms are void.
   - **Example:** Promising to supply "some microcontrollers at a good price" is void for uncertainty.

8. **Possibility of Performance [Section 56(1)]:**
   - **Rule:** The promised task must be physically and legally possible to carry out.
   - **Statutory Effect:** Under Section 56(1), doing an impossible act makes an agreement void from day one (*void ab initio*).
   - **Example:** A promise to discover hidden treasure through magic spells is void *ab initio*.

9. **Agreements Not Expressly Declared Void [Sections 26 to 30]:**
   - **Rule:** The agreement must not fall into any category banned by the Contract Act.
   - **Statutory Classes (M-T-L-U-W):**
     - Restraint of marriage (`Sec 26`)
     - Restraint of trade (`Sec 27`)
     - Restraint of legal proceedings (`Sec 28`)
     - Uncertain agreements (`Sec 29`)
     - Wagering agreements (`Sec 30`)

10. **Legal Formalities (Writing, Registration, and Stamps):**
    - **Rule:** Oral contracts are generally valid. But specific laws require formal written, stamped, or registered documents.
    - **Example:** Contracts transferring land or buildings must be in writing. They must also be registered under Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: The 10 Elements & Statutory Vices
> - **10-Point Story Sentence:** *"An **Offer** with **Intent**, backed by **Cash** and **Capacity**, with **Free Consent** and **Lawful Aim**, makes a **Certain** and **Possible** deal, **Not Void**, in **Formal** writing."*
> - **Free Consent Vices (C-U-F-M-M):**
>   - **C** · Coercion (`Sec 15`)
>   - **U** · Undue Influence (`Sec 16`)
>   - **F** · Fraud (`Sec 17`)
>   - **M** · Misrepresentation (`Sec 18`)
>   - **M** · Mistake (`Sec 20–22`)
> - **5 Expressly Void Agreements (M-T-L-U-W):**
>   - `Sec 26` · **M**arriage restraint
>   - `Sec 27` · **T**rade restraint
>   - `Sec 28` · **L**egal proceedings restraint
>   - `Sec 29` · **U**ncertainty
>   - `Sec 30` · **W**agering agreements

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### 2023 Final Q.3(b) · Conditions for Legal Enforceability of a Contract (04 Marks)

*(Note: The examination paper misprinted "contact" for "contract".)*

**Question:** Summarizes the conditions that must be satisfied for a contact to be legally enforceable. [04m]

**Exam Writing Plan (4 Marks):**

1. **Opening Statutory Definition:**
   - Quote Section 10 of the Contract Act, 1872 word for word.
   - Point out the four key pillars: free consent, competent parties, lawful consideration, and lawful object.

2. **Foundational Formula:**
   - Write down the core formula: $\text{Valid Contract} = \text{Agreement [Sec 2(e)]} + \text{Legal Enforceability [Sec 10]}$.
   - State the core legal maxim: *"All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts."*

3. **Core Checklist of Essential Elements:**
   - Lawful offer and unconditional acceptance with *consensus ad idem* [Sections 2(a), 2(b), 7].
   - Clear intention to create legal relations (*Balfour v. Balfour*).
   - Lawful consideration (*quid pro quo*, something for something) [Sections 2(d), 25].
   - Capacity of parties who are adults and mentally sound [Sections 11, 12].
   - Free consent without coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, or mistake [Sections 13–22].
   - Lawful object and certainty of terms [Sections 23, 29].
   - Agreement not expressly banned by law [Sections 26–30].

4. **Concluding Takeaway:**
   - State that missing any element makes the agreement void or voidable.
   - Courts will not enforce any agreement that fails these tests.

### 2025 Law-CT1 Q.1 · How Essential Elements Ensure Enforceability (10 Marks)

**Question:** Explain how the essential elements of a valid contract ensure the enforceability of agreements under the Contract Act. [10m]

**Exam-Ready Full Solution:**

#### 1. Introduction and Statutory Foundation

Under the **Contract Act, 1872**, every contract starts as an agreement.
Section 2(e) defines an agreement as every promise forming consideration for each other.
Section 2(h) defines a contract as an agreement enforceable by law.

This statutory link creates a foundational legal rule:

> *"All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts."*

An agreement becomes legally enforceable only when it meets all tests in **Section 10**:

$$
\text{Valid Contract} = \text{Agreement [Sec 2(e)]} + \text{Legal Enforceability [Sec 10 Conditions]}
$$

#### 2. How the Essential Elements Filter and Ensure Enforceability

The essential elements act as legal filters.
They ensure that courts enforce only genuine, fair, and lawful bargains:

1. **Lawful Offer and Acceptance [Sections 2(a), 2(b), and 7]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Enforceability requires mutual agreement (*consensus ad idem*). Under Section 7(1), acceptance must be absolute and unqualified.
   - **Legal Protection:** This rule stops courts from enforcing fuzzy talks. It also prevents enforcing one-sided assumptions where the parties never truly agreed.

2. **Intention to Create Legal Relations:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Courts only enforce deals meant to create binding legal duties.
   - **Legal Protection:** Commercial deals automatically carry legal intent. Domestic and social promises do not (*Balfour v. Balfour*). This filter keeps informal family promises out of court.

3. **Lawful Consideration [Sections 2(d) and 25]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Consideration means exchanging real value (*quid pro quo*). Under Section 25, an agreement made without consideration is void.
   - **Legal Protection:** Courts refuse to enforce one-sided free gifts unless they meet rare statutory exceptions. The consideration must also be lawful under Section 23.

4. **Capacity of Parties [Sections 11 and 12]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Contracting parties must be at least 18 years old, mentally sound, and qualified by law.
   - **Legal Protection:** Deals with minors are void from day one (*void ab initio*). The landmark case *Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose* shields vulnerable youths from exploitation.

5. **Free Consent [Sections 13 to 22]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Consent must be genuine and voluntary under Section 13.
   - **Legal Protection:** Flawed consent from coercion, undue influence, fraud, or misrepresentation makes the contract voidable. Under Section 19, the victim has the sole right to cancel it. A mutual mistake of fact makes the agreement void under Section 20. This guarantees fairness and stops bad-faith pressure.

6. **Lawful Object [Section 23]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** The goal of the contract must not break the law, hurt others, or harm public interest.
   - **Legal Protection:** Courts refuse to enforce illegal deals, such as computer hacking, intellectual property theft, or hardware smuggling.

7. **Certainty and Possibility of Performance [Sections 29 and 56(1)]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Terms must be clear and definite. The promised acts must also be physically and legally possible.
   - **Legal Protection:** Courts cannot enforce vague terms or impossible promises, such as discovering treasure through magic.

8. **Agreements Not Expressly Declared Void [Sections 26 to 30]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** The agreement must not fall into banned categories.
   - **Legal Protection:** The law protects open markets and personal freedom. So it bans marriage restraints (Sec 26), trade restraints (Sec 27), legal barriers (Sec 28), and gambling wagers (Sec 30).

9. **Compliance with Legal Formalities:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Specific laws require written, stamped, or registered papers for certain transactions.
   - **Legal Protection:** Under Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908, land sales must be registered. This creates solid written evidence for court.

#### 3. Conclusion

The essential elements work together like a legal sieve.
They weed out casual social promises, protect vulnerable people, and cancel illegal or forced deals.
Because of Section 10, courts only spend time enforcing honest, deliberate, and reliable agreements.

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Trap 1 (Omitting Section 10):** Forgetting to cite **Section 10** as your opening statutory authority loses definition marks immediately.
> - **Trap 2 (Vague Consent Language):** Writing only "consent must be free" loses marks. You must name the five statutory vices (**Sections 15 to 22**).
> - **Trap 3 (Void vs. Voidable Confusion):** Mixing up void and voidable costs marks. Minor agreements are **void ab initio** under Section 11. But coercion or fraud makes a contract **voidable** at the option of the victim under Section 19.
