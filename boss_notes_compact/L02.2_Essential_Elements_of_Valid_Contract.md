# L02.2: Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section A · Shoaib Islam Sir · 🔴 Critical
> - **Estimated Read Time:** ~4 minutes
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2023 Final Q.3(b) (04m), 2025 Law-CT1 Q.1 (10m)
> - **Core Scoring Rule:** Quote Section 10 verbatim, state all 10 essential elements with statutory sections, and cite *Balfour v. Balfour* and *Mohori Bibee*.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

> **Contract [Section 2(h)]:** "An agreement enforceable by law is a contract." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* A contract is a legally binding exchange of promises that a court of law will enforce if either party breaches the agreement.

---

> **Enforceable Contracts [Section 10]:** "All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* An ordinary agreement becomes an enforceable contract only when it passes every statutory test: voluntary consent, legal capacity, lawful consideration, and a lawful purpose.

---

> **Competent Parties [Section 11]:** "Every person is competent to contract who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, and is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* Contracting parties must be adults aged eighteen or older, mentally sound, and free from statutory disqualifications.

---

> **Free Consent [Section 14]:** "Consent is said to be free when it is not caused by (1) coercion... (2) undue influence... (3) fraud... (4) misrepresentation... or (5) mistake, subject to the provisions of sections 20, 21 and 22." *(Contract Act, 1872)*

*Plain meaning:* Consent must be given freely and voluntarily, without threats, emotional pressure, intentional deception, accidental misstatements, or mutual error.

---

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### Foundational Formula & Core Maxim

$$
\text{Valid Contract} = \text{Agreement [Sec 2(e)]} + \text{Legal Enforceability [Sec 10]}
$$

- **Core Legal Maxim:** *"All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts."*
  - **Why all contracts are agreements:** Every contract begins as an agreement formed by exchanged promises under Section 2(e). Without an agreement, no contract can exist.
  - **Why all agreements are not contracts:** An agreement becomes a contract only when it satisfies all conditions of Section 10. Domestic promises, social invites, and illegal bargains lack legal enforceability and remain mere agreements.

### The 10 Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

1. **Lawful Offer and Acceptance [Sections 2(a), 2(b), and 7]:**
   - **Rule:** Every contract requires a lawful proposal made by one party and an unconditional, absolute acceptance by the other (*consensus ad idem*).
   - **Example:** An engineering firm offers to design a custom PCB for Tk 20,000, and the client accepts the exact terms without qualification.

2. **Intention to Create Legal Relations:**
   - **Rule:** The contracting parties must intend to create a binding legal obligation enforceable in court.
   - **Presumption:** Business and commercial transactions carry an automatic presumption of legal intent. In contrast, domestic and social promises lack legal intention (*Balfour v. Balfour*).

3. **Lawful Consideration [Sections 2(d) and 25]:**
   - **Rule:** Consideration represents the price paid for a promise (*quid pro quo*). Each party must exchange something of lawful value.
   - **Statutory Effect:** Under Section 25, an agreement made without consideration is void.
   - **Example:** Paying Tk 50,000 in exchange for writing custom telemetry software.

4. **Capacity of Parties [Sections 11 and 12]:**
   - **Rule:** Parties must be legally competent to contract. Under Section 11, a person must be at least 18 years old, of sound mind, and not disqualified by any law.
   - **Statutory Effect:** An agreement entered into by a minor is void from the beginning (*void ab initio*), as established in *Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*.

5. **Free Consent [Sections 13 to 22]:**
   - **Rule:** Parties must agree upon the same thing in the same sense (*consensus ad idem* under Section 13).
   - **Vices:** Under Section 14, consent is not free when obtained through coercion (Sec 15), undue influence (Sec 16), fraud (Sec 17), misrepresentation (Sec 18), or mistake (Sec 20 to 22).
   - **Statutory Effect:** Flawed consent caused by coercion, fraud, or misrepresentation makes the contract voidable at the option of the aggrieved party under Section 19.

6. **Lawful Object [Section 23]:**
   - **Rule:** The purpose of the agreement must not be forbidden by law, fraudulent, injurious to person or property, or contrary to public policy.
   - **Example:** An agreement to hack a competitor's telecommunications server or smuggle unapproved hardware is void.

7. **Certainty of Terms [Section 29]:**
   - **Rule:** The terms of the agreement must be clear, definite, and unambiguous.
   - **Statutory Effect:** Under Section 29, agreements whose meaning is vague or cannot be made certain are void.
   - **Example:** Promising to supply "some microcontrollers at a good price" is void for uncertainty.

8. **Possibility of Performance [Section 56(1)]:**
   - **Rule:** The promised act must be physically and legally capable of being performed.
   - **Statutory Effect:** Under Section 56(1), an agreement to do an impossible act is void from the start.
   - **Example:** A promise to discover hidden treasure by magic is void *ab initio*.

9. **Agreements Not Expressly Declared Void [Sections 26 to 30]:**
   - **Rule:** The agreement must not fall into any category expressly banned by the Contract Act.
   - **Statutory Classes:** Restraint of marriage (Sec 26), restraint of trade (Sec 27), restraint of legal proceedings (Sec 28), uncertain agreements (Sec 29), and wagering agreements (Sec 30).

10. **Legal Formalities (Writing, Registration, and Stamps):**
    - **Rule:** While oral contracts are generally valid, specific statutes require formal written, stamped, or registered instruments.
    - **Example:** Contracts transferring immovable property must be in writing and registered under Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: The 10 Elements & Statutory Vices
> - **10-Point Story Sentence:** *"An **Offer** with **Intent**, backed by **Cash** and **Capacity**, with **Free Consent** and **Lawful Aim**, makes a **Certain** and **Possible** deal, **Not Void**, in **Formal** writing."*
> - **Free Consent Vices (C-U-F-M-M):**
>   - **C** · Coercion (`Sec 15`)
>   - **U** · Undue Influence (`Sec 16`)
>   - **F** · Fraud (`Sec 17`)
>   - **M** · Misrepresentation (`Sec 18`)
>   - **M** · Mistake (`Sec 20–22`)
> - **5 Expressly Void Agreements (M-T-L-U-W):**
>   - `Sec 26` · **M**arriage restraint
>   - `Sec 27` · **T**rade restraint
>   - `Sec 28` · **L**egal proceedings restraint
>   - `Sec 29` · **U**ncertainty
>   - `Sec 30` · **W**agering agreements

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### 2023 Final Q.3(b) · Conditions for Legal Enforceability of a Contract (04 Marks)

*(Note: The examination paper misprinted "contact" for "contract".)*

**Question:** Summarizes the conditions that must be satisfied for a contact to be legally enforceable. [04m]

**Exam Writing Plan (4 Marks):**

1. **Opening Statutory Definition:** Quote Section 10 of the Contract Act, 1872 verbatim, highlighting that all agreements are contracts if made with free consent, competent parties, lawful consideration, and a lawful object.
2. **Foundational Formula:** Write $\text{Valid Contract} = \text{Agreement [Sec 2(e)]} + \text{Legal Enforceability [Sec 10]}$, noting the core maxim that all contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts.
3. **Core Checklist:** State the essential elements from Section 2 in concise bullet points:
   - Lawful offer and unconditional acceptance with *consensus ad idem* [Sections 2(a), 2(b), 7].
   - Intention to create legal relations (*Balfour v. Balfour*).
   - Lawful consideration (*quid pro quo*) [Sections 2(d), 25].
   - Capacity of parties (age of majority, sound mind, not disqualified) [Sections 11, 12].
   - Free consent, unmarred by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, or mistake [Sections 13–22].
   - Lawful object and certainty of terms [Sections 23, 29].
   - Agreement not expressly declared void by statute [Sections 26–30].
4. **Concluding Takeaway:** Conclude with one sentence stating that an agreement missing any of these elements remains either void or voidable and cannot be enforced in a court of law.

### 2025 Law-CT1 Q.1 · How Essential Elements Ensure Enforceability (10 Marks)

**Question:** Explain how the essential elements of a valid contract ensure the enforceability of agreements under the Contract Act. [10m]

**Exam-Ready Full Solution:**

#### 1. Introduction and Statutory Foundation

Under the **Contract Act, 1872**, contract formation originates from an agreement. Section 2(e) defines an agreement as every promise and every set of promises, forming the consideration for each other. Section 2(h) defines a contract as an agreement enforceable by law.

This statutory relationship gives rise to the foundational legal maxim:

> *"All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts."*

An agreement achieves legal enforceability only when it satisfies all statutory filters specified in **Section 10**:

$$
\text{Valid Contract} = \text{Agreement [Sec 2(e)]} + \text{Legal Enforceability [Sec 10 Conditions]}
$$

#### 2. How the Essential Elements Filter and Ensure Enforceability

The essential elements act as legal filters to ensure that only genuine, lawful, and deliberate bargains are enforced by courts:

1. **Lawful Offer and Acceptance [Sections 2(a), 2(b), and 7]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Enforceability requires mutual assent (*consensus ad idem*). Under Section 7(1), acceptance must be absolute and unqualified.
   - **Legal Protection:** This rule prevents courts from enforcing ambiguous negotiations or unilateral assumptions where minds never met.

2. **Intention to Create Legal Relations:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Courts enforce only bargains intended to create binding legal obligations.
   - **Legal Protection:** Commercial dealings carry a legal presumption of intent, whereas domestic promises do not (*Balfour v. Balfour*). This filter prevents courts from being clogged with informal social promises.

3. **Lawful Consideration [Sections 2(d) and 25]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Consideration represents reciprocal value (*quid pro quo*). Under Section 25, an agreement made without consideration is void.
   - **Legal Protection:** Courts refuse to enforce one-sided, gratuitous gifts unless backed by formal exceptions. Consideration must also be lawful under Section 23.

4. **Capacity of Parties [Sections 11 and 12]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Contracting parties must be at least 18 years of age, of sound mind, and legally qualified.
   - **Legal Protection:** Agreements with minors are void from the beginning (*void ab initio*), as established in *Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*. This shields vulnerable individuals from commercial exploitation.

5. **Free Consent [Sections 13 to 22]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Consent must be genuine and voluntary under Section 13.
   - **Legal Protection:** Consent induced by coercion (Sec 15), undue influence (Sec 16), fraud (Sec 17), or misrepresentation (Sec 18) renders the contract voidable under Section 19. Bilateral mistake of fact makes it void under Section 20. This guarantees fairness and protects against bad-faith dealings.

6. **Lawful Object [Section 23]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** The object of the contract must not break the law, injure others, or oppose public policy.
   - **Legal Protection:** Courts refuse to enforce agreements aimed at illegal acts, such as computer fraud or intellectual property theft.

7. **Certainty and Possibility of Performance [Sections 29 and 56(1)]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Terms must be clear, definite, and physically and legally possible to perform.
   - **Legal Protection:** Courts cannot enforce vague terms or impossible promises, such as discovering treasure through magic.

8. **Agreements Not Expressly Declared Void [Sections 26 to 30]:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** The agreement must not fall into statutory categories prohibited to protect open markets and personal freedoms (marriage restraint under Sec 26, trade restraint under Sec 27, legal proceedings restraint under Sec 28, and wagering under Sec 30).

9. **Compliance with Legal Formalities:**
   - **Enforceability Function:** Where specific statutes require written, stamped, or registered instruments (such as Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908), parties must satisfy these forms to produce admissible evidence in court.

#### 3. Conclusion

These essential elements serve as an indispensable legal sieve. By weeding out casual domestic promises, protecting incompetent individuals, and invalidating illegal or coerced arrangements, Section 10 ensures that the legal system enforces only deliberate, just, and reliable agreements.

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Trap 1 (Omitting Section 10):** Forgetting to cite **Section 10** as your opening statutory authority loses baseline definition marks immediately.
> - **Trap 2 (Vague Consent Language):** Writing simply "consent must be free" without explicitly naming the five statutory vices (**Sections 15 to 22**) results in a loss of one to two marks.
> - **Trap 3 (Void vs. Voidable Confusion):** Conflating void agreements with voidable contracts costs critical accuracy marks. Minor agreements are **void ab initio** under Section 11, whereas coercion or fraud makes a contract **voidable** at the option of the victim under Section 19.
