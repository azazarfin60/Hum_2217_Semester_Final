# O01.1: Inventory Control and Holding Costs

> [!info] ⚡ Exam Snapshot
> - **Section & Priority:** Section B · Dr. Md. Rabiul Islam (MRI Sir) · Core Foundation | **Read Time:** ~4 mins
> - **PYQs Covered:** 2020 Q.4(a) (05m), 2021 Q.4(a) (04m), 2023 Q.7(a) (01m), 2024 Q.4(a) (02m)
> - **Scoring Rule:** State exact definitions, quote 15% to 25% capital cost, list `I-S-H` (with 3 shrinkage types), and contrast with `C-O-S-T-P`.

---

## 📌 1. Verbatim Definitions (For 2-Mark Questions)

1. **Inventory:**
   > "Inventory is created when the receipt of finished goods, materials or parts exceeds their disbursement. It is depleted when their disbursement exceeds receipt." (MRI Sir, Class 9 Lecture Notes)
   - *Plain meaning:* Stored physical stock used to support production or satisfy customer demand.
   - *Physical forms:* Raw materials (silicon wafers), Work-in-Process (assembled PCBs), Finished goods (routers), and MRO supplies (flux, lubricants).

2. **Inventory Control:**
   > "The operational mechanism used to maintain stock levels within desired limits by determining when and how much to order." (Inventory Control Lecture Notes, Page 1)
   - *Plain meaning:* Operational warehouse tracking to reorder items on time at minimum total cost.

3. **Inventory Management:**
   > "The broad managerial function that plans, organizes, directs, and monitors stock policies across the complete supply chain." (Operations Management Syllabus Module 6)
   - *Plain meaning:* Top-level strategy aligning warehouse stock with corporate finance and customer goals.

## ⚡ 2. Core Exam Blocks (Write Directly in Exam)

### A. Inventory Control vs. Inventory Management

| Parameter | Inventory Control (Tactical / Operational) | Inventory Management (Strategic / Policy) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scope & Level** | Specific warehouse items; first-line storekeepers. | Entire supply chain; executive leadership and CFO. |
| **Core Decision** | When and how much ($Q$) to order. | Working capital and stock investment policy. |
| **Tools & Goal** | EOQ, ROP, $Q$-system; minimizes order and holding costs. | Budgets, SLAs; aligns stock with corporate goals. |

### B. Pressures for LOW Inventory (Holding Costs: 15% to 35%/Year)

Carrying costs rise directly with higher stock levels, totaling **15% to 35% of stock value annually**.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: Low Inventory Pressures (`I-S-H`)
> - **I**: Interest / Opportunity Cost of capital (15% to 25%/yr).
> - **S**: Storage and Handling (rent, utilities, equipment, labor).
> - **H**: Hazard, Taxes, Insurance, and Shrinkage (theft, obsolescence, spoilage).

1. **Interest or Opportunity Cost of Capital:** Unsold stock locks up funds that could earn interest or finance R&D. Largest holding cost factor: **15% to 25% per year**.
2. **Storage and Handling Costs:** Warehousing requires rent, air conditioning, security, forklifts, and handling labor.
3. **Taxes, Insurance, and Shrinkage Costs:**
   - *Taxes & Insurance:* Municipal property taxes and fire/theft insurance premiums.
   - *Shrinkage (Three Forms):*
     - **Pilferage (Theft):** Unauthorized removal of valuable chips (e.g., microcontrollers, memory ICs).
     - **Obsolescence:** Fast-evolving parts lose value due to engineering design updates.
     - **Deterioration (Spoilage):** Physical decay during storage (e.g., lead oxidation, flux drying, battery leakage).

### C. Pressures for HIGH Inventory (Shortage and Order Costs)

Five economic factors push managers to maintain higher inventory cushions:

> [!tip] Memory Hook: High Inventory Pressures (`C-O-S-T-P`)
> - **C**: Customer service protection (avoids stockouts, backorders).
> - **O**: Ordering cost reduction (fewer purchase orders).
> - **S**: Setup cost reduction (fewer line changeovers).
> - **T**: Transportation savings (full truckload FTL rates).
> - **P**: Payment discounts (supplier bulk rebates and price hedges).

1. **Customer Service:** Buffer stock prevents stockouts, backorders, customer penalty fees, and lost market goodwill during demand spikes.
2. **Ordering & Setup Costs:** Larger orders cut purchase paperwork. Larger batch runs cut machine setups, tool resetting, and line downtime.
3. **Stable Labor & Facility Utilization:** Steady parts supply keeps assembly lines operating smoothly, preventing idle worker and machine time.
4. **Transportation Savings:** Large shipments qualify for Full Truckload (FTL) rates, cutting per-unit freight costs compared to Less-Than-Truckload (LTL) parcels.
5. **Payment to Suppliers:** Secures volume discounts and rebates while stockpiling raw materials hedges against future price inflation.

### D. Trade-Off Summary Table: Low vs. High Inventory Pressures

| Decision Factor | Low Inventory Pressures (Carrying Costs) | High Inventory Pressures (Shortage Costs) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Working Capital** | Keeps cash liquid; avoids 15% to 25% interest cost. | Ties up funds in unsold stock. |
| **Warehouse Space** | Minimal storage space, racks, and building rent. | Demands large warehouse area and extra shelving. |
| **Material Loss** | Low risk of theft, obsolescence, and damage. | High exposure to shrinkage and dead stock. |
| **Batch & Setup** | Small orders; frequent machine setups and downtime. | Large orders; long, uninterrupted production runs. |
| **Delivery & Service** | Risk of stockouts and assembly line halts. | Rapid customer shipments and steady operations. |
| **Freight & Pricing** | Standard unit prices; high LTL shipping rates. | Bulk freight savings; supplier quantity discounts. |

## 🎯 3. PYQ & Target Answer Key

### Final 2020 Q.4(a) (05 Marks)
**Question:** What is inventory control? Discuss the costs associated with holding high level inventory.

**Answer Blueprint:**
- State the verbatim definition of Inventory Control from §1.
- State that annual carrying costs equal 15% to 35% of stock value.
- Detail the three carrying cost groups (`I-S-H`) from §2 Block B: Capital (15% to 25%), Storage/handling, and Taxes/insurance/shrinkage.

---

### Final 2021 Q.4(a) (04 Marks)
**Question:** What is inventory management? State the costs associated with holding high level inventory.

**Answer Blueprint:**
- State the verbatim definition of Inventory Management from §1.
- List the five holding costs from §2 Block B: Capital cost, Storage rent, Property taxes, Insurance, and Shrinkage losses.

---

### Final 2023 Q.7(a) Part 1 (01 Mark)
**Question:** What is inventory control?

**Answer Blueprint:**
- State verbatim definition from §1: operational mechanism to maintain stock limits via order timing and quantity.

---

### Final 2024 Q.4(a) (02 Marks)
**Question:** State the costs for holding inventory.

**Answer Blueprint:**
- State carrying costs (high stock: capital, storage, taxes, shrinkage) versus shortage costs (low stock: stockouts, setup downtime, lost discounts) from §2.

---

> [!warning] ⚠️ Examiner Traps
> - **Control vs. Management:** Control is operational and warehouse-focused (order size, ROP). Management is strategic and firm-wide (working capital, vendor contracts).
> - **Shrinkage Incompleteness:** Always name all three forms: pilferage (theft), obsolescence (tech changes), and deterioration (physical decay).
> - **Cost of Capital Benchmark:** Always quote **15% to 25% per year** as the opportunity cost of capital. MRI Sir looks for this specific figure.
> - **Two-Sided 2024 Answer:** For 2024 Q.4(a), state both carrying costs (excess stock) and shortage costs (insufficient stock).
