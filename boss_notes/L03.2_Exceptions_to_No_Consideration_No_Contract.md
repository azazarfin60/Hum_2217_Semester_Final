# L03.2: Exceptions to No Consideration No Contract

> **Section:** Section A (CO1 · Legal Issues · Shoaib Islam Sir) | **Priority:** 🔴 Highest | **Exam Frequency:** Prescribed Ch. 2 Core Target
> **Subtopics Covered:** `L03.4` (Exceptions to "No Consideration, No Contract" / *Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio* under `Sec 25` and `Sec 185`)

## Why This Topic Matters

Shoaib Sir marked all contract rules as high-yield for final exams. Consideration is usually mandatory for any contract. But the law allows seven specific exceptions. If you miss even one statutory condition, you lose marks quickly. Memorizing these exact sections ensures full marks.

---

## 📝 Key Definitions

> **No Right of Action from Bare Agreement:** "Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio." (Latin Legal Maxim)

A bare promise made without consideration creates no legal duty. The courts will not enforce it.

> **Agreement Without Consideration:** "An agreement made without consideration is void." (Contract Act, 1872, Section 25)

Every valid contract requires something in return. A one-sided promise is gratuitous. But Section 25 and Section 185 list specific exceptions where agreements stand valid without consideration.

---

## The General Rule: No Consideration, No Contract

Under Section 25 of the Contract Act, 1872, an agreement without consideration is void. This rule protects people from careless promises.

In everyday life, people often make casual promises to give gifts. If the law enforced every casual promise, courts would flood with disputes. Therefore, the law requires consideration. Each party must give something and receive something in return.

However, business and family life need practical flexibility. Genuine gifts, family settlements, and voluntary acts deserve legal protection under strict safeguards. The Contract Act provides seven clear exceptions to this rule.

---

## The Seven Statutory Exceptions to the Rule

### Exception 1: Natural Love and Affection (Sec 25(1))

Under Section 25(1), an agreement made without consideration is valid if it meets four mandatory conditions:

1. **Expressed in writing:** The agreement cannot be oral. It must be written down.
2. **Registered by law:** The document must be registered under the document registration law in force.
3. **Natural love and affection:** The agreement must arise from genuine natural love and affection.
4. **Near relation:** The parties must stand in a near relation to each other.

Near relations include spouses, parents, children, and siblings. But near relation alone is not enough. Genuine affection must exist when signing.

- *Example:* A father promises in a written and registered deed to give his son Tk. 100,000 out of natural love. The agreement is valid and binding.
- *Counter-Example:* In *Rajlukhy Dabee v. Bhootnath Mookerjee*, a husband signed a registered agreement after constant quarrels. He promised maintenance to his wife. The court held it void. It arose from bitterness, not natural love and affection.

### Exception 2: Compensation for Past Voluntary Service (Sec 25(2))

Under Section 25(2), a promise to compensate a person for past voluntary service is valid without fresh consideration.

This exception applies when two conditions are met:
1. The service was performed voluntarily without any prior request from the promisor.
2. The service was done for the promisor, or it was something the promisor was legally bound to do.

- *Example:* D finds B's lost purse and returns it. B then promises to give D Tk. 500. This promise is a binding contract.
- *ECE Example:* An engineer saves a lab's test equipment from a water leak without being asked. The lab director later promises in writing to pay Tk. 5,000 for the help. The promise is legally enforceable.

### Exception 3: Promise to Pay a Time-Barred Debt (Sec 25(3))

Under the Limitation Act, 1908, a creditor cannot file a suit to recover a simple debt after three years. The debt becomes time-barred.

Under Section 25(3), a promise to pay a time-barred debt is enforceable if:
1. The promise is made in writing.
2. The promise is signed by the debtor or his authorized agent.
3. The debt refers to an ascertained or liquidated sum of money.

An oral promise to pay a time-barred debt is completely void. A written and signed promise creates a fresh legal obligation.

- *Example:* A owes B Tk. 50,000. The debt is four years old, so the limitation period has expired. A signs a letter promising to pay B Tk. 30,000 in full settlement. B can sue to recover Tk. 30,000.

### Exception 4: Creation of Agency (Sec 185)

Section 185 of the Contract Act, 1872 explicitly states:
> *"No consideration is necessary to create an agency."*

An agent represents the principal in legal transactions with third parties. An agency agreement is valid even if the agent receives no pay or commission.

- *Example:* P appoints A as his agent to sell equipment without paying an advance fee. The appointment is valid.

### Exception 5: Completed Gifts (Sec 25 Explanation 1)

Explanation 1 to Section 25 states that lack of consideration does not affect the validity of completed gifts.

The rule *"no consideration, no contract"* applies to promises to make gifts in the future. It does not apply to gifts already completed. Once a donor delivers a gift to the donee, the gift becomes final.

- *Conditions:* The gift must comply with the Transfer of Property Act. Moveable property requires delivery. Immovable property requires a written and registered deed.
- *Example:* A delivers an oscilloscope to B as a gift for his workshop. A cannot later demand the instrument back by claiming there was no consideration.

### Exception 6: Contract of Guarantee (Sec 127)

Under Section 127, a contract of guarantee does not require direct consideration between the creditor and the surety.

Section 127 provides:
> *"Anything done, or any promise made, for the benefit of the principal debtor, may be a sufficient consideration to the surety for giving the guarantee."*

The loan or benefit given to the debtor serves as valid consideration for the guarantor's promise.

- *Example:* A bank lends Tk. 500,000 to student founder S because G signs a contract guaranteeing repayment. The loan to S is sufficient consideration for G's promise.

### Exception 7: Charitable Subscription with Detriment

A bare promise to donate money to a charitable fund is usually void for lack of consideration. The donor can back out at any time.

However, the promise becomes legally binding if the promisee takes financial liability based on that promise. This rule was established in *Kedar Nath v. Gorie Mohammad*.

- *Rule:* If the charity incurs debts or starts construction relying on the subscriber's pledge, the subscriber must pay.
- *Example:* M pledges Tk. 100,000 to construct an engineering library. Relying on this pledge, the committee hires a builder and buys cement. M cannot revoke the pledge. M must pay the promised amount.

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive & Model Answers)

### 🎯 GQ1 · Model Exam Question 1 · Rule of Sec 25 and 7 Exceptions (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed High-Yield Model Question (Chapter 2) · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
"An agreement made without consideration is void." Discuss this rule (*Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio*) under Section 25 of the Contract Act, 1872. Explain the statutory exceptions to this rule.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. General Rule and Legal Maxim
Under Section 25 of the Contract Act, 1872, an agreement made without consideration is void. This rule is based on the Latin maxim *Ex nudo pacto non oritur actio*. The maxim means that no right of action arises from a bare agreement. 

Consideration is the price paid for a promise. Without consideration, a promise is purely gratuitous. The law will not enforce a bare promise.

#### 2. Statutory Exceptions to the Rule
The Contract Act provides seven exceptions where an agreement without consideration is valid and enforceable:

1. **Natural Love and Affection [Section 25(1)]:**
   An agreement without consideration is valid if it meets four conditions:
   - It is written.
   - It is registered under document registration law.
   - It is made on account of natural love and affection.
   - It is between parties in a near relation.
   - *Example:* A father writes and registers a deed gifting Tk. 100,000 to his son out of love.

2. **Compensation for Past Voluntary Service [Section 25(2)]:**
   A promise to compensate a person who has already voluntarily done something for the promisor is valid.
   - *Example:* A finds B's lost laptop and returns it. B promises to pay A Tk. 2,000. This promise is binding.

3. **Promise to Pay a Time-Barred Debt [Section 25(3)]:**
   A debt barred by the Limitation Act cannot be sued upon. But a promise to pay it is valid if:
   - It is made in writing.
   - It is signed by the debtor or his authorized agent.
   - *Example:* A owes B Tk. 40,000 on an expired debt. A signs a note promising to pay Tk. 25,000. B can enforce it.

4. **Agency Creation [Section 185]:**
   Under Section 185, no consideration is needed to create an agency. An agent is legally appointed even without payment.

5. **Completed Gifts [Section 25, Explanation 1]:**
   The rule does not apply to gifts actually completed between donor and donee. Once delivered, the donor cannot take the gift back for lack of consideration.

6. **Contract of Guarantee [Section 127]:**
   A contract of guarantee needs no direct consideration between creditor and surety. The benefit given to the principal debtor supports the guarantee.

7. **Charitable Subscription with Detriment:**
   A charity pledge is binding if the promisee incurs financial debt relying on it (*Kedar Nath v. Gorie Mohammad*).

### 🎯 GQ2 · Model Exam Question 2 · Applied 3-Scenario Problem on Consideration Exceptions (06 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed High-Yield Applied Problem (Chapter 2) · (06 Marks)

**Question:**
Evaluate the legal validity of the agreements in the following three independent scenarios under the Contract Act, 1872:

**(i)** An elder brother executes and registers a written deed promising to give his younger brother a residential plot worth Tk. 2,000,000 out of natural love and affection. **(02 Marks)**

**(ii)** An ECE student finds an expensive drone lost by a robotics lab and returns it. The lab director later signs a written note promising to pay the student Tk. 10,000 as a reward. **(02 Marks)**

**(iii)** A telecom firm owes a component supplier Tk. 100,000 for circuit boards delivered four years ago. The supplier demands payment. The firm's director verbally promises over the phone to pay Tk. 60,000. Later, the firm refuses to pay. **(02 Marks)**

**Full Answer:**

#### Scenario A Analysis · Deed of Gift to Brother

1. **Facts of the Case:**
   An elder brother executed and registered a written deed. He promised a land plot worth Tk. 2,000,000 to his younger brother out of natural love, without monetary consideration.
2. **Applicable Law & Section:**
   Section 25(1) of the Contract Act, 1872 (Agreement made on account of natural love and affection).
3. **Legal Analysis:**
   Under Section 25(1), an agreement without consideration is valid if it fulfills four conditions:
   - It is expressed in writing.
   - It is registered under the law for document registration.
   - It is made on account of natural love and affection.
   - It is between parties standing in a near relation.
   Brothers are near blood relatives. The agreement was written, registered, and made out of affection. All four statutory requirements are satisfied.
4. **Direct Conclusion:**
   The agreement is valid and legally enforceable. The younger brother can enforce the transfer in court.

#### Scenario B Analysis · Reward for Found Drone

1. **Facts of the Case:**
   A student voluntarily found and returned a lost research drone. Afterwards, the lab director signed a written promise to pay Tk. 10,000 as a reward.
2. **Applicable Law & Section:**
   Section 25(2) of the Contract Act, 1872 (Promise to compensate for past voluntary service).
3. **Legal Analysis:**
   Under Section 25(2), a promise to compensate someone for past voluntary service is legally valid. 
   - The student acted voluntarily without prior payment request.
   - The act benefited the lab.
   - The director subsequently promised compensation.
   Past voluntary service forms valid legal support for the subsequent promise.
4. **Direct Conclusion:**
   The promise is a valid contract. The student can legally recover the Tk. 10,000 reward from the lab.

#### Scenario C Analysis · Verbal Promise on Old Debt

1. **Facts of the Case:**
   A telecom firm owed Tk. 100,000 for goods delivered four years ago. The limitation period expired. The director verbally promised over the phone to pay Tk. 60,000, but later refused.
2. **Applicable Law & Section:**
   Section 25(3) of the Contract Act, 1872 and the Limitation Act, 1908.
3. **Legal Analysis:**
   Under the Limitation Act, 1908, a simple debt becomes time-barred after three years. The creditor loses the right to sue.
   Under Section 25(3), a promise to pay a time-barred debt is enforceable only if:
   - It is made in writing.
   - It is signed by the debtor or his authorized agent.
   Here, the director made a purely verbal promise over the phone. No written and signed document exists. An oral promise to pay a time-barred debt remains void under Section 25.
4. **Direct Conclusion:**
   The verbal promise is void and legally unenforceable. The supplier cannot recover the money.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **Never forget the four conditions for Section 25(1):** Students often write love and affection. But they forget the deed must be written and registered. All four conditions are mandatory.
2. **Oral vs. Written time-barred debt:** Always check if the promise is in writing and signed. An oral promise on a time-barred debt is void.
3. **Agency needs no consideration:** Cite Section 185 directly. Do not confuse appointing an agent with paying an agent.
4. **Charity rule needs detriment:** A simple promise to donate is void. It binds the donor only if the charity incurs financial debt relying on it (*Kedar Nath v. Gorie Mohammad*).
