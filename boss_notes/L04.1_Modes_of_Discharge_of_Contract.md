# L04.1: Modes of Discharge of Contract

## 📝 Key Definitions

> **Discharge of Contract:** When the rights and obligations created by a contract come to an end, the contract is discharged. Both parties are released from further legal liability.

A contract is born through agreement. It dies when the legal bond between parties ends.

> **Actual Performance:** Fulfilling promises according to agreed terms, time, and manner. (Contract Act, 1872, Section 37)

Both parties do what they promised. The contract ends naturally.

> **Attempted Performance (Tender):** An unconditional offer by the promisor to perform his obligation, which the promisee rejects. (Contract Act, 1872, Section 38)

If a seller offers the correct goods at the right time and the buyer refuses, the seller is discharged.

> **Novation:** Substituting a new contract in place of an existing contract, either between the same parties or with new parties. (Contract Act, 1872, Section 62)

The original contract is cancelled. The new contract takes its place.

> **Remission:** Accepting a lesser sum or lesser performance in place of the original agreed promise. (Contract Act, 1872, Section 63)

A creditor accepts Tk. 30,000 to settle a Tk. 50,000 debt. The remaining debt is wiped out without fresh consideration.

> **Anticipatory Breach:** A party repudiates his liability or disables himself from performing before the scheduled due date. (Contract Act, 1872, Section 39)

Announcing in advance that you will not deliver goods breaks the contract immediately.

## Overview of All Seven Modes of Discharge of Contract

### What Does Discharge Mean?

A contract binds parties together with legal obligations. Discharge means cutting this legal tie. 

When a contract is discharged:
- All primary obligations come to an end.
- Neither party can demand further performance.
- If discharge happens by breach, secondary rights like suing for damages may arise.

### The Seven Statutory Modes (P-M-I-O-L-A-B)

Under the Contract Act, 1872, a contract can end in seven distinct ways:

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 7 Modes of Discharge
> Remember the sentence: **"Promises Must Inspire Our Lawful Agreed Boundaries"**
> - **P**: **P**erformance (`Sec 37–38` · actual or tender)
> - **M**: **M**utual agreement (`Sec 62–63` · novation, rescission, alteration, remission)
> - **I**: **I**mpossibility / Frustration (`Sec 56` · supervening event)
> - **O**: **O**peration of law (death, court order of insolvency, merger)
> - **L**: **L**apse of time (Limitation Act · 3-year deadline)
> - **A**: **A**lteration (unauthorized material change to contract document)
> - **B**: **B**reach of contract (`Sec 39` · actual or anticipatory)

## Mode 1: Discharge by Performance or Tender (Sec 37–38)

Performance is the normal and intended way to end a contract.

### Actual Performance (Sec 37)

Under **Section 37**, contracting parties must either perform or offer to perform their promises.
- Both parties fulfill all obligations completely.
- They must deliver at the agreed time, at the right place, and in the agreed manner.
- Once both sides perform, the contract is completely discharged.
- *Example:* An ECE supplier delivers 100 microcontrollers on 1st March. The buyer pays Tk. 80,000 on delivery. Both sides have performed fully.

### Attempted Performance or Tender (Sec 38)

Sometimes the promisor is ready and willing to perform, but the promisee refuses to accept it. This offer of performance is called **tender** or attempted performance.

Under **Section 38**:
> *"Where a promisor has made an offer of performance to the promisee, and the offer has not been accepted, the promisor is not responsible for non-performance, nor does he thereby lose his rights under the contract."*

- A valid tender of goods or services discharges the promisor from further liability.
- The promisor can then sue the promisee for breach of contract.
- *Tender of Money:* Offering money does not wipe out the debt. It only stops interest from running and protects the debtor from legal costs.

### Three Essentials of a Valid Tender

For tender to have the legal effect of performance under Section 38, it must meet three conditions:
1. **Must be unconditional:** The promisor cannot attach new terms. Offering Tk. 40,000 on condition that the creditor signs a full release of Tk. 50,000 is not a valid tender.
2. **Proper time and place:** It must be made at the scheduled date and during business hours. Delivering goods at midnight to a closed warehouse is invalid.
3. **Reasonable opportunity to inspect:** The promisee must be given an honest chance to check that the goods match the contract.

## Mode 2: Discharge by Mutual Agreement or Consent (Sec 62–63)

What is created by mutual consent can also be dissolved by mutual consent.

### The Statutory Principle of Mutual Consent (Sec 62)

Under **Section 62** of the Contract Act, 1872:
> *"If the parties to a contract agree to substitute a new contract for it, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract need not be performed."*

Mutual consent of all contracting parties is mandatory. One party cannot modify or cancel the agreement alone.

### The Six Forms of Mutual Discharge

#### 1. Novation (Sec 62)
Novation means substituting a new contract for the existing one. The original contract ends completely.
- **Between the same parties:** Terms are replaced with entirely new terms.
- **Between different parties:** A new debtor or creditor is substituted.
- *Example:* A owes B Tk. 100,000. A, B, and C agree that B will accept C as his debtor in place of A. A's liability is discharged.

#### 2. Rescission (Sec 62)
Rescission means cancelling the contract by mutual consent before performance falls due.
- Both parties agree to walk away.
- Neither party owes anything to the other.
- *Example:* A software company agrees to build an inventory app for a retailer. Before any coding begins, both sides mutually agree to drop the project.

#### 3. Alteration (Sec 62)
Alteration means modifying one or more terms of the contract with the consent of all parties.
- Unlike novation, the parties stay the same.
- Only specific terms (like delivery date, price, or payment terms) change.
- *Example:* A contractor agrees to deliver telecom towers on 1st May. By mutual written agreement, both parties push the delivery date to 1st July.

#### 4. Remission (Sec 63)
Under **Section 63**, a promisee may dispense with or remit performance, either wholly or in part.
- The creditor accepts a smaller sum or a different performance to discharge the whole claim.
- **Crucial Rule:** Unlike English law, Bangladesh law requires **no fresh consideration** for remission.
- *Example:* P owes Q Tk. 50,000. Q agrees to accept Tk. 35,000 in full settlement. The whole debt of Tk. 50,000 is discharged.

#### 5. Waiver
Waiver means deliberately giving up a known legal right under the contract.
- When a party waives a right, he cannot demand it later.
- No written deed or fresh consideration is needed.
- *Example:* A buyer waives his right to inspect goods before dispatch. He cannot later complain about lack of pre-shipment inspection.

#### 6. Merger
Merger occurs when an inferior right coincides with a superior right in the same person.
- The inferior right vanishes into the superior right.
- *Example:* A tenant renting an engineering workshop buys the building outright. His tenancy contract merges into his ownership title and is discharged.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: Mutual Discharge Modes
> Remember **N-R-A-R-W-M**:
> - **N**: **N**ovation (`Sec 62` · substitute new contract or parties)
> - **R**: **R**escission (`Sec 62` · mutual cancellation)
> - **A**: **A**lteration (`Sec 62` · modify contract terms)
> - **R**: **R**emission (`Sec 63` · accept lesser amount)
> - **W**: **W**aiver (surrender legal rights)
> - **M**: **M**erger (inferior right vanishes into superior title)

### Comparison of the Six Modes under Sections 62 and 63

| Mode of Discharge | Statutory Basis | Key Feature | New Contract Created? | Parties Changed? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Novation** | Section 62 | Substitutes a new contract for the old one. | Yes | Yes or No |
| **Rescission** | Section 62 | Cancels the entire contract before performance. | No | No |
| **Alteration** | Section 62 | Modifies specific contract terms by mutual consent. | No (same contract modified) | No |
| **Remission** | Section 63 | Accepts lesser payment or satisfaction. | No | No |
| **Waiver** | General Law | Voluntarily abandons an existing contractual right. | No | No |
| **Merger** | Common Law | Inferior contract right dissolves into superior right. | No | No |

## Mode 3: Subsequent Impossibility or Frustration (Sec 56 Overview)

Under **Section 56 (Paragraph 2)**, a contract becomes void if performance becomes impossible or unlawful later.
- An unexpected external event destroys the foundation of the contract.
- Neither party was at fault.
- Both parties are excused from future performance.
- Any advance money received must be returned under **Section 65**.

## Mode 4: Discharge by Operation of Law

A contract terminates automatically by legal rules, regardless of party intentions.

### 1. Death in Personal Contracts
Contracts depending on personal skill, art, or expertise end when the promisor dies.
- *Personal Skill:* A contract to paint a portrait or write software personally ends on death.
- *General Contracts:* In ordinary sales or commercial debts, rights and liabilities pass to legal heirs. Heirs are liable only up to the value of inherited property.

### 2. Insolvency and Court Assignment
When a debtor is declared insolvent by a court:
- His assets and liabilities pass to an Official Assignee or Official Receiver.
- The court issues an order of discharge.
- This court order frees the insolvent person from all prior contractual debts.

### 3. Merger of Rights
When an inferior contract right and a superior property right meet in the exact same person, the inferior contract terminates.
- A leaseholder of an electronics lab buys the freehold ownership of the lab.
- The lease contract ends immediately.

## Mode 5: Discharge by Lapse of Time

The law aids the vigilant, not those who sleep on their rights.

### The Three-Year Limitation Period
Under the **Limitation Act, 1908**, legal actions must be filed within fixed statutory deadlines:
- The limitation period for a breach of simple contract or recovery of an ordinary debt is **3 years**.
- The 3-year clock starts ticking from the exact date the breach or default occurred.

### Legal Effect of Time Bar
If the creditor does not file a lawsuit within 3 years:
- The court bars the legal remedy.
- The debt becomes a **time-barred debt**.
- The contract is legally discharged because it cannot be enforced in court.
- *Exception:* Under Section 25(3), a written and signed promise to pay a time-barred debt revives the obligation.

## Mode 6: Discharge by Unauthorized Material Alteration

Written contracts must remain genuine and untouched.

### Meaning of Material Alteration
A material alteration is an unauthorized physical change to a written contract document that alters its legal effect.
- Common material alterations include:
  - Changing the price or sum of money.
  - Modifying the payment or delivery date.
  - Altering interest rates or credit terms.
  - Inserting names of new parties without consent.

### Legal Consequence on the Instrument
If a party alters a material term without the consent of the other parties:
- The entire contract is discharged.
- The written document becomes void immediately.
- The court will not admit the altered document as evidence.
- The party who altered the document loses all legal rights under it.
- *Minor Changes:* Correcting obvious spelling errors or adding explanatory notes without changing legal rights is an immaterial alteration. It does not discharge the contract.

## Mode 7: Discharge by Breach of Contract (Sec 39)

Breach of contract occurs when a party fails or refuses to perform his contractual duty without a legal excuse.

### Meaning and Nature of Contract Breach
Breach relieves the innocent party from performing his remaining promises. It also gives him an immediate right to sue for damages under Section 73.

Breach takes two forms:
1. **Actual Breach:** Occurs on the due date or during performance.
2. **Anticipatory Breach:** Occurs before the scheduled due date arrives.

### Actual Breach vs Anticipatory Breach

#### 1. Actual Breach
Actual breach happens when:
- A party refuses or fails to perform on the scheduled date of performance.
- Or a party performs only part of his promise during ongoing work.
- *Example:* A vendor agrees to deliver 50 network routers on 10th October. On 10th October, he fails to deliver any routers.

#### 2. Anticipatory Breach (Sec 39)
Anticipatory breach happens before performance is due. A party clearly shows by words or conduct that he will not perform.

Under **Section 39** of the Contract Act, 1872:
> *"When a party to a contract has refused to perform, or disabled himself from performing, his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to the contract, unless he has signified, by words or conduct, his acquiescence in its continuance."*

Anticipatory breach occurs in two ways:
1. **Express Repudiation:** The promisor directly announces by spoken words or letter that he will not perform.
   - *Leading Case:* *Hochster v. De La Tour* (1853). The defendant hired the plaintiff in April to serve as a courier starting in June. In May, the defendant wrote saying he would not need the plaintiff. The court held that the plaintiff could sue immediately in May.
2. **Implied by Disabling Act:** The promisor does an act that makes it impossible to perform.
   - *Example:* X agrees to sell his unique lab spectrometer to Y on 1st November. On 15th October, X sells and delivers the spectrometer to Z. X has disabled himself from performing.

### Comparison: Actual Breach vs Anticipatory Breach

| Comparison Point | Actual Breach | Anticipatory Breach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time of Occurrence** | On the scheduled date of performance, or during performance. | Before the scheduled due date of performance arrives. |
| **Governing Statute** | General breach principles under Section 73. | Explicitly governed by **Section 39**. |
| **How It Occurs** | Failing or refusing to deliver goods on the due date. | Express refusal by letter, or doing an act that makes performance impossible. |
| **Immediate Rescission?** | The innocent party rescinds at once upon default. | The innocent party can rescind immediately OR wait until the due date. |
| **Measure of Damages** | Difference between contract price and market price on the due date. | Difference between contract price and market price when treated as broken. |
| **Leading Case** | Standard breach cases under Section 73. | *Hochster v. De La Tour* (1853); *Frost v. Knight* (1872). |
| **ECE Example** | Failing to install fiber cables on the contract deadline. | Telling a client on Monday that you will not install cables on Friday. |

### Remedies of the Injured Party under Section 39

When an anticipatory breach occurs, Section 39 gives the injured party two clear choices:

#### Choice 1: Treat the Contract as Broken Immediately
The injured party can accept the repudiation and treat the contract as ended right away.
- He does not have to wait until the scheduled performance date.
- He can sue for breach damages immediately (*Hochster v. De La Tour*).
- He is released from all his own obligations.

#### Choice 2: Keep the Contract Alive Until the Due Date
The injured party can refuse to accept the repudiation and keep the contract alive.
- He waits until the scheduled date of performance arrives.
- He gives the promisor a chance to change his mind and perform.
- **Serious Risk:** Keeping the contract open benefits both parties. An unexpected event (like a fire or legal ban) may happen before the due date. The contract then becomes frustrated under Section 56. The promisor is excused, and the injured party loses the damages claim (*Avery v. Bowden*).

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive & Model Answers)

### 🎯 GQ1 · 2024 Final Q.6(c) · Methods of Termination of a Contract (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** 2024 Final Exam · Question 6(c) · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
Discuss the methods of termination of a contract.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Definition of Termination (Discharge) of Contract
A contract creates legal obligations between parties. When these obligations come to an end, the contract is discharged or terminated. Both parties are released from further legal liabilities.

#### 2. Seven Methods of Termination of Contract
Under the Contract Act, 1872, a contract may be terminated in seven ways:

1. **Termination by Performance (Sections 37 and 38):**
   This is the natural mode of ending a contract. Both parties fulfill their promises completely. It includes actual performance and attempted performance (valid tender).
2. **Termination by Mutual Agreement (Sections 62 and 63):**
   The parties agree to cancel, replace, or alter the contract. Forms include novation, rescission, alteration, remission, and waiver.
3. **Termination by Subsequent Impossibility (Section 56):**
   Performance becomes physically impossible or unlawful after the contract is made without fault by either party (Doctrine of Frustration).
4. **Termination by Operation of Law:**
   The contract terminates automatically under legal rules. Causes include death in personal contracts, a court declaration of insolvency, or merger of rights.
5. **Termination by Lapse of Time:**
   Under the Limitation Act, 1908, simple contract claims must be brought within 3 years. If no lawsuit is filed, the court remedy is barred.
6. **Termination by Material Alteration:**
   An unauthorized change in a material term of a written contract discharges the contract. The altered document becomes void in court.
7. **Termination by Breach of Contract (Section 39):**
   One party fails or refuses to perform his obligation. Breach can be an actual breach on the due date or an anticipatory breach before the due date.

### 🎯 GQ2 · Model Exam Question 2 · Discharge by Performance and Essentials of Valid Tender (03 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question · Chapter 2 Target Part (a) or (b) · (03 Marks)

**Question:**
Explain discharge of contract by performance. Distinguish between actual performance and attempted performance (tender). State the essentials of a valid tender under Section 38.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Meaning of Performance (Section 37)
Under **Section 37** of the Contract Act, 1872, contracting parties must either perform or offer to perform their promises. Performance is the standard and natural way to discharge a contract.

#### 2. Actual Performance vs Attempted Performance (Tender)
- **Actual Performance:** Both parties carry out their respective promises fully according to agreed terms. Neither party owes any further obligation.
  - *Example:* A vendor delivers 50 circuit boards on time, and the buyer pays the agreed price.
- **Attempted Performance or Tender (Section 38):** The promisor offers to perform his promise, but the promisee refuses to accept it.
  - *Legal Status:* Under Section 38, a valid tender of goods or services excuses the promisor from non-performance. He does not lose his contractual rights.

#### 3. Essentials of a Valid Tender under Section 38
1. **Unconditional:** The offer to perform must not carry any unagreed conditions.
2. **Proper Time and Place:** The tender must occur at the agreed date, place, and during business hours.
3. **Opportunity to Inspect:** The buyer must get an honest chance to check that goods match specifications.

### 🎯 GQ3 · Model Exam Question 3 · Discharge by Mutual Agreement under Sections 62 and 63 (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question · Chapter 2 Target Part (b) or (c) · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
Explain how a contract is discharged by mutual agreement under the Contract Act, 1872. Define Novation, Rescission, Alteration, Remission, and Waiver with examples.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Statutory Principle (Section 62)
Under **Section 62** of the Contract Act, 1872:
> *"If the parties to a contract agree to substitute a new contract for it, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract need not be performed."*

A contract is created by mutual consent. It can be dissolved or modified by mutual consent of all parties.

#### 2. Five Forms of Mutual Discharge
1. **Novation (Section 62):**
   Parties substitute a new contract for the original one. It can change the terms between the same parties or substitute a new party.
   - *Example:* A owes B Tk. 100,000. A, B, and C agree that C will pay B instead of A. A is discharged.
2. **Rescission (Section 62):**
   Parties agree to cancel the contract before performance is due. Both sides walk away freely.
   - *Example:* An ECE firm agrees to build custom testing hardware. Before work starts, both parties mutually agree to cancel the order.
3. **Alteration (Section 62):**
   Parties modify one or more terms in an existing contract by mutual consent without changing the parties.
   - *Example:* A buyer and seller mutually agree to extend the equipment delivery date from 1st March to 1st June.
4. **Remission (Section 63):**
   Under Section 63, a promisee may accept a lesser sum or lesser performance in full discharge of the promise. No fresh consideration is needed.
   - *Example:* A owes B Tk. 50,000. B agrees to accept Tk. 30,000 in full settlement. The entire debt is discharged.
5. **Waiver:**
   A party voluntarily abandons his contractual rights.
   - *Example:* A buyer waives his right to pre-shipment factory inspection and accepts immediate delivery.

### 🎯 GQ4 · Model Exam Question 4 · Discharge by Operation of Law, Lapse of Time, and Material Alteration (03 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question · Chapter 2 Target Part (a) or (b) · (03 Marks)

**Question:**
Describe how a contract is discharged by operation of law, lapse of time, and unauthorized material alteration.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Discharge by Operation of Law
A contract terminates automatically by legal rules in three situations:
- **Death:** Contracts based on personal skill or talent end upon death. In ordinary commercial contracts, rights and liabilities pass to legal heirs.
- **Insolvency:** When a court declares a debtor insolvent, his assets and debts pass to an Official Assignee. The court discharge frees him from prior debts.
- **Merger:** When an inferior contract right coincides with a superior property right in the same person, the inferior right dissolves. A tenant buying the rented property ends the lease.

#### 2. Discharge by Lapse of Time
Under the **Limitation Act, 1908**, a party must file a legal action within a prescribed time limit. The limitation period for simple contract claims and debts is **3 years**. If no suit is filed within 3 years of default, the court bars the remedy. The contract becomes legally unenforceable.

#### 3. Discharge by Material Alteration
An unauthorized physical change to a written contract that alters its legal effect discharges the contract.
- Changing the payment amount, date, interest rate, or party names without consent makes the instrument void.
- The court will not admit the altered document as evidence. The altering party forfeits all legal rights under it.

### 🎯 GQ5 · Model Exam Question 5 · Actual Breach vs Anticipatory Breach and Section 39 Options (03 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question · Chapter 2 Target Part (b) or (c) · (03 Marks)

**Question:**
Distinguish between Actual Breach and Anticipatory Breach of contract under the Contract Act, 1872. What options are available to the injured party upon an anticipatory breach under Section 39?

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Actual Breach vs Anticipatory Breach

| Comparison Point | Actual Breach | Anticipatory Breach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Occurs on the scheduled due date of performance or during performance. | Occurs before the scheduled date of performance arrives. |
| **Mode** | Party refuses or fails to perform when performance is due. | Party repudiates liability by words or by an act disabling performance. |
| **Governing Law** | General breach principles under Section 73. | Explicitly governed by **Section 39** of the Contract Act, 1872. |
| **ECE Example** | A vendor fails to deliver microchips on the agreed date of 1st October. | A vendor states on 10th September that he will not deliver on 1st October. |

#### 2. Options of the Injured Party under Section 39
When anticipatory breach occurs, Section 39 gives the injured party two legal options:

1. **Rescind Immediately and Sue for Damages:**
   The injured party can treat the contract as broken at once. He does not need to wait until the scheduled due date. He can sue immediately for damages (*Hochster v. De La Tour*).
2. **Keep the Contract Open Until the Due Date:**
   The injured party can refuse to accept the repudiation and keep the contract alive. He waits until the scheduled date to see if the other party performs.
   - *Risk:* An unexpected event (like a fire or legal ban) may occur before the due date. The contract then becomes frustrated under Section 56. The promisor is excused, and the injured party loses the damages claim.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **Remember the 2024 Exam Target:** In 2024 Q.6(c), all 7 methods of termination were asked for 4 marks. Write the definition of discharge first. Then list all 7 methods with their exact Contract Act section numbers.
2. **Section 63 requires no consideration:** English law requires fresh consideration to remit debt. Bangladesh law under Section 63 requires none. Mention this for extra credit.
3. **Notice the risk of keeping the contract open:** In anticipatory breach, explain the danger of waiting. Supervening frustration under Section 56 can destroy the damages claim. Mentioning this earns top marks.
4. **Distinguish Novation from Alteration:** Novation replaces the old contract with a new one and can introduce new parties. Alteration modifies terms between the same parties.
