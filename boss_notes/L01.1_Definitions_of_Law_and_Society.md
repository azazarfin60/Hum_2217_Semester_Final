## 📝 Key Definitions

> **Law (General Meaning):** "Law is a system of rules that govern a society with the intention of maintaining social order, upholding justice, and preventing harm to individuals' property." (SI Sir Lecture Notes & Slides, Chapter 1)

Law sets common rules for everyone in an organized society. Without these rules, peaceful living is impossible. The state enforces these rules to protect rights and stop harm.

> **Holland's Definition:** "Law is a rule of external human action enforced by the sovereign political authority." (Sir Thomas Erskine Holland)

Holland explains that law deals only with outward actions of living people. It does not judge private thoughts. The supreme political power of the state enforces it.

> **Salmond's Definition:** "Law is the body of principles recognized and applied by the State in the administration of justice." (Sir John William Salmond)

Salmond ties law directly to courts and judges. If a court uses a rule to deliver justice, that rule is law.

> **Woodrow Wilson's Definition:** "Law is that portion of the established habit and thought of mankind which has gained distinct and formal recognition in the shape of uniform rules backed by the authority and power of the government." (Woodrow Wilson)

Wilson shows that laws grow from human habits and shared beliefs. When the state writes these habits into fixed rules and backs them with force, they become law.

## Core Theory: What is Law and Why Do We Need It?

### 1. The Command of the Sovereign

In legal theory, law is the command of the sovereign. This classical view highlights three core facts:

1. **Source in Sovereign Authority:** Law comes from the highest political authority in the state.
2. **Backed by Sanctions:** Disobedience brings state penalties, fines, or prison.
3. **Compels a Course of Conduct:** Law is not advice. It forces people to follow a set pattern of behavior.

People living together need shared rules. Without rules, chaos spreads. So, the state creates and enforces rules of conduct to protect society.

### 2. Holland's Three Essential Characteristics of Law

From Holland's famous definition, three key traits emerge:

1. **Rule for Human Action:**
   Law applies only to human beings. It does not govern animals, machines, or natural objects. It sets boundaries for people living in an organized community.
2. **Regulation of External Conduct:**
   Law regulates only outward physical actions and conduct. It does not punish secret desires, thoughts, or private intentions until someone takes physical action.
3. **State Enforcement:**
   A rule is not law just because it is good or moral. It becomes law only when the sovereign political authority recognizes it. The state must stand ready to punish those who break it.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: **H-E-S**
> Remember the three letters **H-E-S** to recall Holland's three traits:
> - **H** $\rightarrow$ **H**uman action (applies only to people)
> - **E** $\rightarrow$ **E**xternal conduct (regulates outward acts, not thoughts)
> - **S** $\rightarrow$ **S**tate enforcement (backed by sovereign power and penalties)

## The Dynamic Cycle: Law, Government, and Society

SI Sir explained the connection between law, government, and society in Class 1. They form a continuous cycle of mutual support:

### 1. The Governance Cycle

The legal system operates through a clear three-step chain:

$$
\text{Law} \longrightarrow \text{Enforcement} \longrightarrow \text{Government / Parliament}
$$

Parliament enacts the written statute. The executive branch and police enforce it across society. The courts resolve disputes and punish offenders.

### 2. The Reciprocal Relationship

Law, government, and society depend directly on one another. Neither can survive alone:

- **Society Needs Law:**
  Human beings need order to live together in peace. Without law, might makes right and anarchy spreads. Law protects weaker individuals, contracts, and property.
- **Government Enacts and Enforces Law:**
  The state creates formal statutes through parliament. The government uses police, inspectors, and courts to enforce these rules.
- **Society Empowers Government:**
  Citizens grant authority and tax resources to the government. They obey laws because they value stability and security.
- **Law Limits Government Power:**
  Law does not just control citizens. It also binds the state. Constitutional law prevents government officials from acting like tyrants.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: **The 4-Way Bond**
> - **Society** demands safety.
> - **Government** writes the rules.
> - **Law** maintains peace.
> - **Courts** punish violations.

## Domestic Labour Law Scope in Bangladesh (BLC 2006)

Under subtopic `L01.5`, you must know how domestic labour law is organized in Bangladesh.

### 1. Consolidation of Past Laws

Before 2006, Bangladesh had many scattered colonial and post-colonial labour statutes. In 2006, the parliament passed **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (Act XLII of 2006)**.
- It repealed and unified **25 separate, outdated labour laws** into one modern code.
- This single code removed confusion and created uniform labour standards nationwide.

### 2. Size and Statutory Structure

The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 contains:
- **326 sections** in total.
- **21 distinct chapters**.
- Several comprehensive schedules defining injuries, compensation rates, and hazardous trades.

### 3. Broad Areas Regulated

The Code regulates all key areas of the employment relationship:
- Conditions of employment and service contracts (Chapter II).
- Employment of adolescents and working hours (Chapters III & XI).
- Maternity leave and cash benefits for female workers (Chapter IV).
- Health, hygiene, and workplace fire safety (Chapters V & VI).
- Welfare facilities: first aid, canteens, rest shelters, and crèches (Chapter VIII).
- Trade union registration, collective bargaining agents (CBA), and strikes (Chapters XIII & XIV).
- Labour Courts, Labour Appellate Tribunal, and legal penalties (Chapters XIX & XX).

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive & Model Answers)

### 🎯 GQ1 · Model Exam Question · Definitions and Characteristics of Law (03 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question (SI Sir Chapter 1 "Study All") · (03 Marks)

**Question:**
Define Law. State the definitions of law given by Holland, Salmond, and Woodrow Wilson, and highlight the three essential characteristics of law.

**Full Answer:**

**1. General Definition:**
Law is a system of uniform rules created and enforced by the state. It regulates human behavior, protects property, upholds justice, and maintains order in society.

**2. Key Juristic Definitions:**
- **Holland:** "Law is a rule of external human action enforced by the sovereign political authority."
- **Salmond:** "Law is the body of principles recognized and applied by the State in the administration of justice."
- **Woodrow Wilson:** "Law is that portion of the established habit and thought of mankind which has gained distinct and formal recognition in the shape of uniform rules backed by the authority and power of the government."

**3. Three Essential Characteristics of Law (Based on Holland):**
1. **Rule for Human Action:** Law applies only to human beings living in an organized society. It does not govern non-human entities.
2. **Regulation of External Conduct:** Law controls outward actions and physical conduct. It does not control private thoughts or silent intentions.
3. **State Enforcement:** Law comes from sovereign authority. The state enforces it through courts, police, and legal penalties.

### 🎯 GQ2 · Model Exam Question · Law, Government, Society, and Labour Code Scope (03 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question (SI Sir Class 1) · (03 Marks)

**Question:**
Explain the reciprocal relationship between law, government, and society. Also, state the primary statutory scope of domestic labour law in Bangladesh.

**Full Answer:**

**1. Mechanics and Governance Cycle:**
Law and governance operate in a continuous cycle:

$$
\text{Law} \longrightarrow \text{Enforcement} \longrightarrow \text{Government / Parliament}
$$

Parliament makes the law. The government enforces it. The legal system keeps society stable.

**2. Reciprocal Relationship:**
Law, government, and society support each other through four distinct ties:
- **Society Requires Law:** People need rules to live together peacefully. Without law, anarchy spreads and individual rights vanish.
- **Government Enacts and Enforces Law:** Parliament passes statutes to meet social needs. The executive branch uses police and courts to enforce compliance.
- **Society Empowers Government:** Citizens grant legitimacy and authority to the government through constitutional processes and consent.
- **Law Constrains Government:** Law prevents rulers from abusing power. It protects citizens against arbitrary state action.

**3. Statutory Scope of Domestic Labour Law:**
Bangladesh codified its labour rules in **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (Act XLII of 2006)**:
- **Consolidation:** It unified and repealed 25 outdated, scattered labour statutes.
- **Structure:** It contains **326 sections** organized into **21 chapters**.
- **Coverage:** It applies to factories, commercial shops, transport services, and industrial workshops across Bangladesh.
- **Core Subject Areas:** It sets rules for working hours, wages, and fire safety. It also covers welfare, maternity benefits, trade unions, CBA, and Labour Courts.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **Quote Verbatim First:** Always write Holland, Salmond, and Woodrow Wilson in quotation marks. Shoaib Sir looks for exact phrases like *"rule of external human action"* and *"administration of justice"*.
2. **Do Not Confuse Holland and Salmond:** Holland emphasizes the *sovereign political authority*. Salmond focuses on the *administration of justice* by courts.
3. **Remember the External Action Rule:** A common student mistake is writing that law controls human thoughts. Law never punishes thoughts. It only regulates outward physical actions.
4. **State the Labour Code Numbers Clearly:** Always cite **Act XLII of 2006**, **326 sections**, and **21 chapters**. These exact figures show strong legal knowledge.
