# L07.2: Machinery Safety and Workplace Duties

> **Section:** Section A (CO1 · Legal Issues · Shoaib Islam Sir) | **Priority:** 🟡 Medium (Tested in 2025 Law-CT2 & 2023 Final; Excluded from 2026 Final by Shoaib Sir Voice Note) | **Exam Frequency:** 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2 (10m), 2023 Final Q.2(c) (04m), 2025 Law-CT2 Q.1 (10m part)
> **Subtopics Covered:** `L07.2` (Work on or near machinery in motion `Sec 63–64` vs. Adolescents on dangerous machines `Sec 40 & 39(3)`), `L07.3` (Responsibilities of employers and employees regarding workplace safety regulations)
> **Sources:** BLC 2006 Chapter VI (`Sections 61, 63, 64, 78A`), Chapter III (`Sections 39, 40`), 2025 Law-CT2 Question Paper, 2023 Final Question Paper, Shoaib Sir Voice Note

## Why This Topic Matters

Shoaib Sir tested machinery safety and adolescent limits in 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2 for 10 marks. He also tested employer and employee safety responsibilities in 2023 Final Q.2(c) for 4 marks. Shoaib Sir excluded general machinery from the 2026 final exam to reduce student workload. But class tests and analytical safety questions regularly test these principles. Understanding Section 64, Section 40, and Section 78A gives you a complete grasp of legal liability and workplace safety.

## 📝 Key Definitions

> **Work on Machinery in Motion (Section 64(1)):** "Where in any establishment it becomes necessary to examine any part of machinery... while the machinery is in motion... such examination or operation shall be carried out only by a specially trained adult male worker wearing tight-fitting clothing whose name has been recorded in the register..." (The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, Section 64(1))

Only trained adult men wearing tight clothes can service running machines. Female workers and adolescents can never touch moving machinery.

> **Adolescents on Dangerous Machines (Section 40(1)):** "No adolescent shall work at any machine unless he has been fully instructed as to the dangers arising in connection with the machine and the precautions to be observed, and has received sufficient training in work at the machine, or is under adequate supervision by a person who has thorough knowledge and experience of the machine." (Section 40(1))

Adolescents aged 14 to under 18 may operate dangerous machines only under strict safety rules. They must receive hazard instructions and either practical training or experienced adult supervision.

> **Personal Protective Equipment Duty (Section 78A(1)–(2)):** "No authority shall engage any worker in work without providing him with personal safety equipments and ensuring uses thereof... If any personal safety equipment is supplied but not used, the worker concerned shall be liable." (Section 78A(1)–(2), inserted in 2013)

Employers must supply safety gear free of charge. But workers are personally liable if they fail to wear supplied equipment on the job.

## Core Theory 1: Fencing and Moving Machinery (Sections 63 & 64)

Chapter VI sets strict engineering controls to prevent horrific mechanical accidents:

```text
                             Machinery Safety Provisions
                                          │
            ┌─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┐
            ▼                                                           ▼
     BLC Section 64                                            BLC Section 40 & 39(3)
(Work on Machinery in Motion)                             (Adolescents on Dangerous Machines)
  • Adult male workers only                                 • Adolescent workers (14 to < 18 years)
  • Tight-fitting protective clothing                       • Full instruction on dangers & precautions
  • Name entered in official register                       • Sufficient operational training
  • Absolute prohibition on women & adolescents             • OR continuous supervision by experienced adult
  • Covers examination, lubrication, belt mounting          • Sec 39(3) bars adolescents from moving machinery
```

---

## 1. Mandatory Fencing of Machinery (Section 63)

Every moving part that poses danger must be shielded by sturdy physical guards:

1. **Prime Movers and Flywheels (`Sec 63(1)(a)`):** Moving parts of engines and connected flywheels must have secure fences.
2. **Water Wheels and Turbines (`Sec 63(1)(b)`):** Head-race and tail-race channels of water turbines need strong guards.
3. **Lathes and Projecting Stock (`Sec 63(1)(c)`):** Bars projecting past a lathe headstock require sturdy guards.
4. **Electrical and Transmission Gear (`Sec 63(1)(d)`):** Generators, motors, and transmission gears must be fenced securely.
5. **Revolving Shafts and Screws (`Sec 63(2)`):** Bolts, keys, and gears on rotating shafts must be encased. This stops loose clothing from catching.
6. **Substantial Construction:** All guards must be sturdy and kept in place while machinery runs.

---

## 2. Work on Machinery in Motion (Section 64)

Sometimes machines must run during inspection, belt mounting, or oiling. Section 64 strictly limits who can do this dangerous work:

1. **Adult Male Workers Only:** Only an adult male worker aged 18 or older may perform this work.
2. **Specialized Training:** The worker must receive special training for servicing moving parts.
3. **Tight-Fitting Clothing:** The employer must supply tight-fitting clothing. The worker must wear it to prevent entanglement in rotating shafts.
4. **Register Entry:** The worker's name must be entered beforehand in the official factory register.
5. **Pulley Belt Limits:** Handling moving belts is permitted only if the belt width is under 15 cm. The belt joint must be laced or flush.
6. **Absolute Bar on Women and Adolescents:** Women and adolescents can never touch moving machine parts (`Sec 64(1)` and `Sec 39(3)`).

## Core Theory 2: Adolescent Protection on Machines (Sections 39(3) & 40)

The law provides special safeguards to protect young workers from industrial hazards:

---

## 3. Absolute Ban on Moving Machinery (Section 39(3))

Section 39(3) creates an unyielding protective rule for adolescents:

1. **Total Prohibition:** No adolescent can clean, lubricate, or adjust any part of machinery while that part is in motion.
2. **Work Between Moving Parts:** No adolescent can work between moving parts of running machinery.
3. **Zero Exceptions:** Prior training, skill certificates, or adult supervision cannot waive this ban.

---

## 4. Regulation of Dangerous Machines (Section 40)

Section 40 governs dangerous machines listed in the government gazette, such as power presses and milling cutters:

1. **Instruction Prerequisite (`Sec 40(1)(a)`):** The adolescent must receive full instruction regarding the dangers of the machine and the required precautions.
2. **Training or Supervision Prerequisite (`Sec 40(1)(b)`):** The adolescent must satisfy at least one of two conditions:
   - Must complete sufficient practical training on the machine.
   - Must work under the adequate supervision of an experienced adult who knows the machine thoroughly.
3. **Gazette Notification (`Sec 40(2)–(3)`):** The Government publishes the official list of dangerous machines and hazardous occupations where adolescents cannot work.

---

## Core Theory 3: Comparative Analysis (Section 64 vs. Section 40 & 39(3))

Comparing these provisions was tested as a 10-mark question in 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2:

### Similarities Between Section 64 and Section 40

1. **Preventive Goal:** Both provisions aim to stop traumatic physical injuries, lost limbs, and fatal shop-floor accidents.
2. **Mandatory Training:** Both sections require safety knowledge and technical instruction before a worker touches high-risk machinery.
3. **Strict Employer Liability:** Both rules place direct legal responsibility on employers. Violations bring criminal penalties under Chapter XIX.
4. **Official Record Keeping:** Both regimes require official documentation and factory registers open to Labour Inspector checks.

### Differences Between Section 64 and Section 40 & 39(3)

| Basis of Comparison | Work on Machinery in Motion (`Sec 64`) | Dangerous Machines & Adolescents (`Sec 40` & `Sec 39(3)`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **BLC Chapter** | Chapter VI (*Safety*) | Chapter III (*Employment of Adolescent Worker*) |
| **Governing Sections** | **Section 64** (general machinery safety) | **Section 40** (dangerous machines) & **Section 39(3)** (moving parts) |
| **Target Worker Class** | **Adult male workers only** ($\ge 18\text{ years}$) | **Adolescent workers** ($14$ to $< 18\text{ years}$) |
| **Eligibility Restrictions** | **Absolute bar** on female workers and adolescents; only certified adult males | Adolescents may operate dangerous machines if trained or supervised |
| **Machine Operating State** | Machinery is **actively running / in motion** during inspection or adjustment | Machine is classified as **dangerous by Government notification**, covering general operation |
| **Clothing Mandate** | Explicit statutory requirement: worker **must wear tight-fitting clothing** | Focuses on danger instruction, operational training, and adult supervision |
| **Supervision Exception** | No exception: supervision cannot replace adult male status or tight clothing | Permissible **under adequate supervision** of an experienced person before training is complete |
| **Prohibition on Moving Parts** | Permitted exclusively for the registered adult male worker | **Section 39(3) strictly bans** adolescents from cleaning or adjusting moving machinery |

## Core Theory 4: Employer and Employee Safety Responsibilities (L07.3)

Workplace safety under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 is a shared legal obligation. This topic appeared in 2023 Final Q.2(c) for 4 marks:

---

## 5. Responsibilities of Employers under Chapter VI

The employer provides the safe physical environment and safety equipment:

1. **Safe Buildings and Machinery (`Sec 61`):** Maintain structurally sound buildings and repair unsafe plants promptly upon Inspector notice.
2. **Fire Safety Systems (`Sec 62`):** Provide emergency staircases, unlocked outward-opening doors, red Bangla signs, fire alarms, and mock drills.
3. **Guarding Machinery (`Sec 63, 67`):** Securely fence all dangerous moving gears and completely encase revolving shafts, set-screws, and keys.
4. **Regulating Work on Running Machines (`Sec 64`):** Permit only registered adult males in tight clothes to handle moving machine parts.
5. **Emergency Power Cut-Offs (`Sec 65`):** Install instant power cut-off switches and belt-shifting gear in every workroom.
6. **Lifting Gear Inspections (`Sec 68, 69`):** Cranes need annual checks. Hoists and lifts need checks every six months by a competent person.
7. **Confined Space and Gas Safety (`Sec 77, 78`):** Supply breathing gear. Test air before workers enter confined spaces with toxic fumes.
8. **Free Supply of PPE (`Sec 78A(1)`):** Supply needed safety gear free of charge. Maintain an official PPE record book.
9. **Worker Safety Training (`Sec 62(7), 78A(3)`):** Teach workers about shop hazards. Train them in emergency evacuation routines.

---

## 6. Responsibilities of Employees under Chapter VI

Workers must act responsibly and follow safety standards on the shop floor:

1. **Mandatory Use of PPE (`Sec 78A(2)`):** Workers must wear and use all personal safety gear supplied by the employer.
2. **Personal Statutory Liability (`Sec 78A(2)`):** The employer may supply protective gear. If the worker fails to wear it, the worker is personally liable under the law.
3. **Active Drill Participation (`Sec 62(7)–(8)`):** Workers must participate in mock fire drills and learn emergency evacuation routines.
4. **Observance of Safe Dress Codes (`Sec 64(1)`):** Wear provided tight clothing. Avoid loose sleeves or scarves near rotating pulleys.
5. **Obedience to Safety Rules (`Sec 77, 78`):** Obey safety orders. Never enter confined spaces without safety ropes and breathing gear.

---

## 7. Analytical Opinion on Shared Safety Liability

Workplace safety cannot succeed through employer investment alone:

- **The Employer's Duty:** Employers control infrastructure, machinery, and equipment. They have the financial duty to buy guards, alarms, and personal safety gear.
- **The Worker's Duty:** Workers operate on the shop floor every day. If workers leave guards off or refuse to wear safety helmets and goggles, accidents happen regardless of employer spending.
- **Why Section 78A Balances Responsibility:** Section 78A strikes an ideal legal balance. Section 78A(1) forces employers to provide safety gear free of charge. Section 78A(2) holds workers personally liable if they choose not to wear it. Real safety requires safe equipment matched with personal worker discipline.

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive & Model Answers)

### 🎯 GQ1 · 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2 · Machinery in Motion vs. Adolescents on Dangerous Machines (10 Marks)

> **Appeared:** 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2 · Section A (CO1) · (10 Marks)

**Question:**
Compare the safety requirements for work on or near machinery in motion with the requirements for dangerous machines involving adolescent workers. What similarities and differences can you identify?

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Issue: Running Machinery vs. Adolescent Worker Protection
The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 sets clear standards to prevent industrial accidents. This question compares two safety frameworks: rules for running machinery versus protections for young workers on dangerous machines.

#### 2. Applicable Sections
1. **Section 64:** *Work on or near machinery in motion* (Chapter VI: Safety).
2. **Section 40:** *Employment of adolescent on dangerous machines* (Chapter III: Employment of Adolescent Worker).
3. **Section 39(3):** *Prohibition of adolescents cleaning or adjusting machinery in motion* (Chapter III: Employment of Adolescent Worker).

#### 3. Comparative Analysis: Similarities and Differences

##### A. Statutory Breakdown
- **Under Section 64:** Running machinery examination, lubrication, or belt mounting requires strict care. Only a specially trained adult male worker may do the work. He must wear tight clothing supplied by the employer. His name must be entered beforehand in the official factory register. Pulley belts must be under 15 cm wide with laced joints. Women and adolescents are strictly barred.
- **Under Section 40:** An adolescent ($14$ to $< 18$ years) cannot operate gazetted dangerous machines without precautions. They must receive danger instructions. They also need sufficient training or continuous supervision by an experienced adult.
- **Under Section 39(3):** Adolescents are strictly prohibited from cleaning, lubricating, or adjusting moving machinery parts. Supervision does not waive this ban.

##### B. Key Similarities
1. **Preventive Goal:** Both provisions prevent catastrophic industrial injuries, mangling, limb loss, and fatalities.
2. **Mandatory Training:** Both require specialized instruction and safety awareness before work begins.
3. **Strict Employer Duty:** Both place legal responsibility on employers, backed by criminal penalties under Chapter XIX.
4. **Registry and Oversight:** Both require documentation available for inspection by Labour Inspectors.

##### C. Key Differences

| Basis of Comparison | Work on Machinery in Motion (`Sec 64`) | Dangerous Machines & Adolescents (`Sec 40` & `Sec 39(3)`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **BLC Chapter** | Chapter VI (*Safety*) | Chapter III (*Employment of Adolescent Worker*) |
| **Governing Sections** | **Section 64** (general safety) | **Section 40** (dangerous machines) & **Section 39(3)** (moving parts) |
| **Target Worker Class** | **Adult male workers only** ($\ge 18\text{ years}$) | **Adolescent workers** (14 to under 18 years) |
| **Eligibility Restrictions** | **Absolute bar** on women and adolescents; only certified adult males | Adolescents may operate dangerous machines if trained or supervised |
| **Machine Operating State** | Machinery is **actively running / in motion** during inspection or adjustment | Machine is classified as **dangerous by Government notification**, covering general operation |
| **Clothing Mandate** | Explicit statutory requirement: worker **must wear tight-fitting clothing** | Focuses on instruction, danger awareness, and operational supervision |
| **Supervision Exception** | No exception: supervision cannot replace adult male status or tight clothing | Permissible **under adequate supervision** of an experienced person before training is complete |
| **Prohibition on Moving Parts** | Permitted exclusively for the designated registered adult male | **Section 39(3) strictly bans** adolescents from cleaning or adjusting moving machinery |

#### 4. Conclusion
Section 64 sets a strict rule for running machinery. Only trained adult men in tight clothing may service it. In contrast, Section 40 sets a training path for adolescents on dangerous machines. Section 39(3) ensures they never touch running parts.

### 🎯 GQ2 · 2023 Final Q.2(c) · Employer and Employee Safety Responsibilities (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** 2023 Final Q.2(c) · Section A (CO1) · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
What responsibilities do employers and employees have regarding workplace safety regulation? Give your opinion.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Issue: Shared Legal Liability in Workplace Safety
Workplace safety regulation divides legal duties between employers and workers to prevent industrial hazards and fatal accidents.

#### 2. Applicable Law
**Chapter VI (Sections 61 to 78A)** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. **Section 78A** (*Personal Safety Equipments*) is the core rule.

#### 3. Division of Responsibilities

##### A. Responsibilities of Employers
1. **Safe Infrastructure (`Sec 61, 72`):** Maintain sound buildings, safe floors, stairs, and clear passageways.
2. **Fire Safety Precautions (`Sec 62`):** Provide emergency stairs, unlocked doors, red Bangla signs, fire alarms, and regular mock fire drills.
3. **Machine Guarding (`Sec 63, 64`):** Fence moving gears securely. Restrict running machine maintenance to trained adult males in tight clothes.
4. **Free Provision of PPE (`Sec 78A(1)`):** Supply needed safety gear free of cost. Maintain an official PPE record book.
5. **Safety Training (`Sec 62(7), 78A(3)`):** Educate workers about workplace hazards and emergency escape routines.

##### B. Responsibilities of Employees
1. **Mandatory PPE Use (`Sec 78A(2)`):** Wear and use all personal safety gear supplied by the employer.
2. **Personal Legal Liability (`Sec 78A(2)`):** The employer may supply safety gear. If a worker fails to use it, the worker faces personal legal liability.
3. **Active Drill Participation (`Sec 62(7)–(8)`):** Attend mock fire drills and learn emergency evacuation routes.
4. **Following Safety Rules (`Sec 64, 77`):** Obey safety orders and wear tight clothes near pulleys. Never enter confined spaces without clearance.

#### 4. Direct Conclusion and Opinion
In my opinion, Section 78A establishes an ideal balance of responsibility. Employers must fund and maintain safe machinery and supply protective gear. But workers must practice personal discipline by wearing supplied gear on the job. Without worker compliance, the finest safety equipment cannot stop accidents. Shared accountability is the only way to ensure true shop-floor safety.

### 🎯 GQ3 · Model Case Study · Unsafe Machine Maintenance and PPE Non-Compliance (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Applied Scenario · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
A factory supervisor instructs a 16-year-old adolescent worker to clean and oil the gears of an active transmission machine. The worker is not given safety goggles or tight-fitting clothes. While oiling the rotating gear, the worker's loose sleeve catches in the mechanism, causing serious arm injuries. Analyze the legal liabilities of the employer and the worker under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Facts and Issues of the Case
A supervisor ordered a 16-year-old adolescent worker to lubricate rotating transmission gears without protective gear or tight clothing. The worker's sleeve caught in the moving gears, resulting in severe injuries. The legal issue is whether the employer violated statutory machinery and adolescent safety provisions and whether the worker shares liability.

#### 2. Applicable Law and Sections
- **Section 39(3):** Absolute ban on adolescents cleaning or lubricating machinery in motion.
- **Section 64(1):** Work on moving machinery restricted to specially trained adult males in tight clothes.
- **Section 78A(1):** Mandatory employer provision of personal safety gear and usage enforcement.
- **Chapter XIX:** Criminal penalties for employer default.

#### 3. Legal Analysis
1. **Violation of Section 39(3):** The injured worker is 16 years old, which classifies him as an adolescent under Section 2(8). Section 39(3) strictly bans adolescents from cleaning, lubricating, or adjusting machinery in motion. Allowing him to touch running gears is an illegal act by management.
2. **Violation of Section 64(1):** Section 64 allows only trained adult males in tight clothes to work on moving machinery. Using a 16-year-old in loose clothes breaks both age and clothing rules.
3. **Violation of Section 78A(1):** The employer failed to provide required personal safety equipment and failed to ensure safe working conditions.
4. **Worker Liability Assessment:** Under Section 78A(2), a worker is liable only when equipment is supplied but not used. Here, the employer never supplied safety gear. Also, the adolescent was ordered into an illegal task. Therefore, the worker bears no contributory liability.

#### 4. Direct Conclusion
The employer is fully liable for gross violations of Section 39(3), Section 64(1), and Section 78A(1). The employer faces criminal fines under Chapter XIX. The employer must also pay injury compensation under Chapter XII.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **Remember the Scope Note:** Shoaib Sir narrowed the final exam to Section 62 fire safety. But machinery questions appeared in 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2 (10 marks) and employer duties appeared in 2023 Final Q.2(c) (4 marks).
2. **Never Confuse Section 39(3) and Section 40:** Section 39(3) is an absolute ban on moving parts with zero exceptions. Section 40 allows dangerous machine work if the adolescent has training or supervision.
3. **State the 5 Conditions of Section 64:** List all five: adult male, trained, tight clothing, registered, and narrow belt.
4. **Highlight Section 78A(2) Worker Liability:** Point out that workers are personally liable if they fail to wear supplied safety gear. This is a favorite exam grading point.
5. **Use the 4-Step Case Framework:** For case questions, always organize into: 1. Facts and Issues, 2. Applicable Law, 3. Legal Analysis, 4. Direct Conclusion.
