## 📝 Key Definitions

> **Unfair Labour Practice:** "Any unfair act or omission that arises between an employer and an employee." (SI Sir Class Notes, Chapter 4)

In simple words, it means any unlawful action that attacks worker rights or abuses management authority.

> **Yellow-Dog Contract:** An agreement where an employer forces a worker to promise not to join a trade union. (BLC 2006, Section 195(a))

Such agreements are completely void and illegal under Bangladesh labour law.

## 🏢 Employer Unfair Labour Practices (Section 195)

Section 195 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 lists 12 specific actions prohibited for employers. No employer, employers' trade union, or agent may commit any of these acts.

### The 12 Statutory Prohibitions

1. **Yellow-Dog Contracts (`Sec 195(a)`):**
   Imposing any condition in an employment contract that limits a worker's right to join a union.
2. **Refusal to Employ (`Sec 195(b)`):**
   Refusing to hire or keep a worker because of union membership or leadership.
3. **Discrimination in Service (`Sec 195(c)`):**
   Discriminating in pay, promotion, or working conditions based on union involvement.
4. **Retaliatory Dismissal and Threats (`Sec 195(d)`):**
   Dismissing, discharging, removing, or threatening a worker for participating in union activities.
5. **Inducement or Bribery (`Sec 195(e)`):**
   Offering money, gifts, or promotions to convince someone to leave union office or avoid joining.
6. **Coercing the CBA (`Sec 195(f)`):**
   Forcing any Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA) officer to sign a deal through threats, confinement, or cuts to utilities.
7. **Interfering in CBA Elections (`Sec 195(g)`):**
   Interfering with or trying to influence secret ballot elections conducted under Section 202.
8. **Recruiting Replacement Workers (`Sec 195(h)`):**
   Hiring new workers during a lawful strike under Section 211. Only an Arbitrator can permit temporary staffing to prevent machine damage.
9. **Ignoring the Participation Committee (`Sec 195(i)`):**
   Deliberately refusing to implement recommendations made by the bipartite Participation Committee.
10. **Refusing Communication with the CBA (`Sec 195(j)`):**
    Failing to reply to letters or notices sent by the CBA about an industrial dispute.
11. **Punitive Transfer of Union Officers (`Sec 195(k)`):**
    Transferring the President, General Secretary, Organizing Secretary, or Treasurer of a trade union without their consent, violating Section 187.
12. **Illegal Lock-Out (`Sec 195(l)`):**
    Starting, continuing, or instigating an illegal lock-out against workers.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 4 Intuitive Clusters for Section 195 (12 Employer Prohibitions)
> Remember all 12 employer prohibitions by grouping them into 4 logical clusters:
> - **1. Attacking Individual Workers (4 acts):** Yellow-dog contract (`a`), refuse to hire (`b`), discriminate in pay/rank (`c`), retaliatory firing (`d`).
> - **2. Subverting Union Structure (3 acts):** Bribery to leave office (`e`), rigging CBA elections (`g`), punitive transfer of key officers (`k`).
> - **3. Undermining Dialogue (3 acts):** Coercing CBA officers (`f`), ignoring Participation Committee (`i`), refusing replies to CBA letters (`j`).
> - **4. Sabotaging Industrial Action (2 acts):** Hiring strike-breakers during lawful strike (`h`), declaring illegal lock-out (`l`).

## 👷 Worker and Union Unfair Labour Practices (Section 196)

Section 196 protects management, non-striking staff, and public property from coercion and violence.

### 1. Union Activities During Working Hours (`Sec 196(1)`)

No worker may engage in trade union activities during working hours without employer permission. But this rule has one key exception. The President or General Secretary of the CBA may attend official dispute meetings. They may also attend conciliation sessions or court proceedings after notifying the employer.

### 2. Prohibited Acts by Workers and Unions (`Sec 196(2)`)

No worker, trade union, or agent shall commit any of these six acts:

1. **Intimidation Regarding Membership (`Sec 196(2)(a)`):**
   Threatening any worker to force them to join, remain in, or quit a trade union.
2. **Inducement via Personal Benefits (`Sec 196(2)(b)`):**
   Offering personal advantages to persuade someone to leave union office or avoid joining.
3. **Coercing Union Dues (`Sec 196(2)(c)`):**
   Forcing a worker to pay or withhold union subscriptions using threats, physical injury, confinement, or cuts to water or power.
4. **Coercing the Employer (`Sec 196(2)(d)`):**
   Forcing an employer to accept demands or sign deals using threats, confinement, assault, eviction, or cuts to utilities.
5. **Illegal Strike and Go-Slow (`Sec 196(2)(e)`):**
   Starting, continuing, or instigating an illegal strike or a deliberate go-slow.
6. **Gherao, Blockade, and Property Damage (`Sec 196(2)(f)`):**
   Resorting to gherao (confinement), blocking transport routes, disrupting communications, or destroying factory property.

### 3. Election Interference (`Sec 196(3)`)

No trade union or agent may interfere in CBA elections through undue influence, threats, impersonation, or bribery.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: The 3-Inward + 3-Outward Split for Section 196(2)
> Remember the 6 worker prohibitions by dividing them into two equal sets:
> - **3 Inward Acts (Against Fellow Workers):**
>   1. Threatening workers on union membership (`(a)`).
>   2. Bribing members to quit office or stay out (`(b)`).
>   3. Forcing dues payments through threats or utility cuts (`(c)`).
> - **3 Outward Acts (Against Management and Factory):**
>   4. Coercing the employer or cutting factory utilities (`(d)`).
>   5. Starting illegal strikes or go-slow actions (`(e)`).
>   6. Staging gherao, blocking transport, or destroying property (`(f)`).

## ⚖️ Statutory Comparison: Section 195 vs. Section 196

| Comparison Dimension | Employer Unfair Practices (`Sec 195`) | Worker and Union Unfair Practices (`Sec 196`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Statute** | Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 195 | Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 196 |
| **Targeted Parties** | Employers, employer associations, and their agents | Individual workers, trade unions, and their agents |
| **Protected Entities** | Workers, union leaders, and the CBA | Enterprise owners, managers, and non-union staff |
| **Total Prohibitions** | 12 specific statutory prohibitions | Working-hour ban, 6 specific acts, and election rule |
| **Banned Coercion** | Forced yellow-dog deals, punitive officer transfers | Physical gherao, utility cuts, go-slows, and riots |
| **Replacement Workers** | Strictly banned during lawful strikes (`Sec 195(h)`) | Not applicable |
| **Statutory Penalties** | Up to 2 years prison, fine up to 10,000 Taka, or both | Worker: up to 1 year or 5,000 Taka; Union: 2 years or 10,000 Taka |

## 🚨 Statutory Penalties for Violations (Section 291)

Violations of Section 195 and Section 196 are criminal offences. The Labour Court hears these cases under Section 291.

### 1. Penalty on Employers (`Sec 291(1)`)

Any employer or person who violates Section 195 faces:
- Imprisonment for up to 2 years, or
- Fine up to 10,000 Taka, or
- Both penalties together.

### 2. Penalty on Individual Workers (`Sec 291(2)`)

Any worker who violates Section 196 faces:
- Imprisonment for up to 1 year, or
- Fine up to 5,000 Taka, or
- Both penalties together.

### 3. Penalty on Trade Unions and Non-Workers (`Sec 291(3)`)

Any trade union or non-worker who violates Section 196 faces:
- Imprisonment for up to 2 years, or
- Fine up to 10,000 Taka, or
- Both penalties together.

### Related Offence Penalties in the Act

- **Illegal Strike or Lock-Out (`Sec 294`):** Up to 1 year prison or 5,000 Taka fine. Workers face 6 months or 200 Taka. Employers face 1 year or 5,000 Taka.
- **Go-Slow (`Sec 296`):** Up to 1 year imprisonment, a fine up to 5,000 Taka, or both.
- **Illegal Lay-Off or Closure (Class Notes):** Up to 1 month prison, 3,000 Taka fine, or both. Also requires 50% basic wage compensation.

## 📊 Applied Impact: Consequences on Employers and Employees

Unfair labour practices break trust and harm both sides of industry.

### 1. Consequences on Employers

- **Criminal Prosecution:** Management faces criminal trials, fines, and up to 2 years imprisonment under Section 291(1).
- **Severe Financial Loss:** Hostility triggers wildcat strikes and production shutdowns. Missing delivery dates leads to heavy contract penalties.
- **Loss of Export Contracts:** International brands and compliance auditors cancel orders when factories violate basic labour laws.
- **High Employee Turnover:** Toxic anti-union workplaces push away skilled engineers and technicians, raising recruitment costs.

### 2. Consequences on Employees

- **Loss of Livelihood:** Retaliatory firings and demotions cut off income for workers and their families.
- **Suppression of Collective Voice:** Bypassing the CBA destroys collective bargaining power. Workers cannot secure fair wages or safe conditions.
- **Workplace Fear and Anxiety:** Threats, secret surveillance, and forced transfers create severe mental stress.
- **Legal Penalties for Worker Misconduct:** Workers committing Section 196 offences face dismissal for misconduct under Section 23. They also face criminal jail terms under Section 291(2).

## 🛡️ Applied Strategy: How Unions Address Unfair Practices

Trade unions must counter employer abuses through legal channels while avoiding illegal actions.

### 1. Statutory and Legal Strategies

- **Labour Court Complaints (`Sec 291`, `Sec 214`):** File criminal cases against managers who violate Section 195. Workers can also seek reinstatement with full back pay under Section 33.
- **Formal Dispute Settlement (`Sec 210`):** The CBA issues a formal dispute notice under Section 210(1). Management must attend bilateral talks within 15 days. If talks fail, government conciliation begins.
- **Reporting to the Director of Labour (`Sec 187`):** Report illegal officer transfers to the Director of Labour. This brings fast administrative action.

### 2. Internal and Organizational Strategies

- **Thorough Evidence Gathering:** Collect written logs, transfer letters, audio recordings, and witness statements. Solid evidence wins Labour Court cases.
- **Using the Participation Committee (`Sec 205`):** Use worker seats on the Participation Committee to resolve grievances early.
- **Engaging International Buyers and Federations:** Partner with national federations and buyer compliance bodies. International brands can pressure factory owners to obey the law.
- **Training Union Members:** Teach members to avoid gherao, violence, and wildcat strikes. Staying peaceful protects the legal immunities granted under Section 197 and Section 198.

### 🎯 GQ1 · Model Question · Employer Unfair Labour Practices under Section 195 (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Prescribed Model Question · Shoaib Sir Exam Blueprint · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
Define unfair labour practice. Explain the actions that constitute unfair labour practices on the part of employers under Section 195 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Full Answer:**

#### GQ1.1 · Definition of Unfair Labour Practice
An unfair labour practice is any unfair act between an employer and employees. It violates statutory labour rights or disrupts factory peace.

#### GQ1.2 · Employer Unfair Labour Practices (Section 195)
Under Section 195 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, no employer, employers' trade union, or agent shall:
1. **Yellow-Dog Contracts (`Sec 195(a)`):** Require a worker to promise not to join or remain in a trade union.
2. **Refusal to Employ (`Sec 195(b)`):** Refuse to employ or dismiss any worker based on union membership.
3. **Discrimination in Service (`Sec 195(c)`):** Discriminate in wages, promotion, or service conditions due to union activity.
4. **Retaliatory Firing and Threats (`Sec 195(d)`):** Dismiss, discharge, or threaten a worker for union participation or expansion.
5. **Inducement or Bribery (`Sec 195(e)`):** Offer benefits or money to persuade someone to leave union office or avoid joining.
6. **Coercing the CBA (`Sec 195(f)`):** Force a CBA officer to sign a deal through threats, assault, or utility cuts.
7. **Interfering in CBA Elections (`Sec 195(g)`):** Interfere with or influence the secret ballot election held under Section 202.
8. **Recruiting Replacement Workers (`Sec 195(h)`):** Hire new workers during a lawful strike under Section 211 without Arbitrator approval.
9. **Ignoring the Participation Committee (`Sec 195(i)`):** Deliberately fail to implement recommendations from the Participation Committee.
10. **Refusing Communication with the CBA (`Sec 195(j)`):** Fail to answer official letters sent by the CBA regarding an industrial dispute.
11. **Punitive Transfer of Union Officers (`Sec 195(k)`):** Transfer key union officers without consent (`Sec 187`). This protects the President, Secretary, and Treasurer.
12. **Illegal Lock-Out (`Sec 195(l)`):** Start, continue, or instigate an illegal lock-out.

### 🎯 GQ3 · Model Case Study · Applied Case Study on Illegal Strike, Gherao, and Coercion (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** Applied Problem Scenario · Shoaib Sir Exam Blueprint · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
During wage negotiations at an electronics factory, local trade union leaders demand an immediate 35% wage increase. They give no strike notice and hold no secret ballot under Section 211. Instead, they cut electricity to the cleanrooms and block factory gates. They confine the General Manager for 12 hours (gherao) until he signs a wage agreement. They also threaten non-striking technicians with physical assault if they enter the plant.

Analyze the legality of these actions under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. Also determine the legal liabilities of the union and participating workers.

**Full Answer:**

#### GQ3.1 · Facts of the Case
Union leaders cut cleanroom electricity, blocked factory gates, and confined the General Manager for 12 hours. They forced him to sign a wage agreement without serving strike notice. They also threatened non-striking technicians with physical violence.

#### GQ3.2 · Applicable Law and Statutory Sections
- **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006:**
  - **Section 196(2)(a):** Intimidating workers regarding union activities or work attendance.
  - **Section 196(2)(d):** Forcing an employer to agree to demands through confinement or utility cuts.
  - **Section 196(2)(e):** Commencing or instigating an illegal strike.
  - **Section 196(2)(f):** Staging gherao, blocking transport, and damaging property.
  - **Section 211:** Mandatory strike notice (7 to 14 days) and secret ballot requirement.
  - **Section 291(2)–(3):** Criminal penalties for worker unfair labour practices.
  - **Section 23(4)(g):** Dismissal for gross misconduct due to illegal strikes and riotous acts.
- **The Contract Act, 1872:**
  - **Section 15 & Section 19:** Agreements signed under coercion are voidable by the coerced party.

#### GQ3.3 · Legal Analysis
1. **Illegal Strike (`Sec 211` & `Sec 196(2)(e)`):** The strike is illegal. Workers served no notice and held no secret ballot. Instigating an illegal strike violates Section 196(2)(e).
2. **Coercing Employer and Utility Cuts (`Sec 196(2)(d)`):** Cutting cleanroom power and confining the manager violates Section 196(2)(d). The law strictly bans forced agreements via confinement or utility cuts.
3. **Unlawful Gherao and Blockade (`Sec 196(2)(f)`):** Blocking factory gates and confining managers constitutes gherao. It directly violates Section 196(2)(f).
4. **Intimidation of Workers (`Sec 196(2)(a)`):** Threatening non-striking technicians with physical violence violates Section 196(2)(a).
5. **Invalidity of Agreement:** The General Manager signed under criminal confinement. Under Section 19 of the Contract Act 1872, coercion makes an agreement voidable. Under Section 196 of the Labour Act, this forced agreement has zero legal effect.
6. **Statutory Penalties and Disciplinary Action:**
   - Participating workers face up to 1 year in prison, a 5,000 Taka fine, or both (`Sec 291(2)`).
   - Union leaders face up to 2 years in prison, a 10,000 Taka fine, or both (`Sec 291(3)`).
   - Management can initiate disciplinary proceedings under Section 24 and dismiss guilty workers under Section 23(4)(g).

#### GQ3.4 · Direct Conclusion on Problem Scenario
All actions by the union leaders and workers violate Section 196. The signed wage agreement is legally void. Leaders and workers face criminal penalties under Section 291 and lawful dismissal for gross misconduct under Section 23.

**Question:**
State the statutory penalties for unfair labour practices under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Full Answer:**

Under **Section 291** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, unfair labour practices carry criminal penalties:
1. **Employers (`Sec 291(1)`):** Anyone violating Section 195 faces prison up to 2 years, fine up to 10,000 Taka, or both.

### 🎯 GQ5 · 2023 Final Q.4(b) · Consequences of Unfair Labour Practices on Employers and Employees (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** 2023 Final Exam · Section A · CO1 · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
Evaluate the potential consequences of unfair labor practices on both employers and employees.

**Full Answer:**

#### GQ5.1 · Problem Statement
Unfair labour practices destroy mutual trust in an enterprise. The core issue is evaluating how labour right violations damage operations, finance, and worker security.

#### GQ5.2 · Applicable Law and Sections for ULP Consequences
- **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006:**
  - **Section 195:** Prohibited employer unfair labour practices.
  - **Section 196:** Prohibited worker and union unfair labour practices.
  - **Section 291:** Criminal penalties for unfair labour practices.
  - **Section 23:** Disciplinary action and dismissal for misconduct.

#### GQ5.3 · Evaluation of Consequences

##### (a) Consequences on Employers
1. **Criminal Prosecution and Penalties:** Managers violating Section 195 face up to 2 years imprisonment and fines under Section 291(1).
2. **Severe Financial and Operational Losses:** Hostility sparks wildcat strikes, work stoppages, and line shutdowns. Missed delivery dates trigger heavy breach-of-contract penalties.
3. **Loss of Export Orders and Certifications:** Global buyers and audit agencies cancel purchase contracts when factories violate basic labour rights.
4. **High Employee Turnover:** Toxic anti-union workplaces drive away skilled engineers and technicians, increasing training and rehiring expenses.

##### (b) Consequences on Employees
1. **Loss of Livelihood and Earnings:** Retaliatory firings or demotions under Section 195(d) leave workers without income.
2. **Loss of Collective Bargaining Voice:** Bypassing the CBA destroys workers' collective power. Workers cannot secure fair wages or safe working conditions.
3. **Workplace Fear and Stress:** Threats, unlawful transfers, and surveillance cause chronic mental stress.
4. **Legal Penalties for Worker Violations:** Workers committing Section 196 offences face dismissal for gross misconduct under Section 23. They also face up to 1 year imprisonment under Section 291(2).

#### GQ5.4 · Direct Conclusion on ULP Consequences
Unfair labour practices cause heavy mutual damage. Employers face criminal trials, financial losses, and cancelled contracts. Workers suffer lost wages, unsafe conditions, and job loss. Strict compliance with Section 195 and Section 196 is essential for industrial stability.

### 🎯 GQ6 · 2023 Final Q.4(c) · Effective Strategies for Unions to Address Unfair Labour Practices (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** 2023 Final Exam · Section A · CO1 · (04 Marks)

**Question:**
Propose strategies that unions can employ to address unfair labor practices effectively.

**Full Answer:**

#### GQ6.1 · Problem Statement and Strategic Context
Trade unions often face employer retaliation, anti-union dismissals, and refusal to negotiate. Unions require lawful legal and organizational strategies to protect worker rights without breaking the law.

#### GQ6.2 · Applicable Law and Sections for Union Action
- **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006:**
  - **Section 195:** Prohibited employer unfair labour practices.
  - **Section 202 & 202(24):** Exclusive collective bargaining rights of the CBA.
  - **Section 205:** Bipartite Participation Committee.
  - **Section 209–210:** Statutory industrial dispute settlement procedures.
  - **Section 214 & Section 291:** Labour Court jurisdiction and criminal penalties.

#### GQ6.3 · Proposed Strategies for Trade Unions

##### (a) Statutory and Legal Strategies
1. **Filing Complaints Before Labour Court (`Sec 214`, `Sec 291`):** The CBA files criminal charges against managers under Section 291(1). The court can impose up to 2 years imprisonment. Also, unlawfully dismissed workers can apply for reinstatement with back pay under Section 33.
2. **Invoking Formal Dispute Settlement (`Sec 210`):** The CBA can issue a written dispute notice under Section 210(1). Management must hold bilateral talks within 15 days. If talks fail, official government conciliation begins.
3. **Reporting to Regulators:** Report illegal officer transfers under Section 187 to the Director of Labour. Also report unlawful lock-outs to factory inspectors for fast intervention.

##### (b) Internal and Organizational Strategies
1. **Systematic Gathering of Evidence:** Keep written logs, copies of punitive transfer letters, audio recordings, and witness statements. Solid evidence ensures fast relief in Labour Court.
2. **Using the Participation Committee (`Sec 205`):** Use worker representatives on the Participation Committee to settle workplace grievances before disputes escalate.
3. **Engaging National Federations and Global Buyers (`Sec 200`):** Affiliate with national labour federations. Report labour violations to international buyer compliance bodies. Buyer audit pressure often forces owners to obey the law quickly.
4. **Training Members on Lawful Conduct:** Educate members on Section 196 restrictions. Avoiding gherao, violence, and illegal strikes preserves the statutory immunities granted under Section 197 and Section 198.

#### GQ6.4 · Direct Conclusion on Union Strategies
Unions can counter unfair labour practices through Labour Court cases under Section 291. They must maintain clear documentary evidence and report abuses to international buyers. Remaining disciplined and avoiding illegal strikes protects their statutory immunities.

### 🎯 GQ7 · 2024 Final Q.8(b) · Potential Rating-Scale Appraisal Problems and Remedies (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** 2024 Final Exam · Section B · Q.8(b) · (04 Marks) *(Exam Routing: Set under CO2 M06.5; answered in full inline here for complete self-sufficiency, and cross-referenced in [M06.2](M06.2_Performance_Appraisal_Methods_and_Rating_Errors.md))*

**Question:**
State potential rating-scale appraisal problems and suggest ways to avoid the problems.

**Full Answer:**

#### GQ7.1 · Potential Rating-Scale Appraisal Problems (H-L-S-C-R-C)

Rating scales often suffer from six common evaluator errors:

1. **Halo / Horn Effect:** A supervisor lets one good trait (halo) or bad trait (horn) bias all ratings.
2. **Leniency Error:** An easy grader rates all subordinates too high to avoid conflict or boost team grades.
3. **Strictness Error:** A harsh evaluator gives everyone unusually low ratings, holding impossible standards.
4. **Central Tendency:** A rater marks everyone near average to play safe and avoid explaining scores.
5. **Recency Error:** The evaluator focuses only on recent weeks instead of the full appraisal year.
6. **Contrast Error:** A manager compares an employee against the previous person interviewed or rated rather than against objective job standards.

#### GQ7.2 · Managerial Remedies to Avoid Appraisal Problems

Organizations use five concrete remedies to avoid rating errors:

1. **Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales (BARS):** Anchor numerical rating points to concrete, observable job behaviors rather than vague adjectives.
2. **Multiple Evaluators (360-Degree Feedback):** Collect feedback from supervisors, peers, subordinates, and clients to balance single-rater bias.
3. **Rater Training:** Train evaluators to spot and prevent halo, central tendency, and leniency biases before appraisal cycles.
4. **Critical Incident Diaries:** Require supervisors to keep continuous written logs of significant employee successes and failures across the full year.
5. **Higher-Level Review:** Human Resources and department heads review rating distributions to catch and correct harsh or lenient raters.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **State Section Numbers Accurately:** Always cite **Section 195** for employers, **Section 196** for workers, and **Section 291** for criminal penalties. Shoaib Sir deducts marks for missing or wrong section numbers.
2. **Do Not Forget Key Officer Transfers:** Under Section 195(k) and Section 187, employers cannot transfer four key officers without consent. These are the President, General Secretary, Organizing Secretary, and Treasurer.
3. **Remember Replacement Worker Restrictions:** Employers cannot hire replacement workers during a lawful strike under Section 195(h). Only an Arbitrator can permit temporary staffing to prevent machine damage.
4. **Use the 4-Step Case Framework:** For applied questions or scenarios, always use: **1. Facts of the Case -> 2. Applicable Law & Section -> 3. Legal Analysis -> 4. Direct Conclusion**.
5. **Differentiate Penalties Clearly:** Employers face up to 2 years or 10,000 Taka (`Sec 291(1)`). Individual workers face up to 1 year or 5,000 Taka (`Sec 291(2)`). Trade unions face up to 2 years or 10,000 Taka (`Sec 291(3)`).
