# L09.1: Termination of Employment (Sections 20 to 27)

> **Section:** Section A (CO1 · Legal Issues · Shoaib Islam Sir) | **Priority:** 🟡 Medium (⭐ Backup Case Study Topic / BLC Chapter II) | **Exam Frequency:** Flagged in Raidah Classnotes §15 as a prospective 10-Mark Applied Case Study topic
> **Subtopics Covered:** `L09.1` (Retrenchment and Seniority Rule, Sec 20–21), `L09.2` (Discharge for Incapacity, Sec 22), `L09.3` (Dismissal for Misconduct and Inquiry, Sec 23–24), `L09.4` (Simple Termination by Employer, Sec 26), `L09.5` (Resignation by Worker, Sec 27)
> **Sources:** Bangladesh Labour Act 2006 (Chapter II), Raidah Class Notes (§15), Topicwise Answer L09

## Why This Topic Matters

Termination rules govern how an employment relationship ends under Bangladesh law. They protect workers from unfair job loss. They also give employers legal paths to handle surplus staff or serious misconduct. Shoaib Sir highlighted these sections for a potential 10-mark applied case study. You must know the exact notice periods, compensation rates, and disciplinary steps. Stating the wrong section number or mixing up notice days will lose marks fast.

---

## 📝 Key Definitions

> **Retrenchment [Section 2(11)]:** "retrenchment means the termination of services of workers by the employer on the ground of redundancy;" (The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, Section 2(11))

*Plain English:* Retrenchment means letting workers go because their jobs are no longer needed. It is not a punishment.

> **Discharge [Section 2(17)]:** "discharge means the termination of service of a worker by the employer for reasons of physical or mental incapacity or continued ill-health;" (The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, Section 2(17))

*Plain English:* Discharge means releasing a worker who can no longer work due to illness or injury. A registered doctor must certify the condition.

> **Dismissal [Section 2(39)]:** "dismissal means the termination of service of a worker by the employer for misconduct;" (The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, Section 2(39))

*Plain English:* Dismissal is a severe punishment for serious wrongdoing. It requires a formal inquiry before any action.

## Five Modes of Separation: Master Overview

Chapter II of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 sets out five distinct ways an employment contract ends:

1. **Retrenchment (Section 20):** Ending service because a job post is redundant.
2. **Discharge (Section 22):** Releasing a worker due to certified physical or mental incapacity.
3. **Dismissal (Sections 23 and 24):** Firing a worker for proven misconduct or criminal conviction.
4. **Simple Termination by Employer (Section 26):** Ending service by giving written notice without citing misconduct.
5. **Resignation by Worker (Section 27):** Voluntary exit initiated by the worker upon giving written notice.

> [!tip] Memory Hook 1: Notice Timeline Ladder
> Remember the statutory notice periods using this simple ladder:
> - **7 days:** Worker submits written explanation for alleged misconduct [`Sec 24(1)(b)`].
> - **14 days:** Notice for temporary daily-rated workers [`Sec 26(2)(b)` and `Sec 27(2)(b)`].
> - **30 days:** Retrenchment notice [`Sec 20(2)(a)`]. Also for temporary monthly workers [`Sec 26(2)(a)`].
> - **60 days:** Resignation notice by permanent workers [`Sec 27(1)`]. Also simple termination for permanent daily workers [`Sec 26(1)(b)`].
> - **120 days:** Simple termination notice by employer for permanent monthly workers [`Sec 26(1)(a)`].

## Retrenchment and Re-Employment (Sections 20 and 21)

Retrenchment happens when an employer downsizes due to automation, business slowdown, or closure of a department.

### Statutory Conditions for Retrenchment [Section 20(2)]

If a worker completes at least 1 continuous year of service, the employer must meet these four requirements:

1. **One Month Written Notice [`Sec 20(2)(a)`]:** The employer must give 1 month notice in writing stating the redundancy reasons. The employer can also pay 1 month wages in place of notice.
2. **Notice Copies [`Sec 20(2)(b)`]:** The employer must send copies of the notice to the Chief Inspector. A copy must also go to the Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA).
3. **Severance Pay [`Sec 20(2)(c)`]:** The employer must pay 30 days' wages for every completed year of service. Or the employer pays gratuity, whichever is higher.
4. **Special Lay-off Rule [`Sec 20(3)`]:** Suppose a worker is laid off past 45 days under Section 16(7). No 1-month notice is needed. But the employer must pay 15 extra days' wages on top of severance.

### Principle of Seniority: The LIFO Rule [Section 20(4)]

The employer must follow the "Last In, First Out" (LIFO) rule within the same job category. The employer must retrench the most recently hired person first. An employer cannot keep a junior technician while letting a senior technician go, unless a written contract states otherwise.

### Priority in Re-Employment [Section 21]

If the employer hires staff for that category within 1 year, retrenched workers must receive notice. The employer must give them first preference. If multiple retrenched workers apply, older workers get priority based on their past seniority.

## Discharge from Service (Section 22)

Discharge frees an employer from keeping a worker who cannot physically perform their duties.

### Grounds and Legal Requirements [Section 22(1)]

A worker may be discharged only for:
- Physical incapacity, or
- Mental incapacity, or
- Continued ill-health.

A registered medical doctor must examine the worker and certify the condition in writing.

### Statutory Compensation [Section 22(2)]

If the discharged worker completed at least 1 year of continuous service, the employer must pay:
- 30 days' wages for every completed year of service, or
- Gratuity, whichever is higher.

### How Discharge Differs from Dismissal

- **No Punishment:** Discharge carries no blame or moral fault. Dismissal punishes wrongdoing.
- **No Inquiry:** Discharge needs only a medical certificate. Dismissal requires a formal charge-sheet and inquiry committee.
- **Full Severance:** A discharged worker receives full statutory compensation. A dismissed worker may lose all compensation.

## Dismissal for Misconduct and Domestic Inquiry (Sections 23 and 24)

Dismissal is the most severe penalty in labour law. Because it damages a worker's livelihood and reputation, the law demands strict due process.

### Grounds for Dismissal [Section 23(1)]

A worker may be dismissed without notice or wages in place of notice if the worker:
1. Is convicted of a criminal offence by a court, or
2. Is found guilty of misconduct after an inquiry under Section 24.

### The 10 Acts of Misconduct [Section 23(4)]

The law defines ten specific acts as misconduct:
1. Willful disobedience to lawful or reasonable orders of a supervisor.
2. Theft, fraud, misappropriation, or dishonesty regarding employer property or business.
3. Taking or giving bribes.
4. Habitual absence without leave, or unexcused absence for more than 10 consecutive days.
5. Habitual late attendance.
6. Habitual breach of any applicable law, rule, or regulation.
7. Riotous behavior, disorder, arson, or breakage in the establishment.
8. Habitual negligence in work.
9. Habitual breach of discipline or conduct rules approved by the Chief Inspector.
10. Damaging, altering, or forging official records of the employer.

### Alternative Lesser Punishments [Section 23(2)]

Under extenuating circumstances, an employer may choose a lighter punishment instead of dismissal:
- Removal from service.
- Demotion to a lower post, grade, or pay scale for up to 1 year.
- Stoppage of promotion for up to 1 year.
- Withholding annual increment for up to 1 year.
- Imposing a fine under Section 25 (max 1/10 of wage period earnings).
- Suspension without wages for up to 7 days.
- Formal warning or censure.

### Severance Compensation under Dismissal [Section 23(3)]

The 2013 amendment set specific compensation rules for dismissed workers:
- **General Misconduct:** A worker with $\ge 1$ year of service receives 15 days' wages per completed year.
- **Severe Misconduct (Zero Compensation):** If dismissed for theft, fraud, or riot [`Sec 23(4)(b), (g)`], the worker gets zero compensation. But the worker still receives earned wages and provident fund dues.

### Mandatory 5-Step Inquiry Procedure [Section 24]

An employer cannot dismiss a worker without following this strict procedure:

```text
Step 1: Written Charge-Sheet (Sec 24(1)(a))
    │
    ▼
Step 2: 7-Day Explanation Period (Sec 24(1)(b))
    │
    ▼
Step 3: Personal Hearing & Representation (Sec 24(1)(c), (4))
    │
    ▼
Step 4: Domestic Inquiry Committee (Sec 24(1)(d))
    │   (Equal reps of employer and worker; concluded within 60 days)
    ▼
Step 5: Managerial Approval of Order (Sec 24(1)(e))
```

1. **Written Allegation [`Sec 24(1)(a)`]:** The employer records the exact charge in writing.
2. **Written Explanation Notice [`Sec 24(1)(b)`]:** The employer serves the charge-sheet. The worker receives at least 7 days to give a written reply.
3. **Personal Hearing [`Sec 24(1)(c)`]:** The worker gets a fair chance to speak. The worker may choose a fellow employee to help in the defense [`Sec 24(4)`].
4. **Bipartite Inquiry Committee [`Sec 24(1)(d)`]:** An inquiry committee investigates the charges. It must include an equal number of employer and worker representatives. The inquiry must finish within 60 days.
5. **Managerial Approval [`Sec 24(1)(e)`]:** The employer or factory manager must formally approve the final order.

### Suspension Pending Inquiry [Section 24(2)]

The employer may suspend the accused worker during the inquiry for up to 60 days. During suspension:
- The worker receives a subsistence allowance equal to half of basic wages.
- The worker receives housing, medical, and other allowances in full.
- If the committee clears the worker, the company pays full back wages with adjustments.

> [!tip] Memory Hook 2: The 5-Step Defense Chain
> Remember `C-E-H-I-A`:
> - **C**harge-sheet in writing [`Sec 24(1)(a)`]
> - **E**xplanation within 7 days [`Sec 24(1)(b)`]
> - **H**earing with coworker help [`Sec 24(1)(c)`]
> - **I**nquiry committee in 60 days [`Sec 24(1)(d)`]
> - **A**pproval by employer [`Sec 24(1)(e)`]

## Simple Termination by Employer upon Notice (Section 26)

Section 26 allows an employer to end employment without proving misconduct or showing economic redundancy.

### Required Notice Periods [Section 26(1), (2)]

| Worker Status | Rating Basis | Notice Period Required |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Permanent Worker** | Monthly-rated | **120 days** in writing |
| **Permanent Worker** | Daily or piece-rated | **60 days** in writing |
| **Temporary Worker** | Monthly-rated | **30 days** in writing |
| **Temporary Worker** | Daily or piece-rated | **14 days** in writing |

### Wages in Lieu and Compensation

1. **Wages in Lieu of Notice [`Sec 26(3)`]:** The employer can terminate immediately by paying wages for the notice days.
2. **Service Compensation [`Sec 26(4)`]:** The employer must pay 30 days' wages for each completed year of service to permanent workers. Or the employer pays gratuity, whichever is higher.
3. **Additional Dues:** This compensation comes on top of earned leave encashment and provident fund savings.

## Termination by Worker: Resignation (Section 27)

A worker has the legal right to resign voluntarily by giving written notice.

### Resignation Notice Periods [Section 27(1), (2)]

- **Permanent Worker:** Must give **60 days** written notice.
- **Temporary Monthly Worker:** Must give **30 days** written notice.
- **Temporary Daily Worker:** Must give **14 days** written notice.
- **Pay in Place of Notice [`Sec 27(3)`]:** The worker can leave immediately by paying wages for the notice period.

### Statutory Service Benefits for Resigning Workers [Section 27(4)]

Permanent workers who resign receive statutory compensation based on years of continuous service:

- **Less than 5 years of service:** No service compensation. The worker receives only unpaid wages and accrued leave money.
- **5 to 10 years of service:** The employer pays **14 days' wages** for each completed year of service.
- **10 or more years of service:** The employer pays **30 days' wages** per completed year of service, or gratuity.

### Deemed Resignation for Unexcused Absence [Section 27(3A)]

If a worker stays absent without permission for more than 10 consecutive days:
1. The employer sends a notice giving the worker 10 days to explain and return.
2. If the worker does not reply, the employer sends a second notice giving 7 more days to defend.
3. If the worker still fails to return, the worker is deemed released from the first day of absence.

> [!tip] Memory Hook 3: The 30 vs 14 Days Severance Rule
> Quick severance pay guide:
> - **Retrenchment (`Sec 20`):** 30 days' wages per year ($\ge 1$ yr service).
> - **Discharge (`Sec 22`):** 30 days' wages per year ($\ge 1$ yr service).
> - **Employer Termination (`Sec 26`):** 30 days' wages per year (permanent worker).
> - **Resignation (`Sec 27`):** 14 days' wages per year (5 to 10 yrs); 30 days' wages per year ($\ge 10$ yrs).
> - **General Misconduct Dismissal (`Sec 23`):** 15 days' wages per year.
> - **Theft / Riot Dismissal (`Sec 23`):** 0 days' wages.

## Terminal Safeguards and Final Settlement (Sections 29 to 32)

1. **Provident Fund Protection (Section 29):** An employer cannot deny provident fund benefits, including company contributions, regardless of how employment ends.
2. **Final Payment Deadline (Section 30):** The employer must pay all settlement dues within **30 working days** from the separation date.
3. **Service Certificate (Section 31):** Every worker has the right to receive a service certificate upon leaving the job.
4. **Housing Vacation (Section 32):** The worker must leave company housing within 60 days. But the employer cannot evict the worker before clearing all final dues.

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive & Model Answers)

### 🎯 GQ1 · Model Question 1 · Retrenchment Definition, Conditions, and Seniority (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** BLC Chapter II Core Model Question · (04 Marks)

**Question:**  
Define retrenchment under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. Explain the statutory conditions, compensation rules, and the principle of seniority under Section 20.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Statutory Definition
Under **Section 2(11)** of the **BLC 2006**, retrenchment means ending workers' services on the ground of redundancy. It is an economic separation, not a punishment.

#### 2. Statutory Conditions under Section 20
If a worker completes at least 1 year of continuous service, the employer must fulfill three rules:
- **Written Notice [`Sec 20(2)(a)`]:** Give 1 month written notice stating redundancy reasons. Or pay 1 month wages in place of notice.
- **Notice to Authorities [`Sec 20(2)(b)`]:** Send notice copies to the Chief Inspector and the registered Collective Bargaining Agent (CBA).
- **Severance Pay [`Sec 20(2)(c)`]:** Pay 30 days' wages for each completed year of service, or gratuity, whichever is higher.

#### 3. Principle of Seniority (LIFO Rule)
Under **Section 20(4)**, the employer must follow the "Last In, First Out" (LIFO) rule within that job category. The employer must retrench the newest hire first.

#### 4. Re-employment Preference
Under **Section 21**, an employer rehiring in that category within 1 year must notify retrenched workers. Applicants get first hiring priority based on past seniority.

### 🎯 GQ2 · Model Question 2 · Master Comparison of Five Separation Modes (05 Marks)

> **Appeared:** BLC Chapter II Core Master Question · (05 Marks)

**Question:**  
Distinguish among retrenchment, discharge, dismissal, termination by notice, and resignation under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. Present a comparison matrix.

**Full Answer:**

The **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006** provides five distinct modes of ending employment under Chapter II:

#### Master Separation Comparison Matrix

| Separation Mode | BLC Section | Primary Cause or Ground | Mandatory Notice Period | Statutory Compensation Rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Retrenchment** | **Sec 20** | Redundancy or surplus labor force | 1 month notice (or pay in lieu) | 30 days' wages per completed year of service |
| **Discharge** | **Sec 22** | Certified physical or mental incapacity | No notice period needed | 30 days' wages per year (if service $\ge 1$ year) |
| **Dismissal** | **Sec 23–24** | Proven misconduct or criminal conviction | No notice (needs formal domestic inquiry) | Zero pay for theft/riot; 15 days/yr for other acts |
| **Termination by Notice** | **Sec 26** | Employer termination without citing cause | 120 days (monthly); 60 days (daily-rated) | 30 days' wages per completed year of service |
| **Resignation** | **Sec 27** | Voluntary separation by the worker | 60 days written notice (permanent) | 14 days/yr (5–10 yrs); 30 days/yr ($\ge 10$ yrs) |

#### Key Distinctions
1. **Punitive vs. Non-Punitive:** Dismissal is punitive and requires an inquiry committee. The other four modes carry no misconduct stigma.
2. **Medical Basis:** Discharge applies strictly to health unfitness certified by a doctor.
3. **Notice Length:** Employer termination under Section 26 requires the longest notice (120 days). Retrenchment needs 30 days. Dismissal needs none.

### 🎯 GQ3 · Model Question 3 · Discharge from Service under Section 22 (03 Marks)

> **Appeared:** BLC Chapter II Core Model Question · (03 Marks)

**Question:**  
Explain the grounds and legal consequences of discharge from service under Section 22 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. How does discharge differ from dismissal?

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Lawful Grounds [Section 22(1)]
An employer may discharge a worker solely for:
- Physical incapacity,
- Mental incapacity, or
- Continued ill-health.

A registered medical practitioner must examine the worker and certify the condition.

#### 2. Statutory Compensation [Section 22(2)]
If the worker completed at least 1 year of continuous service, the employer must pay:
- 30 days' wages for every completed year of service, or
- Gratuity, whichever is higher.

#### 3. Core Differences between Discharge and Dismissal
- **Nature:** Discharge is non-punitive. Dismissal is a disciplinary penalty for misconduct.
- **Due Process:** Discharge requires only a medical certificate. Dismissal requires a formal charge-sheet, written explanation, and inquiry committee under Section 24.
- **Severance Pay:** Discharged workers receive full severance (30 days' wages/year). Dismissed workers get zero pay for theft or riot, and only 15 days' wages/year for other misconduct.

### 🎯 GQ4 · Model Question 4 · Misconduct and Domestic Inquiry Procedure (04 Marks)

> **Appeared:** BLC Chapter II Core Model Question · (04 Marks)

**Question:**  
What acts constitute misconduct under Section 23 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006? Detail the mandatory inquiry procedure prescribed under Section 24.

**Full Answer:**

#### 1. Acts of Misconduct [Section 23(4)]
Under **Section 23(4)**, misconduct includes:
- Willful disobedience to lawful orders.
- Theft, fraud, misappropriation, or dishonesty with company property.
- Taking or offering bribes.
- Habitual absence without leave, or unexcused absence exceeding 10 consecutive days.
- Habitual late attendance.
- Riotous behavior, disorder, arson, or breakage in the workplace.
- Habitual negligence of work duties.
- Damaging, altering, or forging official records.

#### 2. Statutory Inquiry Procedure [Section 24]
No dismissal order is lawful unless the employer completes these five steps:
1. **Written Charge-Sheet [`Sec 24(1)(a)`]:** Record the specific allegation in writing.
2. **7-Day Explanation Period [`Sec 24(1)(b)`]:** Give the worker the charge-sheet and at least 7 days to reply in writing.
3. **Personal Hearing [`Sec 24(1)(c)`]:** Give the worker an opportunity to be heard. The worker may choose a fellow employee to help in the defense [`Sec 24(4)`].
4. **Bipartite Inquiry Committee [`Sec 24(1)(d)`]:** Form an inquiry committee with equal employer and worker representatives. The inquiry must finish within 60 days.
5. **Managerial Approval [`Sec 24(1)(e)`]:** The employer or factory manager must formally approve the dismissal order.

#### 3. Suspension Pending Inquiry [Section 24(2)]
The employer may suspend the worker for up to 60 days. The employer must pay a subsistence allowance of half basic wages plus full housing and medical allowances.

**Question:**  
What are the notice periods and compensation rules for employer termination under Section 26 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006?

#### 2. Compensation and Benefits

- **Service Compensation [`Sec 26(4)`]:** Permanent workers receive 30 days' wages per completed year, or gratuity.
- **Additional Payments:** The worker also receives unavailed annual leave pay and full provident fund dues.

### 🎯 GQ6 · Model Question 6 · Resignation by Worker under Section 27 (03 Marks)

> **Appeared:** BLC Chapter II Core Model Question · (03 Marks)

**Question:**  
Explain the legal rules governing resignation by a worker under Section 27 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. What compensation is payable for different lengths of service?

**Full Answer:**

Under **Section 27** of the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006**, a worker may resign voluntarily.

#### 1. Notice Periods [Section 27(1), (2)]
- **Permanent Worker:** Must give **60 days** written notice.
- **Temporary Monthly Worker:** Must give **30 days** written notice.
- **Temporary Daily Worker:** Must give **14 days** written notice.
- **Payment in Lieu [`Sec 27(3)`]:** A worker may leave immediately by paying wages for the notice days.

#### 2. Statutory Service Compensation for Permanent Workers [Section 27(4)]
- **Under 5 years of service:** No service compensation. The worker receives only unpaid wages and accrued leave money.
- **5 to 10 years of service:** The employer pays **14 days' wages** for each completed year of service.
- **10 or more years of service:** The employer pays **30 days' wages** per completed year of service, or gratuity.

#### 3. Unexcused Absence and Deemed Resignation [Section 27(3A)]
A worker absent without permission for over 10 consecutive days receives a 10-day notice to explain and rejoin. If the worker fails to reply, the employer issues a final 7-day defense notice. Continued failure results in deemed release from service.

### 🎯 GQ7 · Model Case Study · Applied Termination Scenario at Apex Telecommunications (10 Marks)

> **Appeared:** High-Yield Applied Case Study Blueprint · Section A · (10 Marks)

**Case Scenario:**  
*Apex Telecommunications Ltd. operates an assembly and testing facility in Rajshahi. Due to automation, the company cuts 15 technician jobs. Rafiq has served 6 years as a Senior Assembly Technician. In contrast, Tanvir joined only 14 months ago for the same role. Management abruptly dismisses Rafiq without notice, citing cost reduction, while retaining Tanvir. On the same day, a supervisor accuses worker Karim of theft. The supervisor dismisses Karim on the spot without a charge-sheet or inquiry. Finally, Farhana, a software technician, resigns voluntarily after 8 continuous years of service.*

**Questions:**  
(i) Evaluate the legality of Rafiq's termination. Calculate the notice period and statutory compensation owed to him under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. **[03 Marks]**  
(ii) Assess whether Karim's summary dismissal is lawful. State the mandatory legal procedure the management must follow. **[03 Marks]**  
(iii) Determine Farhana's statutory entitlements upon resignation after 8 years of service. Compare her compensation with termination under Section 26. **[04 Marks]**

**Full Answer:**

#### GQ7.1 · Part (i): Legality of Rafiq's Termination and Severance Calculation (03 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:**  
   Apex Telecommunications fired Rafiq without notice due to automation. The company kept junior technician Tanvir in the same post. Rafiq has 6 years of service, while Tanvir has only 14 months.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:**  
   **Section 20 (Retrenchment)** and **Section 20(4) (LIFO Rule)** of the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006**.

3. **Legal Analysis:**  
   - Downsizing due to automation is redundancy under Section 2(11). It must follow retrenchment rules under Section 20.
   - Under Section 20(4), the employer must follow the Last In, First Out (LIFO) rule within that job category. Tanvir has 14 months of service. Rafiq has 6 years. Retrenching Rafiq while keeping Tanvir violates Section 20(4).
   - Under Section 20(2)(a), the employer failed to give 1 month written notice or wages in place of notice.
   - Under Section 20(2)(c), statutory compensation is 30 days' wages per completed year of service:
   
$$
\text{Service Compensation} = 6 \text{ completed years} \times 30 \text{ days' wages} = 180 \text{ days' wages}
$$

   - Rafiq is also owed 1 month (30 days) wages in place of notice. His total compensation equals 210 days' wages.

4. **Direct Conclusion:**  
   Rafiq's termination is unlawful. Apex Telecommunications must retrench junior technician Tanvir first. To retrench Rafiq legally later, Apex must pay 1 month notice wages plus 180 days' wages as severance.

---

#### GQ7.2 · Part (ii): Lawfulness of Karim's Summary Dismissal and Due Process (03 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:**  
   A supervisor fired Karim on the spot for alleged theft. The supervisor gave him no charge-sheet and held no inquiry.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:**  
   **Section 23(4)(b) (Theft as Misconduct)** and **Section 24 (Procedure of Punishment)** of the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006**.

3. **Legal Analysis:**  
   - Theft is misconduct under Section 23(4)(b).
   - Under Section 24(1), dismissal requires strict adherence to natural justice:
     - Issue a written charge-sheet stating the allegation [`Sec 24(1)(a)`].
     - Give Karim at least 7 days to submit a written explanation [`Sec 24(1)(b)`].
     - Provide an opportunity for a personal hearing with a coworker to help him [`Sec 24(1)(c)`, `Sec 24(4)`].
     - Form an inquiry committee with equal employer and worker representatives, completed within 60 days [`Sec 24(1)(d)`].
     - Obtain written approval from the employer or factory manager [`Sec 24(1)(e)`].
   - Firing Karim on the spot bypasses all statutory safeguards.

4. **Direct Conclusion:**  
   Karim's summary dismissal is illegal and void. The company must reinstate him immediately. The company may suspend him under Section 24(2) with subsistence allowance pending a proper inquiry.

---

#### GQ7.3 · Part (iii): Farhana's Resignation Severance vs. Section 26 Termination (04 Marks)

1. **Facts of the Case:**  
   Farhana resigned voluntarily after 8 continuous years of service. Her compensation must be determined and compared with employer termination under Section 26.

2. **Applicable Law & Section:**  
   **Section 27(1), (4)(a) (Resignation)** and **Section 26(1)(a), (4) (Termination by Employer)** of the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006**.

3. **Legal Analysis:**  
   - Under Section 27(1), Farhana must give 60 days written notice.
   - Under Section 27(4)(a), resigning after 5 to 10 years gives 14 days' wages per completed year:
   
$$
\text{Resignation Severance} = 8 \text{ completed years} \times 14 \text{ days' wages} = 112 \text{ days' wages}
$$

   - If the company terminated her under Section 26:
     - The employer would have to give 120 days written notice (or wages in lieu).
     - Compensation under Section 26(4) would be 30 days' wages per completed year:
     
$$
\text{Section 26 Severance} = 8 \text{ completed years} \times 30 \text{ days' wages} = 240 \text{ days' wages}
$$

4. **Direct Conclusion:**  
   Farhana receives **112 days' wages** under Section 27(4)(a), plus unpaid wages and accrued leave money. This is 128 days' wages less than what she would get under employer termination (240 days' wages).

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **Do Not Confuse Notice Days:** Monthly permanent workers get 120 days notice under Section 26. But they only give 60 days notice when resigning under Section 27.
2. **Remember the LIFO Exception:** Section 20(4) requires retrenching the junior worker first. The only exception is an explicit written agreement to the contrary.
3. **Know the Theft Penalty:** Dismissal for theft [`Sec 23(4)(b)`] or riot [`Sec 23(4)(g)`] yields zero compensation under Section 23(3). General misconduct still yields 15 days' wages per year.
4. **Use the 4-Step Case Study Layout:** In case studies, always write: (1) Facts of the Case, (2) Applicable Law & Section, (3) Legal Analysis, and (4) Direct Conclusion. Shoaib Sir expects this exact layout.
