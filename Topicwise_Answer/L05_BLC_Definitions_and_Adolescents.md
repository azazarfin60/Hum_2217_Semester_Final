## L05.1 · Scope and Objective of the Bangladesh Labour Code, 2006

*No standalone final question in past papers. Explicitly highlighted by Shoaib Sir in his exam suggestions as a key introductory concept.*

### L05.1 · Model Exam Question · 02 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Target: Part (a) or Short-Answer Definition*

**Question.** What is the Bangladesh Labour Code, 2006? State its scope and main objectives.

**Answer.**

- **Statutory Identity:** The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (**Act No. XLII of 2006**) was enacted on 11 October 2006. It consolidated and amended 25 separate historical labour laws into a single modern code.
- **Territorial and Sectoral Scope:**
  - Under **Section 1(3)**, the Act extends to the whole of Bangladesh.
  - It applies to industrial establishments, commercial firms, transport services, and agricultural farms employing 5 or more workers.
  - Under **Section 1(4)**, it excludes government offices, security printing presses, defense installations, family-run establishments, and non-profit charitable institutions.
- **Core Statutory Objectives:**
  1. Consolidate fragmented colonial labour laws into one standard legal framework.
  2. Regulate terms of employment, service records, and disciplinary procedures (**Chapter II**).
  3. Enforce occupational safety, health, hygiene, and emergency fire precautions (**Chapters V, VI, VII**).
  4. Protect worker welfare, maternity benefits, and standard working hours (**Chapters IV, VIII, IX**).
  5. Provide an orderly mechanism for industrial dispute settlement via trade unions, collective bargaining, and Labour Courts (**Chapters XIII, XIV**).

## L05.2 · Definitions of Factory and Worker

### 2023 Q.1(a) · 02 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Define factory and worker according to Bangladesh labor code 2006.

**Answer.**

The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 defines factory and worker under **Section 2**:

1. **Factory [`Section 2(7)`]:**
   - "Factory" means any precincts or premises where **five or more workers** ordinarily work on any day of the year.
   - A **manufacturing process** must be carried on in any part of the premises.
   - The statutory definition explicitly **excludes a mine**.
   - *Core statutory formula:*
   $$
   \text{Factory} = \text{Premises with } \ge 5\text{ workers} + \text{Manufacturing Process} - \text{Mines}
   $$

2. **Worker [`Section 2(65)`]:**
   - "Worker" means any person, including an **apprentice**, employed in any establishment or industry.
   - The person may be employed **directly** or through a **contractor**.
   - The employment covers skilled, unskilled, manual, technical, trade promotional, or clerical work for hire or reward.
   - Terms of employment may be expressed or implied.
   - **Statutory Exclusion:** The definition strictly **excludes** any person employed mainly in a **managerial, administrative, or supervisory** capacity.

## L05.3 · Definition of Manufacturing Process

*No standalone final question in past papers. Specifically emphasized in Shoaib Sir's final suggestions and Raidah's class digest.*

### L05.3 · Model Exam Question · 02 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Target: Part (a) Short-Answer Question*

**Question.** Define "Manufacturing Process" under Section 2(2) of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

Under **Section 2(2)** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, "manufacturing process" means any of the following 5 distinct processes:

1. **Making and Treating Articles [`Section 2(2)(a)`]:** Making, altering, repairing, ornamenting, painting, washing, finishing, or packing any article. It also includes treating any substance for use, sale, transport, delivery, or disposal.
2. **Pumping Operations [`Section 2(2)(b)`]:** Pumping oil, gas, water, sewage, or any other liquid sweepings.
3. **Power Generation and Transmission [`Section 2(2)(c)`]:** Generating, transforming, or transmitting power or gas. This includes operating electrical substations or generators.
4. **Shipbuilding and Recycling [`Section 2(2)(d)`]:** Constructing, reconstructing, repairing, finishing, or breaking up ships or vessels.
5. **Commercial Printing [`Section 2(2)(e)`]:** Printing by letterpress, lithography, photogravure, computer, or offset. It also covers book-binding carried on for trade or business gain.

> [!note] Statutory Citation Clarification
> The oral voice note transcript recorded this definition informally as "2(lxi)". The verified statutory section in the official Act is **Section 2(2)**. Always write **Section 2(2)** on exam scripts.

## L05.4 · Age Categories of Workers

*Underpins 2023 Q.1(b) and tested in 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2. Essential classification baseline for Legal Issues.*

### L05.4 · Model Exam Question · 02 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Target: Part (a) Short-Answer Question*

**Question.** Define "Child", "Adolescent", "Adult", and "Minor" according to the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 divides individuals into 4 legal age categories under **Section 2**:

1. **Child [`Section 2(63)`]:** A person under 14 years of age ($< 14\text{ years}$). Child labour is strictly prohibited under **Section 34(1)**. But **Section 44** permits light work for ages 12 to 14.
2. **Adolescent [`Section 2(8)`]:** A person who has completed 14 years but is under 18 ($14 \le \text{age} < 18\text{ years}$). Employment requires a medical fitness certificate under **Section 34(2)** and capped hours under **Section 41**.
3. **Adult [`Section 2(36)`]:** A person who has completed 18 years of age ($\ge 18\text{ years}$). Full employment is permitted under general adult hours (**Section 100**).
4. **Minor:** A person who has not completed 18 years of age ($< 18\text{ years}$). This legal term covers both children and adolescents together.

| Category | Section | Exact Age Limit | Employment Legal Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Child** | `Sec 2(63)` | Under 14 years ($< 14$) | Strictly prohibited (`Sec 34`); light work exception at 12–14 (`Sec 44`) |
| **Adolescent** | `Sec 2(8)` | 14 to under 18 ($14 \le \text{age} < 18$) | Permitted with medical fitness token (`Sec 37`) and capped hours (`Sec 41`) |
| **Adult** | `Sec 2(36)` | 18 years or older ($\ge 18$) | Fully permitted under adult employment standards (`Sec 100–108`) |
| **Minor** | General Law | Under 18 years ($< 18$) | Umbrella class covering all children and adolescents |

> [!tip] Memory Hook · Statutory Age Limits
> - **Child [`Sec 2(63)`]:** $< 14$ years $\to$ Work strictly banned (`Sec 34(1)`). Light work allowed at 12–14 (`Sec 44`).
> - **Adolescent [`Sec 2(8)`]:** $14$ to $< 18$ years $\to$ Needs fitness certificate (`Sec 37`), capped hours (`Sec 41`).
> - **Adult [`Sec 2(36)`]:** $\ge 18$ years $\to$ Standard adult employment (`Sec 100–108`).
> - **Minor [General Law]:** $< 18$ years $\to$ Umbrella term covering all children and adolescents.

## L05.5 · Working Hours for Adolescents

### 2023 Q.1(b) · 04 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Briefly discuss rules regarding the employment of children and adolescent according to Bangladesh Labor code 2006.

**Answer.**

The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 regulates child and adolescent employment through **Chapter III**:

1. **Absolute Ban on Child Labour [`Section 34(1)`]:**
   - No child ($< 14$ years) shall be employed or permitted to work in any occupation or establishment.
   - Under **Section 35**, parents or guardians cannot enter into any agreement pledging the labour of a child.
   - *Exception under `Section 44`:* A child aged 12 to 14 may do light work. Such work must not harm their health, development, or school attendance.

2. **Conditions for Adolescent Employment [`Section 34(2)`, `Section 37`]:**
   - An adolescent ($14$ to $< 18$ years) may work only with a valid **Certificate of Fitness**. A registered medical practitioner must issue this certificate.
   - The certificate remains valid for 12 months (**Section 37(2)**). The employer must pay all medical examination fees (**Section 37(3)**).
   - The adolescent must carry a reference token while at work (**Section 34(2)(b)**).

3. **Statutory Working Hours [`Section 41`]:**
   - **Factory or Mine:** Maximum 5 hours per day and 30 hours per week (**Section 41(1)**). Maximum 36 hours per week with overtime (**Section 41(4)(a)**).
   - **Other Establishments:** Maximum 7 hours per day and 42 hours per week (**Section 41(2)**). Maximum 48 hours per week with overtime (**Section 41(4)(b)**).
   - **Night Work Ban:** No work permitted between 7:00 PM and 7:00 AM (**Section 41(3)**).
   - **Shifts and Spread-over:** At most 2 shifts without overlap. Spread-over cannot exceed 7.5 hours per shift (**Section 41(5)**).
   - **Relay and Holidays:** Relay change allowed at most once in 30 days with Inspector approval (**Section 41(6)**). Weekly holiday is mandatory without exception (**Section 41(7)**).

4. **Safety Restrictions on Hazardous Work [`Section 39`, `Section 40`, `Section 42`]:**
   - Prohibited from cleaning, lubricating, or adjusting machinery in motion (**Section 39(3)**).
   - No dangerous machine work without thorough instruction, training, and direct expert supervision (**Section 40**).
   - Absolute ban on underground and underwater work (**Section 42**).

### L05.5 · Model Exam Question · 04 Marks

*Shoaib Sir's Narrowed Target · Focus Strictly on Section 41*

**Question.** State the statutory rules regarding working hours for adolescent workers under Section 41 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

Under **Section 41** of the Act, eight statutory rules govern adolescent working hours:

1. **Daily and Weekly Caps in Factories and Mines [`Section 41(1)`]:**
   - No adolescent shall work in any factory or mine for more than **5 hours in any day**.
   - Also, weekly work cannot exceed **30 hours in any week**.
2. **Daily and Weekly Caps in Other Establishments [`Section 41(2)`]:**
   - In other establishments, daily work cannot exceed **7 hours**.
   - Weekly work cannot exceed **42 hours**.
3. **Absolute Prohibition of Night Work [`Section 41(3)`]:**
   - No adolescent shall work in any establishment between **7:00 PM and 7:00 AM**.
4. **Overtime Ceilings [`Section 41(4)`]:**
   - Total working hours, inclusive of overtime, must not exceed:
     - **36 hours per week** in any factory or mine.
     - **48 hours per week** in any other establishment.
5. **Shift and Spread-over Limits [`Section 41(5)`]:**
   - Work periods are limited to at most **2 shifts**.
   - Shifts must not overlap.
   - Spread-over shall not exceed **7.5 hours** per shift.
6. **Relay Restrictions [`Section 41(6)`]:**
   - An adolescent must work in only one relay.
   - The relay cannot change more often than **once in 30 days**. The employer must obtain written permission from the Labour Inspector.
7. **Mandatory Weekly Holiday [`Section 41(7)`]:**
   - General weekly holiday rules apply fully to adolescents.
   - The Act strictly bars any exemption or suspension of weekly holidays for adolescent workers.
8. **Double Employment Prohibited [`Section 41(8)`]:**
   - No adolescent shall work or be allowed to work in more than one establishment on any single day.

> [!tip] Memory Hook · Section 41 Working Hours at a Glance
> - **Factories & Mines:** Max **5 hrs/day**, **30 hrs/wk** (Max **36 hrs/wk** with overtime).
> - **Other Establishments:** Max **7 hrs/day**, **42 hrs/wk** (Max **48 hrs/wk** with overtime).
> - **Night Ban:** No work between **7:00 PM and 7:00 AM**.
> - **Shifts & Spread:** Max **2 shifts** (no overlap); spread-over $\le$ **7.5 hours** per shift.
> - **Relay & Holiday:** Relay change allowed at most once in 30 days (Inspector permit); weekly holiday mandatory.
> - **Double Employment:** No work in more than 1 establishment on the same day.

## L05.6 · Other Statutory Provisions for Young Persons

> [!note] Exam Blueprint Note (Shoaib Sir's Guidance)
> Shoaib Sir tested Sections 39 and 40 in 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2. In his final exam voice note, he explicitly excluded these non-hours adolescent sections from standalone final questions. He directed students to focus strictly on **Section 41**. A concise statutory summary is provided below for complete syllabus coverage.

### L05.6 · Model Exam Question · 03 Marks

*Young Persons Statutory Scope · Sections 34–40, 42–44*

**Question.** Summarize statutory restrictions on child and adolescent employment under Sections 34, 37, 39, 40, and 44.

**Answer.**

1. **Prohibition of Child Labour [`Section 34(1)`]:** No child under 14 years can work in any occupation or establishment.
2. **Medical Certificate of Fitness [`Section 34(2)`, `Section 37`]:**
   - An adolescent may work only after obtaining a medical fitness certificate from a registered medical practitioner.
   - The certificate remains valid for 12 months.
   - The employer pays the examination fee and holds the certificate.
   - The adolescent must carry a reference token while on duty.
3. **Prohibition on Machinery in Motion [`Section 39(3)`]:** Adolescents cannot clean, lubricate, or adjust machinery in motion. They must never work between moving parts.
4. **Dangerous Machines [`Section 40`]:** Adolescents cannot operate dangerous machines without training. They require full instruction on hazards and direct supervision by experienced staff.
5. **Underground and Underwater Work [`Section 42`]:** Complete ban on adolescent employment in underground mines or underwater jobs.
6. **Light Work Exception for Children [`Section 44`]:** Children aged 12 to 14 may do light work. Such work must not harm their health or school attendance.

> [!tip] Memory Hook · Young Worker Rules & Section 41 Caps
> - **Child [`Sec 2(63)`]:** Under 14 years. Work strictly banned.
> - **Adolescent [`Sec 2(8)`]:** 14 to under 18 years. Needs certificate and capped hours.
> - **Adult [`Sec 2(36)`]:** 18 years or older. General hours apply.
> - **Sec 41 Factory / Mine:** 5h/day, 30h/wk (36h with OT).
> - **Sec 41 Other Places:** 7h/day, 42h/wk (48h with OT).
> - **Sec 41 Timing:** Night ban 7 PM to 7 AM. Max 2 shifts (spread $\le$ 7.5h).
> - **Safety Caps:** No moving machinery (`Sec 39`). No dangerous machines without training (`Sec 40`). No underground work (`Sec 42`).

## L05.7 · Applied: BLC Effectiveness and Multi-Issue Factory Compliance

### 2023 Q.1(c) · 04 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Assess the effectiveness of a specific Industrial Law in Promoting a safe and fair working environment for workers.

**Answer.**

The **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006** sets statutory baselines for workplace safety and worker fairness. Four operational dimensions show its practical effectiveness:

1. **Workplace Physical Safety Standards (Chapter VI):**
   - **Section 62** mandates alternative fire exits, unobstructed passages, red Bangla exit signs, audible alarms, and biannual mock fire drills.
   - **Sections 63 and 65** mandate secure fencing of prime movers, gears, and emergency power-cutoff switches.
   - *Impact:* These statutory standards significantly lowered electrical and machinery fatalities in readymade garment (RMG) and heavy manufacturing units.

2. **Fair Working Conditions and Rest (Chapter IX):**
   - **Sections 100 and 102** restrict standard work to 8 hours daily and 48 hours weekly.
   - **Section 108** requires double wages for overtime work ($2 \times \text{wage}$).
   - *Impact:* Prevents physical burnout, stops forced unpaid overtime, and guarantees one weekly rest day (**Section 103**).

3. **Worker Dignity and Welfare (Chapters IV & VIII):**
   - Mandates clean drinking water and first-aid boxes (**Section 89**). Also requires canteens (**Section 92**), rest shelters (**Section 93**), and crèches (**Section 94**).
   - **Sections 45–50** guarantee 16 weeks of paid maternity leave and protect female workers from unlawful dismissal.
   - *Impact:* Promotes inclusive female labor participation in export sectors.

4. **Industrial Dispute Resolution and Voice (Chapters XIII & XIV):**
   - Establishes legal protections for trade union formation (**Section 176**) and collective bargaining through a Collective Bargaining Agent (**Section 202**).
   - *Enforcement Gaps:* Statutory provisions are sound on paper. But practical effectiveness requires regular factory inspections by DIFE. Strict prosecution under **Chapter XIX** is also necessary.

### 2025 Law-CT2 Q.1 · 10 Marks

*2nd Class Test · 23 Series · Full Marks: 10*

**Question.** A manufacturing establishment has problems involving excessive working hours, unsafe machinery, inadequate fire protection, and insufficient welfare facilities. Analyze which provisions of the Bangladesh Labour Code apply to each problem.

**Answer.**

The **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (BLC 2006)** provides comprehensive statutory solutions across 4 distinct chapters to resolve each problem:

```text
Manufacturing Establishment Compliance Framework
  ├── 1. Excessive Working Hours  ──> Chapter IX (Sec 100, 101, 102, 103, 105, 108)
  ├── 2. Unsafe Machinery         ──> Chapter VI (Sec 61, 63, 64, 65, 67)
  ├── 3. Inadequate Fire Safety   ──> Chapter VI (Sec 62)
  └── 4. Insufficient Welfare     ──> Chapter VIII (Sec 89, 90, 91, 92, 93, 94)
```

#### 1. Excessive Working Hours (Chapter IX: Working Hour and Leave)

To resolve excessive working hours, the employer must enforce the statutory caps in Chapter IX:

- **Daily Working Hours [`Section 100`]:** No adult worker shall ordinarily work for more than **8 hours in any day**. Work up to 10 hours daily is permitted only with overtime pay under Section 108.
- **Interval for Rest or Meal [`Section 101`]:** Workers must not work continuously without rest. The employer must provide:
  - At least 1 hour of rest for work exceeding 6 hours in a day.
  - At least 30 minutes of rest for work exceeding 5 hours in a day.
- **Weekly Working Hours [`Section 102`]:** Normal weekly hours must not exceed **48 hours**. Overtime can extend weekly work up to **60 hours**. But the yearly average must remain within 56 hours per week.
- **Weekly Holiday [`Section 103`]:** Every factory worker gets **1 full day (24 consecutive hours)** of paid rest weekly. No wage deductions can be made for this holiday.
- **Spread-over Limit [`Section 105`]:** Daily work and rest intervals combined must not exceed **10 hours** in any day.
- **Overtime Allowance [`Section 108`]:** Work beyond standard daily or weekly hours requires **double the ordinary basic wage rate**:
$$
\text{Overtime Wage Rate} = 2 \times (\text{Basic Wage} + \text{Dearness Allowance} + \text{Interim Pay})
$$

#### 2. Unsafe Machinery (Chapter VI: Safety)

The factory must eliminate mechanical hazards by complying with the machine safety provisions in Chapter VI:

- **Urgent Prohibition Orders [`Section 61`]:** If machinery poses danger to life, the Labour Inspector may issue binding orders. These prohibit operation until repairs are certified.
- **Fencing of Machinery [`Section 63`]:** Moving parts of prime movers and flywheels must be securely fenced. Substantial guards must also protect electric motors and transmission gears.
- **Work on Machinery in Motion [`Section 64`]:** Only trained adult males in tight clothes may adjust moving parts. The factory register must record their names.
- **Power Cut-off Devices [`Section 65`]:** Workrooms must have belt-shifting gear. Instant emergency power shut-off switches are also required.
- **Casing of New Machinery [`Section 67`]:** All revolving shafts, bolts, set-screws, and pinions must be encased or sunk. This prevents worker entanglement.

#### 3. Inadequate Fire Protection (Chapter VI: Safety, `Section 62`)

Under **Section 62** (*Precaution in case of fire*), the establishment must institute the following fire protection measures:

- **Alternative Emergency Exits [`Section 62(1)`]:** Every floor must provide an alternative emergency staircase. Functional firefighting equipment must also be available.
- **Exit Door Construction [`Section 62(3)`]:** Exit doors must never be locked while workers are inside. Doors must open outwards towards the escape route or slide smoothly.
- **Visual Signage [`Section 62(4)`]:** Mark all exit routes with prominent **red Bangla letters** (*জরুরী নির্গমন পথ*).
- **Audible Warning Alarms [`Section 62(5)`]:** Audible sirens or whistles must be installed. They must alert all workers instantly during an emergency.
- **Clear Passageways [`Section 62(6)`]:** All gangways, staircases, and corridors must remain completely clear of inventory, cartons, or obstructions.
- **Escape Training [`Section 62(7)`]:** If over 10 workers work upstairs, all staff must receive regular evacuation training.
- **Mock Fire Drills [`Section 62(8)`]:** Plants with **50+ workers** must hold mock fire drills **every 6 months**. The original 2006 Act required annual drills. Also, at least 18% of workers must receive firefighting training.

#### 4. Insufficient Welfare Facilities (Chapter VIII: Welfare Measures)

The establishment must furnish the mandatory physical welfare facilities in Chapter VIII:

- **First-Aid Appliances [`Section 89`]:** Provide at least 1 equipped first-aid box per 150 workers. A trained attendant wearing a first-aid badge must manage it. Factories with 300 or more workers must also provide a dedicated sick room and dispensary.
- **Safety Record Book & Safety Committee [`Section 90`, `Section 90A`]:** Mandatory for establishments employing more than 25 workers.
- **Washing Facilities [`Section 91`]:** Adequate, clean, and screened washing places must be provided separately for male and female workers.
- **Canteens [`Section 92`]:** Mandatory in any establishment employing more than **100 workers**. A joint managing committee with worker representatives must oversee food quality and non-profit prices.
- **Rest Rooms [`Section 93`]:** Required in establishments with over **50 workers**, complete with clean drinking water. If female workers exceed 25, a separate rest space is required.
- **Crèches / Rooms for Children [`Section 94`]:** Mandatory in establishments employing **40 or more female workers**. Rooms must accommodate children under 6 years. They must provide cots, toys, milk, and an enclosed open-air playground.

## L05.8 · Adult Working Hours, Rest Intervals, and Overtime

> [!note] Exam Blueprint Note (Shoaib Sir's Guidance)
> Adult working hours under Sections 100–108 were examined in 2025 Law-CT2 Q.1. Shoaib Sir excluded general adult working hours from standalone final exam questions. He narrowed working hours strictly to **Section 41 (Adolescents)**. This concise model question summarizes Chapter IX for complete exam readiness.

### L05.8 · Model Exam Question · 04 Marks

*Statutory Working Standards · Chapter IX Summary*

**Question.** State statutory rules for adult hours, rest intervals, weekly holidays, spread-over, and overtime under Chapter IX.

**Answer.**

Chapter IX of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 establishes 6 fundamental statutory rules for adult workers:

1. **Daily Working Hours [`Section 100`]:**
   - Standard daily work is limited to **8 hours in any day**.
   - With overtime, daily work may extend up to **10 hours**.
2. **Interval for Rest or Meal [`Section 101`]:**
   - No worker shall work continuously for more than 6 hours without at least **1 hour of rest**.
   - No worker shall work continuously for more than 5 hours without at least **30 minutes of rest**.
3. **Weekly Working Hours [`Section 102`]:**
   - Standard weekly work is limited to **48 hours in any week**.
   - With overtime, total weekly work cannot exceed **60 hours**.
   - The yearly average across all 52 weeks must not exceed **56 hours per week**.
4. **Weekly Holiday [`Section 103`]:**
   - Factory workers are entitled to **1 full day (24 consecutive hours)** of paid rest each week.
   - Commercial establishments and shops provide **1.5 days** of weekly holiday.
   - Wages cannot be deducted for weekly holidays.
5. **Spread-over Limit [`Section 105`]:**
   - Working hours and rest intervals together must not spread over more than **10 hours** on any day.
6. **Extra Allowance for Overtime [`Section 108`]:**
   - Work beyond 8 hours daily or 48 hours weekly requires **double the ordinary basic wage rate**:
$$
\text{Overtime Allowance Rate} = 2 \times \text{Ordinary Rate of Basic Wage}
$$
