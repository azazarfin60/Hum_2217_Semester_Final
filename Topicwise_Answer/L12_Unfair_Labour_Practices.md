### Statutory Comparison of Unfair Labour Practices

| Feature | Employer Unfair Labour Practices (`Sec 195`) | Worker & Union Unfair Labour Practices (`Sec 196`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Statute** | Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 195 | Bangladesh Labour Act 2006, Section 196 |
| **Restricted Parties** | Employers, employer trade unions, and their agents | Workers, worker trade unions, and their agents |
| **Target of Protection** | Freedom of association, union leaders, and CBA | Management staff, non-union workers, and public peace |
| **Prohibited Actions** | 12 specific statutory prohibitions | Working-hour union work, plus 6 statutory bans |
| **Statutory Penalties** | Up to 2 years prison, fine up to 10,000 Taka, or both (`Sec 291(1)`) | Worker: up to 1 year or 5,000 Taka (`Sec 291(2)`); Union: up to 2 years or 10,000 Taka (`Sec 291(3)`) |

## L12.1 · Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Employers

*No standalone final question in past papers. Prescribed under Chapter 4. Tested inside applied questions 2023 Q.4(b) and Q.4(c).*

### L12.1 · Model Exam Question · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 4) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Define unfair labour practice. Explain all employer unfair labour practices under Section 195 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

**Definition:** An unfair labour practice is an unfair act or omission between an employer and employees. Under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, it means illegal acts that violate worker rights or disrupt industrial peace.

Under **Section 195**, employers and their trade unions cannot commit unfair labour practices. The statute prohibits 12 specific actions:

1. **Yellow-Dog Contracts (`Sec 195(a)`):** Force a worker to promise not to join or remain in a trade union.
2. **Refusal to Employ (`Sec 195(b)`):** Refuse to hire or dismiss any worker based on union membership.
3. **Discrimination in Service (`Sec 195(c)`):** Discriminate in pay, promotion, or service conditions based on union involvement.
4. **Retaliatory Dismissal and Threats (`Sec 195(d)`):** Dismiss, demote, or threaten a worker for joining or helping a trade union.
5. **Inducement or Bribery (`Sec 195(e)`):** Offer benefits or money to induce someone to leave union office or avoid joining.
6. **Coercing the CBA (`Sec 195(f)`):** Force a CBA leader to sign a deal using threats, confinement, assault, or utility cuts.
7. **Interfering in CBA Elections (`Sec 195(g)`):** Interfere with or influence the secret ballot election held under Section 202.
8. **Recruiting Strike-Breakers (`Sec 195(h)`):** Hire replacement workers during a lawful strike under Section 211. An Arbitrator may permit limited staffing to prevent machinery damage.
9. **Ignoring the Participation Committee (`Sec 195(i)`):** Deliberately fail to implement agreed measures from the bipartite Participation Committee.
10. **Refusing Communication with the CBA (`Sec 195(j)`):** Fail to reply to letters sent by the CBA regarding an industrial dispute.
11. **Punitive Transfer of Union Officers (`Sec 195(k)`):** Transfer key union officers without consent, violating Section 187. This covers the President, General Secretary, Organizing Secretary, or Treasurer.
12. **Illegal Lock-Out (`Sec 195(l)`):** Start, continue, or encourage an illegal lock-out.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 4 Intuitive Clusters for Section 195 (12 Employer Prohibitions)
> Write all 12 employer prohibitions effortlessly by grouping them into 4 clusters:
> - **1. Target Individual Workers (4 acts):** Yellow-dog contract (`a`), refuse to hire (`b`), discriminate in service (`c`), retaliatory firing (`d`).
> - **2. Attack Union & Leaders (3 acts):** Bribery to leave union (`e`), interfere in CBA elections (`g`), punitive transfer of officers (`k`).
> - **3. Undermine Dialogue & CBA (3 acts):** Coerce CBA officers (`f`), ignore Participation Committee (`i`), refuse CBA letters (`j`).
> - **4. Disrupt Strike Action (2 acts):** Recruit strike-breakers during lawful strike (`h`), declare illegal lock-out (`l`).

## L12.2 · Unfair Labour Practices on the Part of Workers

*No standalone final question in past papers. Prescribed under Chapter 4. Tested inside applied questions 2023 Q.4(b).*

### L12.2 · Model Exam Question · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 4) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Detail the statutory worker and union unfair labour practices under Section 196 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

**Section 196** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, restricts disruptive actions by workers and unions. It bans coercive tactics against employers, colleagues, and the public.

**1. Union Activities During Working Hours (`Sec 196(1)`):**
Workers cannot do union work during duty hours without employer consent. But the President or General Secretary of the CBA can attend official proceedings under the Act.

**2. Prohibited Acts by Workers and Unions (`Sec 196(2)`):**
No worker, trade union, or agent shall commit the following acts:
1. **Intimidating Workers on Membership (`Sec 196(2)(a)`):** Threaten any worker to join, stay in, or leave a trade union.
2. **Inducement via Personal Benefits (`Sec 196(2)(b)`):** Offer personal benefits to make someone leave union office or avoid joining.
3. **Coercing Union Dues (`Sec 196(2)(c)`):** Force a worker to pay or withhold union dues through threats, assault, or utility cuts.
4. **Coercing the Employer (`Sec 196(2)(d)`):** Force an employer to accept demands or sign deals using confinement, assault, or cutting utilities.
5. **Illegal Strike and Go-Slow (`Sec 196(2)(e)`):** Start, continue, or instigate an illegal strike or an intentional go-slow.
6. **Gherao, Obstruction, and Damage (`Sec 196(2)(f)`):** Resort to gherao, block transport routes, disrupt communications, or damage factory property.

**3. Election Interference (`Sec 196(3)`):**
No union or agent may rig CBA elections under Section 202. The law bans intimidation, impersonation, undue influence, and bribery.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: The 3-Inward + 3-Outward Split for Section 196(2)
> Remember the 6 worker prohibitions by splitting them into two groups of 3:
> - **3 Inward Acts (Against Fellow Workers):**
>   1. Threatening workers on union membership (`(a)`).
>   2. Bribing members to quit office or stay out (`(b)`).
>   3. Forcing dues payments through threats or utility cuts (`(c)`).
> - **3 Outward Acts (Against Management & Factory):**
>   4. Coercing the employer or cutting factory utilities (`(d)`).
>   5. Starting illegal strikes or go-slow actions (`(e)`).
>   6. Staging gherao, blocking transport, or damaging factory property (`(f)`).

### L12.2 · Model Case Study · 04 Marks

*Analytical / Case Study Target · Shoaib Sir Exam Blueprint*

**Question.** During wage negotiations at an electronics factory, local trade union leaders demand an immediate 35% wage increase. They serve no strike notice and hold no secret ballot under Section 211. Instead, they cut electricity to the cleanrooms and block factory gates. They confine the General Manager for 12 hours (gherao) until he signs a wage agreement. They also threaten non-striking technicians with physical assault if they enter the plant.

Analyze the legality of these actions under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. Also determine the legal liabilities of the union and participating workers.

**Answer.**

**1. Facts and Legal Issue:**
Union leaders cut cleanroom power, blocked factory gates, and confined the General Manager for 12 hours. They forced him to sign a wage deal without giving strike notice. They also threatened non-striking technicians with violence. The legal issue is whether these acts are unfair labour practices and an illegal strike under the Act.

**2. Applicable Law and Statutory Sections:**
- **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006:**
  - **Section 196(2)(a):** Intimidating workers regarding union activities or work attendance.
  - **Section 196(2)(d):** Forcing the employer to accept demands through confinement, threats, or cutting utilities.
  - **Section 196(2)(e):** Commencing or instigating an illegal strike.
  - **Section 196(2)(f):** Gherao, transport or communication obstruction, and property damage.
  - **Section 211:** Mandatory strike notice (7 to 14 days) and secret ballot requirement ($\frac{2}{3}$ majority).
  - **Section 291(2)–(3):** Criminal penalties for unfair labour practices.
  - **Section 23(4)(g):** Dismissal for misconduct due to illegal strikes and subversive acts.
- **The Contract Act, 1872:**
  - **Section 15 & Section 19:** Agreements made under coercion are voidable by the coerced party.

**3. Legal Analysis:**
1. **Illegal Strike (`Sec 211` & `Sec 196(2)(e)`):** The workers struck without a 7 to 14 days notice or a secret ballot. This makes the strike illegal. Instigating an illegal strike directly violates Section 196(2)(e).
2. **Coercing the Employer and Utility Cuts (`Sec 196(2)(d)`):** Cutting cleanroom power and confining the manager violates Section 196(2)(d). The law strictly bans forcing an agreement through physical confinement or cutting power.
3. **Unlawful Gherao and Obstruction (`Sec 196(2)(f)`):** Blocking factory gates and confining managers constitutes gherao. It also obstructs transport and communication under Section 196(2)(f).
4. **Intimidation of Workers (`Sec 196(2)(a)`):** Threatening non-striking testing technicians with physical assault violates Section 196(2)(a).
5. **Invalidity of Forced Agreement:** The General Manager signed under physical confinement and criminal coercion. Under Section 19 of the Contract Act 1872, coercion makes an agreement voidable. Under Section 196 of the Labour Act, this forced agreement has no legal effect.
6. **Statutory Penalties and Disciplinary Action:**
   - Participating workers face up to 1 year in prison, a 5,000 Taka fine, or both (`Sec 291(2)`).
   - Union leaders face up to 2 years in prison, a 10,000 Taka fine, or both (`Sec 291(3)`).
   - Management can start disciplinary proceedings under Section 24. They can dismiss guilty workers for gross misconduct under Section 23(4)(g).

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
All actions by the union leaders and workers violate Section 196. The signed wage agreement is legally void. Leaders and workers face criminal penalties under Section 291. They also face dismissal for misconduct under Section 23.

## L12.3 · Statutory Penalties for Unfair Labour Practices

*Never asked standalone in past finals or class tests. Prescribed under Chapter 4 and Chapter 19.*

### L12.3 · Model Exam Question · 02 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 4 & Chapter 19) · Target: Part (a)*

**Question.** State the statutory penalties for unfair labour practices under the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

Under **Section 291** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, unfair labour practices carry criminal penalties:

1. **Employers (`Sec 291(1)`):** Anyone violating Section 195 faces up to 2 years in prison, a 10,000 Taka fine, or both.
2. **Individual Workers (`Sec 291(2)`):** Any worker violating Section 196 faces up to 1 year in prison, a 5,000 Taka fine, or both.
3. **Trade Unions and Non-Workers (`Sec 291(3)`):** Unions violating Section 196 face up to 2 years in prison, a 10,000 Taka fine, or both.

**Related Offences in Notes and Statute:**
- **Illegal Strike or Lock-out (`Sec 294`):** Up to 1 year imprisonment or 5,000 Taka fine. Class notes list 6 months prison or 200 Taka for workers. They list 1 year prison or 5,000 Taka for employers.
- **Go-Slow (`Sec 296`):** Up to 1 year imprisonment, a fine up to 5,000 Taka, or both.

## L12.4 · Applied: Consequences of Unfair Labour Practices on Employers and Employees

*Section A · CO1 · 04 Marks in 2023 Final.*

### 2023 Q.4(b) · 04 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Evaluate the potential consequences of unfair labor practices on both employers and employees.

**Answer.**

**1. Facts and Legal Issue:**
Unfair labour practices destroy mutual trust in an enterprise. The core issue is evaluating how labour right violations damage operations, finance, and worker security.

**2. Applicable Law and Statutory Sections:**
- **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006:**
  - **Section 195:** Prohibited employer practices (anti-union discrimination, bad-faith bargaining, illegal lock-outs).
  - **Section 196:** Prohibited worker practices (gherao, illegal strikes, coercion, property damage).
  - **Section 291:** Criminal penalties for unfair practices.

**3. Evaluation of Consequences:**

**(a) Consequences on Employers:**
- **Criminal Prosecution and Penalties:** Violations under Section 195 expose managers to 2 years imprisonment and fines under Section 291(1).
- **Severe Financial and Operational Losses:** Employer hostility triggers wildcat strikes, work stoppages, and plant closures. In engineering and export manufacturing, missed delivery deadlines cause heavy contract penalties.
- **Loss of Export Orders and Compliance Certifications:** International buyers and audit agencies cancel supply contracts when factories violate fundamental labour standards.
- **High Employee Turnover:** Toxic anti-union workplaces drive away skilled engineers and technicians, increasing rehiring costs.

**(b) Consequences on Employees:**
- **Loss of Livelihood and Income:** Punitive dismissals or demotions under Section 195(d) leave workers without income.
- **Suppression of Collective Voice:** Coercing or bypassing the CBA destroys workers' collective bargaining power. Employees cannot secure fair wages or safe working conditions.
- **Workplace Fear and Stress:** Constant threats, unlawful transfers, and surveillance create severe psychological distress.
- **Legal Penalties for Worker Violations:** Workers committing unfair practices under Section 196 face dismissal under Section 23. They also face loss of back pay and up to 1 year imprisonment under Section 291(2).

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
Unfair labour practices create a destructive cycle. Employers face criminal liability, financial losses, and cancelled contracts. Workers suffer poverty, unsafe conditions, and fear. Compliance with Section 195 and Section 196 is essential for industrial peace.

## L12.5 · Applied: Strategies That Unions Can Employ to Address Unfair Labour Practices

*Section A · CO1 · 04 Marks in 2023 Final.*

### 2023 Q.4(c) · 04 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Propose strategies that unions can employ to address unfair labor practices effectively.

**Answer.**

**1. Facts and Strategic Problem:**
Trade unions often face employer retaliation, anti-union firings, and refusal to negotiate. Unions need lawful legal and organizational strategies to counter employer abuses without breaking the law.

**2. Applicable Law and Statutory Sections:**
- **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006:**
  - **Section 195:** Prohibited employer unfair labour practices.
  - **Section 202 & 202(24):** Exclusive collective bargaining rights of the CBA.
  - **Section 205:** Bipartite Participation Committee.
  - **Section 209–210:** Statutory industrial dispute settlement procedures.
  - **Section 214 & 291:** Labour Court jurisdiction and criminal complaints.

**3. Proposed Strategies for Trade Unions:**

**(a) Statutory and Legal Strategies:**
- **Filing Complaints Before the Labour Court (`Sec 214`, `Sec 291`):** The union or CBA can file criminal complaints against guilty managers under Section 291(1). The court can impose up to 2 years imprisonment. Also, workers can seek reinstatement with full back pay under Section 33.
- **Invoking Formal Dispute Settlement (`Sec 210`):** The CBA can issue a written dispute notice under Section 210(1). Management must hold bilateral talks within 15 days. If talks fail, statutory government conciliation begins.
- **Reporting to Regulators:** Report illegal officer transfers (`Sec 187`) to the Director of Labour. Also report unlawful lock-outs to factory inspectors for fast intervention.

**(b) Internal and Organizational Strategies:**
- **Systematic Documentation of Evidence:** Keep written logs, copies of punitive transfer letters, audio recordings, and witness statements. Solid evidence ensures fast relief in Labour Court.
- **Using the Participation Committee (`Sec 205`):** Use worker representatives on the Participation Committee. Settle workplace complaints early before disputes grow.
- **Partnering with National Federations and Buyers (`Sec 200`):** Affiliate with national labour federations. Report violations to international buyer compliance bodies. Buyer audit pressure often forces management compliance quickly.
- **Training Members on Lawful Conduct:** Educate members on Section 196 restrictions. Avoiding gherao, violence, and illegal strikes preserves the legal immunities granted under Section 197 and Section 198.

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
Unions can counter unfair labour practices through Labour Court cases under Section 291. They must gather strong documentary evidence and report abuses to international buyers. Staying disciplined and avoiding illegal strikes protects their legal immunities.
