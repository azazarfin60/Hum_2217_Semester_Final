## L02.1 · Contract and Agreement Maxim

### 2024 Q.2(a) · 02 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2024 Final*

**Question.** Define contract and agreement.

**Answer.**
Under the **Contract Act, 1872**, these two terms are defined as follows:

1. **Agreement [Section 2(e)]:**
   "Every promise and every set of promises, forming the consideration for each other, is an agreement."
   An agreement forms when one party makes an offer and the other party accepts it.

2. **Contract [Section 2(h)]:**
   "An agreement enforceable by law is a contract."
   A contract needs two core parts: an underlying agreement and legal enforceability by courts.

$$
\text{Contract} = \text{Agreement} + \text{Legal Enforceability}
$$

> [!tip] Memory Ladder · Section 2 Definition Chain
> `Sec 2(a) Offer` → `2(b) Promise` → `2(c) Parties` → `2(d) Consideration` → `2(e) Agreement` → `2(g) Void` / `2(h) Contract` / `2(i) Voidable`
>
> **Step-by-step memory flow:**
> 1. `2(a)` **Offer:** A proposes a deal to B.
> 2. `2(b)` **Promise:** B accepts the proposal.
> 3. `2(c)` **Parties:** A is the promisor; B is the promisee.
> 4. `2(d)` **Consideration:** Both sides exchange value.
> 5. `2(e)` **Agreement:** The mutual promises form an agreement.
> 6. **Three Legal Endings:**
>    - `2(g)` **Void:** Unenforceable by law from day one.
>    - `2(h)` **Contract:** Enforceable by law in court.
>    - `2(i)` **Voidable:** Enforceable until the injured party cancels it.

### L02.1 · Model Exam Question · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 2) · Target: Part (b)*

**Question.** "All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts." Explain this statement with examples and distinguish between an agreement and a contract.

**Answer.**
The statement reflects the legal link between agreements and contracts under the **Contract Act, 1872**. Agreement is the broad group. Contract is the narrower legal class.

1. **All contracts are agreements:**
   Every contract starts as an agreement. Under **Section 2(h)**, a contract is an agreement enforceable by law. Parties must first exchange a lawful offer and acceptance under **Section 2(e)**. Without an underlying agreement, no contract can exist.

2. **All agreements are not contracts:**
   An agreement becomes a contract only if it meets all terms of **Section 10**. Agreements that lack legal enforceability never become contracts:
   - **Social or domestic agreements:** An invite to dinner creates no legal ties. If broken, neither party can sue.
   - **Lacking Consent or Capacity:** Minors cannot contract. Any deal with a minor is void from the start (*void ab initio*).
   - **Agreements expressly declared void:** The law bars certain deals. Agreements in restraint of trade (**Section 27**) and wagers (**Section 30**) cannot be enforced in court.

3. **Illustrative Examples:**
   - *Agreement, not a contract:* A invites B to dinner. B accepts. If A fails to serve dinner, B cannot sue. The parties never intended legal consequences.
   - *Agreement and a contract:* A agrees in writing to sell an oscilloscope to B for Tk 50,000. B pays the price. A valid commercial obligation exists. A court will enforce it.

| Comparison Parameter | Agreement | Contract |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Statutory Definition** | Defined under **Section 2(e)**. | Defined under **Section 2(h)**. |
| **Scope** | Broader scope. Covers social, domestic, and commercial promises. | Narrower scope. Limited only to legally binding agreements. |
| **Legal Enforceability** | May or may not be enforceable in court. | Always enforceable by law. |
| **Legal Obligation** | Does not always create a legal duty. | Always creates binding legal duties. |
| **Constituents** | $\text{Offer} + \text{Acceptance}$. | $\text{Agreement} + \text{Legal Enforceability (Section 10)}$. |

## L02.2 · Void Agreement vs Voidable Contract

> [!tip] Quick Link
> Section 10 separates valid contracts from void agreements. See the [[#L02.3 · Essential Elements of a Valid Contract|10-Element Memory Hook]] below.

### 2023 Q.3(a) · 02 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Define void and voidable agreement.

**Answer.**
Under the **Contract Act, 1872**, the two concepts are defined as follows:

1. **Void Agreement [Section 2(g)]:**
   "An agreement not enforceable by law is said to be void."
   A void agreement is void *ab initio* (null from the start). It gives no legal rights to either party. It creates no legal duties. Examples include agreements with minors (**Section 11**) and agreements in restraint of trade (**Section 27**).

2. **Voidable Contract [Section 2(i)]:**
   "An agreement which is enforceable by law at the option of one or more of the parties thereto, but not at the option of the other or others, is a voidable contract."
   *(Note: The Act uses the term "voidable contract" rather than "voidable agreement").*
   It remains a valid contract until the injured party cancels it. Common grounds include coercion (**Section 15**), undue influence (**Section 16**), fraud (**Section 17**), and misrepresentation (**Section 18**).

| Comparison Basis | Void Agreement [Section 2(g)] | Voidable Contract [Section 2(i)] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Validity at Inception** | Null from the start (*void ab initio*). | Valid and binding when made. |
| **Enforceability** | Cannot be enforced by either party. | Enforceable at the option of the aggrieved party only. |
| **Defect** | Lacks an essential element, such as capacity or lawful object. | Consent of one party was not freely given. |
| **Restitution** | Neither party can enforce rights. | Aggrieved party can rescind and claim compensation. |

## L02.3 · Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

> [!tip] Exam Hall Memory Hook · The 10 Elements of Section 10
> **Memory Sentence:**
> *"An **Offer** with **Intent**, backed by **Cash** and **Capacity**, with **Free Consent** and **Lawful Aim**, makes a **Certain** and **Possible** deal, **Not Void**, in **Formal** writing."*
>
> **10-Point Recall Checklist:**
> 1. **Offer & Acceptance** (`Sec 2(a), 2(b)`)
> 2. **Intent** to create legal relations
> 3. **Cash (Consideration)** (`Sec 2(d), 25`)
> 4. **Capacity** of parties (`Sec 11, 12`)
> 5. **Free Consent** (`Sec 13, 14`)
> 6. **Lawful Aim (Object)** (`Sec 23`)
> 7. **Certainty** of terms (`Sec 29`)
> 8. **Possible** performance (`Sec 56`)
> 9. **Not Void** by statute (`Sec 26–30`)
> 10. **Formal** writing and registration

### 2023 Q.3(b) · 04 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Summarizes the conditions that must be satisfied for a contact to be legally enforceable.  
*(Note: "contact" is printed in original; refers to "contract")*

**Answer.**
Under **Section 10** of the **Contract Act, 1872**, all agreements are contracts if they meet specific statutory conditions. For a contract to be legally enforceable, the following elements must be satisfied:

1. **Lawful Offer and Acceptance:**
   One party makes a lawful offer. The other party gives absolute acceptance (**Sections 2(a), 2(b), 7**).

2. **Intention to Create Legal Relations:**
   Parties must intend to create a binding legal obligation. Social or domestic promises do not create legal obligations.

3. **Lawful Consideration [Section 2(d), Section 25]:**
   Each party gives and receives something valuable (*quid pro quo*). The consideration must be real and lawful.

4. **Capacity of Parties [Sections 11 and 12]:**
   Parties must be competent to contract. They must be at least 18 years old, of sound mind, and not disqualified by law.

5. **Free Consent [Sections 13 and 14]:**
   Parties must agree on the same thing in the same sense. Consent must be genuine. It must not stem from coercion (**Section 15**) or undue influence (**Section 16**). It must also be free from fraud (**Section 17**), misrepresentation (**Section 18**), or bilateral mistake (**Section 20**).

6. **Lawful Object [Section 23]:**
   The contract purpose must not be illegal, fraudulent, harmful to others, or opposed to public policy.

7. **Certainty and Possibility [Sections 29 and 56]:**
   Terms must be clear and unambiguous. The act must also be physically and legally possible to perform.

8. **Not Expressly Declared Void [Sections 26 to 30]:**
   The agreement must not fall under statutory void classes, such as restraint of trade or wagering.

### 2025 Law-CT1 Q.1 · 10 Marks

*Department of ECE, RUET · 1st Class Test 2025 (23 Series) · Time: 30 minutes*

**Question.** Explain how the essential elements of a valid contract ensure the enforceability of agreements under the Contract Act.

**Answer.**
The **Contract Act, 1872** defines these foundational concepts. Under **Section 2(e)**, an agreement is *"every promise and every set of promises, forming the consideration for each other"*. Under **Section 2(h)**, a contract is *"an agreement enforceable by law"*.

The core legal maxim states: **"All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts."**

An agreement gains legal enforceability only when it satisfies all requirements set out in **Section 10**:

$$
\text{Valid Contract} = \text{Agreement} + \text{Legal Enforceability (Section 10 Essentials)}
$$

Each essential element protects legal rights and ensures commercial stability in specific ways:

1. **Offer and Acceptance [Sections 2(a), 2(b), 7]:**
   Enforceability requires a meeting of minds (*consensus ad idem*). One party shares a proposal, and the other party accepts it. Under **Section 7(1)**, acceptance must be absolute and unqualified. Both parties must agree on the exact same terms.

2. **Intention to Create Legal Relations:**
   The law enforces only promises made with legal intent. In business transactions, courts presume this legal intent exists. In social and domestic agreements, courts presume it is absent (*Balfour v. Balfour*). This filter keeps courts from handling casual social promises.

3. **Lawful Consideration [Section 2(d), Section 25]:**
   Consideration is the price paid for a promise (*quid pro quo*). Under **Section 25**, an agreement without consideration is void. Courts will not enforce free promises unless they meet statutory exceptions. Also, the consideration must be lawful under **Section 23**.

4. **Capacity of Parties [Sections 11 and 12]:**
   Enforceability requires competent parties. Under **Section 11**, a party must be at least 18 years old and of sound mind. They must not face legal disqualification. An agreement with a minor is void from the start (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*). This rule protects young people from unfair deals.

5. **Free Consent [Sections 13, 14, 19]:**
   Parties must enter the deal freely without pressure. Under **Section 14**, consent is not free when caused by:
   - Coercion (**Section 15**)
   - Undue influence (**Section 16**)
   - Fraud (**Section 17**)
   - Misrepresentation (**Section 18**)
   - Bilateral mistake (**Section 20**)
   If coercion, fraud, or misrepresentation occurs, **Section 19** makes the contract voidable. The injured party can choose to cancel or enforce it. This rule protects basic fairness.

6. **Lawful Object [Section 23]:**
   The purpose of the contract must be lawful. Under **Section 23**, an agreement is void if its purpose violates the law. It cannot commit fraud, cause injury, or harm public interest. Courts will never enforce illegal schemes.

7. **Agreements Not Expressly Declared Void [Sections 26 to 30]:**
   The Act strikes down deals that hurt public interest:
   - Restraint of marriage (**Section 26**)
   - Restraint of trade or profession (**Section 27**)
   - Restraint of legal proceedings (**Section 28**)
   - Wagering agreements (**Section 30**)
   Barring these deals protects personal freedom and fair market trade.

8. **Certainty of Terms [Section 29]:**
   Terms must be clear and precise. Under **Section 29**, uncertain agreements are void. A court cannot enforce terms it cannot understand. Clear technical specifications protect both sides.

9. **Possibility of Performance [Section 56]:**
   Under **Section 56(1)**, an agreement to do an impossible act is void from the start. The law never forces anyone to perform impossible tasks.

10. **Statutory Formalities (Writing and Registration):**
    Oral contracts are usually valid. But some laws require written and registered documents. Examples include land transfers and corporate contracts. Written documents give solid legal proof in court.

**Conclusion:**
These essential elements act as legal filters. They filter out casual talk and protect vulnerable people. They also stop illegal deals. When all elements are met, the law enforces the contract.

## L02.4 · Proposal, Offer, Acceptance, and Parties

### 2024 Q.6(a) · 02 Marks

*Section B · CO1 · Asked in: 2024 Final*

**Question.** Define the term offer and acceptance in a contract.

**Answer.**
Under the **Contract Act, 1872**, offer and acceptance are defined as follows:

1. **Offer (Proposal) [Section 2(a)]:**
   "When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or to abstain from doing anything, with a view to obtaining the assent of that other to such act or abstinence, he is said to make a proposal."
   The person making the proposal is the **promisor** or **offeror** [**Section 2(c)**].

2. **Acceptance [Section 2(b)]:**
   "When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise."
   The person accepting the proposal is the **promisee** or **acceptor** [**Section 2(c)**].

**Example:** An ECE engineer offers to design an automated PCB testing rig for Tk 40,000. When the client accepts this price and scope, the offer becomes a binding promise.

## L02.5 · Rules and Legal Significance of Offer and Acceptance

### 2025 Law-CT1 Q.2 · 10 Marks

*Department of ECE, RUET · 1st Class Test 2025 (23 Series) · Time: 30 minutes*

**Question.** Evaluate the legal significance of offer and acceptance in the formation of a valid contract under the Contract Act.

**Answer.**
Offer and acceptance start the contract formation process under the **Contract Act, 1872**. They establish mutual assent (*consensus ad idem*). As legal scholar Sir William Anson stated:
> "Acceptance is to an offer what a lighted match is to a train of gunpowder. It produces something which cannot be undone or recalled."

1. **Legal Nature and Significance of an Offer [Section 2(a)]:**
   An offer expresses willingness to enter into a contract on specific terms. It seeks the assent of the other party.
   - **Legal Significance:** The offer defines all contractual boundaries. Once accepted without change, the offeror cannot alter the terms.
   - **Offer vs. Invitation to Treat:** An offer differs from an invitation to receive offers. Shop displays, price lists, catalogues, and tender notices are invitations to treat. Submitting a bid or bringing items to a cash desk makes the actual legal offer.
   - **Types of Offer:** Under **Section 9**, an offer may be express in words or implied by conduct. It can be specific to one party or general to the world. In *Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co. (1893)*, a general public offer became a binding contract when a user met its conditions.

2. **Legal Nature and Significance of Acceptance [Section 2(b)]:**
   Acceptance occurs when the offeree signifies assent to the proposal.
   - **Legal Significance:** Acceptance turns an open proposal into a binding promise. Before acceptance, neither side is bound. After acceptance, both parties must perform. Any breach creates a legal claim for damages.

3. **Statutory Rules Governing Valid Acceptance:**
   - **Absolute and Unqualified [Section 7(1)]:** Acceptance must match the offer terms exactly. A conditional reply or counter-offer cancels the original offer.
   - **Usual and Reasonable Manner [Section 7(2)]:** Acceptance must follow the requested method. If no method is set, it must follow normal business practice.
   - **Acceptance by Conduct [Section 8]:** Performing the required conditions counts as valid acceptance. In general offers, the acceptor does not need to send prior notice.
   - **Silence is Not Acceptance:** Silence does not mean acceptance. The offeree must communicate assent.

4. **Rules on Communication and Completion [Section 4]:**
   The timing of communication determines when parties are legally bound:
   - **Communication of Proposal:** Complete when it comes to the knowledge of the offeree.
   - **Communication of Acceptance against Proposer:** Complete the moment the acceptor posts it. The proposer is bound immediately upon dispatch.
   - **Communication of Acceptance against Acceptor:** Complete only when the letter reaches the knowledge of the proposer.

5. **Revocation of Offer and Acceptance [Section 5]:**
   - **Revocation of Offer:** The offeror can revoke an offer before acceptance is posted. Once the acceptor posts acceptance, revocation is barred.
   - **Revocation of Acceptance:** The acceptor can revoke acceptance before it reaches the proposer. Once the proposer learns of it, revocation is barred.
   - **Lapse of Offer [Section 6]:** An offer ends if the proposer revokes it or time expires. It also lapses if a condition fails, or if the proposer dies before acceptance.

**Conclusion:**
Offer and acceptance define the exact moment a contract is born. They fix the legal jurisdiction where the contract was made. They transform voluntary negotiations into binding legal commitments.

## L02.6 · Statutory Rules Regarding Acceptance

### L02.6 · Model Exam Question · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 2) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** State the statutory rules governing a valid acceptance under the Contract Act, 1872.

**Answer.**
Under the **Contract Act, 1872**, acceptance turns an open offer into a binding promise [**Section 2(b)**]. For an acceptance to be legally valid, it must satisfy the following statutory rules:

1. **Absolute and Unqualified [Section 7(1)]:**
   Acceptance must conform strictly to all terms of the offer. Any variation or condition acts as a counter-offer. A counter-offer cancels the original offer.

2. **Usual and Reasonable Manner [Section 7(2)]:**
   Acceptance must follow the requested method. If no method is stated, it must follow normal business practice. If the acceptor uses another method, the offeror must object promptly. Otherwise, the acceptance stands.

3. **Performance of Conditions [Section 8]:**
   Under **Section 8**, performing the requested acts counts as acceptance (*Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co.*). Taking the offered consideration also confirms the deal.

4. **Must be Communicated:**
   Mental acceptance is not enough. The offeree must communicate assent to the offeror. Silence does not amount to acceptance.

5. **Communication Rules [Section 4]:**
   Acceptance binds the offeror the moment it is posted. But it binds the acceptor only when the offeror receives it.

6. **Acceptance Must Precede Revocation [Section 5]:**
   The party must accept while the offer stays open. An offeree cannot accept a dead or revoked offer.

## L02.7 · Applied: Offer and Acceptance in Government Tenders

### 2024 Q.6(b) · 04 Marks

*Section B · CO1 · Asked in: 2024 Final*

**Question.** Apply the rules of offer and acceptance to a situation where a situation where an ECE firm is bidding for a government project.  
*(Note: "a situation where a situation where" is duplicated in original)*

**Answer.**
We analyze this public procurement scenario using the four-step legal framework under the **Contract Act, 1872**:

1. **Facts and Issue:**
   A government agency invites bids for an ECE project, like fiber rollout. A tech firm submits a formal bid. The legal question is clear: which step forms the offer, which is acceptance, and when does a binding contract exist?

2. **Applicable Law and Sections:**
   - **Contract Act, 1872:** **Section 2(a)** (Proposal), **Section 2(b)** (Acceptance), **Section 4** (Communication), **Section 7** (Absolute acceptance).
   - **Legal Doctrine:** Distinction between an **Offer** and an **Invitation to Treat (Invitation to Offer)**.

3. **Legal Analysis:**
   - **Tender Notice is an Invitation to Treat:** A tender advertisement or Request for Proposal (RFP) is not an offer. It is an invitation to treat. The agency invites engineering firms to make offers.
   - **Bid Submission is the Legal Offer [Section 2(a)]:** The ECE firm submits its technical and financial bid. This submission forms the true legal offer. The firm becomes the offeror, and the agency is the offeree.
   - **Evaluation and Counter-Offer [Section 7(1)]:** The agency might request a price cut or a schedule change. This request is a counter-offer. Under **Section 7(1)**, any counter-offer cancels the original bid.
   - **Award Notice is Legal Acceptance [Sections 2(b), 4]:** The agency issues an award letter or Work Order. This acts as formal acceptance. Under **Section 4**, acceptance binds the government the moment it sends the letter.

4. **Direct Conclusion:**
   The tender notice is only an invitation to offer. The firm's bid is the legal offer. A binding contract forms only when the agency issues its official Notification of Award.

## L02.8 · Applied: Contractual Risk Mitigation in ECE R&D

### 2023 Q.3(c) · 04 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Propose strategies for mitigating contractual risks in research and development agreements within the field of ECE.

**Answer.**
ECE R&D projects involve high technical risks and uncertain outcomes. Examples include chip design, IoT firmware, and 5G protocols. To reduce contractual disputes under the **Contract Act, 1872**, ECE firms should adopt six key strategies:

1. **Define Objective Specifications and Scope [Section 29]:**
   Under **Section 29**, vague agreements are void. R&D contracts must have a clear Statement of Work (SOW). They should set measurable targets, error limits, and clear test steps.

2. **Clarify Intellectual Property (IP) Rights:**
   Agreements must separate old background IP from newly created foreground IP. The contract should state patent rights, software licensing, and commercial use in writing.

3. **Adopt Milestone-Based Phased Performance [Sections 51 to 54]:**
   Divide delivery into clear milestones, like prototype testing and field trials. Link client payments to milestone sign-offs. This limits financial risk if a stage fails.

4. **Include Force Majeure and Impossibility Clauses [Section 56]:**
   R&D faces chip shortages and sudden rule changes. Contracts should define force majeure events. This sets clear relief terms before invoking frustration under **Section 56**.

5. **Incorporate Strict Confidentiality Terms (NDA):**
   Protect circuit schematics, source code, and algorithms. Add non-disclosure terms and pre-set damages under **Section 74** for unauthorized leaks.

6. **Specify a Multi-Tier Dispute Resolution Ladder:**
   Set a clear dispute ladder: technical review, executive talks, mediation, and arbitration. This avoids costly court battles and keeps business relations intact.
