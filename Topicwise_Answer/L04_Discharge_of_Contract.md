## L04.1 · Methods of Termination or Discharge of a Contract

### 2024 Q.6(c) · 04 Marks

*Section B · CO1 · Asked in: 2024 Final*

**Question.** Discuss the methods of termination of a contract.

**Answer.**

**1. Definition of Discharge of Contract:**
A contract creates legal obligations between parties. When these obligations end, the contract is discharged. Both parties are then freed from any further liability.

**2. Seven Statutory Methods of Discharge:**
Under the Contract Act, 1872, a contract may terminate in seven distinct ways:

1. **Discharge by Performance (Sections 37 and 38):**
   This is the natural mode of ending a contract. Both parties fulfill their promises completely. It includes actual performance and attempted performance (valid tender).
2. **Discharge by Mutual Agreement or Consent (Sections 62 and 63):**
   Parties agree to end, replace, or modify their contract. Methods include novation, rescission, alteration, remission, and waiver.
3. **Discharge by Subsequent or Supervening Impossibility (Section 56):**
   Performance becomes impossible or unlawful after contract formation. External events beyond party control cause this impossibility. This rule is the Doctrine of Frustration.
4. **Discharge by Operation of Law:**
   A contract terminates through legal rules without regard to party intentions. Causes include death in personal contracts, a court order of insolvency, or merger of rights.
5. **Discharge by Lapse of Time:**
   Under the Limitation Act, 1908, a party must sue within a set period (usually 3 years). If that time expires without a lawsuit, the court bars any legal remedy.
6. **Discharge by Unauthorized Material Alteration:**
   A party might alter a material term in a written contract without consent. This unauthorized change discharges the contract. The altered document becomes void in court.
7. **Discharge by Breach of Contract (Section 39):**
   One party fails or refuses to perform his contractual duty. Breach can be an actual breach on the due date. It can also be an anticipatory breach before the due date.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 7 Modes of Discharge
> Remember **P-M-I-O-L-A-B** (*"Promises Must Inspire Our Lawful Agreed Boundaries"*):
> - **P**: **P**erformance (`Sec 37–38` · actual or tender)
> - **M**: **M**utual agreement (`Sec 62–63` · novation, rescission, alteration)
> - **I**: **I**mpossibility / Frustration (`Sec 56` · supervening event)
> - **O**: **O**peration of law (death, court order of insolvency, merger)
> - **L**: **L**apse of time (Limitation Act · 3 years)
> - **A**: **A**lteration (unauthorized material change)
> - **B**: **B**reach of contract (`Sec 39` · actual or anticipatory)

> [!tip] Exam Strategy for 04 Marks
> State the definition first. Then list all 7 methods with their exact Contract Act section numbers. Keep each description to one or two short sentences.

## L04.2 · Discharge by Performance or Tender

*Tested inside 2024 Q.6(c). Prescribed as a high-yield standalone topic under Chapter 2.*

### L04.2 · Model Exam Question · 03 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 2) · Target: Part (a) or (b)*

**Question.** Explain discharge of contract by performance. Distinguish between actual performance and attempted performance (tender) with relevant statutory provisions.

**Answer.**

**1. Meaning of Performance (Section 37):**
Under **Section 37** of the Contract Act, 1872, contracting parties must fulfill their promises. They must either perform them or offer to perform them. Performance is the most common way to end a contract.

**2. Actual Performance vs Attempted Performance (Tender):**

- **Actual Performance:** Both parties fulfill their promises fully according to the agreed terms, time, and place. Neither party owes any remaining obligation.
  - *Example:* An ECE vendor delivers 50 server racks on time. The buyer then pays the agreed price.
- **Attempted Performance or Tender (Section 38):** The promisor offers to perform his promise, but the promisee refuses to accept it.
  - *Statutory Rule:* Under **Section 38**, a valid tender excuses the promisor from non-performance. He does not lose his contractual rights.

**3. Essentials of a Valid Tender under Section 38:**
1. It must be unconditional.
2. It must be made at a proper time and place.
3. The promisee must have a reasonable chance to inspect the goods or services.

## L04.3 · Discharge by Mutual Agreement or Consent

*Tested inside 2024 Q.6(c). Prescribed under Chapter 2.*

### L04.3 · Model Exam Question · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 2) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Explain how a contract is discharged by mutual agreement under the Contract Act, 1872. Define Novation, Rescission, Alteration, Remission, and Waiver with examples.

**Answer.**

**1. Statutory Principle (Section 62):**
Under **Section 62** of the Contract Act, 1872:
> *"If the parties to a contract agree to substitute a new contract for it, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract need not be performed."*

Mutual consent of all contracting parties is necessary. The old contract is discharged by creating the new agreement.

**2. Five Forms of Mutual Discharge:**

1. **Novation (Section 62):**
   Parties replace the original contract with a new one. Novation can change terms between the same parties. It can also substitute a new debtor or creditor.
   - *Example:* A owes B Tk. 100,000. A, B, and C agree that B will accept C as his debtor in place of A. A's debt is discharged.
2. **Rescission (Section 62):**
   Parties agree to cancel the contract before performance is due. Both sides are released from their promises.
   - *Example:* An ECE firm agrees to develop custom firmware for a client. Before work starts, both parties agree to cancel the project.
3. **Alteration (Section 62):**
   Parties modify one or more terms of an existing contract by mutual consent. The original terms end, and the altered terms take effect.
   - *Example:* A buyer and seller agree to change the delivery date from 1st March to 1st May.
4. **Remission (Section 63):**
   Under **Section 63**, a promisee may accept a lesser sum or lesser performance. No fresh consideration is needed.
   - *Example:* A owes B Tk. 50,000. B agrees to accept Tk. 30,000 in full satisfaction of the debt. The entire debt is discharged.
5. **Waiver:**
   A party intentionally abandons his contractual rights. When a party waives a right, the other party is released from that obligation.
   - *Example:* A buyer waives his right to inspect goods before shipment and accepts immediate delivery.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: 5 Forms of Mutual Discharge
> Remember **N-R-A-R-W**:
> - **N**: **N**ovation (`Sec 62` · substitute new contract or new parties)
> - **R**: **R**escission (`Sec 62` · cancel contract before performance)
> - **A**: **A**lteration (`Sec 62` · modify contract terms by consent)
> - **R**: **R**emission (`Sec 63` · accept lesser amount or performance)
> - **W**: **W**aiver (voluntarily surrender legal rights)

> [!tip] Memory Hook: Preview of Frustration Grounds (D-D-L-N-W) vs Excuses (C-D-S-F-P)
> See full details in [[#L04.4 · Subsequent or Supervening Impossibility (Doctrine of Frustration)|L04.4]]:
> - **5 Valid Grounds (D-D-L-N-W):** **D**estruction, **D**eath, **L**aw change, **N**on-occurrence, **W**ar.
> - **5 Rejected Excuses (C-D-S-F-P):** **C**ommercial hardship, **D**efault of 3rd party, **S**trikes, **F**ailure of one object, **P**arty self-induced.

## L04.4 · Subsequent or Supervening Impossibility (Doctrine of Frustration)

*Confirmed by Shoaib Sir [01:15–02:22] as the core target for the 10-mark Applied Case Study and final exam theory questions.*

### L04.4 · Model Exam Question · 05 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 2) · Target: Part (b), (c), or Applied Case Question*

**Question.** Explain the Doctrine of Supervening Impossibility (Doctrine of Frustration) under Section 56 of the Contract Act, 1872. State the valid grounds of frustration and the situations that do not discharge a contract.

**Answer.**

**1. Meaning and Section 56:**
Initial impossibility makes an agreement void from the start (*void ab initio*).
Subsequent impossibility arises after contract formation. The act was possible at first. An unforeseen event later makes it impossible or unlawful.

Under **Section 56 (Paragraph 2)** of the Contract Act, 1872:
> *"A contract to do an act which, after the contract is made, becomes impossible, or, by reason of some event which the promisor could not prevent, unlawful, becomes void when the act becomes impossible or unlawful."*

This rule is the **Doctrine of Frustration**. When frustration occurs, the contract ends automatically. Under **Section 65**, any party who received an advantage must return it.

**2. Five Valid Grounds of Frustration:**

1. **Destruction of Subject Matter:** The essential subject matter is destroyed without fault of either party (*Taylor v. Caldwell*). *Example:* An accidental fire burns down the factory where goods were to be produced.
2. **Change of Law:** A new law or government order makes performance illegal. *Example:* A government order bans importing specific telecom transmitters.
3. **Failure of the Ultimate Purpose:** An event forming the foundation of the contract does not occur (*Krell v. Henry*). *Example:* A person rents a room solely to view a state parade that is later canceled.
4. **Death or Personal Incapacity:** In personal service contracts, death or serious illness discharges the contract. *Example:* An expert engineer falls severely ill and cannot lead a scheduled technical training.
5. **Outbreak of War:** War turns the other contracting party into an alien enemy. This war terminates the contract.

**3. Situations That Do NOT Frustrate a Contract (Non-Qualifying Excuses):**

1. **Commercial Hardship:** Higher material prices, currency drops, or lost profits do not discharge a contract. Performance is still physically possible.
2. **Strikes, Lock-outs, and Civil Disturbances:** Labor strikes or factory lock-outs do not discharge a contract. They excuse delay only if the contract has a clear *force majeure* clause.
3. **Default by a Third Party:** A supplier or subcontractor may fail to deliver parts. This third-party default does not excuse the promisor from his duty to the buyer.
4. **Partial Impossibility:** A contract may have several purposes. If only one purpose fails, the contract remains valid for the surviving purpose.
5. **Self-Induced Impossibility:** The impossibility may arise from a party's own deliberate act or neglect. In that case, the event is a breach of contract, not frustration.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: Valid Frustration Grounds vs. Rejected Excuses (Sec 56)
> Keep these two mnemonics ready for Section 56 questions:
>
> **5 Valid Grounds of Frustration (D-D-L-N-W):**
> - **D**: **D**estruction of subject matter (*Taylor v. Caldwell*)
> - **D**: **D**eath or personal incapacity in personal services
> - **L**: **L**aw change making performance illegal
> - **N**: **N**on-occurrence of foundational event (*Krell v. Henry*)
> - **W**: **W**ar outbreak (alien enemy)
>
> **5 Rejected Non-Qualifying Excuses (C-D-S-F-P):**
> - **C**: **C**ommercial hardship (price surge or lost profit)
> - **D**: **D**efault of third party or subcontractor
> - **S**: **S**trikes, lock-outs, or civil unrest
> - **F**: **F**ailure of one object (partial impossibility)
> - **P**: **P**arty's self-induced impossibility (deliberate act or neglect)

### L04.4 · Model Case Study · Supervening Impossibility vs Commercial Hardship · 10 Marks

*High-Yield 10-Mark Applied Target (Chapter 2 · Doctrine of Frustration) · 3 Sub-Questions totaling 10 Marks*

#### Case 1 Factual Scenario

*Apex Electronics Ltd.* (an ECE manufacturing firm in Dhaka) contracts with *PowerGrid Bangladesh Ltd.* to supply 100 custom-designed industrial rectifiers for Tk. 5,000,000 by 15th December 2025. PowerGrid pays an advance of Tk. 1,000,000 upon signing.

On 1st November 2025, a global shortage hit the semiconductor market. The price of specialized microcontroller chips jumped by 300%. If Apex fulfills the contract, it will suffer a financial loss of Tk. 1,500,000. Apex writes to PowerGrid to cancel the deal due to market hardship. PowerGrid refuses.

On 20th November 2025, a municipal power surge sparked an accidental fire. The fire burned down Apex's only manufacturing plant without any fault by Apex. All machinery and 60 partially finished rectifiers were destroyed. Apex informs PowerGrid that performance is impossible and that the contract is terminated.

PowerGrid sues Apex for breach of contract. It demands Tk. 2,000,000 in damages for delay. PowerGrid also argues that Apex used the fire to escape higher chip prices.

#### Case 1 Sub-Questions

- **(i)** Can Apex claim discharge of contract based on the chip price surge? Explain under relevant legal principles. **(03 Marks)**
- **(ii)** Did the accidental factory fire cause Supervening Impossibility under Section 56? What is the legal status of the contract? **(03 Marks)**
- **(iii)** Is PowerGrid entitled to damages for breach of contract? Can PowerGrid recover its Tk. 1,000,000 advance payment? **(04 Marks)**

#### Case 1 Model Solution

##### Case 1 Solution to Sub-Question (i) · 03 Marks

**1. Facts of the Case:**
Apex faced a 300% price surge for semiconductor chips. Fulfilling the order would cause Apex a loss of Tk. 1,500,000. Apex sought to cancel the contract on this ground.

**2. Applicable Law & Section:**
Contract Act, 1872, **Section 56** (Doctrine of Supervening Impossibility) and the rule governing **Commercial Hardship**.

**3. Legal Analysis:**
The law distinguishes physical or legal impossibility from commercial difficulty. A contract is not frustrated simply because performance becomes more expensive or unprofitable.
- The semiconductor chips were still available on the open market, although at a higher price.
- High raw material costs and reduced profits are ordinary commercial business risks.
- Under Section 56, commercial hardship never excuses a party from performing an agreed promise.

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
Apex Electronics Ltd. cannot claim discharge of contract based on the chip price surge. The contract remained fully binding on 1st November 2025.

---

##### Case 1 Solution to Sub-Question (ii) · 03 Marks

**1. Facts of the Case:**
On 20th November 2025, an accidental fire burned down Apex's sole factory. A municipal power surge caused the fire without any fault by Apex. All work-in-progress rectifiers were destroyed.

**2. Applicable Law & Section:**
Contract Act, 1872, **Section 56 (Paragraph 2)** and the **Doctrine of Frustration** (*Taylor v. Caldwell* rule on destruction of subject matter).

**3. Legal Analysis:**
Under Section 56, an unforeseen event may destroy the essential subject matter. If this occurs beyond the promisor's control, performance becomes physically impossible.
- The rectifiers were custom-designed units. Apex could only build them in that specific facility before 15th December 2025.
- An external municipal power surge caused the fire. This fact proves that Apex was not negligent.
- The fire destroyed the manufacturing facility and ruined the foundation of performance.

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
The accidental fire constitutes genuine Supervening Impossibility under Section 56. The contract became void automatically on 20th November 2025. Both parties were freed from future performance.

---

##### Case 1 Solution to Sub-Question (iii) · 04 Marks

**1. Facts of the Case:**
PowerGrid sued Apex for Tk. 2,000,000 in breach damages. PowerGrid also demanded the return of its Tk. 1,000,000 advance payment.

**2. Applicable Law & Section:**
Contract Act, 1872:
- **Section 56** (Discharge by Frustration)
- **Section 65** (Restitution of advantage received under void agreement)
- **Section 73** (Compensation for loss caused by breach)

**3. Legal Analysis:**
- *Claim for Damages (Section 73):* A party can claim damages only after a wrongful breach of contract. Here, the contract was discharged under Section 56 due to supervening impossibility. The contract became void without fault by Apex. Apex committed no breach of contract. Therefore, PowerGrid cannot claim any compensation or damages.
- *Claim for Advance Return (Section 65):* Under **Section 65**, a contract may become void. Any party who received an advantage under that agreement must return it. Apex received an advance of Tk. 1,000,000 for rectifiers it can no longer deliver. Apex cannot keep this money.

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
PowerGrid is not entitled to recover Tk. 2,000,000 in damages. But PowerGrid is legally entitled to a full refund of its Tk. 1,000,000 advance under Section 65.

## L04.5 · Discharge by Operation of Law, Lapse of Time, and Material Alteration

*Tested inside 2024 Q.6(c). Standard final exam short question.*

### L04.5 · Model Exam Question · 03 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 2) · Target: Part (a) or (b)*

**Question.** Describe how a contract is discharged by operation of law, lapse of time, and unauthorized material alteration.

**Answer.**

**1. Discharge by Operation of Law:**
A contract terminates automatically by legal rules in three main situations:
- **Death:** In contracts based on personal skill, death ends the contract. In other contracts, rights and duties pass to legal heirs.
- **Insolvency:** A court order may declare a debtor insolvent. His assets and debts then pass to an Official Assignee. This court decision frees the debtor from prior contract liabilities.
- **Merger:** An inferior right may merge into a superior right held by the same person. When this happens, the inferior contract terminates. *Example:* A tenant buys the leased property.

**2. Discharge by Lapse of Time:**
Under the **Limitation Act, 1908**, a party must sue within a set time limit. The limitation period for simple contract claims is **3 years** from the breach date. If no suit is filed within 3 years, the court bars the remedy. The contract then becomes legally unenforceable.

**3. Discharge by Material Alteration:**
A party might alter a material term in a written contract without consent. Doing so discharges the contract and makes it void.
- A material alteration changes the legal effect or duties of the parties. Common examples include changing the price, the delivery date, or party names.
- Courts will not admit the altered document as evidence. The altering party loses all legal rights under it.

## L04.6 · Discharge by Breach of Contract

*Tested inside 2024 Q.6(c). High-yield comparison topic.*

### L04.6 · Model Exam Question · 03 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 2) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Distinguish between Actual Breach and Anticipatory Breach of contract under the Contract Act, 1872. What remedies are available to the injured party upon an anticipatory breach?

**Answer.**

**1. Meaning of Breach of Contract:**
A breach occurs when a party fails or refuses to perform without a legal excuse. This breach frees the injured party from his obligations. It also gives him the legal right to claim damages.

**2. Actual Breach vs Anticipatory Breach:**

| Comparison Parameter | Actual Breach | Anticipatory Breach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing of Breach** | Occurs on the due date of performance or during actual performance. | Occurs before the scheduled date of performance arrives. |
| **Mode of Breach** | Party refuses or fails to perform when performance is due. | Party repudiates liability by words (express) or acts (implied) in advance. |
| **Governing Law** | General breach principles under Section 73. | **Section 39** of the Contract Act, 1872. |
| **ECE Example** | A vendor fails to deliver microchips on the agreed date of 1st October. | A vendor announces on 10th September that he will not deliver on 1st October. |

**3. Options of the Injured Party under Section 39:**
When anticipatory breach occurs, the injured party has two choices:
1. **Rescind Immediately:** Treat the contract as ended at once. Sue for damages without waiting for the due date.
2. **Keep the Contract Alive:** Refuse the repudiation and wait until the due date. The injured party can then demand performance. But an unexpected impossibility may strike before the due date. In that case, the contract ends, and the right to damages is lost.

### L04.7 · Model Case Study · Mutual Novation and Anticipatory Breach in Telecom Deployment · 10 Marks

#### Case 2 Factual Scenario

*FiberNet Ltd.* (a nationwide telecom provider) contracts with *TeleSoft Solutions Ltd.* on 1st January 2025. TeleSoft agrees to develop a fiber billing platform for Tk. 3,000,000 by 30th June 2025. FiberNet pays an advance of Tk. 600,000 upon signing the contract.

On 1st April 2025, TeleSoft sent an official letter to FiberNet. It stated: *"Our key software architects have left. We cannot deliver the software on 30th June 2025. We repudiate the agreement and withdraw from the project."*

FiberNet did not sue right away. Instead, FiberNet called a meeting on 15th April 2025 with TeleSoft and *CloudCore Systems Ltd.*

At this meeting, all three parties signed a written tripartite agreement:
1. TeleSoft is fully released from all future obligations under the original contract.
2. CloudCore agrees to take over the codebase and complete the billing platform by 31st August 2025 for Tk. 2,400,000.
3. FiberNet accepts CloudCore in place of TeleSoft.

Two weeks later, FiberNet demanded the return of its Tk. 600,000 advance. TeleSoft refused to pay. It argued that the old contract was discharged. It also claimed that FiberNet lost all refund rights by signing the new deal.

#### Case 2 Sub-Questions

- **(i)** What was the legal effect of TeleSoft's letter on 1st April 2025? What legal options did FiberNet hold under Section 39? **(03 Marks)**
- **(ii)** Evaluate the tripartite agreement signed on 15th April 2025 under Section 62. Is TeleSoft discharged from its original contractual liabilities? **(03 Marks)**
- **(iii)** Must TeleSoft return the Tk. 600,000 advance payment to FiberNet? Justify your decision under the principles of Novation and Restitution. **(04 Marks)**

#### Case 2 Model Solution

##### Case 2 Solution to Sub-Question (i) · 03 Marks

**1. Facts of the Case:**
On 1st April 2025, TeleSoft sent a letter withdrawing from the project. This notice arrived three months before the agreed due date of 30th June 2025.

**2. Applicable Law & Section:**
Contract Act, 1872, **Section 39** (Anticipatory Breach of Contract).

**3. Legal Analysis:**
Anticipatory breach occurs when a party renounces his liability before performance is due. TeleSoft clearly stated that it would not perform.
Under **Section 39**, FiberNet had two lawful courses of action:
1. *Accept the repudiation immediately:* Treat the contract as ended on 1st April 2025. Sue at once for breach and damages.
2. *Keep the contract alive:* Refuse the repudiation and hold TeleSoft liable. Then wait for actual performance on 30th June 2025.

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
TeleSoft's letter was an Anticipatory Breach of contract. FiberNet had two legal choices. It could rescind at once and sue for damages, or wait until the due date.

---

##### Case 2 Solution to Sub-Question (ii) · 03 Marks

**1. Facts of the Case:**
FiberNet, TeleSoft, and CloudCore signed a written agreement. This pact substituted CloudCore for TeleSoft and released TeleSoft from future duties.

**2. Applicable Law & Section:**
Contract Act, 1872, **Section 62** (Discharge by Novation).

**3. Legal Analysis:**
Novation means replacing an existing contract with a new one. This substitution can happen between the same parties or involve a new party.
- For a valid novation, all parties (the creditor, the original debtor, and the new debtor) must give mutual consent.
- FiberNet, TeleSoft, and CloudCore all signed the agreement willingly.
- CloudCore promised to complete the work, and FiberNet accepted CloudCore. This formed the consideration for releasing TeleSoft.
- Under Section 62, a valid novation extinguishes the original contract completely.

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
The tripartite agreement is a valid Novation under Section 62. TeleSoft is legally discharged from all future duties under the original contract.

---

##### Case 2 Solution to Sub-Question (iii) · 04 Marks

**1. Facts of the Case:**
TeleSoft refused to return the Tk. 600,000 advance after novation. It claimed the original contract was discharged without an express refund clause.

**2. Applicable Law & Section:**
Contract Act, 1872, **Section 62** (Novation terms) and **Section 65** (Restitution / unjust enrichment).

**3. Legal Analysis:**
- Under novation, the new agreement replaces the rights and duties of the old contract.
- The original contract price was Tk. 3,000,000. CloudCore agreed to complete the project for Tk. 2,400,000.
- This left the exact Tk. 600,000 balance that FiberNet had already paid.
- TeleSoft delivered no software. If it keeps the advance, it enjoys unjust enrichment without consideration.
- Under **Section 65**, a party must return any advantage received under a void or rescinded contract. This rule holds unless the parties agreed in writing to forfeit the advance.
- Novation freed TeleSoft from future work. But it gave TeleSoft no right to keep unearned advance funds.

**4. Direct Conclusion:**
TeleSoft must return the Tk. 600,000 advance payment to FiberNet. Keeping the advance without delivering value violates Section 65 restitution rules.
