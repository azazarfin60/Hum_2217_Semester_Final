## L01.1 · Definitions of Law

*No direct standalone question in past finals or class tests. Prescribed under Chapter 1 ("Study All").*

### L01.1 · Model Exam Question 1 · 03 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 1) · Target: Part (a) or (b)*

**Question.** Define Law. State the definitions of law given by Holland, Salmond, and Woodrow Wilson, and highlight the key characteristics of law.

**Answer.**

**1. General Definition:**
Law is a system of uniform rules created and enforced by the state. It regulates human conduct, maintains social order, upholds justice, and protects individual rights and property. In legal theory, law is the command of the sovereign backed by sanctions.

**2. Key Juristic Definitions:**
- **Holland:** "Law is a rule of external human action enforced by the sovereign political authority."
- **Salmond:** "Law is the body of principles recognized and applied by the State in the administration of justice."
- **Woodrow Wilson:** "Law is that portion of the established habit and thought of mankind which has gained distinct and formal recognition in the shape of uniform rules backed by the authority and power of the government."

**3. Three Essential Characteristics of Law (Holland's Analysis):**
1. **Rule for Human Action:** Law applies only to human beings living in an organized society.
2. **Regulation of External Conduct:** Law regulates outward physical actions and behavior, not internal thoughts or private beliefs.
3. **State Enforcement:** Law comes from sovereign political authority and is backed by state penalties for disobedience.

> [!tip] Memory Hook: **H-E-S** -> **H**uman action, **E**xternal conduct, **S**tate enforcement.

## L01.2 · Definitions of Industrial Law and Labour Law

### 2023 Q.2(a) · 02 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Define Industrial Law and Labor Law.

**Answer.**

**1. Industrial Law:**
Industrial law regulates working conditions in factories, workshops, and industrial establishments. It also governs the relationship between employers and employees.

It has two primary objectives:
- Preserving the health, safety, and welfare of industrial workers.
- Maintaining good relations and settling disputes between employers and employees.

**2. Labor Law:**
Labor law is the legal framework that governs employment relationships. It defines the statutory rights, duties, and terms of employment for workers, employers, and trade unions.

It specifically regulates:
- Minimum wages and payment schedules.
- Working hours, rest intervals, and statutory leaves.
- Maternity protection and financial benefits for female workers.
- Occupational safety, accident compensation, and collective bargaining rights.

## L01.3 · Industrial Law vs. Labour Law

*No direct standalone question in past finals or class tests. High-yield distinction from Chapter 1.*

### L01.3 · Model Exam Question 1 · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 1) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Distinguish between Industrial Law and Labour Law using a structured comparison table.

**Answer.**

Industrial law regulates factory operations and industrial enterprises broadly. In contrast, labour law focuses specifically on worker protection and the terms of employment contracts.

The table below summarizes their key differences:

| Parameter | Industrial Law | Labour Law |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Primary Focus** | Regulates industrial operations, factory environments, and enterprise management. | Regulates employment terms, worker rights, and labor welfare. |
| **Scope of Subjects** | Covers factory licensing, working conditions, environmental rules, and industrial relations. | Covers wages, leaves, maternity benefits, trade unions, and compensation for injury. |
| **Core Objective** | Promotes industrial peace, workplace safety, and smooth factory production. | Protects workers from exploitation and balances bargaining power. |
| **Applicable Statutes** | Includes factory legislation, environmental protection acts, and corporate compliance laws. | Centers on **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006** and trade union regulations. |
| **Target Entities** | Applies broadly to entire industrial establishments, machines, and operational systems. | Applies directly to employees, employers, and trade union organizations. |

## L01.4 · Why ECE Students Should Learn Law

*No direct standalone question in past finals or class tests. Taught in SI Sir Class 1 (feeds applied engineering legal questions).*

### L01.4 · Model Exam Question 1 · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 1) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Explain why students of Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE) should learn law. State and explain the nine key reasons.

**Answer.**

Modern engineering practice extends far beyond circuit design and programming. Technical systems operate within strict legal, commercial, and regulatory frameworks.

Students of Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE) should learn law for nine distinct reasons:

> [!tip] Memory Hook (3x3 Rule): **Tech & IP** (IPR, Compliance, Regulatory changes) · **Business & Deals** (Contracts, Startups, Communication) · **Safety & Ethics** (Liability, Ethics, Risk management).

1. **Intellectual Property Rights (IPR):**
   ECE engineers design proprietary integrated circuits, firmware, and software algorithms. Understanding patent, trademark, and copyright law helps protect technical innovations from theft and infringement.

2. **Regulatory Compliance:**
   Engineers must design hardware that complies with technical standards. These include telecommunications regulations, radio frequency spectrum allocations, and electronic waste disposal rules.

3. **Contracts and Agreements:**
   Engineers frequently negotiate with vendors, clients, and subcontractors. Contract law helps them evaluate, draft, and enforce procurement contracts, service-level agreements, and non-disclosure agreements (NDAs).

4. **Liability and Responsibility:**
   System failures in medical electronics, power grids, or automated control systems can cause severe physical harm. Knowledge of tort law and product liability helps engineers avoid professional negligence.

5. **Ethical Considerations:**
   Engineers develop systems handling sensitive personal data and network traffic. Cyber law and data privacy statutes guide ethical practices regarding surveillance, encryption, and data protection.

6. **Startups and Entrepreneurship:**
   Many ECE graduates launch hardware and software startups. Business law guides them in corporate incorporation, founder equity splits, intellectual property assignment, and venture funding.

7. **Regulatory Changes:**
   Technology laws evolve rapidly in artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, and frequency licensing. Staying informed on statutory updates prevents regulatory fines and operational shutdowns.

8. **Communication and Collaboration:**
   Engineers must collaborate with corporate attorneys, government regulators, and international partners. A solid grounding in legal terminology enables clear and precise cross-disciplinary communication.

9. **Risk Management:**
   Recognizing legal exposures early helps engineers reduce technical and operational hazards. This proactive approach protects tech firms from costly litigation and contract disputes.

## L01.5 · Law, Government, and Society; Statutory Scope of Labour Law

*Background conceptual material from SI Sir Class 1.*

### L01.5 · Model Exam Question 1 · 03 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 1) · Target: Part (a) or (b)*

**Question.** Explain the reciprocal relationship between law, government, and society. Also, state the primary statutory scope of domestic labour law in Bangladesh.

**Answer.**

**1. Mechanics of Law and Governance:**
Law and state governance function through a continuous structural cycle:

$$
\text{Law} \longrightarrow \text{Enforcement} \longrightarrow \text{Government / Parliament}
$$

**2. Reciprocal Relationship:**
Law, government, and society depend directly on one another:
- **Society Requires Law:** Peaceful collective living requires common rules. Without law, anarchy spreads and citizens lose their basic rights.
- **Government Creates and Enforces Law:** Parliament enacts statutes to address societal needs. The executive branch and police enforce compliance through legal penalties.
- **Society Empowers Government:** Citizens give authority and legitimacy to the government through constitutional processes and voluntary compliance.
- **Law Constrains Government:** The legal system prevents abuse of power and protects individual civil liberties.

So, society shapes law, government enforces law, and law stabilizes society.

**3. Statutory Scope of Domestic Labour Law:**
In Bangladesh, domestic labour standards are codified in **The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (Act XLII of 2006)**.

Key aspects of its statutory scope include:
- **Consolidation:** The Act repealed and unified 25 separate, outdated labour laws into a single modern code.
- **Size and Structure:** It comprises **326 sections** organized into 21 distinct chapters.
- **Jurisdiction:** It applies across factories, commercial establishments, transport services, and industrial workshops throughout Bangladesh.
- **Core Subject Areas:** It regulates conditions of service, working hours, occupational health, fire safety, welfare facilities, and maternity benefits. It also governs trade unions, collective bargaining, and dispute resolution in Labour Courts.
