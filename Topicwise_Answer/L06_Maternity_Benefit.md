> [!tip] Memory Hook (Sec 45–50 Ladder)
> Remember Chapter IV using this 6-step statutory ladder:
> - **Sec 45 (Work Ban):** Absolute work ban for 8 weeks after delivery. No arduous work 10 weeks before delivery.
> - **Sec 46 (Right & 3 Conditions):** Right to 16 weeks paid benefit (8 before + 8 after). Requires 6 months service and fewer than 2 surviving children.
> - **Sec 47 (Notice & 3 Payout Options):** Give notice before or within 7 days after delivery. Pick from 3 payment options (split, delayed, or lump sum).
> - **Sec 48 (Wage Formula):** Must pay in cash. Daily wage = past 3 months wages $\div$ actual days worked. Total = daily wage $\times 112$ days.
> - **Sec 49 (Death Rules):** If mother dies but child lives, pay child's caretaker. If both die, pay nominee or legal representative.
> - **Sec 50 (Dismissal Ban):** No dismissal without sufficient cause from 6 months before delivery to 8 weeks after delivery.

## L06.1 · Prohibition of Engagement of Women Workers (Section 45)

*Never asked in past final papers or CTs. Prescribed by Shoaib Sir as the top priority topic in Chapter 3.*

### L06.1 · Model Exam Question · 03 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Target: Part (a) or (b)*

**Question.** Under what circumstances does the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 prohibit the employment of women workers before and after childbirth? State the statutory rules under Section 45.

**Answer.**

**Section 45** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 restricts the employment of women around childbirth.
The section lays down three clear rules:

1. **Absolute Post-Delivery Prohibition:**
   - **Section 45(1)** forbids an employer from knowingly employing a woman during the 8 weeks after delivery.
   - **Section 45(2)** forbids the woman worker herself from working in any establishment during those same 8 weeks.
   - This rule creates a strict dual ban binding both the employer and the worker.

2. **Pre-Delivery and Post-Delivery Restrictions on Arduous Work:**
   - Under **Section 45(3)**, an employer cannot assign a woman worker to arduous work or long standing.
   - Any work likely to harm her health is also barred.
   - This restriction applies if the employer believes or she states that delivery is expected within 10 weeks.
   - It also applies if she delivered a child within the preceding 10 weeks to the employer's knowledge.

3. **Statutory Exception for Tea Plantations:**
   - The proviso to **Section 45(3)** allows a woman worker in a tea plantation to perform light work.
   - An approved medical practitioner must certify that she is physically fit for such work.
   - The employer pays normal wages for this work alongside her statutory maternity benefit.

## L06.2 · Right to and Liability for Maternity Benefit (Section 46)

*Never asked in past final papers or CTs. Prescribed by Shoaib Sir.*

### L06.2 · Model Exam Question 1 · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Explain the conditions governing maternity benefit rights and employer liability under Section 46 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

Under **Section 46** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, the right to maternity benefit depends on three statutory conditions:

1. **Statutory Benefit Period (Section 46(1)):**
   - Every employed woman is entitled to paid maternity benefit for 16 weeks total.
   - The period covers 8 weeks before delivery and 8 weeks immediately following delivery.
   - The employer is legally bound to pay this benefit.

2. **Qualifying Service Requirement (Section 46(1) Proviso):**
   - The worker must have worked under the employer for at least 6 months before delivery.
   - With less than 6 months of continuous service, she receives no cash maternity benefit.

3. **Restriction Based on Surviving Children (Section 46(2)):**
   - No maternity benefit is payable if she has 2 or more surviving children at delivery.
   - In that case, she receives only ordinary leave due to her, like casual or sick leave.

| Statutory Condition | Legal Rule under Section 46 | Outcome / Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration of Benefit** | 16 weeks (8 weeks pre-natal + 8 weeks post-natal) | Paid leave with full average wages |
| **Minimum Qualifying Service** | Not less than 6 months before delivery | Disqualified from cash benefit if $< 6$ months |
| **Parity Limit** | Must have fewer than 2 surviving children | If $\ge 2$ surviving children, unpaid or ordinary leave only |

### L06.2 · Model Exam Question 2 · 05 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Comprehensive Legal Framework*

**Question.** Discuss the statutory rules regarding maternity welfare facilities and benefits under Chapter IV of the Bangladesh Labour Code, 2006.

**Answer.**

Chapter IV (**Sections 45 to 50**) of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 protects female workers around childbirth.
The statutory framework establishes six core rules:

1. **Prohibition of Work (Section 45):**
   - Employers cannot employ a woman for 8 weeks after delivery.
   - Workers cannot perform arduous or long-standing work during the 10 weeks before delivery.

2. **Right and Duration of Benefit (Section 46):**
   - Female workers receive 16 weeks of paid maternity leave (8 weeks before and 8 weeks after delivery).
   - Entitlement requires at least 6 months of continuous service before delivery.
   - Women with 2 or more surviving children receive unpaid ordinary leave only.

3. **Notice and Claim Procedure (Section 47):**
   - The worker gives oral or written notice before delivery or within 7 days after delivery.
   - The notice names a nominee who can collect benefits if she dies.
   - Payment must be made within 3 working days of producing medical proof.

4. **Calculation of Benefit (Section 48):**
   - The employer must pay the entire benefit in cash.
   - The rate equals the daily average wage earned over the preceding 3 months divided by actual working days.

5. **Death Contingencies (Section 49):**
   - If the mother dies leaving a living child, the employer pays benefit to the child's caretaker.
   - If both mother and child die, benefit is paid to her nominee or legal representative.

6. **Dismissal Protection (Section 50):**
   - An employer cannot dismiss or terminate a woman worker without sufficient cause.
   - This legal protection lasts from 6 months before delivery until 8 weeks after delivery.

## L06.3 · Procedure for Notice and Payment (Section 47)

*Never asked in past final papers or CTs. Prescribed by Shoaib Sir.*

### L06.3 · Model Exam Question · 04 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Describe the procedure regarding notice and disbursement of maternity benefits under Section 47 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. State the three statutory options available for payment.

**Answer.**

Under **Section 47** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, notice and benefit payment follow a clear procedure:

1. **Notice Requirements:**
   - **Notice Before Delivery (Section 47(1)):** A pregnant woman gives oral or written notice expecting delivery within 8 weeks. She also names a nominee to collect payment if she dies. The employer must grant leave starting the next day.
   - **Notice After Delivery (Section 47(2)):** If she gave no pre-natal notice, she must give notice within 7 days of delivery. The employer must grant leave for 8 weeks from the delivery day.

2. **Three Statutory Payment Options (Section 47(4)):**
   The employer must disburse the benefit using the method chosen by the woman:
   - **Option 1 (Two Installments: Before and After Delivery):**
     - First 8 weeks: Paid within 3 working days of submitting a doctor's certificate of expected delivery.
     - Next 8 weeks: Paid within 3 working days of submitting proof of birth.
   - **Option 2 (Two Installments: Both After Delivery):**
     - First 8 weeks: Paid within 3 working days of submitting proof of birth.
     - Next 8 weeks: Paid within 8 weeks after submitting that proof.
   - **Option 3 (Single Lump Sum Payment):**
     - The employer pays the entire 16 weeks of benefit in one lump sum.
     - This payment is due within 3 working days of submitting proof of birth.

3. **Statutory Proof and Time Limit (Section 47(4) Proviso & 47(5)):**
   - The worker must submit proof of birth within 3 months of delivery.
   - A birth certificate under the *Births and Deaths Registration Act, 2004* serves as valid proof.
   - A registered medical practitioner's certificate is also valid.
   - Any other proof acceptable to the employer also suffices.

## L06.4 · Amount and Calculation of Maternity Benefit (Section 48)

*Never asked in past final papers or CTs. Prescribed by Shoaib Sir.*

### L06.4 · Model Exam Question · 03 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Target: Part (a) or (b)*

**Question.** Explain how the amount of maternity benefit is calculated under Section 48 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. Illustrate with a concise numerical example.

**Answer.**

**Section 48** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 sets the calculation method and payment mode:

1. **Payment Mode (Section 48(1)):**
   - The employer must pay maternity benefit wholly in cash.
   - It is calculated using daily, weekly, or monthly average wages.

2. **Statutory Formula (Section 48(2)):**
   - Divide total wages earned during the preceding 3 months by actual days worked in that period:

$$
\text{Daily Average Wage} = \frac{\text{Total Wages Earned in Preceding 3 Months}}{\text{Total Days Actually Worked in That Period}}
$$

   - Total entitlement covers the full statutory period of 16 weeks (112 calendar days):

$$
\text{Total Maternity Benefit} = \text{Daily Average Wage} \times 112\text{ days}
$$

3. **Numerical Illustration:**
   - Suppose a female worker earns BDT 36,000 across 75 actual working days during the preceding 3 months.
   - Her daily average wage is:

$$
\text{Daily Average Wage} = \frac{\text{BDT } 36{,}000}{75\text{ days}} = \text{BDT } 480\text{ per day}
$$

   - Her total benefit for 16 weeks (112 days) is:

$$
\text{Total Benefit} = \text{BDT } 480 \times 112 = \text{BDT } 53{,}760
$$

   - The employer must pay this entire sum in cash.

## L06.5 · Benefit Payment upon Death of Woman or Child (Section 49)

*Never asked in past final papers or CTs. Prescribed by Shoaib Sir.*

### L06.5 · Model Exam Question · 03 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Target: Part (a) or (b)*

**Question.** State the rules under Section 49 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 when a mother or newborn child dies.

**Answer.**

**Section 49** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 governs payment when a woman worker dies during her maternity period:

1. **Death at Delivery or During the Following 8 Weeks (Section 49(1)):**
   - **If the newborn child survives:** The employer pays all due benefits to the child's caretaker.
   - **If the child does not survive:** The employer pays the benefit to her nominee under Section 47(1). If she left no nomination, the money goes to her legal representative.

2. **Death Prior to Childbirth (Section 49(2)):**
   - Suppose the woman dies before delivery during her pre-natal leave period.
   - The employer is liable only for the period up to and including her death day.
   - The employer pays any unpaid benefit to her nominee or legal representative.

3. **Protection Against Recovery (Section 49(2)):**
   - The employer might pay advance benefit exceeding the amount earned before her death.
   - The employer cannot recover this excess payment from her legal representative.

## L06.6 · Protection Against Dismissal and Termination (Section 50)

*Never asked in past final papers or CTs. Prescribed by Shoaib Sir for applied problem-solving.*

### L06.6 · Model Case Study · 04 Marks

*Applied Legal Scenario · Target: Part (c) or Applied Case Study*

**Question.** An employer dismisses a female quality inspector citing departmental restructuring. The dismissal notice was issued when she was six months pregnant, shortly after she informed management of her condition. The company refused to disburse maternity benefits, arguing that she was no longer on company payroll on her delivery date. Evaluate the legality of the employer's actions under Section 50 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

#### 1. Facts and Issue of the Case
An employer dismissed a pregnant worker after learning of her condition. The employer cited restructuring to avoid paying maternity benefits. The legal issue is whether this dismissal is valid and whether she retains her maternity benefits.

#### 2. Applicable Law and Section
**Section 50** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (*Restrictions on termination of employment of a woman in certain cases*).

#### 3. Legal Analysis
- **Section 50** protects women workers from dismissal around childbirth.
- An employer cannot dismiss or terminate a woman worker without sufficient cause.
- This legal protection lasts from 6 months before delivery until 8 weeks after delivery.
- Routine administrative restructuring does not count as sufficient cause under the Act.
- If an employer terminates her without sufficient cause, she still receives all maternity benefits.
- She gave proper notice and met the qualifying service rules. Her statutory entitlement remains fully intact.

#### 4. Direct Conclusion
The employer's dismissal and refusal to pay maternity benefits are illegal under **Section 50**. The employer must pay her the full 16-week maternity benefit in cash.
