## L07.1 · Precaution in Case of Fire (Sec 62)

### 2023 Q.2(b) · 04 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** Briefly discuss the rules regarding the safety of workers in the workplace according to the Bangladesh Labor code 2006.

**Answer.**

Workplace safety is governed by **Chapter VI (Sections 61 to 78A)** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. The law protects factory workers from mechanical hazards, physical collapse, and fire disasters.

Key statutory safety rules under the Act include:

1. **Safety of Buildings and Machinery (Section 61):** The factory Inspector can order repairs on dangerous buildings or machinery. If danger is imminent, the Inspector can prohibit their use.
2. **Precaution in Case of Fire (Section 62):** Every floor must have an alternative emergency staircase and firefighting gear. Exit doors must open outward and stay unlocked during work. Exits must carry red Bangla signage. Establishments with 50 or more workers must hold regular mock fire drills.
3. **Fencing of Machinery (Section 63):** Employers must securely fence prime movers and flywheels. Transmission shafts and moving gears also require sturdy guards.
4. **Work on Machinery in Motion (Section 64):** Only trained adult male workers may service running machines. They must wear tight-fitting clothing and appear in the factory register.
5. **Cranes, Hoists, and Lifts (Sections 68–69):** Lifting machines must undergo regular inspection by a competent person. Cranes require annual checks. Hoists and lifts require checks every 6 months.
6. **Toxic Fumes and Explosive Gases (Sections 77–78):** Confined spaces require air tests before entry. Workers must use breathing apparatus. Flammable gas areas require explosion-proof enclosures.
7. **Personal Protective Equipment (Section 78A):** Employers must provide personal safety equipment free of cost. Workers must wear the supplied gear.

### L07.1 · Model Exam Question 1 · 05 Marks

*Suggested Topic (Chapter 3) · Sole Prescribed Safety Section for 2026 Final · Target: Part (b) or (c)*

**Question.** Detail the rules regarding precaution in case of fire under Section 62 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006.

**Answer.**

**Section 62** of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 sets mandatory fire safety precautions for every industrial establishment. The section contains 8 sub-clauses:

1. **Alternative Staircase and Equipment (`Sec 62(1)`):** Every establishment must provide at least one alternative staircase per floor. It must also keep prescribed firefighting appliances on every floor.
2. **Inspector Compliance Orders (`Sec 62(2)`):** The Inspector inspects fire exits and equipment under Fire Service license terms. If safety is deficient, the Inspector issues a written order with a strict deadline.
3. **Unlocked Outward-Opening Doors (`Sec 62(3)`):** Exit doors must never be locked or fastened during working hours. Workers must be able to open them immediately from inside. Exit doors must open outwards, unless they slide. Doors between rooms must open toward the nearest building exit. Sub-sections `(3a)` to `(3c)` ban any barrier or lock on escape routes.
4. **Red Bangla Fire Exit Signs (`Sec 62(4)`):** Emergency exits other than ordinary doors must be clearly marked. Markings must use red Bangla letters of adequate size, or other clear signs.
5. **Audible Fire Alarm (`Sec 62(5)`):** Establishments must install a clearly audible whistle or alarm. The alarm must instantly warn every worker in case of fire or danger.
6. **Unobstructed Free Passageways (`Sec 62(6)`):** Employers must maintain wide, unobstructed passageways in every room. Every worker must have free access to fire exits at all times.
7. **Worker Escape Training (`Sec 62(7)`):** Establishments must train workers on fire escape routines. This rule applies if over $10$ workers work above ground, or if flammables are used.
8. **Mock Fire Drills and Record Book (`Sec 62(8)`):** For factories and establishments with $50$ or more workers:
   - **Original 2006 Act Text (`Chapter-3.md`):** Required a mock fire drill *"at least once a year"*.
   - **2013 Amendment (`Act No. XXX of 2013`):** Shortened the interval to *"at least once every 6 months"*. Also, at least $18\%$ of the core workforce must receive formal firefighting training.
   - The employer must log every drill and inspection in an official record book.

> [!tip] Memory Hook (Sec 62(1)–(8) Sequence)
> Picture an escaping worker moving from inside the workshop out to safety:
> 1. **(1) Stairs & Extinguishers:** Spot the emergency staircase and fire gear on your floor.
> 2. **(2) Inspector Order:** Trust that the Inspector ordered these to safety standards.
> 3. **(3) Doors Unlocked & Outward:** Push the exit door open outward without keys or latches.
> 4. **(4) Red Bangla Signs:** Follow red Bangla signs along the escape path.
> 5. **(5) Audible Alarm:** Hear the loud warning whistle or siren sounding continuously.
> 6. **(6) Clear Aisles:** Sprint down wide, clear passageways without tripping over stock.
> 7. **(7) Escape Training:** Recall evacuation drills (mandatory if $> 10$ workers work upstairs or flammables exist).
> 8. **(8) Mock Fire Drill:** Gather at the assembly area ($\ge 50$ workers, every 6 months + $18\%$ trained crew).

## L07.2 · Machinery in Motion vs. Adolescents on Dangerous Machines (Sec 64 vs. Sec 40)

> [!note] Syllabus & Voice Note Status
> Shoaib Sir tested this comparative problem in **2025 Law-CT2 Q.2** (10 Marks). His voice note excluded non-fire machinery safety from the final exam to reduce student workload. This question provides the complete 10-mark comparison.

### 2025 Law-CT2 Q.2 · 10 Marks

*CO1 · Asked in: 2025 Law CT-2 · Excluded from 2026 Final by Shoaib Sir*

**Question.** Compare the safety requirements for work on or near machinery in motion with the requirements for dangerous machines involving adolescent workers. What similarities and differences can you identify?

**Answer.**

#### 1. Issue: Machinery in Motion vs. Adolescent Safety

The Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 sets clear rules to prevent machinery accidents. The core issue compares two safety regimes. First, safety rules for active machinery in motion. Second, protective restrictions for young adolescent workers on dangerous machines.

#### 2. Applicable Sections: Sec 64, Sec 40, and Sec 39(3)

1. **Section 64:** *Work on or near machinery in motion* (Chapter VI: Safety).
2. **Section 40:** *Employment of adolescent on dangerous machines* (Chapter III: Employment of Adolescent Worker).
3. **Section 39(3):** *Prohibition of adolescents cleaning or adjusting machinery in motion* (Chapter III: Employment of Adolescent Worker).

```text
                             Machinery Safety Provisions
                                          │
            ┌─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┐
            ▼                                                           ▼
     BLC Section 64                                            BLC Section 40 & 39(3)
(Work on Machinery in Motion)                             (Adolescents on Dangerous Machines)
  • Adult male workers only                                 • Adolescent workers (14 to < 18 years)
  • Tight-fitting protective clothing                       • Full instruction on dangers & precautions
  • Name entered in official register                       • Sufficient operational training
  • Absolute prohibition on women & adolescents             • OR continuous supervision by experienced adult
  • Covers examination, lubrication, belt mounting          • Sec 39(3) bars adolescents from moving machinery
```

#### 3. Comparative Analysis: Similarities and Differences

##### A. Statutory Provisions Breakdown

- **Under Section 64 (Work on or near machinery in motion):**
  - Only a **specially trained adult male worker** may examine, lubricate, or adjust running machinery.
  - The worker must wear **tight-fitting clothing** supplied by the employer.
  - The worker's name must be entered in the official factory register before starting work.
  - Belt handling on moving pulleys is restricted to belts under $15\text{ cm}$ wide. The belt joint must be flush or laced.
  - **Absolute Prohibition:** Women and adolescents cannot work on moving machinery parts (`Sec 64(1)` and `Sec 39(3)`).

- **Under Section 40 (Employment of adolescent on dangerous machines):**
  - An adolescent worker ($14$ to $< 18$ years, `Sec 2(8)`) cannot operate dangerous machines unless:
    1. They received **full instruction** about machine dangers and safety precautions.
    2. They completed **sufficient training** on the machine, **OR** work under **adequate supervision** by an experienced adult.
  - Under **Section 39(3)**, adolescents cannot clean, lubricate, or adjust machinery in motion. Prior training or supervision does not waive this ban.

##### B. Key Similarities

1. **Preventive Objective:** Both provisions protect workers from severe injury, limb loss, and fatal machinery accidents.
2. **Mandatory Instruction and Training:** Both rules require technical knowledge before a worker touches or operates machinery.
3. **Strict Employer Liability:** Both provisions place legal duties on employers and factory managers. Violations bring criminal penalties under Chapter XIX.
4. **Registry and Oversight:** Both provisions require official documentation for inspection by factory officials.

##### C. Key Differences

| Basis of Comparison | Work on Machinery in Motion (`Sec 64`) | Dangerous Machines & Adolescents (`Sec 40` & `Sec 39(3)`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **BLC Chapter** | Chapter VI (*Safety*) | Chapter III (*Employment of Adolescent Worker*) |
| **Governing Sections** | **Section 64** (general machinery safety) | **Section 40** (dangerous machines) & **Section 39(3)** (moving parts) |
| **Target Worker Class** | **Adult male workers only** ($\ge 18\text{ years}$) | **Adolescent workers** ($14$ to $< 18\text{ years}$) |
| **Eligibility Restrictions** | **Absolute bar** on female workers and adolescents; only certified adult males | Adolescents may operate dangerous machines if trained or supervised |
| **Operational State of Machine** | Machinery is **actively running / in motion** during inspection or adjustment | Machine is classified as **dangerous by Government notification**, covering general operation |
| **Clothing & PPE Mandate** | Explicit statutory requirement: worker **must wear tight-fitting clothing** | Focuses on safety instruction, operational awareness, and supervision |
| **Supervision Exception** | No exception: supervision cannot replace adult male status or tight clothing | Permissible **under adequate supervision** of an experienced person before training is complete |
| **Prohibition on Moving Parts** | Permitted exclusively for the registered adult male worker | **Section 39(3) strictly bans** adolescents from cleaning or adjusting moving machinery |

#### 4. Conclusion: Regulatory Differentiation

Section 64 enforces strict rules for running machinery. Only trained adult men in tight clothing can service it. In contrast, Section 40 offers a training path for adolescents on dangerous machines. Section 39(3) ensures they never touch running parts.

## L07.3 · Employer and Employee Safety Responsibilities

### 2023 Q.2(c) · 04 Marks

*Section A · CO1 · Asked in: 2023 Final*

**Question.** What responsibilities do employers and employees have regarding workplace safety regulation? Give your opinion.

**Answer.**

#### 1. Issue: Shared Legal Liability in Workplace Safety

Workplace safety rules divide legal duties between employers and workers. This shared responsibility prevents shop-floor hazards and catastrophic accidents.

#### 2. Applicable Sections: Chapter VI and Section 78A

Workplace safety duties appear in **Chapter VI (Sections 61 to 78A)** of the Act. Key rules include **Sections 61–64**, **Section 72**, and **Section 78A** (*Personal Protective Equipment*).

#### 3. Legal Analysis: Division of Employer and Worker Duties

##### A. Responsibilities of Employers

1. **Safe Physical Infrastructure (`Sec 61, 72`):** Employers must maintain sound buildings, safe floors, stairs, and clear access routes.
2. **Fire Safety Systems (`Sec 62`):** Employers must provide emergency stairs, unlocked doors, red Bangla signs, and fire alarms.
3. **Machine Safeguarding (`Sec 63, 64`):** Employers must fence moving machinery parts securely. They must restrict repairs on running machines to trained adult males in tight clothing.
4. **Provision of Personal Protective Equipment (`Sec 78A(1)`):** Employers must supply personal safety equipment free of cost. They must ensure its use and keep a safety record book.
5. **Safety Training (`Sec 62(7), 78A(3)`):** Employers must train workers on escape routes, occupational hazards, and emergency routines.

##### B. Responsibilities of Employees

1. **Mandatory Use of PPE (`Sec 78A(2)`):** Workers must wear all safety gear supplied by the employer.
2. **Statutory Worker Liability (`Sec 78A(2)`):** Workers are legally liable if they fail to use supplied safety gear.
3. **Active Drill Participation (`Sec 62(7)–(8)`):** Workers must participate in mock fire drills and learn emergency evacuation routes.
4. **Observance of Safety Rules (`Sec 64, 77`):** Workers must avoid loose clothing near moving gears. They must obey safety instructions and never enter confined spaces without authorization.

#### 4. Conclusion and Opinion: Balance of Safety Responsibility

Workplace safety cannot succeed through employer action alone. Employers must provide safe equipment, clear exits, and protective gear. But workers must actively follow safety rules on the shop floor. In my opinion, Section 78A strikes the right balance. It compels employers to supply safety gear free of charge. It also penalizes workers who refuse to wear it. Real safety requires employer compliance matched with worker personal discipline.
